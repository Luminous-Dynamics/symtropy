The two statements differ by date, source quality and reporting basis. The nominal numerical difference is 1.1 million barrels, but **it is not evidence of a verified 1.1-million-barrel drawdown**: the March answer was given in response to an explicit crude-oil question, while the September figure is still secondary-source-attested and has not been reconciled to a primary transcript, underlying stock-measurement date, inclusion rules or common inventory boundary. Neither statement supplies a tank-by-tank measurement ledger.

## 2. Days of cover are denominator-specific

The September report's “about 30 days” is described as crude-oil cover against crude-processing capacity, not as days of national refined-product consumption. Newsday's 5 October account of the parliamentary answer quotes the denominator as **200,000 barrels/day of crude-processing capacity**. A transparent arithmetic check gives:

\[
6{,}900{,}000\ \text{barrels} \div 200{,}000\ \text{barrels/day}
= 34.5\ \text{nominal processing days}.
\]

That is approximately 35 days at the reported rounded inputs, while the reported parliamentary statement says “about 30 days”. This is not enough to declare the Minister's figure wrong: the underlying usable-stock numerator, exact throughput denominator, rounding convention, measurement date and any operational deductions have not been published in the secondary account. The difference is a **reconciliation question**, not a proven error. Request the exact calculation and inputs.

The MP's question, as reported by Newsday, asked how many days of national consumption the stock represented; the reported answer instead grounds the estimate in crude-processing capacity. Keep those denominators distinct.

It must not be silently translated into:
- 30 days of diesel consumption;
- 30 days of all refined petroleum product demand;
- 30 days of immediately deliverable fuel at a specified depot or service zone; or
- 30 days of national essential-service continuity.

The Cabinet's 1 July 2026 statement described a *proposed policy target* of 60 days of net imports for both crude and refined products, rising to 90 days over the long term. That is a different policy denominator and stockholding obligation from “days of crude processing needs.” It is a future target, not proof of stock on hand.

## 3. Required source reconciliation before promotion

For a stock claim to become an evidence-qualified inventory input, request and reconcile:

1. Dated tank-by-tank stock ledger, with stable tank IDs, product and grade.
2. Physical measurement basis, temperature/density correction, instrument identity, calibration and uncertainty.
3. Quality status, holds, contamination/quarantine and release evidence.
4. Ownership and control, lien/pledge/contract status, and legal withdrawal authority.
5. Transfer route, pumping/berth/terminal availability and time-to-deliver to a named demand boundary.
6. Sustainable withdrawal rate and the downstream refinery/process compatibility for crude stocks.
7. Reconciliation of physical observation, audited accounting quantity, reported ministerial totals and source-record digests.
8. A separately declared denominator for every “days cover” calculation: crude processing demand, refined product demand by grade, net imports, or essential-service demand.

Until those fields are reconciled, retain each statement as an attributed claim, not measured accessible inventory. Do not convert crude barrels into diesel litres or service-hours without independently supported yield, conversion, logistics and time-boundary evidence.
