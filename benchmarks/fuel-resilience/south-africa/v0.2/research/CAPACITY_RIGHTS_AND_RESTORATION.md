# South African fuel capacity rights, market access and domestic restoration options

**Research cut:** 24 November 2026  
**Purpose:** separate the legal/commercial right to access infrastructure from its physical ability to deliver product, and separate future capacity-restoration targets from current production.

## 1. Island View Precinct — rights are not throughput

Parliament's 13 August 2026 statement describes Island View Precinct (IVP) in Durban as a strategic liquid-bulk/petroleum storage complex with access to 10 port berths, and says the majority of South Africa's fuel is imported through it.

The statement reports several distinct market-access milestones:
- The revised S79 directive includes a 10% allocation of total IVP capacity for new market participants.
- Terminal operators reportedly committed approximately 13% of their capacity, embedded in their terminal operator agreements.
- Later phases aim to raise Black participation to approximately 27.8% of total IVP capacity through land parcels and new liquid-bulk terminals.
- Parliament urges timely shipping/berth allocation and access to distribution networks, including the fuel pipeline.

These statements must **not** be added together or interpreted as the same physical-flow metric. They describe different policy/contract/phase relationships and may use different denominators or time bases. The source does not certify that 10%, 13% or 27.8% is currently available physical throughput.

### Required capacity state model

For every resource (berth, terminal tank, loading arm, pump, pipeline, power feeder, depot receipt window), SAFRB must distinguish:

1. **Nameplate/installed capacity:** engineering rating under defined conditions.
2. **Operable capacity:** capacity available after maintenance, outages and constraints.
3. **Legal/contractual access entitlement:** who may request or use the capacity and under what terms.
4. **Committed capacity:** a provider/operator has accepted a specific obligation for a time window.
5. **Reserved capacity:** provisionally held, not yet a completed physical transfer.
6. **Scheduled capacity:** planned slot/throughput, subject to operational changes.
7. **Available capacity:** the residual capacity the owner is willing and able to offer at a named revision/time.
8. **Delivered throughput:** independently evidenced physical flow/receipt.
9. **Usable product:** physically received, quality-released, grade-compatible and accessible at the receiving boundary.

Never infer one state from another. A 10% capacity access right does not mean 10% of annual throughput was delivered. A berth reservation is not a vessel discharge. A discharged cargo is not usable stock until its product/quality/access gates pass.

## 2. Strategic stock statements — preserve attribution

In the National Assembly on 25 March 2026, the Minister said the Strategic Fuel Fund held eight million barrels of strategic fuel stock and said it was not being used pending a real crisis. In the same answer, the Minister described processed products as approximately 60% imported and 40% processed by South African refineries and Sasol.

These are official parliamentary statements, but they are not a stock audit. The cited answer does not identify whether the eight million barrels are crude, refined products or a mixture; it does not state grade, physical tank boundary, measurement timestamp, quality, accessibility, withdrawal rate or current stock level.

Therefore:
- preserve the eight-million-barrel statement as an attributed, dated claim;
- do not convert it into diesel litres or diesel days of cover;
- do not merge it with the 60/40 processed-product sourcing statement;
- request primary SFF tank, product, ownership, quality and accessibility records before any reconciliation.

## 3. Refinery restoration — model as a staged option, not current supply

CEF's 23 September 2026 presentation says the Mossel Bay GTL refinery and FA Platform have remained offline and on preservation since November 2020. The refinery-reinstatement business case was completed in August 2026. CEF describes:
- phase 1 target: approximately 18,000 barrels/day, estimated R5.8bn investment;
- phase 2 target: approximately 46,000 barrels/day, estimated additional R8.5bn investment.

The wording does not establish whether the 46,000 barrels/day is total phase-2 output or an incremental addition. SAFRB preserves the target as stated and does not add the two values.

CEF also reports material financial, operational and liquidity challenges at PetroSA, and states product suppliers increasingly require letters of credit, prepayments or parent guarantees, while banks/insurers are cautious and collateral/terms can be restrictive. These are relevant execution dependencies for a restoration option, not proof that a project will or will not complete.

### Required staged project-evidence gates

A future capacity option should contribute zero firm production until its actual state is evidenced:
1. feasibility and approved business case;
2. funded capital and financing instruments;
3. feedstock/energy supply secured;
4. engineering design and independent condition assessment;
5. procurement and construction progress;
6. mechanical completion and commissioning;
7. measured sustained throughput by product/grade;
8. quality release and downstream logistics qualification.

Project targets belong in a scenario/planning layer. Commissioned and independently measured output belongs in the physical supply balance. Project expenditure and financing obligations belong in Mycelix's financial evidence/commitment model.

## 4. Cross-stack ownership

- **Symtropy:** physical capacity, operating envelopes, flow constraints, time-expanded dispatch, asset-condition scenarios and deterministic replay.
- **Mycelix:** provider identity, legal/contractual access, entitlement, reservation/commitment, finance and settlement obligations; providers remain authority for their own capacity.
- **Sol-Atlas:** port/terminal/berth/pipeline spatial context, coverage, geospatial evidence and rights-aware infrastructure projections.
- **Symthaea:** source conflict detection, hypothesis comparison, decision-focused value of information and human-review briefs. It cannot turn a forecast or document claim into physical fact.

## 5. Benchmark scenarios

1. A terminal has installed capacity but no available berth window.
2. A provider has a legal access entitlement but no current offer/commitment.
3. Ten percent is allocated for new entrants but physical capacity is already operationally constrained.
4. Operators report a 13% capacity commitment but time windows overlap or resource identities are ambiguous.
5. New-terminal development reaches a land/access milestone but has not commissioned.
6. A cargo has a valid berth slot but pipeline or receiving-tank constraints prevent inland movement.
7. Refinery restoration is announced/funded/under construction/commissioned at separate stages; no future output is counted before verified production.
8. An apparently independent supplier relies on the same berth, tank farm, power feeder or pipeline.

## 6. Required metrics

Report separately:
- installed vs operable capacity;
- entitlement coverage and exclusion rate;
- committed/reserved/scheduled/available slot overlap;
- berth and terminal utilization;
- throughput at each physical boundary;
- quality-released product;
- time from entitlement to first delivered molecule;
- capacity-access concentration and allocation-authority concentration;
- project stage conversion / schedule variance;
- source contradiction and missing-evidence rates.
