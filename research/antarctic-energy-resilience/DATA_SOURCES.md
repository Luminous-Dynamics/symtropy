# ANTARCTIC-ENERGY-001 — source and benchmark data inventory

**Prepared:** 2026-10-22  
**Scope:** evidence for operational energy and resilience benchmarks, not a station design or equipment recommendation.

## P0 — Published operational observations

### Australian Antarctic Division Annual Report 2024–25, Table 73

Source: https://www.dcceew.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/annual-report-2024-25.pdf  
Machine-readable extraction: `data/aad_2024_25_annual_observations.json`

The structured file records a small, source-attributed set of Table 73 values. Among them:
- 5,612,426 kWh electricity generated by diesel;
- 1,004,020 kWh renewable electricity generated from Mawson wind and Casey solar;
- 2,500,150 litres of Special Antarctic Blend operational diesel covering electricity generation, vehicles, plant, incinerators and boilers;
- 8,264,800 litres of Distillate Marine Fuel for shipping;
- 1,391,142 litres of aviation turbine kerosene for aviation operations, plus a separately reported 150,350 litres for the Million Year Ice Core operation.

The ratio of the two listed electricity-generation rows is about 15.2% renewable. It is explicitly a *derived share of those two reported electricity rows only*, not the share of all Antarctic energy, all mission fuel, useful heat, or all nations' operations.

Boundary rules:
1. Do not infer generator efficiency from combined operational diesel that includes vehicles, plant, incinerators and boilers.
2. Keep station electricity, station operational diesel, shipping DMA and aviation ATK as different metrics/fuel streams; do not add raw litres of dissimilar fuels into one unqualified total.
3. Do not double-count the separately noted MYIC SAB estimate where the report says it is included in station fuel.
4. The report's total water value is only reported through 1 April 2025; it is partial-year.
5. Annual aggregates lack hourly alignment among weather, load, fuel, failures and storage. They can benchmark annual reporting consistency and model boundary—not dispatch accuracy, polar-night service reliability or causal savings.
6. The file contains factual extracted values, not the PDF. Record the precise report version, table and access date; confirm reuse/licensing terms before redistributing the source document.

## P0 — Historical measured monthly station electricity

Australian Antarctic Data Centre, SOE Indicator 59: https://data.aad.gov.au/aadc/soe/display_indicator.cfm?soe_id=59

The metadata describes monthly electricity use at Casey, Davis, Mawson and Macquarie Island, read directly from station gauges and recorded in station plant inspectors' monthly reports. The display page's table reports January 1986–February 2016 (1,432 points), but its metadata summary says the temporal range starts 1 January 1990. This inconsistency must be resolved against the raw archive before using the early years; do not silently choose one date. The interactive data view says login is required, while the linked AADC archive endpoint is identified as a download resource; access still needs to be verified during ingestion. The display page contains a CC BY attribution statement and the AADC site separately states CC BY 4.0—preserve the exact record and confirm the applicable data terms at ingestion.

This is historical and not a modern four-station census. It provides an opportunity for monthly load-profile tests, seasonal-change analysis and external validation of a synthetic service-load model, but no hourly balancing or actual renewable availability.

## P0 — Historical monthly station generator/boiler fuel

Australian Antarctic Data Centre, SOE Indicator 56: https://data.aad.gov.au/aadc/soe/display_indicator.cfm?soe_id=56  
Metadata record: https://data.aad.gov.au/metadata/records/SOE_generator_boiler_fuel_usage

The metadata describes monthly litres used by generator sets and boilers at Casey, Davis, Mawson and Macquarie Island, from gauge readings in plant inspectors' monthly reports. It says a copy covering January 1993–February 2016 is publicly available for download, while the temporal-coverage field separately says 1990-01-01–2016-02-29; resolve the difference against the raw file and do not assume that 916 monthly values imply a complete station-by-month rectangular panel. The AADC citation-instructions page gives Ratcliffe, Sheers and Bonnice (2001), Version 1, DOI 10.26179/5d2ea1b98cba3, while the full fuel metadata page currently displays a citation year of 2019 for the same DOI. The electricity metadata gives 2001 and lists a 2019 revision date. Preserve this citation-year discrepancy as unresolved metadata and ask the originator which bibliographic form to use; do not assume that a record revision date is the dataset publication year. CC BY 4.0 is declared. The notes warn that fuel varies with ambient temperature and that a past failure to resupply fuel affected consumption at Casey. That makes this series particularly useful for testing seasonality, extreme conditions and resupply disruptions, once the archived data file is actually retrieved and its station coverage/units checked.

It is a combined generator/boiler measure, not fuel for all station vehicles, incinerators, ships or aircraft; do not fit a generator-only fuel curve to it.

## P1 — Weather and renewable-resource context

### Copernicus/ECMWF ERA5 hourly single-level reanalysis

Dataset: https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/datasets/reanalysis-era5-single-levels?tab=overview  
DOI: 10.24381/cds.adbb2d47  
Licence: CC-BY as shown on the dataset portal.

ERA5 provides hourly gridded atmospheric, land-surface and ocean-wave estimates from 1940 to the present on a 0.25° regular latitude/longitude grid for the reanalysis, with a coarser grid for uncertainty products. It includes temperature, wind and solar-related variables suitable for constructing historical weather scenarios. It is a reanalysis, not a station-level mast measurement. Antarctic complex terrain, katabatic flow, coastal winds, icing and local wake effects can be under-resolved at this scale. It must not be converted directly into station-turbine output without a documented turbine curve, hub-height treatment, icing/outage model and validation against site data.

Use ERA5 to stratify whole weather years, find low-wind/low-solar spells and perform train/holdout splits. Preserve data version and retrieval settings. The portal requires registration/acceptance of terms for downloads.

### Station-specific weather

Prefer on-station wind mast, irradiance, temperature, snow/icing and power-control telemetry where the responsible programme makes it accessible and licensing permits. Reanalysis and station measurements are different evidence classes. Do not reconstruct missing weather measurements from annual generation numbers.

## P1 — Published Antarctic microgrid benchmarks

- Hybrid electric–hydrogen–thermal storage and multi-timescale dispatch: *Energy Conversion and Management* (2025), DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enconman.2025.120353
- Two-stage robust dispatch for an Antarctic islanded microgrid with wind turbine reserve integration: IEEE *Transactions on Smart Grid*, early access 20 August 2026, DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/TSG.2026.3725835
- Princess Elisabeth Antarctica station's wind/solar/smart-grid design and demand reduction: https://www.antarcticstation.org/station . Treat its declared occupancy/seasonal operations profile explicitly; do not assume it is a year-round polar-night proof.

These papers provide comparison methods and model structures, not automatically accessible calibration data. Extract a benchmark only after its case values, model boundaries, data availability, solver budget and reported outputs have been verified. Do not claim reproduction from an abstract alone.

## Data admission pipeline

For each candidate artifact:
1. Save the original locally before processing, where terms allow; record original filename, publisher URL, access date, licence, SHA-256, version and source table/page.
2. Create a source manifest identifying station/site, reporting period, temporal resolution, units, aggregation level, measurement method, missingness, and whether data are reported, measured, derived, simulated or inferred.
3. Separate electricity, useful heat, generator fuel, boiler fuel, vehicles, incinerators, shipping, aviation, water, headcount/person-days and research load. Any aggregation requires an explicit definition and compatible boundary.
4. Keep raw data immutable; normalized rows must have a source-row/page reference and transformation version.
5. Detect missing months, duplicate periods, meter resets, reporting-boundary changes and inconsistent units. Do not silently interpolate gaps.
6. Freeze complete weather years/operational periods into train/tune/holdout sets before policy tuning, avoiding adjacent-time leakage.
7. Publish a missing-data and comparability report. If a source cannot be retrieved, preserve it as a candidate with access status—not as benchmark data.

## Benchmark implication

The first defensible real-data benchmark is not to promise a fuel-saving percentage. It is to demonstrate:
- correct reproduction of published aggregates and historical monthly data where downloadable;
- correct time-boundary, scope and unit handling;
- load/weather/fuel correlation analysis with confounders reported;
- a fair split between model calibration and held-out validation;
- an uncertainty-aware scenario corpus for polar night, storms and resupply outages;
- a robust dispatch tournament against rule-based, deterministic MPC and published robust-optimization baselines under matched data and evaluation budgets.

Only after those gates can a percentage fuel-saving target be preregistered. A synthetic fixture cannot prove operational performance.

## Data-stewardship requirement

The AADC citation instructions state that it is expected practice for intended users to contact the data originator to discuss intended use before applying Australian Antarctic Program data. The metadata describes the relevant historical electricity/fuel series as CC BY 4.0, but the project should preserve both the licence and this explicit stewardship instruction. Contact should be confirmed and documented before the historical observations are used for analysis. A retrieval attempt or publicly reachable URL is not equivalent to permission/consultation completion. See https://data.aad.gov.au/metadata/records/SOE_generator_boiler_fuel_usage/citation and https://data.aad.gov.au/metadata/records/SOE_SFU.

The fail-closed local acquisition utility is `scripts/acquire-antarctic-sources.sh`. Raw files default to a user cache outside the git worktree, must not be committed automatically, are checked for accidental HTML responses, and receive an acquisition record plus SHA-256. `list-usgs` retrieves USGS ScienceBase release metadata so the actual files can be listed before selection. Raw release data are still not ingested by this repository until the local manifest proves the bytes and subsequent schema/licence validation succeeds.
