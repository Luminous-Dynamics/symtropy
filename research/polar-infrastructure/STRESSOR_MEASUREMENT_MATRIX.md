# Polar stressor-to-measurement and falsification matrix

**Status:** research plan. The 15 stressors remain `not_run`. This matrix names the minimum observables and falsification cases needed to move each one from a label to a test. It does not assign universal numerical limits.

A scenario becomes testable only when its boundary conditions, units, model/data version, expected failure, admissibility limits and independent reference are specified. If the physical model for a row does not exist, mark it `blocked_by_model`; do not infer a passing outcome from the service scorer alone.

| ID / stressor | Required observable(s) | Minimum model/data evidence before scoring | Deliberate negative control / falsification probe | Context limit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| POLAR-001 Polar-night renewable deficit | Chronological load, available generation, storage state, fuel reserve and service delivery | Site/time-specific resource and demand series; component constraints; energy-balance residual | Extend the low-renewable interval beyond stored energy and test that declared critical service shortfall is exposed | Arctic land / Antarctic station separately |
| POLAR-002 Wind icing and derating | Wind/resource conditions, icing state, power curve, de-icing/maintenance load | Manufacturer or field-supported power curve and operating envelope for the relevant icing condition | Inject icing derating; a control that keeps nameplate output unchanged must be detected as a defective input/model | Site-specific Arctic / Antarctic |
| POLAR-003 Cold battery derating | Cell/pack temperature, SOC, usable kWh, charge/discharge limits, thermal-management energy | Validated temperature-dependent battery curves, control and thermal envelope | Compare cold-condition run with unchanged nameplate power; if no derating model exists, the scenario is blocked rather than passed | Include mobile platforms only where equipment/environment supports it |
| POLAR-004 Prime-power trip | Trip/fault timestamp, available reserve, start/transfer timeline, delivered power and critical loads | Generator/protection/startup constraints and backup-transfer sequence from qualified technical data | Disable backup start or transfer and confirm the episode shows the resulting critical-service shortfall | Arctic land / Antarctic station |
| POLAR-005 Inverter/controller loss | Control state, watchdog/alarm, fallback mode, inverter/protection states and service | Versioned control configuration, protection model and declared fallback dependency path | Remove the primary controller and separately remove its supposedly independent fallback; common dependency must not be counted as redundancy | Each topology separately |
| POLAR-006 Delayed fuel/resupply | Opening inventory, time-resolved burn, usable reserve, shipment arrival and constraints | Actual inventory/burn basis or explicitly synthetic scenario; logistics lead-time assumptions and bounds | Withhold the assumed shipment through the locked delay interval; reject an inventory ledger that replenishes before the declared arrival | Remote communities/stations; distinguish transport systems |
| POLAR-007 Communications outage | Link/state telemetry, message age, local alarms, safe control state, recovery time | Communication-dependency graph, loss detection thresholds and offline procedures | Partition the supervisory link and then a shared fallback link; a stale “last known good” command must not be treated as a fresh acknowledgement | Network-specific |
| POLAR-008 Sensor drift / stale telemetry | Measurement timestamp, uncertainty, residual against independent reference, alarm and fallback state | Sensor error/refresh specification, calibration records and declared detection/fallback rules | Inject slowly drifting but plausible readings and stale timestamps; point-in-time validity must fail closed | Sensors and channels explicitly declared |
| POLAR-009 Cold restart / black start | Ordered startup events, prerequisites, initial SOC/fuel, start time, stable service recovery | Qualified startup dependency graph, black-start capability and startup reserve | Remove one required startup resource or start permissive and confirm recovery is not falsely declared complete | Equipment- and site-specific |
| POLAR-010 Water/wastewater freeze | Flow, pressure, pipe/plant temperature, inventory, quality and service hours | Utility topology, thermal/hydraulic model and locally applicable water/wastewater quality limits | Freeze/interrupt the primary path and disable the assumed redundant path; service continuity cannot be inferred from pump availability alone | Arctic / Antarctic utility topology; approved quality limits |
| POLAR-011 Envelope heat loss | Zone temperatures, envelope flux, infiltration/ventilation, humidity/condensation and heat supply | Calibrated building thermal model and measured or justified boundary conditions | Increase leakage/heat loss and remove active heat; verify the model predicts declining stored thermal margin, not static comfort by assumption | Building/platform construction-specific |
| POLAR-012 Permafrost thaw / settlement | Ground temperature, displacement/settlement, foundation demand and utility strain | Site geotechnical study, ground monitoring, foundation model and design-life assumptions | Apply the sourced thaw/settlement envelope; a fixed-foundation model with no ground response must be marked blocked, not robust | **Arctic land only** unless site-specific geotechnical evidence justifies otherwise |
| POLAR-013 Snow/ice access blockage | Route availability, egress time, clearance state, accessible tools/parts and emergency access | Site layout, local wind/snow envelope, route/egress plan and maintenance access requirements | Block the principal route and test the explicitly declared alternate; do not assume emergency access remains available | Site-specific Arctic / Antarctic |
| POLAR-014 Correlated common-cause failure | Component/dependency graph, shared feeder/controller/fuel/cooling paths, simultaneous state transitions | Versioned dependency graph and justified joint-failure scenarios; probabilities only if defensible | Fail the shared dependency of nominally redundant assets; the model must not count both as independent surviving units | Every design topology |
| POLAR-015 Gloved maintenance / delayed spares | Fault diagnosis time, task steps, tool count, PPE/interface constraints, spare arrival and repair duration | Observed task data or explicitly labelled task model; inventory and resupply assumptions | Remove a required spare and add a declared access/tool constraint; instantaneous repair or invisible parts must be detected | Actual operating tasks/PPE; do not generalize across groups |

## Cross-cutting acceptance gates

1. **Conservation and invariants:** energy, fuel, water and storage balances must close within a documented tolerance justified by data resolution and model class. Report the residual; don't hide it.
2. **Time coupling:** retain chronological state across steps (storage, thermal mass, inventory, failure/repair and startup). Independently shuffled timesteps are not a valid adequacy test.
3. **Reference comparison:** every numerical subsystem needs a transparent baseline plus a reference implementation, manufacturer curve, approved engineering calculation, or observed trace suitable for its claim.
4. **Uncertainty:** run justified parameter ranges/sensitivity analysis; a point estimate without an uncertainty basis does not support worst-case claims. Scenario probabilities are optional and must not be invented.
5. **Fault-harness efficacy:** each test family includes at least one deliberately defective model/controller and verifies that the observable or gate catches the known flaw.
6. **Reproducibility:** record exact code/data/model/configuration digests, units, time bases, scenario IDs, commands, outputs, failures and exclusions.
7. **Claim ceiling:** passing code tests only validates the tested software contract. Move to S2/S3/S4 only when the distinct acquisition, independent replay and domain-appropriate external evidence requirements are actually met.

## Industry alignment

The [IEC TS 62898-3-5:2026 public scope](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/84175) names HIL, commissioning and periodic testing of microgrid monitoring, control and energy-management systems. Its detailed test families include dispatch optimization, forecast, demand-side integration, alarm/anti-maloperation, voltage/frequency regulation, device switching, mode transitions, power control, islanding detection, black start and end-to-end HIL. This project matrix is a planning crosswalk, not a clause-level standards assessment; the full applicable standard, licensed requirements and qualified reviewers must drive any actual conformity test.

A 2026 review of renewable microgrid reliability emphasizes chronological/time-coupled adequacy and correlated resource scarcity, reinforcing the need to test complete trajectories rather than only installed capacity or one averaged reliability score: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590174526003612
