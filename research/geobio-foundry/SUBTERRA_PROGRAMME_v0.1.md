# Subterra — Planetary Subterranean Intelligence, Exploration and Infrastructure

**Programme version:** 0.1  
**Date:** 2026-11-16  
**Home:** Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy, research-only  
**Relationship:** A related programme within GeoBio Foundry; not a replacement for it

## 1. Mission

Develop reusable methods and engineering systems to discover, characterize, explore, access, monitor and eventually use subterranean environments across Earth, the Moon, Mars, Venus and selected icy or small bodies.

The programme treats the subsurface as a distinct domain of geology and operations—not merely a place to install a habitat. Potential functions include:
- scientific archives of volcanic, tectonic, impact, aqueous and cryogenic processes;
- environmental shielding and thermal buffering;
- protected robotic staging and logistics;
- resource prospecting and carefully bounded extraction;
- engineered galleries, storage and industrial districts;
- eventually, independently qualified pressurized modules and biological/industrial production.

**Subterra does not presume that a candidate opening is a cave, that a cave is connected, that a connected void is accessible, or that an accessible void is structurally or biologically suitable.**

## 2. Seven paradigm shifts

### 2.1 From cave detection to evidence-qualified subsurface understanding

Separate six claims:
1. Surface morphology: what is directly visible?
2. Geological origin: what formation mechanisms remain plausible?
3. Subsurface connectivity: is there an entrance-connected void?
4. Accessibility: can a robot reach, enter, traverse and leave?
5. Engineering suitability: can a declared structure or operation be supported within a validated envelope?
6. Use suitability: is the site appropriate for a specific scientific, industrial, storage or habitation purpose?

These claims must have independent evidence and confidence. Never collapse them into one weighted “site score”. A site may have high scientific value but poor industrial accessibility, or strong morphological evidence but unknown connectivity.

### 2.2 From uniform tunnels to data-derived morphology

Model profiles, branches, chambers, constrictions, collapse zones, floor roughness, boulders, fractures, roof and wall geometry, and longitudinal variation. Do not assume cylindrical or constant cross-sections.

The November 2026 Ape Cave study is a useful analogue: it mapped nearly 3.4 km, extracted more than 1,400 cross-sections, and identified five recurring profile classes. Its result motivates data-derived geometry, not direct transfer of Earth material properties to the Moon or Mars.

### 2.3 From single-robot exploration to resilient heterogeneous teams

Treat exploration as a system of complementary agents:
- a rim/base station preserving the surface-orbiter link;
- a tethered scout for steep descent and controlled recovery;
- a legged or tracked mapper for rough floors and boulder fields;
- a small aerial robot only where the atmosphere, gravity, propulsion and dust environment permit;
- relay nodes and instrument packages;
- a recovery or service robot where the mission architecture can support it.

The fleet must be able to lose a robot without automatically losing all mission data, localization or retreat capability.

### 2.4 From mapping coverage to actionable situational awareness

A dense map is not mission success. The system must identify and localize hazards, geological boundaries, access routes, scientific targets, resource indicators, communication relays and safe staging areas—and retain the evidence supporting each detection.

This directly addresses lessons from the DARPA Subterranean Challenge: broad map coverage did not guarantee detection, localization or reporting of all mission-relevant artifacts.

### 2.5 From communication as a radio problem to communication as mission geometry

Plan line-of-sight, relay placement, robot topology, tether routes, link budgets, store-and-forward data recovery and mission partitioning together with exploration paths.

Candidate relay strategies include:
- breadcrumb relay nodes;
- robot-to-robot mesh;
- tethered fiber/high-gain backhaul;
- store-and-forward offline operation;
- communication-aware autonomous relay deployment.

These are established subterranean robotics strategies. Subterra's opportunity is to make their placement jointly optimize scientific value, navigation confidence, retreat options, energy and data integrity.

### 2.6 From geological shelter to independently engineered envelopes

Natural rock may provide shielding and environmental buffering. It is not presumed to be a pressure vessel, fire compartment, thermal barrier or certified structural support.

Human or biological use requires engineered, independently sealed compartments; qualified penetrations and anchors; pressure and thermal cycling; leak detection; fire and contamination isolation; and independent acceptance testing. Geological stability and engineered pressure-boundary integrity remain separate claims.

### 2.7 From universal deployment to planetary-specific design envelopes

The same information model can travel across bodies; the same hardware, process or safety case cannot be assumed to transfer. Gravity, atmospheric pressure and chemistry, temperature, radiation, dust, volatile inventory, geology, illumination, communication and planetary-protection constraints are body- and site-specific.

## 3. Subsurface environment taxonomy

| Environment class | Defining process/structure | Representative targets | Key distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volcanic conduit | Drained or partly drained lava channel/tube/pyroduct | Earth, Moon, Mars, possible Venus | Geometry and roof condition are highly variable |
| Collapse access point | Pit, skylight, collapse chain, subsidence | Moon, Mars, Venus candidates | An opening is not proof of an accessible connected cave |
| Dissolution/karst void | Soluble rock removed by aqueous or other chemical processes | Earth; candidate Martian settings | Origin and past fluid history need evidence |
| Tectonic/fracture void | Faults, fissures, dilation zones and connected discontinuities | Earth, Mars and other rocky bodies | Often discontinuous and mechanically complex |
| Ice-related void | Cavities, crevasses, conduits or basal/subglacial voids | Earth analogues; icy bodies as exploratory targets | Ice rheology, thermal history and preservation constraints dominate |
| Engineered gallery | Excavated and reinforced subsurface space | Earth first; future planetary installations | Engineering records and as-built verification required |
| Small-body porosity/void | Fractured, porous or rubble-pile interior | Asteroids and cometary bodies | Not equivalent to a stable cave; anchoring and microgravity dominate |

## 4. Planetary applicability matrix

This is a research-priority matrix, not a claim that all listed structures have been confirmed or are accessible.

| Body | Candidate subsurface environments | Initial research role | Principal constraints |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earth | Lava tubes, karst, mines, tunnels, volcanic caves, ice caves | Instrument validation, robotics, physical analogues, commercial subterranean infrastructure | Heterogeneous geology, groundwater, hazards, access and land/heritage rights |
| Moon | Mare lava tubes, skylights, pits, regolith-covered voids, fractures | First extraterrestrial geophysical and robotic validation target | Vacuum, radiation, dust, extreme thermal cycles, low gravity, no natural atmospheric pressure |
| Mars | Volcanic cave candidates, collapse pits, possible karstic/water-modified and ice-related settings | Orbital-to-in-situ evidence pipeline; science and preservation-led robotic exploration | Planetary protection, dust, cold, thin CO2 atmosphere, unknown interior conditions |
| Venus | Radar-inferred pyroduct candidate(s), volcanic collapse structures | Remote-sensing and high-temperature geophysical research; long-term robotic science | Extreme surface temperature/pressure and corrosive atmosphere; no habitat assumption |
| Mercury | Hypothesized volcanic/tectonic voids and polar shadowed subsurface settings | Remote-sensing hypothesis development only until better target evidence exists | Solar/thermal extremes, low gravity, limited operational windows and sparse subsurface characterization |
| Europa / Enceladus | Icy-shell fractures, possible cavities and conduits above/within ice | Scientific reconnaissance and preservation-first exploration | Very high planetary-protection sensitivity; subsurface ocean access is not the same as an accessible cave |
| Titan | Cryogenic/ice-related or cryovolcanic structures as hypotheses | Long-horizon geology and atmospheric/cryogenic robotics research | Very cold environment, hydrocarbon chemistry, low solar energy and uncertain cavity evidence |
| Asteroids / comets | Porosity, fractures, rubble voids, regolith cavities | Contact mechanics, anchoring, interior characterization and resource science | Microgravity, unknown cohesion, weak anchoring, rebound and uncertain internal structure |

### 4.1 Planetary protection and preservation

Mars and potentially habitable icy worlds require destination- and activity-specific planetary-protection review. Subterra must distinguish:
- uncrewed geophysical reconnaissance;
- contact with potentially sensitive materials;
- sample acquisition and return;
- installation of equipment;
- introduction of terrestrial organisms or biological production.

The default programme is uncrewed and non-biological. Engineered biology remains contained in qualified reactors and is excluded from scientifically sensitive environments unless a formal planetary-protection case explicitly permits the activity. Europa and Enceladus are preservation-first science targets, not near-term industrial biofactory sites.

## 5. Research architecture

### SubterraAtlas — spatial and geological evidence
Versioned planetary site records, native-scale source data, coordinate frames, sensor calibration, topography, geology, morphology, environmental measurements, scientific value and preservation constraints. Source observations remain immutable.

### Subterra Evidence Graph
Links a surface feature, competing geological-origin hypotheses, geophysical observations, inferred subsurface geometry, robot traverses, hazards, environmental measurements and engineering assessments. Every node records provenance, units, uncertainty, validity interval and evidence tier.

### Subterra Morphometry
Stores non-uniform cross-section profiles, chambers, branches, bottlenecks, boulders, fractures, surface roughness, roof/wall/floor geometry and surveyed/unobserved intervals. Morphology is not a stability assessment.

### Subterra Explorer
Plans sensing and multi-robot exploration, including descent, tether/anchor assessment, local SLAM, communication relays, store-and-forward, scientific target inspection and verified retreat.

### Subterra Engineering
Evaluates geomechanics, thermal conditions, radiation shielding, pressure-module siting, engineered support, penetrations, fire/contamination zoning, utilities and construction logistics within explicit model domains.

### GeoBio Foundry
Evaluates local resources, process routes, material/energy/exergy balances and contained biological/abiotic production only after the site and resource claims are sufficiently qualified.

| Mycelix | Resource, sample, robot, capacity and access attestations; inter-operator coordination | Physical presence, availability or quality of a resource from a participant's unsupported claim |

## 7. Staged site-evidence progression

These are distinct claim states, not an ordinal “safety level”:
1. `candidate_feature`: a surface morphology merits investigation.
2. `multi_sensor_supported`: independent modalities support a potential subsurface interpretation.
3. `local_geophysics_available`: local sensing constrains geometry or material structure.
4. `access_demonstrated`: a robot has physically reached a defined access point.
5. `interior_mapped_within_extent`: an entrance-connected interior has been directly surveyed over a declared extent.
6. `engineering_assessed_for_envelope`: structural, environmental and operational analyses exist for a declared use and envelope.
7. `independently_qualified_for_envelope`: the declared system and envelope have passed independent physical qualification.

A site can advance in one dimension and remain unknown in another. No stage automatically implies habitability or permission for biological activity.

## 8. First terrestrial demonstrator

Use a surveyed, legally accessible terrestrial lava tube or engineered subterranean analogue. Begin uncrewed:
- independently registered 3D reference survey;
- terrestrial LiDAR/photogrammetry plus inertial and thermal data;
- surveyed cross-section stations and explicit blind/occluded regions;
- one mobile mapper and a second robot or deployed relay;
- controlled communication shadow zones and link interruptions;
- repeat traverse for map consistency and drift assessment;
- seeded but safe navigation hazards;
- task targets that require detection, localization, evidence recording and successful data return;
- independent ground truth withheld from the mapping/planning system.

Do not perform roof alteration, pressure testing, biological release or occupied operations as part of this first demonstrator.

## 9. Definition of a meaningful breakthrough

A breakthrough is not merely a larger point cloud or longer robot traverse. It is a reproducible increase in the amount of **decision-relevant, independently verified subsurface understanding** per unit of mission time, energy, mass and risk, while preserving unknowns, communicating evidence, and returning the robot and data.

The first research objective is to demonstrate that the same evidence model and benchmark protocol can compare different geological classes without pretending that their physics or operating envelopes are interchangeable.
