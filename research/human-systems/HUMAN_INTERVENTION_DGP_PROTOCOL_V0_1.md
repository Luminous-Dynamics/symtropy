# Human intervention and exposure DGP protocol v0.1

**Status:** scenario protocol and machine-readable fixture; no causal estimator or human-valid intervention model is implemented here.  
**Parent:** [HUMAN-SYSTEMS-SIM-001 / Symtropy #5625](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/5625)  
**Track:** [HUMAN-INTERVENTION-DGP-001 / Symtropy #5707](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/5707)  
**Generic causal benchmark owner:** [Symthaea #11954](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/issues/11954)

## 1. Motivation: separate intervention assignment from exposure and response

A synthetic human-day or human-operator simulator can make a category error if it collapses:
- a recommended or randomized action;
- whether the action was delivered;
- whether the simulated person/environment actually received the exposure;
- whether latent state changed;
- what the sensor managed to record; and
- what the candidate system knew when it chose its next action.

A scheduled rest period may not be taken. A light command may be blocked by geometry or eye orientation. An exposure may happen but its sensor observation may be missing or delayed. A latent response may be present but unidentifiable from the received observations.

These are separate events in separate causal/time domains.

- sensor observation events generated by the HOBS observation process;

Symthaea #11954 owns:

- observed-data versus oracle-truth separation;

- generic estimand accounting and causal benchmark oracle.

Symthaea #12407 owns the FEP observation-event semantics. Symthaea #9817 owns decision-focused active sensing and sensing cost. Symtropy #4392 owns matched benchmark methodology, scientific adequacy and runtime accounting.

Do not implement a second IPW estimator, survival-analysis stack, potential-outcomes oracle, FEP belief state or generic active-sensing planner in this track.

## 3. Typed event chain and time

Use a sequence like:

```
assignment (randomization / recommendation)
      |
      v
delivery attempt (authority, availability, feasibility)
      |
      v
delivered action (what actually occurred)
      |
      v
physical exposure (what reached the synthetic person/world)
      |
      v
latent response (world's state transition)
      |
      v
sensor capture (possibly noisy / absent)
      |
      v
observation availability (when candidate can use it)
      |
      v
candidate decision
      |
      v
outcome / competing event / censoring
      |
      v
oracle-only potential outcome scoring
```

Keep assignment time, delivery time, exposure time, response/event time, capture time, availability time and decision time explicit. The candidate must not use future observations or outcome labels. The evaluator can access potential outcomes and hidden states only through a separate oracle interface/process.

## 4. Initial context and explicit non-goals

Start with non-clinical actions in human-day/operator scenarios:
- scheduling, delaying or canceling a rest opportunity;
- task demand, interruption or task-order changes;
- simulated environmental light exposure;
- activity/movement opportunities or physical constraints;
- acquiring an additional sensor measurement.

All first parameters and outcomes are invented synthetic fixtures. This protocol does not model medication, diagnosis, psychiatric trajectories, treatment assignment or treatment recommendations. A synthetic effect is only a declared mathematical mechanism. It is not evidence that a real-world action improves sleep, health, cognition, productivity or safety.

## 5. Scenario catalogue and identification controls

The machine-readable catalogue contains 11 initial scenarios: [HUMAN_INTERVENTION_DGP_SCENARIOS_V0_1.json](HUMAN_INTERVENTION_DGP_SCENARIOS_V0_1.json).

The essential controls are:

- **HIDGP-00 null effect:** false causal discovery must be visible.
- **HIDGP-01 homogeneous effect:** known population ITT under clean randomization.
- **HIDGP-02 heterogeneous effect:** stratification by pre-assignment covariates; do not expose hidden strata.
- **HIDGP-03 assignment/exposure divergence:** ITT remains tied to assignment, not received exposure.
- **HIDGP-04 time-varying receipt selection:** naive as-treated comparison is intentionally biased.
- **HIDGP-05 missing or delayed exposure observation:** true exposure is distinct from sensor visibility.
- **HIDGP-06 positivity failure:** unsupported strata block extrapolation/IPW.
- **HIDGP-07 competing event and censoring:** competing terminal events, telemetry loss and administrative censoring are distinct.
- **HIDGP-08 observationally equivalent worlds:** different causal mechanisms imply different potential outcomes despite identical observed histories.
- **HIDGP-09 interference:** shared resources or environmental effects violate a naive no-interference assumption.
- **HIDGP-10 model discrepancy:** a candidate transition model is wrong under a held-out regime.

Do not tune the candidate against the scorer's hidden truth or inspect the held-out scenario mechanisms before evaluation.

## 6. Estimands and baselines

Name the estimand before evaluating:
- intention-to-treat (assignment effect);
- per-protocol or received-exposure effect, only with an explicit identification strategy;
- treatment-on-treated/complier effect, only with its assumptions;
- direct, indirect and total effects under a declared interference/exposure mapping;
- fixed-horizon risk or event-time estimand where censoring is present.

Baseline comparisons:
1. oracle population effect (evaluator-only ceiling);
2. randomized ITT estimate;
3. pre-assignment-stratified descriptive baseline;
4. naive as-treated contrast as a deliberate failure control;
5. complete-case estimate under declared censoring regimes;
6. known-mechanism IPW only where conditional independence and positivity hold;
7. hard refusal under positivity failure;
8. observation-only predictor versus action-conditioned causal model.

The oracle is not a deployable estimator. Do not equate prediction of observed outcomes with identification of counterfactual outcomes.

## 7. Metrics — preserve the denominator and report separately

- population and stratum-specific effect bias;
- interval coverage and width;
- calibration of outcome distributions;
- false-positive causal-claim rate under null controls;
- false certainty under observational equivalence;
- ITT versus naive as-treated discrepancy;
- delivery and exposure fidelity;
- positivity support, overlap and effective sample size;
- censoring and competing-event denominator accounting;
- policy-value regret under a declared utility;
- direct/indirect/total effect error where interference is present;
- observation age and event-time correctness;
- abstention rate and correctness;
- resource vector and runtime.

No weighted "causal human realism" scalar. No individual counterfactual accuracy claim from one assigned episode.

## 8. Randomness and pairing

Pairing can reduce variance only when the random variables are genuinely intervention-invariant. Freeze and label each stream:
- baseline physiology/world noise;
- common environment disturbances;
- assignment randomization;
- delivery/adherence randomization;
- sensor noise/dropout;
- transport delay and packet faults;
- outcome/competing-event randomness.

Do not reuse one random draw merely because two worlds share a seed. An intervention may alter exposure, downstream state, later sampling policy or observation availability; those descendants must be recomputed. The Symtropy causal-world runner must compose branch-stable randomness and event-identity owners already in Symtropy rather than create another generic random replay framework.

## 9. Qualification stages

- **D0 — schema:** assignment/delivery/exposure/observation/outcome event separation, units and timestamps.
- **D1 — oracle correctness:** exact population potential-outcome and ITT values under the known DGP.
- **D2 — identification controls:** null, positivity, selection, censoring, competing risk and observational equivalence.
- **D3 — observation integration:** HOBS missingness/latency and the Symthaea #12407 envelope.
- **D4 — policy comparison:** matched baselines, held-out scenario families, decision utility and sensing burden.
- **D5 — external evidence:** only a separately governed human study can support external or individual claims.

Passing D0–D4 means the synthetic benchmark is internally qualified. It does not validate a human intervention model.

## 10. Acceptance gates

- Estimand and assignment mechanism are registered before model fitting.
- Candidate-facing artifacts omit unassigned potential outcomes, hidden structures and future labels.
- Assignment, delivery, exposure, response, capture and availability are independently typed and timestamped.
- A missing exposure sensor readout does not erase a delivered exposure from the evaluator's truth.
- A scheduled action is not treated as delivered unless the delivery event confirms it.
- ITT remains the assignment estimand.
- Positivity failure prevents unsupported inverse-probability weighting or extrapolation.
- Censoring, competing events and telemetry loss retain distinct denominators.
- Interference assumptions and randomization unit are explicit.
- Identical exogenous random streams are paired only when the DGP declares them intervention-invariant.
- The benchmark is deterministic for a pinned configuration, DGP version and seed.
- Every report declares synthetic evidence and its claim ceiling.
