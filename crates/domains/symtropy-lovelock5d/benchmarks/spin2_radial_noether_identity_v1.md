# Radial Noether identity and the spin-squared constraint

**Status:** the radial reparameterization identity has been derived and checked exactly against the one-dimensional action-level Euler-Lagrange system. Its spin-squared projection identifies the role of \(E_g^{(2)}\) as a propagated radial constraint, not a fourth independent evolution equation.

## 1. Action-level identity

Use the reduced Lagrangian \(L_{\rm eff}(b,f,g,h,w;b',f',g',h',w')\) from derive_slow_rotation_second_order.py, with Euler-Lagrange expressions

\[
\mathcal E_q=\frac{\partial L_{\rm eff}}{\partial q}
-\frac{d}{dr}\frac{\partial L_{\rm eff}}{\partial q'}.
\]

Under an infinitesimal radial reparameterization generated by \(\xi(r)\), the radial metric function transforms as

\[
\delta f=\xi f'-2f\xi',
\]

while \(b,g,h,w\) transform as radial scalars:

\[
\delta b=\xi b',\quad
\delta g=\xi g',\quad
\delta h=\xi h',\quad
\delta w=\xi w'.
\]

The action changes only by a boundary term. Integrating the coefficient of \(\xi\) by parts gives the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
b'\mathcal E_b+f'\mathcal E_f+g'\mathcal E_g+
h'\mathcal E_h+w'\mathcal E_w+
2(f\mathcal E_f)'=0.
}
\]

This is an identity among the full equations before imposing \(g=r^2\). It is not a numerical approximation and does not depend on the spin expansion.

## 2. Radial-gauge projection

After imposing \(g=r^2\), one has \(g'=2r\), so

\[
2r\mathcal E_g=
-\left[
b'\mathcal E_b+f'\mathcal E_f+h'\mathcal E_h+
w'\mathcal E_w+2(f\mathcal E_f)'
\right].
\]

For \(r>0\), the exact equations \(\mathcal E_b=\mathcal E_f=\mathcal E_h=\mathcal E_w=0\) therefore imply \(\mathcal E_g=0\).

Consequently, \(E_g^{(2)}\) should remain in the independent residual/constraint qualification bundle, but it must not be counted as a fourth independent differential evolution equation or used to add a redundant boundary condition.

## 3. Spin-squared projection

\[

f=F+a^2\widehat F_2+O(a^4),\quad
h=r^2+a^2\widehat H_2+O(a^4),\quad

The first-order angular equation is solved by the constant Noether angular-momentum flux, so \(\mathcal E_w^{(1)}=0\). Projecting the exact radial identity onto order \(a^2\) gives

\[
\boxed{
F'\,E_b^{(2)}+2rE_g^{(2)}+2rE_h^{(2)}
+3F'E_f^{(2)}+2F\frac{dE_f^{(2)}}{dr}=0.

This formula fixes the correct dependency structure of the even-sector equations.

The differential evolution subset is \(E_b^{(2)}=0\) and \(E_h^{(2)}=0\), with \(E_f^{(2)}=0\) used for algebraic reconstruction of \(\widehat F_2\). The \(E_g^{(2)}\) row is retained as a full-domain residual, constraint-propagation check, and negative-control gate. The identity explains why it must not be counted as another independent evolution equation.
