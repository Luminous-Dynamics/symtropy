# WPT trajectory-conditioned receiver setpoint oracle v0

Tracks [WPT trajectory-control oracle #3924](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/3924). This is the control-timescale reference companion to the route/fleet dispatch and trajectory evidence packages.

## Question answered

Given a short ordered trajectory and already-qualified per-sample envelopes, what integer receiver setpoint sequence maximizes useful delivered energy while satisfying:
- the supplied maximum setpoint for every sample;
- a symmetric per-interval slew limit, including the transition from the declared initial setpoint;
- the cumulative additive asset-stress/resource budget;
- the receiver request cap and a declared minimum useful-energy service floor?

The dynamic program uses state `(previous_setpoint_quanta, cumulative_stress_units)`. For each state, it retains the highest delivered-energy value and deterministic lexicographic witness. Future feasibility depends only on this state, so the bounded DP is exact over the supplied integer model.

## What the envelope means

Each interval must bind a trajectory sample ID, a source envelope ID/digest, `WITHIN_VALIDATED_SUPPORT`, maximum permitted receiver setpoint, useful mWh per setpoint quantum and additive stress units per quantum.

Those coefficients are **inputs**. They must be generated by a separate, validated electromagnetic/metrology and asset model. This crate does not derive coupling, efficiency, field strength, heat, pavement strain or fatigue from lateral offset or vehicle speed.

The additive stress coefficient is a resource proxy, not thermal dose or physical fatigue. A future physical controller must preserve the upstream resource model's validity and uncertainty and must retain hard independent protection gates.

## Baselines and outputs

The report contains:
- exact optimal schedule;
- candidate controller schedule and its optimality gap when its hard control constraints pass;
- highest-energy constant fixed-setpoint baseline;
- forward greedy baseline;
- per-interval setpoints, useful-energy contribution and declared resource usage;
- envelope receipts, DP search-node count and peak DP-state count.

A policy's gap is only comparable when it consumes the same trajectory, envelope receipts, setpoint quantization, request, ramp and resource constraints.

## Known-truth fixture

Four ordered synthetic samples:
- setpoint ceilings [3, 2, 3, 2];
- useful-energy yields [2, 2, 1, 2] mWh per quantum;
- stress coefficients [2, 1, 1, 1];
- initial setpoint 1, maximum slew 1, cumulative stress budget 7;
- useful-energy request 12 mWh, service floor 9 mWh.

The hand-enumerated exact schedule is [1, 2, 1, 2], delivering 11 mWh for 7 stress units. The candidate [1, 1, 1, 1] and fixed baseline deliver 7 mWh; the exact energy gap is 4 mWh. The forward greedy baseline is included and evaluated under the same constraints. These are synthetic known-truth values, not a physical setpoint/power/thermal result.

## Boundedness and fail-closed behaviour

- 2–32 intervals, setpoint ceilings up to 32 quanta;
- at most 1,000,000 search transitions;
- explicit failure when the search bound is exceeded;
- no approximate schedule, hidden pruning or silently truncated result;
- exact trajectory sample identity and envelope digest syntax;
- no out-of-domain envelope admission.

## Validation

`cargo test --locked -p wpt-trajectory-control-oracle`

Ten test declarations cover the known-truth schedule/gap, per-sample ceilings and slew, impossible service floor, out-of-domain support, duplicate samples/bad digests, candidate schedule ramp violations, hard stress budget, search bound exhaustion, input row order and request/integer controls.

## Claim ceiling

Exact bounded optimization for synthetic integer setpoint envelopes and an additive declared stress proxy. No nonlinear converter dynamics, multiport coupling, magnetics, thermal conduction, pavement constitutive response, PCC/AC, EMC, human exposure, safety, field-control authorization or SOTA superiority is claimed.
