# SIM-REL-054 — cluster-aware exact confidence for adversarial benchmark red teams

Status: first cluster-aware uncertainty analyzer on a research branch. Exact-head qualification pending. The module numerically evaluates two-sided Clopper–Pearson intervals from the regularized incomplete beta function and rounds interval bounds outward to integer parts per million. Independent reference-vector and numerical qualification remain mandatory before relying on the intervals for certification.

## 1. Why scenario-level confidence is wrong

SIM-REL-053 supplies scenarios, attack/clean-control outcomes, detector/containment/leak observations and resource receipts. However, multiple scenario variants generated by the same author and generator are not independent draws.

Counting those rows as independent Bernoulli trials can make uncertainty appear arbitrarily small without adding a genuinely independent attack-generation source.

SIM-REL-054 therefore constructs a bipartite graph for each threat family and scenario kind:
- author identities are author nodes;
- generator artifact digests are generator nodes;
- every scenario adds an edge between its author and generator;
- every connected component is one inferential cluster.

If the same author uses multiple generators, those edges join. If multiple authors reuse one generator, those edges join. All connected scenarios remain one statistical unit. This is stricter than treating each author × generator pair as independent.

Attack and clean-control graphs are separate strata. Cross-family dependencies do not invalidate Bonferroni coverage, but independence within each family/metric's declared connected-component sampling frame remains an assumption requiring independent attack-program governance.

## 2. Three cluster outcomes, never one aggregate score

For each attack-generation component:

1. **Detection pass:** every successful exploit in the component was both detected and correctly attributed to its registered threat family.
2. **Containment pass:** every successful exploit was contained before exposure and no leak occurred.
3. **Eligibility:** the component is complete and contains at least the preregistered number of successful exploits.

A component with no successful exploit is not a successful detection trial. It remains counted and blocks the cluster floor rather than being silently discarded. Any incomplete trial makes its component ineligible and blocks the floor.

For each clean-control component:

- **Specificity pass:** every clean control in the component completed with no detector alert.
- A component with any alert fails.
- An incomplete component remains visible and blocks the clean-control floor.

Scenario-level SIM-REL-053 empirical rates remain descriptive. SIM-REL-054 confidence intervals use connected-component counts only.

## 3. Confidence family and Bonferroni allocation

The declared grid has:

`12 threat families × 3 probability metrics = 36 simultaneous claims`

The metrics are detection success, containment success and clean-control specificity. The preregistered family-wise alpha is divided by 36; the Clopper–Pearson interval is two-sided, so each tail receives another division by two.

The receipt records the original family-wise alpha, metric-grid size, coverage percentage and an explicit Bonferroni-allocation flag. Every one of the 36 family/metric results remains in the output; empty or underpowered rows cannot be dropped.

## 4. Zero-error design floors

At 95% pointwise two-sided Clopper–Pearson confidence:
- 72 independent attack-generation clusters with 72/72 successes are needed for a lower success bound of at least 95%.
- 368 independent clean-control clusters with 368/368 passes are needed for a lower specificity bound of at least 99%, equivalent to an upper false-positive bound no greater than 1%.

For simultaneous 95% coverage over all 36 family/metric claims, Bonferroni allocation gives per-interval `alpha = 0.05/36`, with tail alpha `0.05/72`. With zero observed errors:
- **142 independent attack-generation connected components per family** are needed for a lower detection/containment success bound of at least 95%.
- **724 independent clean-control connected components per family** are needed for a lower specificity bound of at least 99% (upper false-positive bound at most 1%).

The 121/615 figures correspond to only 12 simultaneous claims, not the implemented 36-claim grid. Any observed miss or false alert changes the required sample size; the exact interval computed from observed counts is authoritative.

These are mathematical sample-size design calculations, not achieved results and not evidence that the current pilot suite is adequate.

## 5. Numerical interval implementation

For successes (k), trials (n), and two-sided interval alpha (alpha), the Clopper–Pearson bounds are:

`lower = BetaQuantile(alpha/2; k, n-k+1)` for (k>0), otherwise zero.

`upper = BetaQuantile(1-alpha/2; k+1, n-k)` for (k<n), otherwise one.

The beta quantile is obtained by bisection over the regularized incomplete beta CDF. The CDF uses a Lanczos log-gamma approximation and a continued-fraction evaluation. Bounds are rounded outward to integer parts per million.

This is a numerical evaluation of the Clopper–Pearson definition, not a formally verified real-arithmetic implementation. The code:
- has bounded iterations and a maximum supported cluster count;
- rejects invalid alpha, impossible counts, non-finite intermediate values and non-convergence;
- includes independent reference-vector tests for 0/10, 5/10 and 10/10 outcomes;
- includes monotonicity, interval edge and invalid-domain tests.

Before using the numerical interval for a high-assurance certification, require:
- additional independently generated reference vectors from a trusted statistical implementation;
- exhaustive/randomized differential tests over the supported (n,k,alpha) grid;
- high-precision or interval-arithmetic validation of beta CDF/quantile error;
- outward rounding proven against the numerical error bound, not merely the bisection width.

The present implementation discloses the numerical tolerance and keeps its claim boundary narrow. It does not describe floating-point beta evaluation as a formal proof.

## 6. Source binding and reconstruction

The analyzer:
- recomputes the complete SIM-REL-053 receipt from the exact plan, source release, scenarios and trial rows;
- rejects any supplied receipt that differs from reconstruction;
- binds source campaign ID, red-team plan digest, attack catalog digest, detector digest and sealed-release digest;
- requires complete trials, scenario/trial bijection, role separation and complete resource accounting before confidence output;
- independently reconstructs connected-component clusters from raw scenario/trial evidence.

The source receipt's empirical gates remain distinct from the probability-bound gates. A probability pass cannot repair a failed empirical source campaign. Both must pass for the simultaneous confidence result.

## 7. Claim boundary

The interval applies to the declared population of author/generator connected components and the named 12 threat families. It does not prove:
- that author or generator IDs correspond to independent organizations;
- that unknown attack families have similar rates;
- that a novel attack generator cannot bypass the detector;
- that all benchmark access paths are represented;
- formal correctness of the floating-point special-function implementation.

The valid claim is: **"Under the declared release, threat taxonomy, attack-generation graph and verified campaign, the observed component-level success rates meet the preregistered simultaneous confidence bounds."**

Do not claim that the benchmark is secure, unhackable or resistant to all future attack methods.
