# Firstlight Worldspace, Place Memory, and Traversal Production Bible

## Purpose

Firstlight is the first argument that Symtropy's world is worth inhabiting.

Its geography must support:

- physical pleasure;
- practical work;
- social recurrence;
- danger;
- ecological legibility;
- multiple professions;
- changing routes;
- historical layering;
- ordinary life;
- future horizons.

The worldspace should be dense enough that the player learns it bodily, yet broad enough that weather, elevation, vehicles, and time of day produce different experiences.

> **A landmark becomes emotionally powerful when the player has worked there, waited there, taken a shortcut through it, met someone there, and returned after it changed.**

# 1. Reference Scale

The vertical slice occupies approximately:

- 3.5 by 4.5 kilometers of high-detail exterior terrain;
- 1 dense civic and residential core;
- 2 industrial belts;
- 1 wetland and watershed edge;
- 1 upland or escarpment route;
- 1 nightlife and market district;
- 1 transit and freight yard;
- 1 dangerous underground service complex;
- 1 restricted corporate remnant;
- 1 accessible launch or orbital-view horizon;
- several lower-detail visible districts beyond playable boundaries.

This is not a miniature theme-park city. It is one district cluster in a larger region whose edges remain materially plausible.

# 2. Worldspace Structure

## 2.1 Seedworks Commons

The civic heart.

Contains:

- residence block;
- common kitchen;
- shared workbench and tool library;
- notice wall;
- small market;
- rain court;
- public sanitation;
- childcare room;
- roof garden;
- emergency gathering space.

Sensory signature:

- warm reflected light under old structural frames;
- rain on patched roof fabric;
- food, wet soil, machine oil, and laundry;
- several overlapping conversations;
- a drainage channel that becomes a children's racing course after storms.

Changes during the opening:

- cleanup from the feeder incident;
- festival preparation;
- triage overflow;
- public notices and rumors;
- memorial or hearing materials;
- changed seating and route barriers after the crisis;
- one familiar vendor moving elsewhere during player absence.

## 2.2 Clinic and Lantern Annex

A public clinic connected to a quieter harm-reduction and recovery annex.

Contains:

- reception and triage;
- treatment bays;
- private consultation room;
- sanitation and supply zone;
- assistive-device workshop;
- small courtyard;
- quiet recovery room;
- staff break area inaccessible without invitation;
- source-chain consultation room.

The clinic should feel worked in, not pristine.

Players learn where supplies actually end up, which doors are kept open, where families wait, and which stool Amadi uses during difficult conversations.

## 2.3 Fabricator Hall and Repair Court

A former warehouse adapted into:

- machine shop;
- fabrication bays;
- teaching benches;
- materials store;
- certification station;
- scrap exchange;
- crane court;
- worker kitchen;
- union and guild notice area.

Sound carries profession information:

- healthy spindle tone;
- worn bearing pulse;
- cutter chatter;
- compressed-air leak;
- distant argument over tool custody.

## 2.4 Transit Yard

The point where the region reveals movement beyond walking distance.

Contains:

- tram platforms;
- cargo crawlers;
- route control;
- maintenance pits;
- charging and energy exchange;
- driver rest room;
- loading court;
- evacuation staging zone;
- informal food stall;
- old route maps.

Nia's profession and social world live here.

## 2.5 Floodline Market and Night District

A mixed-use area on raised walkways near the old waterline.

Contains:

- daytime food and repair market;
- performance venues;
- worker-owned adult venue where enabled by content settings;
- gaming room;
- small hotels;
- bath and laundry house;
- rehearsal rooms;
- public clinic outpost;
- late-shift transit stop;
- residential rooms above shops;
- quiet alleys and service access.

It is not a pure entertainment zone. Children, retirees, workers, congregations, tourists, and people seeking privacy coexist here.

The district must function at dawn during cleanup and afternoon during preparation, not only at night.

## 2.6 Watershed Terraces

A working ecological and utility landscape.

Contains:

- constructed wetlands;
- pump houses;
- flood gates;
- filtration beds;
- food gardens;
- wildlife routes;
- public footpaths;
- monitoring stations;
- older abandoned channels;
- one disputed development edge.

Sera reads this place differently from the player at first.

The player gradually learns:

- current direction;
- healthy and unhealthy sound;
- water level marks;
- insect and bird changes;
- odor and sediment cues;
- which paths flood first;
- where informal crossings damage habitat.

## 2.7 Old Lift and Escarpment

A vertical traversal route connecting the lower district to upland infrastructure and a distant view of orbital activity.

Includes:

- public lift;
- stair and climbing alternative;
- abandoned freight track;
- storm-exposed maintenance walk;
- overlook used for dates, quiet, and political speeches;
- old commemorative plaques with inconsistent history.

The route becomes inaccessible or dangerous during one opening phase and later reopens differently.

## 2.8 Lower Works

A layered underground complex beneath the Commons and Floodline districts.

Current functions:

- drainage;
- utility routing;
- transit service;
- emergency shelter;
- archive conduit;
- obsolete corporate control rooms.

Hazards:

- floodwater;
- live power;
- unstable surfaces;
- confined spaces;
- contaminated sections;
- old autonomous routines;
- hostile access through forgotten routes.

The Lower Works must never feel like an unrelated fantasy dungeon. Every chamber should have or have had a legible function.

## 2.9 Corporate Remnant — Aureline Annex 4C

A small, restricted infrastructure and data facility inherited through disputed contracts.

It contains:

- useful equipment;
- service locks;
- obsolete customer-experience spaces;
- behavioral records;
- a currently maintained cooling system;
- one inhabited worker station;
- sealed areas;
- conflicting ownership claims.

It is not abandoned simply to provide loot.

## 2.10 Sky and Horizon

Firstlight's sky communicates scale through:

- orbital traffic;
- weather systems;
- launch windows;
- distant aircraft;
- night satellites;
- occasional atmospheric entry;
- one visible but inaccessible mass-driver or launch structure.

These are not constant spectacle. Their rarity creates attention.

# 3. Twelve Anchor Landmarks

## L1 — The Bent Pump House

First tactile intervention and later test site.

Memory layers:

- storm damage;
- Bram's injury or avoided injury;
- temporary patch;
- later inspection;
- neighborhood joke about its crooked door;
- possible memorial mark after the major crisis.

## L2 — Commons Rain Court

Social arrival, market, festival setup, triage overflow, public notices, changed return.

## L3 — Amadi's Courtyard Bench

Not owned by Amadi, but associated with private conversations, recovery, and waiting.

## L4 — Tomas's Shared Repair Table

Collects dents, initials, heat marks, tool arguments, apprentices, and hidden objects.

## L5 — Morrow-7's Bird Route

A service path where the robot deliberately slows to avoid frightening nesting birds and children.

## L6 — Floodline Light Bridge

Nightlife threshold, flood marker, protest site, evacuation bottleneck, sunrise cleanup view.

## L7 — Nia's Route Board

Three contradictory maps, shift changes, detours, migration notices, personal annotations.

## L8 — Lower Works Junction Nine

Functional hub, historical inconsistency, combat or rescue junction, later shortcut.

## L9 — Old Lift Overlook

Vista, quiet social space, distant-world horizon, possible damaged return.

## L10 — The Wrong Technician Statue

Public memorial depicting the wrong worker; beloved meeting point despite historical error.

## L11 — Lantern Annex Quiet Room

A private place that must not become a collectible container or scripted exposition booth.

## L12 — Firstlight Departure Platform

Initially background infrastructure; later becomes a place of choice, farewell, labor, and possible return.

# 4. Traversal Grammar

## 4.1 Walking

Walking must support:

- route choice;
- weather;
- social observation;
- carrying objects or people;
- uneven surfaces;
- shortcuts;
- accessibility alternatives;
- profession-specific noticing.

Default travel between core landmarks should be two to five minutes on foot.

## 4.2 Climbing and Service Access

Climbing is grounded in:

- ladders;
- maintenance frames;
- roof routes;
- drainage walls;
- scaffolds;
- damaged structures.

No ubiquitous painted ledges.

IRIS may identify certified anchor points but should not outline an entire route unless assistance is enabled.

## 4.3 Compact Vehicle

The slice provides access to a shared utility crawler or small transit vehicle.

Vehicle play includes:

- load placement;
- terrain response;
- visibility;
- energy;
- braking;
- passenger safety;
- maintenance;
- route etiquette.

The vehicle is not a disposable mount. It has scheduling and shared-use consequences.

## 4.4 Public Transit

Transit continues without the player and changes with:

- shifts;
- damage;
- weather;
- public events;
- labor action;
- emergency use.

The player can wait, talk, observe, or choose another route.

## 4.5 Water and Flood Movement

Water depth and flow affect:

- walking;
- vehicle traction;
- electrical risk;
- contamination;
- rescue;
- wildlife;
- access.

Flooding must alter routes rather than merely tint the ground.

# 5. Route Memory

The world should teach through repeated route transformation.

Example route evolution:

1. player walks the market path during rain;
2. uses the catwalk when dry;
3. finds a maintenance shortcut with Sera;
4. loses the catwalk during crisis;
5. helps reopen a lower route;
6. returns after absence to find vendors occupying the temporary path.

A route can become socially meaningful through who uses it and who is excluded.

# 6. Time of Day

## Dawn

- cleanup;
- deliveries;
- quiet commuters;
- low light in the wetlands;
- exhausted nightlife workers;
- sanitation crews;
- clinics changing shift.

## Day

- school;
- maintenance;
- markets;
- inspections;
- ecological work;
- construction;
- ordinary civic administration.

## Dusk

- shift overlap;
- food preparation;
- rehearsal;
- transit surge;
- dramatic but not constant light.

## Night

- performance;
- social life;
- reduced visibility;
- different wildlife;
- industrial maintenance windows;
- quiet residential boundaries;
- danger in some routes but not universal menace.

# 7. Weather

The slice requires:

- dry and clear;
- ordinary rain;
- heavy storm;
- heat or humidity stress;
- mist or low visibility;
- post-storm drainage.

Weather changes:

- routes;
- sound;
- crowds;
- tool use;
- risk;
- profession opportunities;
- ecological behavior.

It must not simply become a global stat modifier.

# 8. Sound Zones

## Commons

Conversation, rain fabric, cooking, children, drainage.

## Fabricator Hall

Rhythmic machinery, hearing protection transitions, warning tones, radio, tool diagnostics.

## Wetlands

Water, insects, birds, pumps, wind, distant transit.

## Floodline

Music leakage, generators, transit, kitchens, cleanup equipment, varied crowd density.

## Lower Works

Flow, resonance, mechanical pulses, electrical noise, human voice propagation, silence after isolation.

Sound must carry useful information and emotional memory.

# 9. Interior Rules

Interiors should:

- have work and storage logic;
- support multiple states;
- preserve evidence of occupants;
- contain accessibility and evacuation paths;
- avoid excessive unique rooms used once;
- show ventilation, power, sanitation, and maintenance;
- change after damage and repair.

Private spaces require permission, relationship, emergency, warrant, or explicit trespass—not protagonist privilege.

# 10. Crowd and Population Strategy

Use layered population fidelity.

## Named Residents

24 to 36 recurring people with schedules, relationships, and persistent state.

## Familiar Locals

Approximately 60 visually and behaviorally consistent residents with limited but persistent identities.

## Event Population

Additional lower-detail people used for markets, festivals, evacuation, and transit, promoted when interaction matters.

Crowd density should not imply that every person is a disposable ambient actor.

# 11. Secrets and Easter Eggs

Worldspace secrets include:

- machine maintenance graffiti;
- a laundry notice that later becomes a political slogan;
- the failed-fastener museum under Tomas's bench;
- an incorrect plaque layer;
- a hidden bird observation point;
- an old corporate hospitality jingle in a service terminal;
- a maintenance room containing only filters and lunches;
- a route opened only after a repair changes water flow;
- a private joke visible through repeated visits.

At least half should not produce material rewards.

# 12. Boundary Treatment

Playable boundaries must arise from:

- damaged routes;
- transit schedules;
- weather;
- authority;
- terrain;
- incomplete infrastructure;
- current player equipment;
- real travel time.

Avoid invisible walls where a physical boundary can exist.

# 13. Performance Targets

The high-detail region should support:

- stable first-person interaction latency;
- destructible and repairable subsets;
- dynamic water in bounded zones;
- 30 to 50 high-fidelity nearby agents during ordinary play;
- event crowds through lower-detail simulation;
- persistent landmark changes;
- audio propagation in key interiors;
- rapid save and deterministic replay of authoritative state.

Specific hardware budgets remain evidence-gated.

# 14. Worldspace Failure Conditions

The worldspace fails if:

- landmarks are visually distinct but functionally empty;
- travel is mostly waypoint following;
- the city exists only during quests;
- nightlife has no daytime metabolism;
- the wetland is decoration;
- the underground complex lacks infrastructure logic;
- every private door opens for the player;
- revisits show no accumulated history;
- distant scale overwhelms local attachment;
- the world is beautiful but not useful.

## Worldspace Maxim

> **Firstlight should become navigable as memory, not only as a map.**
