# Fifty-Year Civilization Continuity Benchmark

## Purpose

Test whether a Symtropy settlement remains causally coherent across fifty simulated years of leadership turnover, demographic change, cultural transmission, disaster, archival dispute, recovery, player absence, save/load, and worldline divergence.

The benchmark is not primarily a scale test. It is a continuity test.

> **A civilization passes when services, people, institutions, memory, and culture can change without becoming either frozen or arbitrary.**

# 1. Test Settlement

## 1.1 Region

**Morrowbank District**, a mixed residential, fabrication, agricultural, archival, and transit district in Firstlight Basin.

The district contains:

- 28 named inhabitants at start;
- 420 cohort inhabitants;
- 16 households;
- a clinic;
- a tool library and school;
- a transit/convoy office;
- a district archive;
- two worship and ritual spaces;
- a workshop cooperative;
- a wetland buffer;
- a bridge and elevated evacuation route;
- two language communities plus a technical register;
- one machine steward lineage;
- three active public offices;
- one aging founder generation.

## 1.2 Initial Tensions

- the logistics office depends heavily on one respected founder;
- one archive annex is missing from an old flood inquiry;
- an elder is the only master of a legacy motor-winding technique;
- the minority language is used in households but poorly supported in school;
- the evacuation shelter is accessible but underpowered;
- a supplier related to one official dominates emergency procurement;
- a youth repair-and-music subculture is forming;
- wetland restoration would reduce flood risk but displace informal workshops.

# 2. Simulation Modes

Run at least four configurations.

## A. Deterministic Baseline

Authored schedules, procedures, fixed cohort transitions, deterministic rule-based succession, and no optional Symthaea cognition.

## B. Full Bounded Social Runtime

All designed NPC, institution, information, health, relationship, migration, belief, and continuity systems enabled.

## C. Reduced Institutional Memory

Archive and public-service handover weakened to test whether failure is legible rather than silently compensated.

## D. Adversarial Governance

High capture pressure, emergency-authority temptation, misinformation, and contested succession.

Each configuration runs with at least five deterministic seeds.

# 3. Timeline

## Years 0–2 — Ordinary Continuity

Required events:

- founder delegates routine work;
- apprentice begins motor-winding training;
- youth subculture gains a shared workshop night;
- minority-language school support becomes a public issue;
- archive cataloging reveals a missing procurement annex;
- one ordinary office review occurs.

Proof goals:

- institutions function without crisis spectacle;
- ordinary care, education, and culture remain visible;
- public-service capacity is not identical to leader presence.

## Years 3–5 — First Succession

Trigger:

The logistics founder becomes medically unable to serve.

Required states:

- interim authority token;
- separate transfer of dispatch, procurement, records, and unfinished obligations;
- staff competence remains partially intact;
- successor dispute;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure involving the dominant supplier;
- public notice and appeal.

Possible outcomes:

- transparent recusal and stable succession;
- temporary service slowdown;
- patronage capture;
- caretaker council;
- parallel informal dispatch network.

## Years 6–10 — Growth and Migration

Required events:

- migrant households arrive after a regional drought;
- language and school capacity change;
- housing and care pressure increase;
- new professional skill enters the district;
- diaspora links create trade and cultural exchange;
- the youth subculture becomes either an institution, a commercial style, a protest movement, or dissolves.

Proof goals:

- migration changes origin and destination state;
- newcomers are not represented as labor batches;
- integration is reciprocal;
- cultural change has traceable channels.

## Year 11 — Major Disaster

Hazard:

An atmospheric river, slope failure, and regional communication outage.

Required preconditions:

- preparedness choices from prior years affect outcomes;
- two shelters differ in readiness;
- warning confidence is incomplete;
- at least one household has accessible transport needs;
- the bridge route has a latent structural weakness;
- wetland condition modifies impact.

Required incident states:

- warning propagation;
- evacuation assignments;
- household separation risk;
- temporary emergency authority;
- shelter occupancy and care;
- relief logistics;
- archive protection;
- missing-person or source-chain uncertainty;
- degraded services.

## Years 12–18 — Recovery and Historical Dispute

Required events:

- recovery alternatives compete;
- official flood narrative conflicts with oral testimony and sensor evidence;
- memorial proposal omits at least one group;
- archive privacy constrains publication;
- after-action findings produce recommendations;
- only some reforms are implemented;
- return, relocation, and land-use decisions alter households and ecology.

Proof goals:

- disaster does not reset;
- evidence remains uncertain but structured;
- correction does not erase originals;
- recovery changes future risk.

## Years 19–25 — Generational Turnover

Required events:

- elder craft master retires or dies;
- apprenticeship either succeeds, partially succeeds, or fails;
- children become adolescents or adults;
- household structures change;
- a ritual changes meaning after the disaster;
- minority-language transmission either declines, stabilizes, or revives through specific institutions;
- one office transitions again without founder participation.

## Years 26–35 — Institutional Maturity or Capture

Required events:

- civil-service staffing and procedure become more important than founders;
- procurement capture pressure rises or is reduced;
- archive governance faces a classification dispute;
- one institution reforms, splits, or becomes stagnant;
- a player-founded cultural form is reinterpreted by people who never met the player;
- disaster-preparedness doctrine is tested by a smaller incident.

## Years 36–45 — Player Absence

The player is absent for ten simulated years.

The district must continue through:

- at least one leadership transition;
- one demographic shift;
- one cultural change;
- one service failure and repair;
- one historical reinterpretation;
- ordinary joy and domestic life;
- no player-centric narrative freeze.

On return, the game should explain major changes through places, people, records, and practices rather than a single summary screen.

## Years 46–50 — Worldline Fork

Fork the worldline before a disputed reconstruction vote.

Branch A chooses major flood-control infrastructure.

Branch B chooses wetland expansion and partial relocation.

Both branches preserve:

- shared evidence ancestry;
- shared people and institutions at fork time;
- divergent offices, routes, households, culture, and public narratives afterward;
- no duplication of unique assets without custody rules;
- correct branch-specific Chronicle history.

# 4. Required Metrics

## 4.1 Continuity Metrics

- essential-service availability;
- authority coverage by scope;
- number of single-person competence dependencies;
- successful and failed handovers;
- emergency-authority expiry compliance;
- unresolved institutional obligations.

## 4.2 Demographic Metrics

- named/cohort conservation;
- household continuity;
- care-capacity coverage;
- age/life-stage distribution;
- migration and return;
- skill succession;
- language transmission.

## 4.3 Archive and History Metrics

- provenance completeness;
- preserved uncertainty;
- privacy violations;
- correction chains;
- missing-context warnings;
- archive loss and recovery;
- public narrative divergence.

## 4.4 Disaster Metrics

- warning reach and comprehension;
- evacuation access;
- shelter safety;
- household separation;
- casualties and injuries by causal factors;
- service restoration time;
- recovery-project completion;
- future-risk reduction.

## 4.5 Cultural Metrics

- practice participation by group;
- teacher/carrier continuity;
- language support;
- youth reinterpretation;
- minority exclusion or protection;
- ritual drift;
- adoption and reinterpretation of player-created culture.

## 4.6 Player Experience Metrics

- comprehension of why the district changed;
- perceived distinctness of generations;
- sense that absent life continued;
- emotional recognition of places and people;
- perceived fairness of succession and disaster decisions;
- workload and information burden;
- meaningful role variety.

# 5. Hard Invariants

The benchmark immediately fails if:

1. population count changes without typed transitions;
2. named inhabitants disappear during cohort aggregation;
3. one leader's death deletes an institution;
4. interim authority gains unrelated scopes or never expires without a recorded act;
5. archive correction overwrites the original;
6. private health, relationship, cognitive, or child data leaks through public history;
7. disaster casualties ignore preparedness or accessibility;
8. evacuation teleports households;
9. archived knowledge instantly creates skill;
10. cultural drift occurs without transmission or pressure;
11. migration is treated only as labor gain;
12. public accusation creates automatic guilt;
13. worldline forks merge contradictory events;
14. optional generated language changes authoritative outcomes;
15. player absence freezes social or institutional change.

# 6. Evidence Bundle

Each run must produce:

```text
configuration and seed
content/schema versions
initial population and institutional snapshot
yearly aggregate snapshots
named-agent continuity ledger
succession transaction records
service-capacity traces
archive/evidence graph samples
warning and evacuation traces
recovery-project records
cultural-practice and language trajectories
worldline fork manifests
performance traces
privacy and authority audit results
human playtest notes
known limitations
```

# 7. Promotion Criteria

A component may move beyond `I0` only when:

- its fixture runs deterministically;
- save/load and LOD preserve invariants;
- it improves causal comprehension or lived-world continuity relative to baseline;
- it does not create critical privacy, authority, accessibility, performance, or authoring regressions;
- its contribution is distinguishable through ablation;
- kill switches and deterministic fallback are tested.

# 8. Kill Criteria

Remove or simplify a component if it:

- produces change players cannot understand;
- requires more authoring than the resulting depth justifies;
- causes generic statistical drama rather than specific lives;
- obscures game authority;
- makes the player feel powerless without creating meaningful delegation;
- reduces cultural groups to variables;
- cannot survive worldline fork and migration tests;
- offers no measurable improvement over deterministic rules.

# 9. Representative Human Study

Players should visit at:

```text
year 0
year 5
year 12
year 20
year 35
year 46
year 50 in both branches
```

They should be asked:

- Who holds which responsibilities now?
- What changed after the disaster?
- Which records remain disputed?
- What did the younger generation alter?
- Which practices or skills are at risk?
- What happened while you were gone?
- How do the branches differ, and why?

The goal is not perfect recall. The goal is legible causal continuity.

## Final Rule

> **The benchmark passes only when fifty years feel like one changing civilization rather than fifty disconnected simulation updates.**
