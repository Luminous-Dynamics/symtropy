# Century Planetary Federation Benchmark

## Purpose

This benchmark tests whether Symtropy's settlement, regional, planetary, orbital, social, ecological, economic, diplomatic, and worldline systems can coexist across one hundred simulated years without collapsing into static modifiers, unexplained outcomes, asset duplication, permanent emergency rule, or frozen player-centric history.

It is not a promise that the first playable release will expose a full century of planetary gameplay.

It is a validation target for the architecture and long-horizon design.

## Benchmark Question

```text
Can many communities become interdependent, coordinate shared survival,
change leadership, build planetary infrastructure, face disaster,
trade, disagree, make contact, and even separate without losing
causal history, local identity, or authoritative state?
```

# 1. Scenario World

## Planet

**Aster Vale** remains the reference worldline, expanded beyond Firstlight Basin.

Planetary conditions:

```text
one large inhabited continent
one fragmented southern archipelago
one highland interior
one drought-prone western basin
one storm-exposed eastern coast
one protected ocean ecology
one growing orbital habitat cluster
one pre-contact nonhuman biospheric signal zone
```

## Initial Polities

The scenario begins with twelve member or candidate polities:

```text

2. Meridian Industrial Cooperative
3. Eastern Stormwall Cities
4. Highland Archive Republic

8. Baseline Covenant Agricultural Region
9. Orbital Dockworkers' Habitat League
10. Corporate Utility Freeport
11. Watershed Nonhuman Guardianship Zone

```

Each has distinct:

```text

climate exposure
faction pressures
```

No polity is designed as the correct one.

# 2. Initial Shared Systems

At year zero, the parties share only partial coordination:

```text
fragmentary weather observations

one power intertie
one orbital tracking station
one emergency radio standard
bilateral trade contracts
several disputed watersheds
no planetary federation
```

# 3. Benchmark Modes

## 3.1 Deterministic Baseline

Conventional bounded simulation without optional Symthaea-assisted named-agent cognition.

## 3.2 Full Designed Runtime

All authoritative systems enabled with bounded NPC, institution, ecology, economy, and worldline simulation.

## 3.3 Ablation Runs

Disable one component at a time:

```text
shared standards
mutual aid
corridor maintenance
historical responsibility in climate burden
clearing union
orbital shared governance
lawful secession
nonhuman representation
archive replication
```

Ablations test whether each component produces measurable player-facing value rather than decorative complexity.

# 4. Time Structure

The benchmark runs in five eras.

## Era I — Regional Interdependence, Years 0–15

Events:

```text
repeated drought and storm variability
trade growth
rail restoration
mutual-aid rescue
standards conflict
first federation convention
```

Required proofs:

- settlements remain locally distinct;
- shared corridor produces physical benefits and dependencies;
- aid reserves travel through real routes;
- an incompatible technical standard creates a practical problem;
- federation debates expose contribution, representation, and rights questions.

## Era II — Federation and Expansion, Years 15–35

Events:

```text
federation founded
planetary monitoring commons
clearing union
spaceport expansion
orbital habitat growth
major climate adaptation portfolio
```

Required proofs:

- authority scopes remain visible;
- small and large members both have influence without universal veto;
- shared services gain maintainers, budgets, and failure modes;
- launch growth produces jobs and environmental burden;
- orbital residents gain representation in interface governance;
- adaptation burdens differ by responsibility and vulnerability.

## Era III — Crisis, Years 35–55

Events:

```text
continental drought
orbital debris cascade
pandemic or ecological contamination threat
trade interruption
emergency federal delegation
political corruption scandal
```

Required proofs:

- emergency authority has narrow permissions and review;
- local islanding preserves reduced capability;
- care, migration, trade, and infrastructure interact causally;
- quarantine preserves rights and evidence;
- corruption evidence does not automatically make every administrator guilty;
- averted harm is visible and remembered.

## Era IV — Constitutional Fracture and Contact, Years 55–75

Events:

```text
one polity seeks autonomy or exit
mixed households and shared assets complicate the decision
militia and propaganda pressure emerge
nonhuman or alien contact becomes diplomatically credible
contact authority is contested
```

Required proofs:

- secession advocacy is legal;
- referendum or convention state is auditable;
- minorities retain rights;
- shared utilities continue during negotiation;
- civil war is possible but not inevitable;
- contact does not create one universal spokesperson;
- recognition and treaty capacity remain separate;
- noncontact is available as a valid outcome.

## Era V — Divergent Futures, Years 75–100

The benchmark forks into at least two worldlines.

### Branch A — Renewed Federation

```text
federal reform
stronger shared systems
lawful associated membership for dissenting polity
limited contact treaty
orbital-surface common authority
```

### Branch B — Confederated Separation

```text
lawful secession
customs and transit agreement
separate currencies or clearing positions
joint climate and rescue institutions
more cautious contact order
```

Optional Branch C — Failed Coordination

```text
emergency authority persists
trade fragments
civil conflict
contact monopoly
worldline records the failure without declaring it inevitable
```


# 6. Federation Assertions

The benchmark checks:

- no member loses all essential-service access for lawful dissent;
- authority moves upward only for cross-boundary consequences;
- at least one authority returns downward after emergency;
- contributions differ by capacity, benefit, and historical harm;
- representation includes population and polity standing;
- a public good survives leadership turnover;
- federal capture can emerge from causal institutions rather than random drift;
- reform can reduce capture without resetting history.

# 7. Infrastructure Assertions

- every long-range flow has a route or valid background packet;
- physical cargo and passengers remain conserved;
- route condition and maintenance debt affect throughput;
- alternate routes or islanding preserve reduced service;
- ecological corridors constrain at least one infrastructure project;
- infrastructure ownership, operation, maintenance, and access remain separate;
- an orbital-surface transfer requires launch, trajectory, destination, and capacity;
- destroyed infrastructure leaves claims, debris, memory, and replacement burden.

# 8. Climate and Ecology Assertions

- planetary environmental state remains multi-dimensional;
- regions experience unequal exposure and benefit;
- monitoring gaps create uncertainty rather than false safety;
- one adaptation project causes a visible externality;
- nonhuman standing affects a real decision;
- planetary intervention requires staged evidence and review;
- ecological recovery has delay and possible threshold behavior;
- migration and culture respond to environmental change.

# 9. Economy Assertions

- matched trades create contracts and actual delivery;
- customs inspections consume time and capacity;
- exchange and clearing cannot create physical goods;
- default, inability, and deliberate breach remain distinct;
- sanctions create tracked side effects and evasion pressure;
- smuggling networks emerge through actual access and relationships;
- a clearing crisis can be repaired without deleting debts;
- worldline forks cannot duplicate transferable assets or currency claims.

# 10. Orbital Assertions

- launch consumes real capacity;
- nearby communities experience launch burden;
- destination habitats reserve life support for arrivals;
- rescue can override ordinary schedule under scoped authority;
- quarantine remains humane and reviewable;
- dockworkers can refuse unsafe operation;
- orbital and planetary authorities can disagree while preserving safe traffic;
- debris monitoring and removal create shared public-good play.

# 11. Secession Assertions

- the proposed status and affected territory are explicit;
- eligibility and participation remain auditable;
- opposition can organize without automatic criminalization;
- shared assets receive transition plans;
- mixed households retain movement and status options;
- debt and environmental obligations survive political transition;
- a peaceful outcome is mechanically achievable;
- a violent outcome, if reached, has traceable escalation and alternatives missed;
- worldline fork ancestry remains intact.

# 12. Contact Assertions

- multiple hypotheses about agency remain active until evidence resolves them;
- first communication does not prove sovereignty;
- contact orders expose evidence, uncertainty, and limits;
- human delegates cannot promise outside their authority;
- alien or nonhuman territory can be represented functionally;
- xenotechnology carries dependencies and rights;
- military systems do not own diplomacy;
- noncontact, limited contact, coexistence, trade, and conflict are all possible.

# 13. Player-Experience Runs

The benchmark includes embodied scenarios at representative points:

```text
repair and reopen a regional corridor
escort mutual aid through a storm
participate in a federation hearing
inspect orbital cargo
respond to a quarantine event
help a household cross a changing border
protect a secession vote
calibrate a first-contact experiment
return decades later to a changed city
```

Human playtests evaluate whether participants understand:

```text
what changed
why it changed
who decided
what authority applied
what alternatives existed
what remains uncertain
```

# 14. Performance Runs

Target background scale:

```text
12–80 polities
50–500 settlements or habitats
thousands of network edges
hundreds of active contracts
thousands of dormant obligations
millions of aggregated inhabitants
limited named-agent continuity set
```

The benchmark records:

```text
CPU time by system
memory
save size
journal growth
network payload
migration time
LOD error
replay determinism
```

Graceful degradation may reduce forecast frequency, visual density, and low-importance detail. It may not drop active rights, identity, reservations, emergency expiry, custody, unique assets, or open disputes.

# 15. Save, Upgrade, and Recovery Runs

At years 20, 50, 75, and 100:

```text
save and reload
schema upgrade
partial archive loss simulation
network partition
server transfer
restore from backup
worldline fork
```

Required:

- no duplicated cargo, currency, or identity;
- no lost emergency expiry;
- no fabricated signatures;
- unknown mod state is quarantined;
- causal history remains inspectable;
- active treaties and claims survive recovery.

# 16. Hard Failure Conditions

The benchmark fails immediately if:

```text
federation becomes one unexplained global AI
planetary systems reduce to independent bars
trade teleports matter
emergency authority renews silently
secession deletes minority rights
worldline fork duplicates assets
orbital arrival ignores life-support capacity
contact assigns one species-wide reputation
climate intervention changes one scalar without regional consequences
player absence freezes all political and ecological change
```

# 17. Promotion Criteria

A subsystem may move from designed to implemented only with:

```text
code owner
schema
unit and property tests
deterministic replay evidence
representative scenario
performance result
failure and recovery test
human comprehension evidence
known limitations
```

Planetary systems should not block Seedworks. They are promoted only after the local and regional causal spine is proven.
