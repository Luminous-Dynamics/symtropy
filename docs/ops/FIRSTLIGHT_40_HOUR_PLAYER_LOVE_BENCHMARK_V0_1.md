# Firstlight 40-Hour Player Love Benchmark

## Purpose

This is the promotion benchmark for v2.6.

It does not ask whether every planned system exists. It asks whether the implemented opening makes players care about returning to one place through satisfying physical play, learned skill, recurring people, ordinary life, danger, and persistent consequence.

> **The benchmark passes only if players remember what happened to them in Firstlight—not merely that Symtropy contains many systems.**

# 1. Test Build

The reference build includes:

- Bent Feeder opening;
- Seedworks Commons;
- clinic and Lantern Annex;
- Fabricator Hall;
- Transit Yard;
- Floodline market/night district;
- Watershed Terraces;
- Old Lift;
- Lower Works;
- Annex 4C subset;
- six flagship characters;
- at least twenty-four recurring residents;
- maintenance, field medicine and civic witness;
- four action scenarios;
- one cultural event;
- one institutional hearing;
- one absence and return;
- IRIS;
- one shared vehicle.

# 2. Cohorts

Minimum research cohorts should include:

- players drawn to action;
- players drawn to life simulation and relationships;
- systems and strategy players;
- role-playing and narrative players;
- players unfamiliar with complex simulations;
- players using significant accessibility support;
- players with short-session schedules;
- returning testers who have not played for seven real-world days.

No single cohort substitutes for the others.

# 3. Test Stages

## Stage A — Ten-Minute Contact Test

Observe:

- time to meaningful control;
- clarity of immediate human need;
- physical interaction success;
- IRIS comprehension;
- camera and input comfort;
- whether players notice at least one environmental clue beyond the objective.

**Pass direction:** players can describe what they did and why without repeating tutorial text.

## Stage B — First-Hour Test

Observe:

- orientation to home base;
- recognition of three people;
- understanding of rest, privacy and storage;
- self-selected direction among three profession doors;
- first safe failure;
- willingness to look around without prompt.

## Stage C — Five-Hour Test

Observe:

- one learned profession cue;
- one revisited landmark;
- one ordinary activity;
- one danger sequence;
- one remembered consequence;
- perceived pace and overload.

## Stage D — Ten-Hour Test

Measure:

- person recall;
- place recall;
- profession identity;
- understood institution;
- personal next desire;
- perceived physical satisfaction;
- comprehension of IRIS limits;
- whether players believe NPCs continue without them.

## Stage E — Forty-Hour Test

Measure:

- project continuity;
- companion boundaries and independent life;
- major event excitement;
- aftermath comprehension;
- absence and return;
- successful and failed outcome value;
- desire to remain or leave for player-chosen reasons;
- attachment without pressure.

## Stage F — Seven-Day Delayed Recall

After seven real-world days without exposure, ask unaided:

- name three people;
- name or describe four places;
- describe one professional cue learned;
- recall one promise;
- recall one ordinary moment;
- recall one mystery;
- explain one consequence;
- state what they wanted to do next;
- state whether they wanted to return and why.

# 4. Quantitative Gates

Initial validation profile targets are hypotheses, not marketing claims.

Required directional gates:

- median meaningful-control time below two minutes;
- at least 80% can explain the first task's practical goal after ten minutes;
- at least 70% recall three recurring people after ten hours;
- at least 70% recall four landmarks after ten hours;
- at least 65% demonstrate one learned cue without explicit prompt;
- at least 70% articulate a self-chosen next desire at ten hours;
- at least 60% retain three people, three places and one practice cue after seven days;
- at least 70% report that the major action event was exciting or tense for good reasons;
- at least 70% understand one persistent consequence;
- fewer than 15% report feeling guilted or manipulated into returning;
- no critical accessibility path remains blocked;
- deterministic replay and persistence gates pass.

These thresholds require revision through actual evidence.

# 5. Qualitative Gates

At least half of positive return statements should reference one or more of:

- person;
- place;
- practice;
- promise;
- mystery;
- self-directed project;
- physical action.

Statements focused only on “seeing more systems” indicate insufficient concentration.

Required player-language examples should resemble:

- “I want to see whether that pump repair held.”
- “I want to help Nia get the night route working.”
- “I think Mara's evidence is incomplete.”
- “I just liked being in the market after rain.”
- “I finally learned what the bad bearing sounds like.”

# 6. Physical Play Evidence

Capture:

- input latency;
- movement error;
- interaction abandonment;
- aim and assistance use;
- tool-contact success;
- learned cue recognition;
- rescue path choice;
- combat retreat and surrender behavior;
- destruction persistence;
- replay desire.

# 7. Emotional Evidence

Capture with consent:

- spontaneous name use;
- voluntary revisits;
- player-created pins;
- time spent in ordinary activities;
- remembered boundaries;
- reactions to companion refusal;
- cleanup participation;
- response to absence changes;
- whether players treat IRIS as tool, witness, companion, or annoyance;
- whether people are discussed as persons rather than content nodes.

# 8. Profession Evidence

For each profession, require:

- one cue learned;
- one procedure completed;
- one judgment with alternatives;
- one handover;
- one follow-up;
- one failure understood;
- one player-described practice style.

# 9. Worldspace Evidence

Require:

- four landmarks navigable without marker after repeated use;
- one sound-only or sound-assisted recognition;
- one route changed by weather or damage;
- one route changed by social use;
- one private boundary respected;
- one landmark visibly changed after absence.

# 10. Companion and IRIS Evidence

Require:

- one lawful companion refusal;
- one independently completed companion action;
- one relationship not involving the player;
- one practiced joint procedure;
- one remembered companion boundary;
- one IRIS uncertainty statement understood;
- one configurable IRIS behavior;
- no false guilt message.

# 11. Consequence Evidence

The build must preserve and replay:

- opening repair quality;
- injury or avoided injury;
- Bridge Seven state;
- one public record claim;
- festival preparation state;
- major-event damage;
- one hearing outcome;
- one companion project;
- one absence change;
- one aged repair or follow-up.

# 12. Accessibility Evidence

Test:

- color-independent cues;
- subtitle completeness;
- remapping;
- motion settings;
- interaction assistance;
- audio alternatives;
- cognitive-load controls;
- return summary;
- mature-content controls;
- short-session stop points.

Assistance use cannot invalidate player-love metrics.

# 13. Anti-Manipulation Review

Review all:

- notifications;
- companion contact;
- return messages;
- timed events;
- optional content;
- progression rewards;
- telemetry proposals.

Reject any mechanism that depends on:

- real-time FOMO;
- guilt;
- false urgency;
- threatened relationship loss due to logout;
- opaque variable rewards;
- paid relief from designed frustration.

# 14. Worldline Variants

The benchmark runs at least three valid opening histories.

## Public Repair

Player supports public utility, transparent evidence and worker-led festival safety.

## Corporate Stabilization

Player accepts corporate capacity and tighter service control to restore systems rapidly.

## Fragmented Recovery

Early failures, limited evidence and local improvisation produce a less stable but still playable Firstlight.

All branches must contain joy, competence, ordinary life and future possibility.

# 15. Failure Conditions

The benchmark fails if:

- players admire scope but do not want to return;
- people are recalled only by job title;
- landmarks require markers after repeated use;
- professions feel interchangeable;
- action is not enjoyable on its own;
- ordinary life feels like filler;
- failure mainly causes reload;
- IRIS is intrusive;
- companion refusal feels random;
- absence feels punitive;
- the major event erases all quieter content;
- inaccessible players cannot reach the same causal outcomes.

# 16. Promotion Rule

Passing documents, schemas and automated tests proves only design and implementation consistency.

Promotion above D3/I0 requires:

- playable build;
- signed build identity;
- deterministic replay;
- performance evidence;
- accessibility evidence;
- privacy review;
- qualitative playtest evidence;
- delayed recall evidence;
- issue closure or declared limitations.

## Benchmark Maxim

> **The first forty hours pass when players can stop, remember a life they began in Firstlight, and choose to return without the game having to beg them.**
