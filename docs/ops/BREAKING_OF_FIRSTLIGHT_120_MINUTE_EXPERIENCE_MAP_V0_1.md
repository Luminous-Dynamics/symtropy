# Breaking of Firstlight 120-Minute Experience Map

## Purpose

Define a bounded, replayable, emotionally credible opening that teaches Symtropy through a life already in progress, makes the player care about a unique Firstlight, breaks that stable home through causal systems, and transfers play into the Continuance Crawler.

This is not a two-hour cinematic corridor.

It is a compressed living-world proof with variable people, activity, warning, route state, cargo, rescue, and aftermath.

# 1. Experience Goals

By the title reveal, a first-time player should be able to say:

- who at least two people are;
- what one person wanted beyond survival;
- what Firstlight sounded or felt like;
- one thing they personally changed;
- one warning they noticed or missed;
- one person or object they chose to prioritize;
- why the mobile base has limitations;
- one unresolved reason to continue.

The player should not need to understand every system.

# 2. Pacing Envelope

Reference duration: 90 to 120 minutes.

Accessibility, exploration, dialogue, difficulty, and player choice may extend the sequence. The opening must tolerate interruption and save at all noncritical boundaries.

```text
0–10      embodiment and first practical act
10–25     arrival and first people
25–45     ordinary life and emergent practice
45–65     wonder, aspiration and warning
65–85     preparation and social fracture
85–110    the Breaking and evacuation
110–120   loading, departure, title and first horizon
```

# 3. Minute 0–3: Immediate Embodiment

## Required State

The player begins in a stopped municipal transit crawler, service vehicle, small dwelling, or roadside shelter on the Firstlight approach.

The exact starting shell may vary by player origin, but it must include:

- immediate camera and movement control;
- one tactile object;
- one weather or environmental pressure;
- IRIS in a bounded, nonexpository role;
- one nearby person engaged in practical action.

## Player Actions

Possible first actions:

- open a jammed access panel;
- move debris;
- brace a cable;
- inspect an injured person;
- retrieve a tool;
- reroute local power;
- climb to a better view.

No choice locks a profession.

## Prohibited

- character creator followed by a long cutscene;
- tutorial text covering more than one immediate action;
- a voice explaining the entire setting;
- objective markers for every interactable;
- a fake emergency that resets after teaching controls.

# 4. Minute 3–10: The Bent Feeder

The player's route is blocked by a real infrastructure problem affecting Firstlight's edge district.

The scenario supports several contributions:

- material repair;
- medical help;
- evidence preservation;
- traffic management;
- physical rescue;
- exploration of an alternate path.

## First Generated Residents

Only two high-salience names should be introduced.

One is tied to the problem. One is tied to another person or obligation.

They should display:

- competence;
- a relationship with each other or someone absent;
- an ordinary irritation or joke;
- a future plan mentioned indirectly.

## State Written

- actions attempted;
- sensory cues noticed;
- person helped;
- evidence disturbed or preserved;
- tool handling preference;
- warning data obtained;
- route condition.

# 5. Minute 10–18: Firstlight Reveal

The player travels into Firstlight on foot or aboard a partially restored vehicle.

The reveal must combine:

- inhabited density;
- ecological specificity;
- infrastructure;
- pleasure and culture;
- a visible larger horizon;
- one impossible or ambiguous phenomenon.

Possible signature wonder events:

- rain bending around a low metric-trim craft;
- the abandoned tower responding to the player's Field Deck;
- a giant wetland organism surfacing beneath industrial pylons;
- a launch plume lighting the district;
- a machine procession carrying habitat cultures;
- an alien signal expressed through public lights and pump rhythm.

The event is observable but not immediately converted into a quest.

# 6. Minute 18–30: Welcome Without Worship

The player enters an active neighborhood.

Required beats:

- a meal, market, workshop, transit platform, clinic corridor, or festival-preparation scene already underway;
- no ceremonial welcome;
- one place to store a small possession;
- one chance to personalize an object or space;
- one resident unavailable because of another obligation;
- one resident with a self-interested ambition;
- the Continuance Crawler visible in ordinary use.

The player may:

- eat;
- gamble;
- help carry equipment;
- listen to music;
- inspect the crawler;
- ask IRIS about a person or place;
- decline social interaction;
- wander within a bounded district.

# 7. Minute 30–45: Emergent Practice

The world offers overlapping situations instead of three labeled profession doors.

## Maintenance Thread

A system exhibits a learnable sensory cue and can be improved, bypassed, documented, or left for specialists.

## Care Thread

A person needs assessment, assistance, privacy, transport, or advocacy rather than automatic treatment.

## Witness Thread

A custody discrepancy, contradictory record, unsafe shortcut, or missing authorization may be observed.

## Mobility Thread

The player may help reposition the crawler, load equipment, or learn a route.

## Cultural Thread

The player may assist a performance, meal, game, or public installation.

The runtime notes demonstrated interest and later residents respond with invitations, trust, skepticism, or work offers.

No UI asks the player to choose a class.

# 8. Minute 45–55: Personal Aspiration

The opening must ask what the player wants through play and conversation.

Possible desires surfaced:

- a workshop;
- money;
- professional mastery;
- a relationship;
- access to ruins;
- a vehicle;
- a home;
- public influence;
- artistic recognition;
- body modification;
- travel to orbit;
- eventual interstellar passage.

A resident may ask directly, but answers must include silence, uncertainty, and later change.

The game records an initial aspiration as a revisable character intention, not a permanent class.

# 9. Minute 55–65: Wonder Becomes Warning

The earlier anomaly repeats with new physical consequences.

Examples:

- clock disagreement;
- localized gravity shift;
- impossible echo;
- machine memory corruption;
- water moving against the expected gradient;
- tower emissions matching no registered system;
- wildlife evacuation behavior;
- IRIS uncertainty rising sharply.

The player can collect evidence, help prepare, dismiss the issue, report it, or follow another person's interpretation.

At least two institutions disagree about the meaning or urgency.

# 10. Minute 65–75: Preparation Window

The player receives no universal `prepare for disaster` checklist.

They may:

- refuel or repair the crawler;
- move medical supplies;
- check a route;
- warn a household;
- secure archive custody;
- assist festival safety;
- repair drainage;
- follow the rival;
- pursue personal business;
- rest.

Each action changes later state.

Doing nothing remains a valid action with consequences.

# 11. Minute 75–85: Social Fracture

Warnings become public.

Required systemic tensions:

- conflicting authority;
- route access inequality;
- rumor;
- private transport leaving early;
- an essential worker refusing an unsafe order;
- a household disagreement;
- the rival offering a tempting alternative;
- a person the player likes making a choice they dislike.

The player should not yet know whether a full evacuation is necessary.

# 12. Minute 85–92: Threshold Crossing

A legible combined failure makes Firstlight's previous continuity impossible.

Possible sequence:

- power cascade;
- water or structural failure;
- metric pulse;
- transit shutdown;
- hostile seizure;
- fire or contamination;
- district separation.

The player witnesses one irreversible environmental change.

IRIS states uncertainty and immediate priorities, not a lore explanation.

# 13. Minute 92–110: The Breaking

The evacuation is a systemic space with several simultaneous fronts.

## Front A: Route

Keep one viable path open through repair, driving, negotiation, combat, or alternate terrain.

## Front B: Care

Stabilize, move, or hand over people with different needs.

## Front C: Continuity

Preserve selected records, tools, cultures, or source-chain evidence.

## Front D: Household

Resolve or fail to resolve separation among generated residents.

## Front E: Threat

Handle opportunistic violence, institutional seizure, environmental danger, or Null behavior.

## Front F: Crawler

Bring the Continuance Crawler from municipal asset to functioning evacuation platform.

The player cannot complete every front.

Companions and residents act independently according to relationships and prior preparation.

# 14. Evacuation Design Rules

The player should experience:

- at least one fast physical action;
- at least one judgment under uncertainty;
- at least one cooperative procedure;
- at least one refusal;
- at least one changed route;
- at least one visible consequence of earlier preparation;
- at least one outcome resolved without the player;
- at least one person leaving by another route;
- at least one unresolved fate.

Combat is possible but cannot be the only route through the evacuation.

# 15. Minute 110–116: The Load

The crawler cannot carry everything.

The loading interface must remain diegetic and spatial:

- seats;
- stretchers;
- cargo spaces;
- water mass;
- power draw;
- axle load;
- compartment access;
- route clearance.

Choices may include:

- people;
- medicine;
- tools;
- food cultures;
- ecological samples;
- archive and evidence;
- machine body or core;
- sentimental objects;
- industrial components;
- fuel or water.

Residents have opinions and may refuse player priority.

No single combination is presented as the good ending.

# 16. Minute 116–120: Departure

The crawler leaves or is forced onto the remaining route.

Firstlight remains visible long enough for the player to understand that events continue behind them.

Possible observations:

- another convoy taking a different route;
- a district losing power;
- the tower entering a new state;
- a person remaining at a control point;
- a broadcast contradicting what the player witnessed;
- wildlife moving through the city;
- a route collapsing after passage.

Inside the crawler:

- survivors occupy real space;
- someone asks where they are going;
- someone is angry;
- someone performs an ordinary task;
- IRIS identifies immediate vehicle limits;
- the player sees the object or modification they made earlier.

The title appears.

# 17. First Post-Title Choice

The player chooses an immediate horizon, not a moral alignment:

- nearest refuge;
- medical facility;
- safe water;
- another convoy's signal;
- evidence route;
- return-adjacent observation point;
- rival-controlled safe passage;
- wilderness concealment.

This choice begins the real first forty hours.

# 18. Variation Matrix

The opening must vary at least:

- generated cast;
- starting shell;
- signature wonder;
- anomaly expression;
- weather;
- profession invitations;
- rival identity;
- public authority;
- preparation opportunities;
- route failure;
- crawler condition;
- capacity;
- survivor outcomes;
- final visible state.

Production may author several bounded scenario families rather than unrestricted procedural catastrophe.

# 19. Tutorial Coverage

The sequence should teach through use:

- movement and camera;
- interaction;
- Field Deck and IRIS;
- tool handling;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- care and consent;
- traversal;
- inventory as physical capacity;
- relationships;
- vehicle operation;
- saving and returning;
- consequences.

It should not attempt to teach:

- full construction;
- advanced combat trees;
- politics at scale;
- Atlas chronology;
- spacecraft operation;
- all professions;
- civilization founding.

Those remain horizons.

# 20. Research Gates

The two-hour opening passes only when:

- 80% of first-time players can name or describe two generated people;
- 70% remember one personal ambition not assigned by a quest;
- 75% understand that they could not save everything but did alter outcomes;
- 70% report curiosity rather than resentment about unresolved survivors;
- 80% understand why the crawler has limited capacity;
- fewer than 15% describe the attachment phase as fake or manipulative;
- fewer than 10% believe everyone necessarily dies;
- repeat players recognize meaningful cast and event variation;
- accessibility users preserve equivalent causal choices;
- at least 65% voluntarily continue past the title in an uncoerced test environment.

## Experience Maxim

> **Give the player enough ordinary life to love, enough agency to matter, enough loss to carry, and enough road ahead that departure feels like the beginning rather than the end.**
