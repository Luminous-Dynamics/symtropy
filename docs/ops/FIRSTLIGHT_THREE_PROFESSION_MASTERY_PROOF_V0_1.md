# Firstlight Three-Profession Mastery Proof

## Purpose

The vertical slice proves three professions deeply enough that players can feel themselves becoming competent:

1. maintenance technician;
2. field medic;
3. civic witness and investigator.

These professions were selected because they cover matter, bodies, and truth. Together they touch nearly every Firstlight system without requiring a class lock.

> **A player should remember the moment they first heard the bad bearing, recognized shock before a monitor did, or noticed that a supposedly old document had been folded yesterday.**

# 1. Shared Progression Structure

Each profession advances through six forms of growth:

- **perception** — noticing useful cues;
- **execution** — physically performing procedures;
- **judgment** — choosing among imperfect options;
- **tool fluency** — using, maintaining and adapting equipment;
- **social standing** — being trusted for bounded work;
- **continuity** — documenting, handing over and revisiting outcomes.

There is no single profession level.

# 2. Maintenance Technician

## 2.1 Fantasy

Read machines and infrastructure as living histories of load, repair, compromise, and dependency.

## 2.2 First-Hour Cues

The player learns to distinguish:

- energized from isolated equipment;
- active leak from residual water;
- fastener type;
- hot surface;
- temporary patch;
- certified access point.

IRIS can label these with assistance enabled.

## 2.3 Five-Hour Cues

The player begins to recognize:

- pump cavitation;
- bearing roughness;
- overloaded cable smell;
- seal failure pattern;
- water hammer;
- structural vibration;
- mismatched replacement part.

These cues are delivered through sound, animation, temperature, vibration, and history—not only icons.

## 2.4 Core Procedure

```text
work request
→ interview operator
→ isolate hazards
→ inspect in chosen order
→ test hypothesis
→ expose component
→ repair or stabilize
→ restore under controlled load
→ certify, restrict or condemn
→ clean and hand over
→ inspect later
```

## 2.5 Opening Cases

### M1 — Bent Feeder

Emergency isolation and safe temporary action.

### M2 — Quiet Pump

A pump appears healthy on public telemetry but sounds wrong under load.

### M3 — Shared Crawler

Braking and load-distribution fault with scheduling pressure.

### M4 — Floodline Venue

Unsafe improvised power arrangement during festival preparation.

### M5 — Junction Nine

Multi-system diagnosis under flood and hostile pressure.

### M6 — Aged Repair

Return after absence to inspect how the player's earlier work changed.

## 2.6 Judgment Dilemmas

- shut down a service or permit bounded operation;
- use compatible but uncertified material;
- preserve evidence or restore service quickly;
- accept a worker's tacit diagnosis over clean telemetry;
- perform emergency work outside credential scope;
- disclose a hidden defect that may close housing.

## 2.7 Mastery Signals

The player experiences mastery when:

- they diagnose before IRIS suggests it;
- they prepare the right tool without a prompt;
- a companion trusts their isolation plan;
- they can perform under time pressure with fewer errors;
- they leave a handover another worker can use;
- a repair survives a later event.

# 3. Field Medic

## 3.1 Fantasy

Protect a person's agency and physiology under uncertainty, pressure, limited resources, and social complexity.

## 3.2 First-Hour Cues

The player learns:

- consent and capacity check;
- scene safety;
- responsiveness;
- breathing and circulation signs;
- visible bleeding;
- pain communication;
- when not to move someone.

## 3.3 Five-Hour Cues

The player begins to recognize:

- shock before catastrophic readings;
- heat illness;
- inhalation injury;
- intoxication versus panic;
- medication interaction;
- hidden bleeding indicators;
- prosthetic or firmware dependency;
- when a patient is preparing to leave.

## 3.4 Core Procedure

```text
scene safety
→ introduce role
→ request consent or establish emergency basis
→ primary assessment
→ stabilize immediate threat
→ gather history and observations
→ choose intervention
→ monitor response
→ communicate uncertainty
→ hand over
→ follow up
```

## 3.5 Opening Cases

### C1 — Bram's Hand

Simple injury complicated by live infrastructure and work pressure.

### C2 — Residence Fall

Mobility aid, pain, privacy, and unsafe stair design.

### C3 — Transit Incident

Several people with different urgency and one loud but less critical patient.

### C4 — Nightlife Interaction

Possible contaminated substance, medication interaction, panic, or coercion.

### C5 — Festival Triage

Finite staff and supplies across simultaneous incidents.

### C6 — Reconstitution Household

Medical, identity, memory and family boundaries without diagnosing legitimacy.

## 3.6 Judgment Dilemmas

- prioritize under incomplete information;
- respect refusal while explaining risk;
- preserve privacy against venue or employer pressure;
- use scarce supplies now or reserve them;
- report a public-health risk without exposing a patient;
- recognize when the problem is housing, transport, violence, or access rather than treatment.

## 3.7 Mastery Signals

- the player notices deterioration early;
- prepares correct supplies;
- communicates calmly without scripted empathy points;
- avoids unnecessary intervention;
- gives a clear handover;
- follows up and sees the person as more than a case.

# 4. Civic Witness and Investigator

## 4.1 Fantasy

Reconstruct what can responsibly be known, preserve evidence, understand institutions, and help people act without pretending uncertainty has disappeared.

## 4.2 First-Hour Cues

The player learns:

- observation versus inference;
- physical trace;
- source identity;
- custody;
- private versus public record;
- claim confidence;
- conflicting testimony.

## 4.3 Five-Hour Cues

The player begins to recognize:

- edited logs;
- time inconsistency;
- staged damage;
- missing context;
- institutional jargon hiding responsibility;
- witness contamination;
- a technically authentic record used misleadingly;
- memory confidence versus narrative confidence.

## 4.4 Core Procedure

```text
secure or document scene
→ identify authorities and affected people
→ observe before interpretation
→ collect with custody
→ interview without feeding answers
→ compare records and material traces
→ form bounded hypotheses
→ seek disconfirming evidence
→ protect private material
→ submit claim with confidence and limits
→ revisit after new evidence
```

## 4.5 Opening Cases

### W1 — Feeder Sequence

Was the landslip cause, consequence, or coincidence?

### W2 — Bridge Seven

Expired machine authority, maintenance records, and opportunistic interference.

### W3 — Wrong Technician Statue

Public memory, archival error, and a beloved inaccurate landmark.

### W4 — Annex 4C

Corporate records, worker testimony, private behavioral data, and disputed ownership.

### W5 — Firstlight Night

Multiple scenes, missing minutes, rumor, damaged sensors, and public pressure.

### W6 — Continuity Hearing

Source-chain evidence with privacy boundaries and no automatic personhood judgment.

## 4.6 Judgment Dilemmas

- preserve privacy or disclose public danger;
- release incomplete evidence under political pressure;
- trust a witness with inconsistent memory;
- separate legal custody from moral credibility;
- protect evidence while people need the damaged infrastructure restored;
- admit that no responsible conclusion is available yet.

## 4.7 Mastery Signals

- the player notices provenance problems without highlighting;
- asks better questions;
- preserves evidence during action;
- distinguishes a lie from an honest error;
- submits narrower but stronger claims;
- changes their hypothesis when new evidence arrives.

# 5. Cross-Profession Cases

The professions should intersect rather than live in separate quest lines.

## Bridge Seven

- technician isolates systems;
- medic triages trapped people;
- witness preserves authority and event records.

## Flooded Annex

- technician routes power and water;
- medic manages contamination and injury;
- witness handles corporate evidence and private records.

## Firstlight Night

- each profession sees a different causal front;
- no one perspective reveals complete truth;
- companions cover missing capabilities;
- player choices shape which evidence and people survive.

# 6. Practice Spaces

## Fabricator Hall Test Bench

Safe component practice with real tools and faults.

## Clinic Simulation Room

Consent-based practice, assistive settings, and replayable cases without treating simulated bodies as disposable spectacle.

## Public Record Lab

Custody, timeline, redaction, and contradiction exercises using historical cases.

Practice is always available and never framed as shameful remediation.

# 7. Assistance Tiers

## Guided

- visible procedure sequence;
- highlighted hazards;
- IRIS explanations;
- generous timing;
- automatic documentation scaffolding.

## Supported

- tools and hazards remain legible;
- prompts appear on request or after hesitation;
- IRIS offers bounded suggestions.

## Independent

- minimal prompts;
- cues remain physical and sensory;
- procedures are not hidden;
- post-action review remains available.

## Expert

- full cue density;
- optional reduced HUD;
- no extra economic reward;
- suitable for players who enjoy self-directed diagnosis.

Assistance is not a difficulty morality scale.

# 8. Progression Across Forty Hours

## Hours 0–5

Safe fundamentals, first interruption, visible teacher support.

## Hours 5–10

Intermediate case, cross-profession exposure, first independent judgment.

## Hours 10–20

Project work, optional complex case, tool modification, first handover from player to NPC.

## Hours 20–30

Major event under pressure, incomplete information, profession-specific contribution.

## Hours 30–40

Follow-up, hearing, aged repair or patient outcome, peer recognition, teaching or delegation.

# 9. Reputation

Professional reputation is audience-specific.

Possible audiences:

- maintenance crews;
- clinic staff;
- patients and households;
- record workers;
- regulators;
- corporate managers;
- informal networks;
- companions.

Being trusted as a technician does not create medical or political authority.

# 10. Failure

Profession failure should be diagnosable.

Examples:

- wrong hypothesis;
- correct hypothesis, poor execution;
- insufficient isolation;
- intervention beyond available evidence;
- missed follow-up;
- bad handover;
- institutional constraint;
- unavoidable outcome.

The game should not collapse all failures into lower quality numbers.

# 11. Research Questions

- Do players report genuine improvement?
- Which physical cues are learned without UI?
- Can players explain why an outcome failed?
- Do they develop a preferred practice style?
- Do they value follow-up?
- Do professions feel distinct in tempo and emotion?
- Does IRIS support learning without solving cases?
- Do players respect expertise in NPCs?

## Proof Maxim

> **The first profession should not tell the player who they are. It should give them enough practice to discover what kind of worker they want to become.**
