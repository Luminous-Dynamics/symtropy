# Twenty-Year Player-Founded Region Benchmark

## Goal

Prove that a player can help found a settlement that becomes a civilization with functioning services, independent inhabitants, amendable institutions, adopted culture, succession, contested memory, and history during absence.

The benchmark must not prove only that buildings persist.

# 1. Reference Region

Use **Firstlight South Cut** as the working local name.

Administrative seed:

```text
Firstlight Municipal Expansion Zone 3
```

Common alternatives:

```text
South Cut
Lower Works
Ward Three
Pump Side
```

The benchmark begins around an abandoned materials yard, a damaged water line, a partially usable clinic shell, a transport spur, and a seasonal wetland boundary.

# 2. Initial Population

Minimum:

- 12 named adults or equivalent agents;
- 4 households or durable care units;
- 2 children or dependent life stages;
- 1 service machine;
- 1 person who opposes permanent settlement;
- 1 person planning to leave;
- 1 external claimant;
- 1 ecological or nonhuman stakeholder represented through valid evidence.

At least six professions:

- repair technician;
- clinician;
- sanitation worker;
- transit operator;
- food or ecological worker;
- archivist or administrator.

# 3. Starting Assets

- one unreliable water or life-support utility;
- one clinic shell;
- one workshop;
- one shared kitchen;
- one transport stop;
- one waste path;
- twelve provisional shelters;
- one contested storage building;
- one protected wetland or equivalent ecological zone;
- one Field Deck/IRIS public terminal with bounded access.

# 4. Founding Trigger

A regional service failure displaces several households while a corporate operator abandons the yard.

The player can assist, but the founding cohort already has reasons to organize.

# 5. Three Compact Paths

## Path A — Emergency Stewardship Compact

Strengths:

- fast decisions;
- clear service authority;
- strong continuity during crisis.

Risks:

- emergency powers persist;
- technical specialists dominate;
- residents without credentials lose voice.

## Path B — Household Assembly Compact

Strengths:

- broad direct participation;
- care needs remain visible;
- strong resident legitimacy.

Risks:

- meeting burden;
- households may suppress internal members;
- slow technical decisions.

## Path C — Worker and Service Cooperative Compact

Strengths:

- labor control;
- service accountability;
- shared assets.

Risks:

- nonworkers and dependents may be underrepresented;
- professional guild capture;
- external financing difficulty.

All three must be viable and amendable.

# 6. Required Public Services

1. water or life support;
2. clinic and public health;
3. sanitation and waste;
4. workshop and repair;
5. food distribution;
6. transport;
7. records and civic process.

Each service requires:

- workers;
- shifts;
- consumables;
- capacity;
- access rules;
- budget;
- maintenance;
- contingency;
- succession.

# 7. Founding Year Scenario

## Days 1–7

- discover prior claims;
- establish emergency shelter;
- repair partial utility capacity;
- meet households at work;
- record one person refusing permanent settlement;
- preserve the wetland boundary;
- create a provisional compact.

## Days 8–30

- open clinic and sanitation service;
- establish work schedules;
- confront storage ownership dispute;
- begin charter convention;
- create one ordinary gathering;
- experience one service failure caused by rushed preparation;
- record one promise.

## Months 2–6

- ratify or reject a charter;
- establish offices;
- transfer one temporary authority;
- add accessible housing;
- develop one cultural practice;
- complete one landmark candidate;
- handle one resident departure;
- establish external trade or service relationship.

## Months 7–12

- first budget cycle;
- one labor dispute;
- one ecological pressure;
- one public hearing;
- one successful festival or ordinary celebration;
- one institutional reform;
- one project completed without player participation.

# 8. Five-Year Simulation

At year five:

- population changes by at least 25 percent;
- at least one founding household has changed form;
- one child or dependent reaches a new life stage;
- one office has transferred twice;
- one service has changed provider or governance;
- one building has been adaptively reused;
- one cultural practice has mutated;
- one founder attribution is disputed;
- one neighboring relationship has changed;
- the wetland or ecological boundary shows consequence.

The player is absent for at least two of these years.

# 9. Founder Death or Reconstitution Test

At year six or later:

- the player dies or becomes legally absent;
- office succession proceeds;
- promises are classified;
- property and public assets are separated;
- IRIS/source-chain evidence is preserved;
- the player returns through verified but incomplete continuity.

The returned player must not automatically regain office, public access, or private relationships.

# 10. Ten-Year Constitutional Event

Trigger one major event:

- amendment;
- secession attempt;
- institutional merger;
- public-service municipalization;
- external protectorate offer;
- emergency-power challenge;
- machine or nonhuman standing case.

The event must involve at least three constituencies and more than one viable outcome.

# 11. Twenty-Year Outcomes

Generate at least three worldline branches.

## Branch A — Durable Civic Commons

Services remain broadly accessible. The founding charter is substantially amended. The player is remembered as one contributor among many.

## Branch B — Technical Service Republic

Highly reliable infrastructure and strong professional institutions emerge, with ongoing exclusion and political reform pressure.

## Branch C — Fragmented Mobile Successor

The original site partially empties. Households and institutions move along a transport route, preserving some law and culture while leaving ruins and disputed property.

No branch is the universal good ending.

# 12. Player Return Tests

Run returns after:

- 30 days;
- 2 years;
- 5 years;
- 20 years.

Each return must show:

- changed people;
- changed places;
- current services;
- expired permissions;
- unresolved commitments;
- cultural change;
- one ordinary scene;
- one irreversible consequence;
- one positive development not caused by the player.

# 13. Multiplayer Test

Two players participate in founding with different roles.

Required cases:

- disagreement over compact;
- one player holds office;
- one leaves the server;
- office succession proceeds;
- the remaining player cannot use the absent player's authority;
- both return after worldline divergence;
- shared construction credit remains plural;
- host migration does not change civic authority.

# 14. Emergent Campaign Tests

The campaign detector must identify:

- service neglect becoming a public dispute;
- a broken promise becoming a household and legal conflict;
- a cultural practice becoming institutionally adopted;
- a campaign resolved without the player;
- a situation where the player lacks standing;
- one private issue that remains private;
- one issue that becomes routine administration rather than dramatic questing.

# 15. Emotional and Recall Test

After seven real-world days away, research participants should recall:

- four residents by name;
- three professions;
- one household relationship;
- one public service dependency;
- one changed place;
- one promise;
- one charter conflict;
- one cultural practice;
- one person who disagreed with the player;
- one event completed without the player.

# 16. Quantitative Gates

Minimum gates:

```text
zero authority-token duplication
zero host-only civic bypasses
100% charter amendments preserve ancestry
100% office transitions produce handover or explicit failure
100% public services conserve inputs and outputs
>= 3 distinct constituency legitimacy views
>= 2 non-player initiated campaigns
>= 1 peaceful founder refusal
>= 1 successful successor government without player control
>= 1 cultural artifact changed by community use
>= 1 landmark arising from use rather than player designation
zero private-state disclosures without valid information path
```

# 17. Qualitative Gates

Researchers must find that:

- residents feel present before founding;
- the settlement does not feel owned by the player;
- charter choices alter lived routines;
- work and maintenance remain visible;
- absence produces understandable change;
- return creates emotional continuity rather than confusion;
- successors feel legitimate even when they disagree with the player;
- at least one player feels pride in being outgrown;
- no single build order appears morally canonical.

# 18. Failure Conditions

The benchmark fails if:

- residents spawn as rewards for buildings;
- the founder can dismiss inhabitants arbitrarily;
- services function without labor or inputs;
- charters are passive bonuses;
- the town freezes while the player is absent;
- all campaigns wait for the player;
- culture changes through menu selection;
- the successor exists only to betray or praise the founder;
- reconstitution restores office automatically;
- worldline branching duplicates authority;
- players cannot explain why the settlement changed.

# 19. Evidence Bundle

Publish:

- benchmark seed;
- fixture data;
- Chronicle event log;
- compact and charter versions;
- claims registry;
- household graph;
- service traces;
- budget traces;
- office transitions;
- campaign candidates and suppressions;
- cultural adoption traces;
- landmark history;
- absence summaries;
- worldline branches;
- replay hashes;
- player recall results;
- accessibility and safety review.

# Closing Benchmark

> **The proof is not that the player can build a town. The proof is that twenty simulated years later, the town can explain what the player began, what everyone else changed, and why it still matters.**
