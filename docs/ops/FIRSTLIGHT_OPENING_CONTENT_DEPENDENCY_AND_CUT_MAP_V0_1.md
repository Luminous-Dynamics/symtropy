# Firstlight Opening Content Dependency and Cut Map

## Purpose

The opening can fail through overproduction as easily as underproduction. This document identifies which content proves the game and which content merely decorates an unproven foundation.

# 1. Proof Spine

The minimum viable opening requires:

```text
embodiment
→ tactile intervention
→ recurring place
→ recurring people
→ learnable practice
→ physical danger
→ ordinary recovery
→ delayed consequence
→ major shared event
→ absence and changed return
```

No visual scale, lore volume, or procedural population substitutes for this spine.

# 2. Asset Reuse Without Repetition

The same spaces must support multiple meanings.

## Seedworks Commons

Used as:

- arrival shelter;
- work handover site;
- food and market space;
- festival staging area;
- emergency triage overflow;
- public notice site;
- changed return location.

## Clinic and Lantern Annex

Used as:

- ordinary care;
- training;
- private consultation;
- crisis treatment;
- grief and source-chain communication;
- public-health follow-up.

## Lower Works

Used as:

- infrastructure;
- dangerous traversal;
- historical discovery;
- hostile access path;
- repair project;
- later shortcut.

## Transit Yard

Used as:

- vehicle introduction;
- profession workplace;
- evacuation staging;
- labor dispute;
- social gathering after hours;
- route-horizon reveal.

# 3. Character Reuse Rules

Recurring cast should appear in different modes:

- working;
- resting;
- disagreeing;
- caring for someone;
- asking for help;
- refusing the player;
- acting competently without the player;
- being wrong;
- making jokes unrelated to the plot.

No flagship character should be introduced only through a dramatic biography speech.

# 4. Required Disciplines

The proof cannot be built by narrative design alone.

Required owner pairings:

- physical interaction + animation;
- simulation + level design;
- NPC cognition + authored characterization;
- profession mechanics + audio cues;
- IRIS UX + accessibility;
- destruction + persistence;
- campaign state + ordinary schedules;
- research + telemetry + qualitative interviews.

# 5. Production Gates

## Gate 1 — Greybox Love Test

Before final art:

- players can navigate four landmarks without a marker;
- one tactile task becomes easier through learning;
- players remember three residents after twenty-four hours;
- the ordinary evening is enjoyable in greybox;
- the danger sequence is physically exciting.

## Gate 2 — First Five Hours

Before building hours ten through forty:

- the first hour has no critical confusion;
- at least two player archetypes find self-directed activity;
- IRIS does not overtalk;
- one delayed consequence is understood;
- players choose to revisit a place without objective pressure.

## Gate 3 — Ten-Hour Identity

Before galactic teasers or broad content expansion:

- players articulate a profession identity;
- remember four people and four places;
- understand one institution;
- want at least one personally chosen next action.

## Gate 4 — Forty-Hour Continuity

Before calling the vertical slice production-ready:

- major event and aftermath persist;
- absence/return works;
- companions retain agency;
- world state remains replayable;
- no retention mechanic depends on guilt or real-time pressure.

# 6. Cut Tiers

## Tier A — Never Cut

- tactile opening;
- recurring home and four landmarks;
- Sera, Amadi, Morrow-7, Nia, Mara, and Lio or equivalent six-person web;
- maintenance, medicine, and witness proof;
- danger and rescue;
- ordinary evening;
- major event;
- aftermath;
- absence and return;
- IRIS uncertainty and memory;
- accessibility pathways.

## Tier B — Cut Only After User Test

- vehicle ownership beyond shared-use vehicle;
- second major ruin;
- additional combat faction;
- extended romance content;
- advanced construction;
- multiple festival variants;
- large crowd density.

## Tier C — Cut First

- cinematic prologue variants;
- unique interiors used once;
- lore collectibles without interaction value;
- duplicate procedural errands;
- distant-world exposition scenes;
- elaborate menu visualizations unsupported by play.

# 7. Warning Against False Completeness

The slice is not complete because it has:

- a polished skybox;
- every menu category;
- one representative item from fifty systems;
- voiced biographies;
- a scripted boss;
- a montage of future planets.

It is complete when the player cares what happens next in Firstlight.

## Cut Maxim

> **Protect recurrence, consequence, and physical pleasure before breadth. A smaller place that acquires memory is more valuable than a larger place the player merely visits.**
