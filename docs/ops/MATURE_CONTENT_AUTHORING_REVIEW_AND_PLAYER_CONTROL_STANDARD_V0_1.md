# Mature Content Authoring, Review, and Player Control Standard

## Purpose

This standard governs content involving bodies, sex, coercion, addiction, mental illness, self-harm, domestic abuse, war crimes, detention, displacement, grief, and other potentially distressing experiences.

It exists to make the work braver and more responsible, not safer through vagueness.

## 1. Primary Test

Before approval, the team must answer:

> **What human, social, or systemic truth becomes playable here that could not be communicated through a less invasive scene?**

“Shows the game is mature,” “raises stakes,” and “makes the villain worse” are insufficient answers.

## 2. Content Record

Every mature scene or systemic packet receives a record containing:

```text
content_id
themes
intensity
presentation channels
player role
consent requirements
privacy scope
required warnings
skip behavior
accessibility alternatives
multiplayer restrictions
subject review
localization notes
downstream consequences
cut rationale
```

## 3. Intensity Dimensions

Content is classified separately by:

- visual intensity;
- audio intensity;
- textual detail;
- interactivity;
- loss of control;
- intimacy;
- threat;
- duration;
- surprise;
- relevance to personal history;
- multiplayer exposure.

A scene can be visually mild and emotionally intense.

## 4. Player Controls

Controls should include, where relevant:

- advance content notice;
- category toggles;
- reduced-detail presentation;
- fade or summary;
- skip;
- pause;
- motion-safe rendering;
- audio substitution;
- text substitution;
- private-mode notifications;
- multiplayer opt-in or separation.

Skipping cannot produce worse moral, relationship, progression, or resource outcomes solely because content was skipped.

## 5. Surprise

Some stories require discovery and uncertainty. Content warnings need not spoil every plot point.

However, high-impact categories such as sexual violence, self-harm, domestic abuse, reproductive loss, torture, and graphic bodily content require discoverable category-level controls before exposure.

The game may surprise the player with an event. It should not ambush them with a content category they explicitly disabled.

## 6. Player Role

The player's role is classified as:

- witness;
- helper;
- affected person;
- investigator;
- authority holder;
- alleged or established perpetrator;
- bystander;
- family or community member.

The role determines interaction boundaries.

The game must not provide interactive sexual assault, torture, or abusive-control mechanics as ordinary player expression.

## 7. Subject Review

Relevant content should receive review by people with professional, lived, cultural, or scholarly expertise.

Review goals:

- identify stereotype and exploitation risk;
- verify institutional and material plausibility;
- improve language and aftermath;
- preserve internal diversity;
- identify inaccessible presentation;
- identify real-world procedural detail that should be removed;
- assess whether the scene centers the harmed person or merely uses them.

One reviewer does not represent an entire affected group.

Disagreement is documented rather than hidden.

## 8. Privacy Within Fiction

Authoring must specify who knows what and how.

Private facts do not become ambient dialogue topics.

Required checks:

- Did the NPC witness this?
- Was the record published?
- Does the speaker have lawful access?
- Is the information rumor, testimony, or verified fact?
- Is the player entitled to see it?
- Does IRIS have permission to retrieve or repeat it?

## 9. Aftermath Requirement

A serious harm scene cannot end at emotional climax.

At least two aftermath domains must continue:

```text
medical
legal
material
relational
occupational
spiritual
political
housing
care
privacy
historical memory
```

The scene may remain unresolved. It may not vanish because the player left the room.

## 10. Ordinary-Life Requirement

Characters involved in mature content require ordinary-life material before or after the event.

Examples:

- work;
- meals;
- humor;
- sport;
- parenting;
- repair;
- art;
- boredom;
- friendship;
- personal projects.

This is not tonal relief added by formula. It protects the character from becoming only a wound or lesson.

## 11. No Suffering Competition

The game must not escalate every storyline to remain impressive.

A difficult scene may involve:

- embarrassment;
- refusal;
- exhaustion;
- an inaccessible bathroom;
- a medication shortage;
- an unanswered message;
- a relationship ending;
- institutional delay.

Not every mature story requires death, assault, war, or catastrophe.

## 12. Visual and Audio Restraint

Use detail when it serves care, evidence, or physical understanding.

Avoid:

- lingering camera attention on vulnerability;
- erotic framing of injury or medical care;
- repeated screams as atmosphere;
- gore used to establish seriousness;
- voyeuristic surveillance;
- inaccessible distortion;
- exploitative marketing captures.

Promotional materials follow the same standard as gameplay.

## 13. Language

Dialogue should avoid forcing affected characters to explain their experience perfectly.

People may:

- use slang;
- avoid a label;
- change their account;
- remember incompletely;
- make jokes;
- be angry;
- be boring;
- refuse to talk;
- speak through another person or tool.

Generated language remains constrained by verified semantic frames and privacy scopes.

## 14. Localization

Localization review must consider:

- legal terminology;
- medical terminology;
- religious context;
- gender and relationship language;
- age and adulthood markers;
- stigma;
- slang;
- content warnings;
- cultural differences in directness;
- whether a metaphor becomes insulting or misleading.

A direct translation of a mature scene may not preserve its ethical meaning.

## 15. Multiplayer

Mature NPC content does not authorize player harassment.

Multiplayer controls include:

- opt-in for intimate roleplay systems;
- blocking;
- private-space permissions;
- separation from shared households or organizations;
- anti-retaliation tools;
- moderation evidence;
- content-category settings;
- server policy.

No player may force another into sexual, abusive, humiliating, or traumatic fiction through inventory, housing, life support, body control, or social authority.

## 16. IRIS Use

IRIS may:

- present warnings;
- offer privacy-safe resources;
- preserve evidence;
- assist with accessibility;
- summarize skipped scenes;
- explain authority and consent boundaries;
- disclose its uncertainty.

IRIS may not:

- diagnose from one scene;
- identify hidden attraction;
- declare guilt;
- manipulate attachment;
- reveal confidential records;
- pressure a player to continue difficult content;
- dramatize distress to prevent skipping.

## 17. Validation Questions

Before shipping, reviewers ask:

1. Is every participant clearly an adult where sexual content exists?
2. Can the content be skipped or reduced without penalty?
3. Are consent and authority states explicit in the runtime?
4. Does private information have a causal path?
5. Does the affected person have life beyond the event?
6. Does aftermath continue?
7. Is the player made a savior by default?
8. Does the scene rely on stereotype?
9. Does the scene provide dangerous procedural detail?
10. Would the content still be worthwhile without shock value?
11. Are multiplayer and accessibility boundaries present?
12. Is there a clear cut condition?

## 18. Cut Conditions

Cut or substantially revise content when:

- its primary function is shock;
- it duplicates another scene's theme;
- it makes a vulnerable person decorative;
- it requires disproportionate review or localization cost for little playable value;
- it cannot preserve player control;
- it depends on graphic detail;
- it creates unsafe multiplayer mechanics;
- it contradicts privacy or consent authority;
- it crowds out ordinary life;
- the aftermath cannot be supported by the simulation.

## Closing Principle

> **Going there is not a claim made by the trailer. It is the discipline of staying with people before, during, and after experiences that other games turn into spectacle or omit entirely.**
