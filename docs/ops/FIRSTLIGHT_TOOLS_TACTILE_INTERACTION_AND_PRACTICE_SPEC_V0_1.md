# Firstlight Tools, Tactile Interaction, and Practice Specification

## Purpose

The opening toolset must feel like a small collection of real instruments rather than an inventory of abstract abilities.

# 1. Proof Toolset

## Field Multimeter and Isolation Probe

Reads voltage, continuity, field state and declared lockout status. Can be miscalibrated or used incorrectly.

## Acoustic and Vibration Probe

Translates contact sound and vibration into audio, haptic and visual alternatives. Supports learned comparison rather than automatic diagnosis.

## Compact Driver and Torque Tool

Supports fastener identification, torque control, wear and access-angle constraints.

## Seal and Patch Kit

Temporary stabilization with surface preparation, material compatibility, cure time and later inspection.

## Medical Field Roll

Contains gloves, dressings, airway support, thermal protection, monitoring, medication references and documentation tools according to setting and role.

## Portable Monitor

Shows measurements, trends and sensor confidence. It cannot replace observation or consent.

## Evidence Capture and Custody Sleeve

Records context, time, handler, seal state and privacy scope.

## Field Deck / IRIS

Provides overlays, references, history, communication and bounded inference according to permission.

# 2. Interaction Layers

Each tool can support:

- quick use under pressure;
- deliberate use;
- setup;
- calibration;
- cleaning;
- repair;
- storage;
- handover.

Not every use requires full manual simulation. The interaction burden scales with consequence, player setting, and familiarity.

# 3. Feedback

Tools communicate through combinations of:

- hand animation;
- contact alignment;
- sound;
- vibration;
- resistance;
- visible material response;
- instrument reading;
- IRIS interpretation;
- companion reaction.

No critical cue may exist only in color.

# 4. Wear and History

A tool may acquire:

- calibration drift;
- worn grip;
- repaired casing;
- owner marks;
- changed sound;
- replacement parts;
- procedural restrictions;
- sentimental association.

Wear should create maintenance and choice, not constant inventory punishment.

# 5. Familiarity

Repeated use may improve:

- animation economy;
- setup speed;
- stability;
- cue recognition;
- error recovery;
- available shortcuts.

Familiarity does not create supernatural output bonuses.

# 6. Preparation and Cleanup

Preparation matters when it changes risk:

- sanitation;
- power isolation;
- surface preparation;
- calibration;
- evidence context;
- correct supply selection.

Cleanup matters when it affects:

- contamination;
- tool availability;
- custody;
- next shift;
- public space.

Routine steps may compress after mastery unless interrupted or consequential.

# 7. Practice

Practice environments provide:

- repeatable faults;
- pause and replay;
- explanation;
- accessibility adjustment;
- companion demonstration;
- self-comparison;
- no consumable cost in training mode where appropriate.

# 8. Failure Feedback

After a procedure, the player may review:

- observations available at the time;
- chosen hypothesis;
- tool readings;
- missed or ambiguous cues;
- execution deviations;
- outcome;
- uncertainty.

The review must not reveal hidden simulation truth that the character could not responsibly know.

# 9. Co-op

Tools may be shared through actual handover.

Two people can coordinate:

- one stabilizes, one repairs;
- one treats, one monitors;
- one documents, one collects;
- one operates controls, one works at the system.

A remote player cannot manipulate a tool through account ownership.

# 10. Production Gate

Do not expand the tool catalogue until at least one tool in each proof profession demonstrates:

- satisfying contact;
- learned cue;
- setup or calibration;
- pressure use;
- cleanup or handover;
- persistent history;
- accessible alternative feedback.

## Tool Maxim

> **A beloved tool is not rare because the loot table says so. It is rare because the player knows its feel, trusts its behavior, and remembers what they did with it.**
