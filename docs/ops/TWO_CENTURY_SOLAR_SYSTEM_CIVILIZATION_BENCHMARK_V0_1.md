# Two-Century Solar System Civilization Benchmark

## Purpose

This benchmark tests whether Symtropy can preserve physical causality, rights, identity, political divergence, and meaningful player consequence across a populated solar system for two hundred simulated years.

It is not a content-volume test.

It is a systems-integrity test.

## Core Question

> **Can many worlds remain connected enough to share rescue, knowledge, trade, standards, and history while becoming autonomous enough that delay, generation, and local survival produce real divergence?**

# 1. Benchmark Scale

The fixture contains:

```text
1 inhabited Earth region and orbital network
2 lunar polities
2 Mars settlements
1 Ceres logistics and water polity
2 asteroid industrial settlements
1 rotating free habitat
1 generation ship departing for the outer system
1 rescue and traffic compact
1 interplanetary clearing union
1 multi-world archive network
1 nonhuman or agency-uncertain contact zone
```

Population representation:

```text
150 named agents across the fixture
2,000 simulated households
background cohorts representing 60,000–250,000 inhabitants by phase
machines and nonhuman agents where appropriate
```

The test may use reduced counts during early implementation, but state shape and invariants remain the same.

# 2. Time Horizon

```text
Phase A: years 0–10       corridor formation
Phase B: years 10–35      settlement growth and dependency
Phase C: years 35–70      autonomy and generational change
Phase D: years 70–110     crisis, fleet escalation, and relief
Phase E: years 110–150    constitutional divergence and first contact
Phase F: years 150–200    recovery, generation-ship decision, and worldline fork
```

# 4. Initial Conditions

## Earth-Orbit Compact

Strengths:

```text
large industry
archives
medical knowledge
launch infrastructure
```

Weaknesses:

```text
political fragmentation
legacy claims
dependence on orbital materials
slow federal mandate
```

## Lunar Polities

One is a mature worker-governed industrial settlement. The other is a heritage and scientific preserve with strict dust, water, and access rules.

## Mars Settlements

One remains dependent on Earth finance and proprietary life-support systems. One is a multigenerational autonomous charter settlement with limited industrial capacity.

## Ceres

A logistics and water polity that operates depots, rescue infrastructure, and a clearing node. It is strategically important but population-small.

## Asteroid Settlements

One cooperative material processor and one company-controlled extraction habitat with debt-bound transport.

## Free Habitat

A rotating habitat founded by migrants from several worlds. It is culturally plural and politically associated rather than subordinate.

## Generation Ship

A long-duration vessel with a founding mission, 8,000 inhabitants, multiple generations planned, and a lawful mission-renewal constitution.

# 5. Event Sequence

## Event 1 — Delayed Treaty Formation

The worlds negotiate a rescue and navigation compact.

Required proof:

```text
bounded representative mandates
messages with physical arrival times
provisional signatures
sequential ratification
local disagreement
shared technical standards without shared sovereignty
```

## Event 2 — Missed Transfer Window

A critical shipment of membrane catalysts misses a Mars window.

Consequences must propagate through:

```text
habitat maintenance
market prices
credit
politics
migration plans
alternate manufacturing
```

## Event 3 — Habitat Drift

Sensor calibration hides a slow atmospheric and microbial imbalance aboard the free habitat.

The benchmark tests:

```text
uncertainty
maintenance succession
public environmental data
care burden
emergency authority and expiry
```

## Event 4 — Settlement Autonomy Convention

The dependent Mars settlement seeks autonomy after becoming multigenerational and materially capable.

The test requires:

```text
founding debt claims
resident standing
company ownership conflict
life-support rights
transport and exit
minority protection
lawful renegotiation or separation
```

## Event 5 — Deep-Space Distress

A passenger vessel suffers thermal and propulsion failure between Mars and Ceres.

A cargo tug can divert but carries settlement-critical medicine. A patrol ship can respond later. Messages arrive at different times.

Required proof:

```text
real intercept feasibility
consumable horizon
non-economic rescue priority
partial rescue possibility
contract and shortage consequences
post-incident review
```

## Event 6 — Trade and Clearing Crisis

Crossing offers, stale inventory reports, a relay outage, and a currency confidence shock create incompatible market views.

Required proof:

```text
no global price truth
no cargo duplication
clearing-round finality with correction
credit and collateral limits
physical settlement
fraud investigation based on evidence
```

## Event 7 — Blockade and Humanitarian Corridor

A dispute over company-controlled asteroid labor escalates into interdiction.

Required proof:

```text
real patrol and route coverage
civilian and medical exemptions
inspection and appeal
rescue obligations
blockade leakage
local commander discretion under delayed orders
ceasefire and demobilization
```

## Event 8 — Contact Zone

Mining activity encounters an agency-uncertain lithic or distributed intelligence.

Required proof:

```text
survey evidence
pause of irreversible activity
plural human representation
noncontact and territory interpretation
settlement and extraction claims revised
no species-wide spokesperson invented
```

## Event 9 — Generation-Ship Renewal

Decades after launch, descendants receive evidence that the destination may be unsuitable while an alternate settlement option exists.

Required proof:

```text
mission archive and dissent
current local knowledge
constitutional renewal
right to redirect or settle
household and cultural consequences
no founder mission as eternal authority
```

## Event 10 — Player Absence

The player is absent for:

```text
6 months
10 years
50 years
```

The solar system must continue through deterministic background simulation, preserve named continuity, and produce legible return summaries without resolving every conflict in the player's favor or freezing the world.

## Event 11 — Worldline Fork

At year 150, fork the fixture before a major autonomy and contact decision.

Branch A:

```text
shared rescue compact strengthens
Mars gains associated autonomy
mining pauses for contact
```

Branch B:

```text
security compact centralizes
company claims survive longer
contact becomes quarantine conflict
```

Both branches must preserve pre-fork identities, messages, cargo ancestry, debts, claims, and archive evidence without transferable-asset duplication.

# 6. Invariants

## Physical

```text

```


# 9. Hard Failure Conditions

The benchmark fails immediately if:

1. A distant actor reads current remote state without delivered evidence.
2. A message arrives earlier than physically possible.
3. Cargo, currency-backed assets, or people duplicate across transit or fork.
4. A habitat's air, water, food, or heat changes without causal process.
5. A remote order directly overrides local survival authority outside mandate.
6. A colony remains permanently subordinate solely because a founder financed it.
7. Minimum life support is revoked for labor refusal or debt.
8. A distress vessel is ignored because its passengers have low economic value.
9. Unknown or alien contact becomes a hostile target by category alone.
10. A blockade functions as a scalar modifier without route coverage.
11. Surrender or boarding deletes private records, custody, or care obligations.
12. A generation ship's descendants cannot reconsider the mission.
13. Player absence freezes the system.
14. Save migration loses in-flight messages, shipments, or habitat state.
15. Worldline fork duplicates transferable assets or erases pre-fork obligations.

# 10. Promotion Criteria

The v1.5 system family remains `D3 / I0` until the benchmark or staged sub-fixtures provide dated evidence.

Minimum staged promotion path:

```text
1. two-node delayed-message harness
2. one closed-loop habitat fixture
3. one transfer-window shipment and rescue fixture
4. one delayed contract and clearing fixture
5. one fleet identification and ceasefire fixture
6. one settlement-autonomy fixture
7. integrated 20-year reduced solar-system run
8. full two-century benchmark
```

## Final Line

> **The benchmark succeeds when two centuries of distance produce not a synchronized empire or a pile of disconnected maps, but a solar system whose people can still rescue, trade, argue, remember, separate, and recognize one another across time.**
