# Breaking of Firstlight and Continuance Base Benchmark

## Purpose

Define the smallest credible proof that Symtropy can make players love a unique place and people within two hours, break that stable home without invalidating attachment, and transfer emotional and systemic continuity into a mobile base.

This benchmark does not prove the galaxy, full NPC cognition, civilization simulation, or forty-hour production readiness.

It proves the opening foundation.

# 1. Reference Build Scope

The benchmark build contains:

- one bounded Firstlight district and approach;
- one validated catastrophe family;
- twelve cast seeds;
- eight high-salience role anchors;
- sixteen to twenty-four lower-salience residents;
- the Bent Feeder interaction;
- one signature wonder event;
- three emergent practice threads;
- one personal-space modification;
- one preparation window;
- three evacuation route variants;
- one Continuance Crawler chassis;
- five functional vehicle systems;
- constrained loading;
- departure and first post-title route choice;
- one short post-title camp or travel sequence.

# 2. Cast Seeds

At least twelve fixed seeds must be authored, generated, and reviewed.

They must vary:

- names;
- bodies;
- voices;
- households;
- professions;
- role overlaps;
- rival identity;
- ambitions;
- faults;
- catastrophe behavior;
- survival outcomes.

No two seeds may reuse the full flagship cast.

At least three seeds should produce:

- a nonhuman or machine utility steward;
- a clinician who does not evacuate with the player;
- a cultural worker who becomes technically central;
- a rival who performs a genuine rescue;
- a base keeper who disputes player control.

# 3. Scenario Seed

Reference scenario: `Watershed Resonance / Tower Wake`.

## Initial Conditions

- severe but credible storm;
- drainage maintenance debt;
- overloaded public feeder;
- abandoned tower emitting low-confidence anomalies;
- Continuance Crawler in municipal service with deferred brake and sanitation work;
- public festival preparation increasing load;
- private organization seeking tower data;
- three viable evacuation directions with different constraints.

## Guaranteed Historical Pressure

The tower and watershed systems enter a coupled state that prevents Firstlight from continuing as one stable settlement.

## Variable State

- warning timing;
- public response;
- route failure order;
- hostile pressure;
- crawler readiness;
- household location;
- supplies;
- survivor decisions;
- evidence.

# 4. Two-Hour Session Procedure

Participants play without developer intervention.

Researchers may observe but should not explain systems unless the participant reaches an accessibility or blocking condition covered by the protocol.

Measures occur at:

- 10 minutes;
- 30 minutes;
- 60 minutes;
- title reveal;
- 20 minutes after title;
- 24 hours;
- 7 days.

# 5. Ten-Minute Gates

The participant should:

- gain control within 20 seconds;
- perform one material action;
- identify one immediate human need;
- hear IRIS without receiving a lore lecture;
- understand at least one consequence of their action;
- encounter no more than two names worth remembering.

Failure if:

- controls feel detached;
- interaction reads as a disguised progress bar;
- text dominates action;
- players cannot tell why the feeder matters;
- IRIS feels like an objective narrator.

# 6. Thirty-Minute Gates

The participant should:

- recognize Firstlight as inhabited;
- remember two people by action or relationship;
- see the Continuance Crawler in ordinary use;
- make or modify one persistent object or space;
- express at least one self-directed interest;
- observe one striking world-scale phenomenon;
- choose where to spend attention without a class screen.

Research prompts:

- “Who have you met?”
- “What do they seem to care about?”
- “What would you like to do here?”
- “What was strange?”

# 7. Sixty-Minute Gates

The participant should:

- have one emerging practice identity;
- understand that warnings are uncertain and contested;
- know one resident has plans unrelated to the player;
- have made one preparation decision;
- have a reason to care about at least one object, route, person, or place;
- not yet believe the settlement is guaranteed to be destroyed.

Failure if the player predicts a standard scripted massacre with high confidence because of obvious genre signaling.

# 8. Breaking Gates

During evacuation, the build must demonstrate:

- at least three simultaneous fronts;
- visible NPC independent action;
- one lawful or understandable refusal;
- one route change;
- one result from earlier preparation;
- one unresolved fate;
- one outcome the player could not personally control;
- physical loading constraints;
- no universal perfect-save path.

The participant must not be trapped by failure in one optional practice thread.

# 9. Departure Gates

At title reveal, the participant should understand:

- Firstlight is not simply deleted;
- some people escaped elsewhere;
- some fates are unresolved;
- the crawler is physically limited;
- loaded objects are actually present;
- survivors have opinions about route and authority;
- the next destination is a player choice within real constraints.

# 10. Post-Title Gates

Within twenty minutes after title:

- the crawler must require one practical action;
- one survivor must act independently;
- one ordinary-life scene must occur;
- one loading choice must produce a visible benefit or cost;
- one possible destination must become legible;
- the player must gain a bounded personal space;
- no “tutorial complete” banner may appear.

# 11. Delayed Recall

## Twenty-Four Hours

Ask participants to recall:

- people;
- place;
- warning;
- chosen priority;
- object carried;
- crawler limitation;
- desired next action.

## Seven Days

Ask without screenshots first:

- “Who do you remember?”
- “What happened to Firstlight?”
- “What did you choose to carry or protect?”
- “What did your crawler feel or sound like?”
- “Whose fate do you still wonder about?”
- “What would you do next?”

# 12. Quantitative Promotion Gates

Required minimums in representative testing:

- 80% recognize two cast members at title reveal;
- 70% recall two people after seven days;
- 65% recall one relationship between NPCs;
- 75% identify a self-directed desire from the opening;
- 80% understand one earlier action changed evacuation state;
- 75% understand no perfect evacuation was physically possible;
- 70% want to learn at least one unresolved fate;
- 75% identify the crawler as inherited continuity rather than reward loot;
- 70% recall one specific interior sound, object, or inconvenience;
- 65% voluntarily continue past title in an uncoerced session;
- fewer than 15% describe the loss as manipulative;
- fewer than 10% believe every named resident necessarily dies;
- fewer than 10% report that player actions were obviously irrelevant.

# 13. Unique-Cast Gates

Across twelve seeds:

- no full flagship cast repeats;
- each seed passes role coverage;
- each flagship voice is distinguishable;
- households remain coherent;
- at least six people are visually recognizable without nameplates;
- cast-specific evacuation decisions vary;
- no seed produces only admirable allies and obvious villains;
- no person is generated solely as casualty motivation;
- multiplayer clients agree on all identities.

# 14. Catastrophe Runtime Gates

For fixed seeds and actions:

- deterministic replay hashes match;
- threshold cause graph matches;
- routes conserve travel and capacity;
- survivor ledger has no impossible location changes;
- missing remains distinct from dead;
- cargo and residents do not duplicate;
- player departure does not freeze Firstlight;
- save/load preserves warnings, preparation, and outcomes.

# 15. Crawler Physics Gates

The reference crawler must demonstrate:

- perceptible mass difference between two load plans;
- braking or handling effects from load distribution;
- power tradeoff;
- water or sanitation constraint;
- one damage and repair chain;
- physical cargo access;
- one resident transport accommodation;
- one route incompatible with the current configuration;
- no inventory duplication in multiplayer.

# 16. Emotional Safety Gates

Testers must be able to:

- adjust graphic intensity;
- reduce panic audio and camera effects;
- pause or slow nonnetworked critical sequences;
- receive navigation and interaction support;
- skip optional intimate grief scenes;
- return after a break without fictional guilt messaging;
- understand that not personally rescuing everyone is not equivalent to moral failure.

Research must distinguish emotional intensity from coercive distress.

# 17. Ablation Studies

Run controlled variants.

## 17.1 No Ordinary-Life Window

Purpose: prove whether attachment depends on pre-catastrophe ordinary life.

Expected result: worse person and place recall.

## 17.2 Universal Authored Cast

Purpose: compare recognition quality and worldline ownership against generated casts.

## 17.3 No Loading Constraint

Purpose: test whether unrestricted rescue weakens continuity meaning.

## 17.4 Cinematic Catastrophe

Purpose: compare player agency and consequence comprehension against systemic evacuation.

## 17.5 Pristine Reward Vehicle

Purpose: test whether an unlocked clean base feels less emotionally continuous than the damaged crawler.

## 17.6 No Signature Wonder

Purpose: test whether the opening becomes too civic and earthbound.

# 18. Kill Criteria

Stop expanding the opening if:

- movement and interaction are not enjoyable in greybox;
- cast voices blur together;
- players do not remember Firstlight before it breaks;
- the catastrophe feels like a fake choice;
- load tradeoffs are not legible;
- the crawler interior cannot be navigated from memory;
- generated cast validation consumes more production than it creates value;
- accessibility support changes outcomes rather than execution burden;
- the two-hour opening cannot replay deterministically;
- ordinary life is consistently skipped because it lacks intrinsic interest.

# 19. Promotion Decision

Passing this benchmark promotes only:

- the two-hour opening structure;
- unique cast generation at bounded scale;
- one catastrophe family;
- one crawler chassis;
- the first post-title travel loop.

It does not authorize:

- hundreds of generated flagship NPCs;
- full planet simulation;
- all mobile-base forms;
- Atlas implementation;
- forty-hour content production;
- claims that players love the final game.

## Benchmark Maxim

> **The proof is not that players felt sad when Firstlight broke. The proof is that they can still name what they loved, explain what their actions preserved, and describe why the damaged vehicle ahead feels like their life rather than the game's next feature.**
