# Historical Texture and World Coherence Benchmark

## Objective

Prove that historical texture changes what a region looks like, how it works, what people know, and what the player can do.

The benchmark does not reward the number of generated names.

It rewards causal distinction, discoverability, continuity, and remembered consequence.

# 1. Fixture

The reference fixture contains:

```text
3 connected regions
8 named settlements or habitats
6 corporate or successor lineages
5 diaspora networks
4 shadow institutions
12 historical figures
8 major events
6 artistic or ritual movements
3 archival schools
1 suspected nonhuman contact zone
```

Time span:

```text
120 years of generated history
30 days of playable present
20-year player absence
3 worldline branches
```

# 2. Required Present-Day Sites

At minimum:

- one public or post-corporate utility city;
- one orbital habitat with disaster memory;
- one migrant corridor city;
- one archive center;
- one industrial or ecological ruin;
- one mobile community;
- one quiet or noncontact region;
- one corporate civilization still functioning well enough to attract residents.

# 3. Historical Generation Pass

The generator must produce:

- material causes for each major event;
- bounded participants;
- institution and successor ancestry;
- displaced or transformed populations;
- physical scars;
- laws or customs;
- evidence with custody;
- at least two interpretations of major events;
- cultural responses;
- current gameplay dependencies.

# 4. Player Discovery Run

A player enters with no encyclopedia unlocks.

Within ordinary play, they should be able to infer:

- why one city exposes all pressure telemetry;
- why another treats shade as a commons;
- why a route family distrusts commercial rescue dispatch;
- why a food is associated with mourning;
- why an old corporate logo remains on public infrastructure;
- why two archives disagree about one famous figure;
- why a shadow clinic is both necessary and dangerous.

Discovery should occur through action and environment, not only text logs.

# 5. Corporate Outcome Branches

One corporate lineage is resolved three ways:

```text
Branch A: worker-customer mutualization
Branch B: paternalist service state
Branch C: machine receivership and Null drift
```

The branches must differ in:

- access rules;
- architecture and interface;
- worker life;
- repair options;
- public narratives;
- client relationships;
- successor claims;
- player missions.

# 6. Event Memory Branches

One disaster receives:

```text
official heroic narrative
survivor and labor interpretation
machine-witness evidence
later correction
```

The correction must not reset all beliefs instantly.

NPC responses vary by trust, access, identity, and relationship to the event.

# 7. Long-Absence Run

After twenty simulated years:

- at least one historical figure has died or retired;
- one movement has commercialized, fragmented, or revived;
- one diaspora has changed its return politics;
- one shadow institution has legalized or become predatory;
- one monument has been altered;
- one corporate successor dispute has advanced;
- young NPCs understand an old event differently from elders;
- physical infrastructure still reflects ancestry.

# 8. Hard Failures

The benchmark fails immediately if:

- history produces names without causal edges;
- every NPC knows the same account;
- private archives appear in public knowledge without transmission;
- a corporation disappears and all dependencies vanish instantly;
- a diaspora behaves as one opinion;
- an art movement spreads without carriers;
- places share interchangeable architecture and daily life;
- a historical figure alone causes a system-scale event without capability;
- correction overwrites prior evidence;
- worldline branches duplicate unique people, artifacts, or assets;
- player absence freezes culture;
- lore can only be discovered through encyclopedia text.

# 9. Quantitative Evidence

Record:

```text
entity and consequence counts
causal-edge completeness
contradiction count
orphaned entity count
worldline ancestry errors
knowledge-boundary violations
save/load determinism
content memory and storage cost
historical LOD error
player discovery coverage
```

# 10. Human Evaluation

Blind reviewers receive captured play sessions without location labels.

They answer:

1. Can you distinguish the settlements by material and social identity?
2. Can you infer at least one important historical cause from ordinary play?
3. Do corporate societies have real attractions as well as harms?
4. Do mobile peoples feel like societies rather than quest populations?
5. Are historical disagreements understandable without implying all accounts are equal?
6. Does art appear in ordinary life?
7. Does history create meaningful gameplay rather than exposition?
8. Do places remain memorable one week later?

# 11. Promotion Gate

Historical texture is promoted only when:

- all hard failures pass;
- deterministic replay succeeds;
- worldline variants remain causally distinct;
- human reviewers identify at least six of eight locations above chance;
- no single corporate, cultural, or historiographic template dominates the fixture;
- content remains within declared simulation and storage budgets;
- rights, privacy, and accessibility reviews pass.
