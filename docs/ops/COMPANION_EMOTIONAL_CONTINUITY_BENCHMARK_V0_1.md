# Companion Emotional Continuity Benchmark

## Goal

Prove that players remember companions as people with independent lives and that shared power emerges through practiced coordination rather than approval bonuses.

# 1. Benchmark Cast

Use at least six named companions from the Firstlight atlas:

```text
Sera Vale
Tomas Reed
Amadi Nko
Morrow-7
Nia Kgosana
Mara Quill
```

At least two additional inhabitants must remain important without becoming active traveling companions.

# 2. Thirty-Day Scenario

## Days 1–3 — Ordinary Contact

- meet companions at work rather than recruitment cutscenes;
- observe household and institution ties;
- perform one limited joint procedure;
- encounter one companion who declines travel.

## Days 4–7 — Shared Work

- complete two different multi-agent procedures;
- record one coordination mistake;
- review a failure;
- establish one domain-specific trust permission;
- discover one mundane secret without loot.

## Days 8–12 — Conflicting Obligations

- request help while a companion has another commitment;
- face one public disagreement;
- preserve one private boundary;
- choose whether to misuse a companion's institutional standing;
- observe companions acting together without the player.

## Days 13–18 — Crisis

- infrastructure failure or evacuation;
- companion initiative saves someone other than the player;
- one lawful refusal;
- one companion injury or leave state;
- one joint procedure under communication failure;
- one secret changes interpretation of earlier evidence.

## Days 19–23 — Consequence

- work, pay, household, and care consequences;
- conflict review;
- possible apology, restitution, or continuing distance;
- companion project milestone;
- player receives no universal “relationship level up.”

## Days 24–30 — Departure and Continuity

- one companion leaves active travel voluntarily;
- one remains but changes role;
- one becomes temporarily unavailable;
- one private joke or shared procedure recurs;
- ordinary life continues after the main crisis.

# 3. Absence Tests

Simulate:

- seven days;
- six months;
- five years.

On return, verify:

- companions continued projects and careers;
- households changed causally;
- messages obeyed delivery rules;
- availability differs;
- relationships changed through actual events;
- no named companion disappeared into aggregation;
- IRIS reports only legitimately known changes.

# 4. Worldline Branches

Run three branches:

## Branch A — Cooperative Continuity

The player respects refusal, supports independent projects, and maintains reliable handovers.

## Branch B — Instrumental Leadership

The player repeatedly treats companions as operational resources while remaining materially effective.

## Branch C — Fracture and Return

A major privacy or authority breach causes departure; later events create possible limited cooperation without guaranteed reconciliation.

Branches must preserve unique entity and source-chain identity.

# 5. Coordination Metrics

Measure:

- explicit communication steps per practiced procedure;
- preparation accuracy;
- challenge detection;
- interruption recovery;
- error correction;
- duplicated work;
- unsafe assumption rate;
- role clarity;
- refusal handling.

Improvement must result from practiced history and review, not hidden global bonuses.

# 6. Emotional Recall Study

After a delayed playtest session, ask players to identify for each flagship companion:

- profession;
- one habit;
- one boundary;
- one independent project;
- one relationship not involving the player;
- one associated place;
- one change during absence.

Target: median correct recall of at least five categories for four companions after one week away from the game.

# 7. Agency Study

Players should agree that companions:

- had understandable reasons;
- sometimes acted without waiting;
- could refuse meaningfully;
- had lives outside the party;
- were useful in different ways;
- did not exist only to praise or criticize the player.

# 8. Secret-Content Study

Verify:

- players discover secrets through multiple perception modes;
- no critical tutorial or consent information is hidden;
- at least one discovered secret has no material reward;
- at least one secret remains unresolved;
- worldline secrets do not leak;
- private disclosures require permission;
- the region does not expose completion percentages by default.

# 9. Performance and Persistence

Capture:

- active companion cognition cost;
- background update cost;
- relationship and project storage growth;
- replay determinism;
- save migration;
- network authority;
- privacy redaction;
- LOD transitions;
- branch recovery.

# 10. Hard Failure Conditions

Fail immediately if:

- companions obey every request;
- affection unlocks authoritative powers;
- a companion teleports to the player;
- romance provides strongest combat or profession bonuses;
- refusal cannot survive economic dependency;
- off-screen companions freeze;
- death transfers the same emotional role to a replacement;
- IRIS reveals private cognition;
- player absence causes no meaningful change;
- secrets become a loot checklist;
- worldline branches duplicate a unique companion;
- generated dialogue changes consent, delegation, or historical truth.

# 11. Promotion Evidence

Promotion requires:

```text
source revision
scenario seed
build identity
save files
replay bundle
causal traces
privacy review
performance report
player recall results
agency survey
accessibility review
failure log
ablation comparison
```

Design remains D3 / I0 until this evidence exists.
