# First Atlas Reconnection and Galactic Reach Benchmark

## Purpose

This benchmark is the bounded proof for v2.4.

It does not attempt to simulate the whole galaxy. It proves that one seed mission can cross the original distance, found a destination civilization, develop an independent history, choose whether to build an Atlas Anchor, reconnect without time travel, and survive one route emergency without duplicating people or erasing local sovereignty.

> **The benchmark passes only if the destination feels like a world that lived—not a level waiting for the player to unlock it.**

# 1. Reference Scenario

## 1.1 Origin

**Sol Exterior Metric Transfer Authority Endpoint 2**

Traffic call: **Echo Two**

Worker name: **The Cold Yard**

Starting conditions:

- mature solar-system civilization;
- one incomplete Atlas Anchor program;
- no operational interstellar route;
- high-relativistic metric-sail capability;
- contested funding and migration policy;
- thirty-six named origin NPCs in benchmark scope;
- two active players.

## 1.2 Seed Vessel

Legal name: **Municipal Seed Vessel C-17**

Crew name: **Muni Seventeen**

Starting population:

- 480 inhabitants;
- 126 households;
- 34 children;
- 19 elders;
- 12 service machines with distinct legal statuses;
- 1 player traveler;
- 6 recurring companion or relationship anchors;
- 24 critical professional roles with apprenticeship plans.

The vessel carries an Atlas Seed but no authority to activate it at destination.

## 1.3 Destination

Survey designation: **Hestia Survey Object 441-c**

Launch-era informal name: **Target C**

Later legal settlement: **H441 Cooperative Settlement Authority**

Local common name after two generations: **Far Station**

Conditions:

- one marginal but viable inhabited moon;
- native microbial and distributed electrochemical ecology;
- no confirmed technological civilization at launch;
- high radiation season;
- large subsurface water inventory;
- viable anchor construction materials;
- prior autonomous probes with incomplete data.

# 2. Temporal Profile

Reference values are tunable but authoritative in the fixture.

```text
ordinary distance: 41.8 light-years
voyage Atlas duration: 96.4 years
traveler proper duration: 31.7 years
suspension duration experienced biologically: 24.0 years excluded from active aging under declared medical model
active player experienced time: 7.7 years
origin-to-destination light-speed message delay: 41.8 years
first Atlas corridor nominal latency: 11 days
first corridor minimum latency: 8 days
```

The values are not intended as exact real-world propulsion predictions. They create a representative divergence among Atlas, proper, biological, civic, and knowledge time.

# 3. Benchmark Cast

Minimum named recurring roles:

- player traveler;
- player remaining at origin;
- ship navigator;
- metric-sail engineer;
- habitat ecologist;
- clinician;
- teacher and apprenticeship coordinator;
- sanitation steward;
- elected mission delegate;
- dissent organizer;
- service machine witness;
- child who becomes an adult before arrival;
- origin route engineer;
- origin family relationship;
- destination-born anchor opponent;

- microbial-ecology interpreter;

At least six relationships must not involve the player.

# 4. Phase Structure

## Phase 0 — Ground Truth Fixture

Initialize:

- worldline;
- origin and destination regions;
- route graph with no interstellar FTL edge;
- named population;
- ship mass and inventory;
- civic authority;
- player temporal locations;
- knowledge envelopes;
- deterministic seeds.

**Gate:** hashes reproduce exactly.

## Phase 1 — Mission and Consent

Players and NPCs decide:

- mission scope;
- household participation;
- player suspension profile;
- governance compact;
- reproductive and education rights;
- mission-amendment procedure;
- Atlas Seed custody;
- office and property handovers at origin.

One companion refuses to travel.

**Gate:** refusal does not block the mission through hidden party ownership and does not erase the relationship.

## Phase 2 — Launch and Relativistic Acceleration

Simulate:

- launch infrastructure;
- metric-sail deployment;
- acceleration;
- proper-time integration;
- crew health;
- initial messages;
- one minor field fault.

**Gate:** no FTL occurs; propulsion, energy, heat, and navigation are conserved.

## Phase 3 — Quiet Years

Advance twelve Atlas years through accelerated continuity without arbitrary crisis insertion.

Preserve:

- ordinary work;
- relationships;
- schooling;
- births;
- elections;
- maintenance;
- culture;
- resource state.

**Gate:** the player can inspect a coherent history rather than a blank skip summary.

## Phase 4 — Mission Inheritance

A child at launch reaches civic adulthood and joins a movement challenging settlement assumptions.

The mission debates:

- remaining mobile;
- changing the landing site;
- limiting human settlement;
- preserving the Atlas Seed without activation.

**Gate:** launch-era founders cannot veto descendants through immutable mission code.

## Phase 5 — Origin Divergence

The origin player continues for twenty-eight years of local play and bounded simulation.

Changes include:

- a new government;
- Echo Two completion;
- one route-safety scandal;
- death of one launch-era relationship;
- revision of migration policy;
- a message sent after the ship's original authority has expired.

The message reaches the vessel decades later.

**Gate:** current origin truth is not copied into vessel knowledge before valid reception.

## Phase 6 — Arrival Survey

The vessel reaches H441-c.

The destination invalidates two launch assumptions:

- one intended settlement basin is part of a distributed electrochemical ecology;
- the preferred anchor site creates unacceptable radiation focusing during one season.

**Gate:** player cannot place a settlement or Anchor by overriding prior ecological claims.

## Phase 7 — Founding Far Station

Run a twenty-year local founding interval with:

- households;
- public services;
- professions;
- local charter;
- ecological boundaries;
- machine standing;
- ordinary culture;
- player absence periods;
- succession.

**Gate:** the settlement survives if the player leaves office.

## Phase 8 — Atlas Anchor Debate

Three political positions emerge:

### Open Reconnection

Build a public route with migration and trade.

### Seasonal Limited Pairing

Operate during ecologically safer windows with strict throughput.

### Deliberate Isolation

Preserve the Seed but refuse pairing.

**Gate:** all positions have credible constituencies, benefits, and costs.

## Phase 9 — Pairing Protocol

For the two route-opening branches, perform:

1. identity exchange;
2. clock comparison;
3. route survey;
4. low-energy field echo;
5. information pulse;
6. instrument transit;
7. mass-standard transit;
8. recoverable probe;
9. cargo test;
10. crew-rated certification.

**Gate:** each step produces evidence and may fail without arbitrary catastrophe.

## Phase 10 — Chronology Attack Test

A simulated proposed route through an alien moving-anchor observation would create a nonpositive cycle under the current uncertainty bounds.

**Gate:** the route graph rejects activation and records the proof.

## Phase 11 — First Human Transit

The first human crossing carries:

- one small delegation;
- one medical package;
- one archive object;
- no military force;
- a declared positive latency;
- complete uniqueness reconciliation.

The origin player and traveler may reunite with major age and historical divergence.

**Gate:** relationship continuity is negotiated, not restored automatically.

## Phase 12 — Route Emergency

During the third scheduled passenger window:

- cooling contamination reduces thermal margin;
- route capacity falls;
- one vessel is committed;
- nearby worker housing faces evacuation;
- political authorities pressure operators to continue.

Required outcomes:

- worker refusal can stop new commitment;
- the committed vessel enters one recovery state;
- rescue capacity is finite;
- player priority is not automatic;
- evidence is preserved;
- one persistent Atlas Scar remains.

## Phase 13 — Five-Year Aftermath

Advance five years.

Track:

- scar evolution;
- worker health;
- route inquiry;
- family effects;
- economic disruption;
- political reform or denial;
- memorialization;
- route restoration, limitation, or retirement.

## Phase 14 — Multiplayer Host Migration

Migrate hosting during:

- one inactive region advance;
- one committed route reservation;
- one player disconnect in suspension.

**Gate:** no duplicate avatar, cargo, authority token, or route transaction appears.

# 5. Worldline Outcomes

## Branch A — Public Reconnection Commonwealth

Far Station opens a public bridge under shared route governance.

Benefits:

- migration;
- medicine;
- research;
- family contact;
- economic growth.

Conflicts:

- housing pressure;
- origin corporate claims;
- worker safety;
- ecological throughput limits;
- unequal travel access.

## Branch B — Seasonal Route Compact

The route opens only during safe ecological and metric windows.

Benefits:

- local control;
- reduced environmental load;
- deliberate migration.

Conflicts:

- long queues;
- smuggling;
- emergency exceptions;
- pressure to expand.

## Branch C — Independent Far Station

The destination refuses passenger pairing.

It maintains:

- slow communication;
- courier contact;
- local metric research;
- technical ability to reconsider later.

The branch must remain a complete civilization, not a failed unlock state.

# 6. Conservation Gates

Reconcile across all phases:

- people;
- bodies and source chains;
- player avatars;
- companions;
- ship mass;
- cargo;
- energy;
- route throughput;
- authority tokens;
- offices;
- messages;
- archives;
- critical biological samples;
- unique artifacts.

# 7. Chronology Gates

1. Atlas Time is monotonic.
2. Proper time is integrated once.
3. Civic calendars derive correctly.
4. Knowledge does not arrive early.
5. Atlas route latency is positive.
6. No route graph cycle is nonpositive.
7. A later origin government cannot issue authority retroactively to the ship.
8. Region frontiers reconnect only through compatible descendants.
9. Save, replay, and host migration preserve ancestry.
10. No player returns to their own past.

# 8. Social and Emotional Gates

After delayed recall testing, players should remember:

- at least four voyage characters;
- the companion who refused;
- one child who became a political adult;
- one ordinary ship tradition;
- why Far Station debated the route;
- one difference between origin memory and destination memory;
- what changed during route closure;
- why the Atlas Scar matters to workers or families.

# 9. Performance Gates

Representative targets:

- deterministic 120-year low-detail advancement;
- high-detail active ship region;
- two asynchronously advanced regions;
- one route graph with at least 64 simulated edges for cycle-test scaling;
- no full-galaxy per-frame simulation;
- bounded memory for knowledge envelopes;
- replayable route emergency;
- host migration under benchmark load.

# 10. Safety and Accessibility Gates

- irreversible time advance is clearly disclosed;
- route emergency content is adjustable and skippable where appropriate;
- no flashing metric effect lacks accessibility alternatives;
- clock and knowledge states are understandable without advanced physics literacy;
- player continuity and branch choices are described plainly;
- no companion migration is implied by party membership;
- quarantine does not expose private medical data by default.

# 12. Promotion Rule

No FTL system advances beyond I0 based on documentation alone.

Promotion requires:

- isolated chronology prototype;
- route transaction harness;
- deterministic regional advancement;
- multiplayer uniqueness proof;
- one complete benchmark run;
- human playtest confirming that travel expands the galaxy without making it feel trivial.

# Production Maxim

> **The first Atlas crossing should feel smaller than the century that made it possible—and more consequential than any loading screen could be.**
