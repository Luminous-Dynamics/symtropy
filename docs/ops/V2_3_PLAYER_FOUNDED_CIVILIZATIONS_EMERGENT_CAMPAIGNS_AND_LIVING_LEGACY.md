# v2.3 Player-Founded Civilizations, Emergent Campaigns, and Living Legacy

## Campaign Question

Can Symtropy let players found a civilization without reducing inhabitants to settlers, buildings to upgrades, charters to bonuses, culture to customization, or history to a founder reputation score?

## Answer

The v2.3 design makes founding a collective transition into durable obligations.

The player may contribute extraordinary work and become historically important. The settlement retains the right to govern, amend, remember, rename, succeed, or reject independently.

# Patch Sequence

1. player-founded civilization and living legacy contract;
2. settlement campaign packet standard;
3. charter, institution, office, and public-service runtime;
4. emergent campaign and causal player-history runtime;
5. promise, office, reputation, attribution, and legacy runtime;
6. player-built places and architectural memory bible;
7. player-created culture, ritual, art, and symbol runtime;
8. return, inheritance, and successor campaign packets;
9. twenty-year benchmark, schemas, fixtures, and ticket backlog;
10. canon, dependency, roadmap, readiness, registry, manifest, and audit integration.

# Canonical Decisions

- no empty-land assumption;
- no ownership of residents through construction;
- no permanent founder sovereignty;
- no global settlement reputation;
- no charter perk menu;
- no instant cultural adoption;
- no frozen world during player absence;
- no automatic office restoration after reconstitution;
- no host-derived civic authority;
- no single canonical historical judgment.

# Representative Proof

The South Cut benchmark uses:

- twelve named residents;
- four households;
- a protected wetland boundary;
- an abandoned utility yard;
- three compact paths;
- seven public services;
- three offices;
- a charter and amendment;
- one resident departure;
- one cultural mutation;
- one landmark;
- five-year player absence;
- death or reconstitution;
- successor government;
- three twenty-year worldlines.

# Emotional Target

The player should be able to feel:

- pride in founding;
- attachment to ordinary places;
- responsibility for design flaws;
- grief during absence;
- discomfort at expired authority;
- relief when successors are more capable;
- surprise at cultural mutation;
- satisfaction that the settlement no longer needs them.

# Technical Target

The campaign specifies authoritative entities for:

- claims;
- participants;
- compacts;
- charters;
- institutions;
- offices;
- public services;
- budgets;
- commitments;
- reputation views;
- attribution claims;
- legacy traces;
- cultural adoption;
- landmarks;
- absence and worldline ancestry.

# Evidence Limits

Documents, schemas, fixtures, Git commits, and patch reconstruction establish design integrity only.

They do not prove:

- code;
- runtime correctness;
- simulation performance;
- fun;
- emotional investment;
- multiplayer safety;
- accessibility;
- content safety;
- persistence reliability.

All v2.3 features remain **D3 / I0**.

# Governing Principle

> **The player may become part of history. They do not become history's owner.**
