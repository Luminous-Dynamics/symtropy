# Firstlight First 10 and 40 Hour Experience Map

## Purpose

This map converts the opening contract into a bounded production sequence.

It is not a mandatory forty-hour quest chain. It defines:

- what the player should be able to encounter;
- when key promises must become legible;
- which beats are fixed;
- which beats are conditional;
- how several player motivations remain supported;
- how later consequences are prepared without obvious foreshadowing.

The reference player begins at the outer Seedworks service line during a storm-driven infrastructure disruption. The player may be locally born, recently arrived, returned through reconstitution, or transferred through an origin packet. All origins converge physically, not biographically.

# 1. Opening Design Rules

## 1.1 Control Before Explanation

The player receives movement and camera control within thirty seconds after any accessibility setup.

## 1.2 One Immediate Human Need

The first objective is not “save the city.” It is to help a stranded maintenance crew isolate a failed feeder before water and power faults cascade into occupied housing.

## 1.3 One Beautiful Contradiction

The player first sees Firstlight as:

- wetland terraces and old industrial frames;
- solar fabric snapping in storm wind;
- inhabited worker housing;
- bright nightlife signs still visible in daylight rain;
- a distant launch structure;
- an apparently abandoned tower blinking in an irregular rhythm.

The vista promises scale while the task remains local.

## 1.4 No False Urgency After Stabilization

Once immediate danger is contained, the player is free to explore, rest, talk, or continue work. No countdown continues merely to prevent wandering.

# 2. Minute-by-Minute Opening

## Minute 0–2 — Embodiment

The player:

- wakes or regains situational awareness in a stopped transit crawler;
- hears rain, strained metal, and an intermittent pump alarm;
- receives one concise IRIS statement distinguishing known facts from inference;
- frees the door manually or through a low-assistance interaction;
- steps into navigable space.

IRIS line structure:

> “Transit power is isolated. I do not know whether the feeder failed before the landslip. Two people are outside the vehicle.”

The line provides orientation, uncertainty, and a human reason to move.

## Minute 2–6 — First Contact

The player meets **Sera Vale** and one non-flagship worker, **Bram Osei**, trying to stop water from entering a damaged cabinet.

The player may:

- brace the cabinet;
- route drainage;
- retrieve an insulated tool;
- inspect the feeder;
- assist an injured Bram;
- ask IRIS for a simplified view;
- ignore the cabinet and check the surrounding slope.

At least three valid contributions exist. The player is not graded on choosing the designer's preferred one.

## Minute 6–10 — First Material Result

The team isolates the immediate fault.

The outcome varies:

- clean isolation preserves downstream pumps;
- hurried isolation causes a later replacement burden;
- medical attention prevents Bram's hand injury from worsening;
- slope inspection discovers the landslip is still moving;
- failure triggers a controlled secondary event rather than death.

Sera does not congratulate the player generically. She reacts to the actual action and gives a specific next instruction or boundary.

# 3. First Hour

## 0:10–0:25 — The Walk In

The player travels from the service line to the Seedworks Commons.

This sequence introduces:

- traversal across wet ground, ladders, service paths, and inhabited thresholds;
- workers performing cleanup;
- children delayed on their way to class;
- one local joke about the drainage system;
- the sound of a band rehearsing badly inside a closed venue;
- a route choice between the fast exposed catwalk and the slower market path.

No combat occurs here.

## 0:25–0:40 — Home Base Without Ownership

The player receives temporary access to a small room, locker, and workbench in the Commons residence block.

The room is:

- safe;
- modifiable within limits;
- embedded among neighbors;
- not free real estate awarded for being the protagonist.

The player learns:

- resting and saving;
- private storage;
- Field Deck privacy;
- local time and weather;
- how to find people without omniscient tracking.

A neighbor asks the player not to leave wet equipment in the corridor. This minor boundary matters later.

## 0:40–1:00 — Three Doors

The player is offered three immediate but nonexclusive directions:

1. accompany Sera to inspect the feeder and begin maintenance practice;
2. visit Amadi Nko because Bram's injury or the player's own condition needs review;
3. bring a damaged custody unit to Mara Quill at the public record room.

The options seed the three proof professions:

- maintenance;
- field medicine;
- civic witness and investigation.

The player may visit the market, transit stop, or residence first.

# 4. Hours One to Three — The Player Becomes Useful

## Maintenance Route

The player learns:

- isolation;
- inspection order;
- acoustic and thermal cues;
- temporary versus certifiable repair;
- handover notes.

A successful repair does not finish instantly. It must be tested under load later.

## Clinical Route

The player assists with:

- consent;
- sanitation;
- basic stabilization;
- pain and anxiety communication;
- resource tradeoffs;
- follow-up.

The patient is not a tutorial dummy and may refuse parts of the interaction.

## Witness Route

The player learns:

- observation versus inference;
- custody;
- testimony;
- privacy;
- competing claims;
- how records become public or remain restricted.

## Shared Beat — The First Interruption

Between hours two and three, an avoidable but credible danger occurs:

- a weakened service bridge partially collapses;
- a cargo carrier loses control;
- a Null-affected utility machine continues an expired evacuation routine;
- hostile scavengers exploit the storm outage.

The exact event is selected from world state.

The sequence combines movement, rescue, tactical judgment, and possible combat. It should last twelve to twenty minutes and lead directly into aftermath work.

# 5. Hours Three to Five — Firstlight After Adrenaline

The player returns to ordinary life while the event continues to matter.

Required beats:

- someone cleans the site;
- a transit route changes;
- one injured person receives follow-up;
- a rumor misstates what happened;
- a public worker asks for an accurate record;
- one venue remains open despite damage;
- the player can eat, rest, repair tools, or attend rehearsal.

## First Social Evening

The player is invited—but not compelled—to a small market evening or venue rehearsal.

Possible experiences:

- Lio Dlamini testing a faulty sound array;
- Nia Kgosana arguing about the transit detour;
- Morrow-7 carefully avoiding a group of children;
- a low-stakes card or betting game;
- food purchased or shared;
- a companion declining to discuss work;
- a song that later changes after the region-wide event.

The first evening should be warm enough that later danger feels like danger to a place, not merely a mission hub.

# 6. Hours Five to Ten — Identity Formation

## 6.1 Profession Commitment Without Lock-In

The player undertakes one intermediate task in a chosen profession and assists in at least one other.

Examples:

- diagnose a recurring pump fault and decide between repair paths;
- stabilize several people during a transport incident;
- reconstruct an event from physical evidence and conflicting testimony.

The player's emerging practice identity is inferred from actions and can remain mixed.

## 6.2 The Sealed Lower Works

Clues introduced during ordinary play point toward a restricted underground service complex.

Entry may be justified through:

- maintenance authorization;
- medical rescue;
- public-record investigation;
- exploration and trespass;
- companion access;
- later emergency.

The Lower Works contain:

- real infrastructure;
- an old corporate layer;
- a historical inconsistency;
- environmental danger;
- at least one secret unrelated to the main mystery;
- routes that later become shortcuts.

## 6.3 First Institutional Conflict

A public hearing or work meeting considers responsibility for the storm failure.

The player may:

- testify;
- submit evidence;
- remain silent;
- support emergency repairs without endorsing a blame claim;
- challenge custody;
- speak privately to affected people;
- leave.

No speech check resolves the issue alone.

## 6.4 The Ten-Hour Horizon

By the end of this phase, the player should have seen:

- orbital traffic or a launch window;
- evidence of distant settlements;
- one nonhuman or machine-history trace;
- a map or story suggesting other regions;
- one credible reason to remain in Firstlight;
- one credible reason to leave eventually.

# 7. Hours Ten to Twenty — The Region Opens

## 7.1 Project Work

The player joins one multi-session project:

- restore the old water bypass;
- expand the clinic's mobile capacity;
- reopen a transit route;
- establish a public evidence archive;
- secure the Lower Works;
- prepare a cultural event.

Projects require materials, people, scheduling, and handovers. They progress without the player but may change direction.

## 7.2 Companion Practice

At least two joint procedures become available through repeated work.

Examples:

- Sera predicts which isolation point the player will choose but challenges unsafe shortcuts;
- Amadi prepares the next stabilization step and notices the player's tunnel vision;
- Morrow-7 manages access and physical positioning during evidence recovery;
- Nia synchronizes movement during evacuation.

## 7.3 Optional Danger Expedition

The player may undertake one longer dangerous excursion:

- flooded substation;
- hostile extraction site;
- Null factory annex;
- unstable wetland sink;
- crashed aerial vehicle;
- forgotten corporate shelter.

This sequence must support:

- stealth;
- combat;
- rescue;
- technical bypass;
- withdrawal.

## 7.4 Festival Preparation

The region prepares for a public event regardless of whether the player helps.

Preparation reveals:

- labor;
- safety planning;
- rehearsal;
- food and sanitation logistics;
- political disagreement;
- commercial pressure;
- people who dislike the event.

# 8. Hours Twenty to Thirty — Crisis and Meaning

## 8.1 The Firstlight Night

The reference regional event combines:

- festival crowds;
- transport pressure;
- infrastructure strain;
- a contested public record;
- one hostile or opportunistic intervention;
- one companion pursuing an independent priority;
- one system failing for reasons prepared earlier.

This is not a fixed betrayal plot.

Possible crisis shapes include:

- power and crowd-control failure;
- contaminated substance batch;
- targeted sabotage of water or transit;
- raid through the Lower Works;
- false evacuation order propagated by a damaged machine system;
- ecological breach worsened by celebration infrastructure.

## 8.2 Player Role

The player cannot solve every front.

They choose where to be useful:

- rescue;
- repair;
- treatment;
- evidence custody;
- defense;
- evacuation;
- public communication;
- protecting a vulnerable household;
- securing a dangerous system.

Other characters act elsewhere.

## 8.3 Aftermath

The event produces:

- injuries or avoided injuries;
- physical damage;
- rumors;
- gratitude and anger;
- labor claims;
- public investigation;
- memorial or cleanup work;
- altered routes;
- one relationship strain;
- one unexpected act of solidarity.

The player can rest before attending every follow-up.

# 9. Hours Thirty to Forty — Time, Return, and Choice

## 9.1 Hearing and Repair

The player may participate in a public hearing, but the hearing cannot replace rebuilding.

Evidence may remain incomplete. Institutional action can include:

- emergency funding;
- suspension;
- repair orders;
- privacy protections;
- labor concessions;
- failed accountability;
- appeal.

## 9.2 Death or Reconstitution Case

At least one worldline presents a bounded continuity case involving a resident, worker, or machine.

The player may assist with:

- source recovery;
- testimony;
- body or machine care;
- household communication;
- identity hearing;
- private grief.

The case is not always the player's death and does not exist only to explain lore.

## 9.3 Absence

The player experiences a credible time passage or leaves the region for several days, weeks, or months.

On return:

- at least one project has progressed;
- one person is elsewhere;
- one place has changed use;
- one early repair has aged;
- one rumor has become local memory;
- the game summarizes without pretending nothing happened.

## 9.4 The Forty-Hour Decision

The opening culminates in several valid horizons:

- deepen a profession;
- help formalize a settlement project;
- pursue the Lower Works mystery;
- travel to another region;
- join orbital work;
- remain for relationships;
- begin a new character perspective later;
- prepare for the distant interstellar future.

The game does not announce that the tutorial is complete.

# 10. Optionality Matrix

| Beat | Fixed | Variable | Optional |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| feeder disruption | yes | resolution | no |
| first social evening | event exists | venue and cast | attendance |
| first danger | danger exists | event type | approach |
| profession exposure | yes | order and depth | specialization |
| Lower Works | present | access path | early entry |
| hearing | institution acts | evidence and outcome | testimony |
| festival | region prepares | form and pressures | participation |
| major crisis | yes | causal composition | player role |
| continuity case | worldline dependent | subject | deep involvement |
| absence and return | yes | duration | timing window |
| wider horizon | yes | chosen desire | destination |

# 11. Session Design

The experience must support:

- 20-minute maintenance or social sessions;
- 45–90 minute profession or campaign sessions;
- 2–3 hour expedition or festival sessions;
- safe stop points after major action;
- clear return summaries;
- no mandatory attendance tied to real-world time.

# 12. Cut Order

If production scope contracts, cut in this order:

1. secondary optional expedition variants;
2. duplicate venue interiors;
3. additional minor profession cases;
4. extra festival activities;
5. distant-horizon teasers.

Do not cut:

- tactile opening action;
- ordinary-life evening;
- three profession proofs;
- at least four revisited landmarks;
- six recurring people;
- danger with aftermath;
- one delayed consequence;
- absence and changed return;
- accessible IRIS support.

## Experience Maxim

> **The first ten hours establish a life. The next thirty prove that life can change without becoming disposable.**
