# v1.5 Interplanetary Civilization Campaign

## Purpose

The v1.4 corpus defined how many sovereign communities coordinate a planet. v1.5 defines what changes when civilization spreads across distances that prevent shared immediacy.

The campaign adds eight design/runtime specifications and one integrated benchmark:

```text
interplanetary civilization and distributed sovereignty
light-delay communication and asynchronous coordination
closed-loop habitats and generation ships
habitat metabolism and population
deep-space logistics, rescue, and salvage
interplanetary fleets, blockades, and restraint
settlement autonomy and non-extractive expansion
interplanetary trade, finance, and delayed settlement
two-century solar-system benchmark
```

## Campaign Thesis

> **Distance must change what people know, what authorities may command, what rescue can promise, and what communities have the right to become.**

## Scope Boundary

This campaign is a horizon architecture. It does not add solar-system implementation to the representative Seedworks build. All new capabilities remain `D3 / I0` until dated code, replay, performance, migration, privacy, rights, and playtest evidence exists.

## Major Corrections

1. Space is not a larger fast-travel map.
2. Remote governments cannot possess current local truth.
3. Habitats are material and social metabolisms, not oxygen bars.
4. Rescue depends on messages, trajectories, consumables, and responder risk.
5. Fleets carry rescue and law as well as weapons.
6. Settlements gain autonomy through lived risk and continuity, not only founder contracts.
7. Financial claims coordinate physical assets but cannot create them.
8. Generational missions remain subject to descendants' consent and constitutional renewal.

The first code proof should not be a full solar system. It should be two nodes, one delayed command, one habitat, one physical shipment, and one rescue case.
