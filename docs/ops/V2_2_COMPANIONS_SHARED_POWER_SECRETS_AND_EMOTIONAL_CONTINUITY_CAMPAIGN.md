# v2.2 Companions, Shared Power, Secrets, and Emotional Continuity Campaign

## Purpose

The corpus already supports deep NPC cognition, professions, relationships, households, IRIS, history, and worldlines. v2.2 connects those systems into one human-scale emotional proof.

The campaign does not create a conventional obedient party system.

It defines companions as inhabitants whose capabilities, relationships, projects, boundaries, and history repeatedly intersect with the player.

# Patch Groups

1. Companion shared power and autonomous agency.
2. Coordination, delegation, and domain-specific trust runtime.
3. Consent, refusal, departure, and dependency boundaries.
4. Household, project, absence, and worldline continuity.
5. Firstlight flagship companion relationship web.
6. Shared-practice and disagreement scenes.
7. Secret-history, Easter-egg, and worldline discovery runtime.
8. Private jokes and hidden-history anthology.
9. Emotional-continuity benchmark, backlog, schemas, and fixtures.
10. Canon, dependency map, roadmap, readiness, registry, audits, and packaging.

# Non-Goals

v2.2 does not authorize:

- dozens of recruitable characters;
- universal combat-party controls;
- approval-meter romance;
- companion stat equipment;
- omniscient IRIS relationship analysis;
- hidden critical safety information;
- implementation claims without code and playtest evidence.

# Representative Proof

The target is:

```text
6 flagship companions
2 important non-traveling relationship anchors
2 joint procedures
1 practiced coordination loop
1 lawful refusal
1 independent departure
1 six-month absence
1 disputed restoration or fork case
8 optional secrets
1 delayed emotional-recall study
```

# Success Definition

Players should remember companions through:

- work;
- habits;
- places;
- projects;
- boundaries;
- relationships with others;
- changes during absence.

They should feel more capable because they learned to coordinate with particular people, not because the people became inventory.

# Stop Conditions

Pause expansion if:

- players remember abilities but not people;
- refusal feels cosmetic;
- companions freeze while absent;
- relationship progression is interpreted as a reward ladder;
- secrets become checklist content;
- privacy review finds IRIS cognition leakage;
- implementation cost prevents ordinary NPC life from remaining credible.

# Governing Principle

> **Players become powerful because shared practice creates trust, access, memory, and responsibility—not because other people become extensions of the player.**
