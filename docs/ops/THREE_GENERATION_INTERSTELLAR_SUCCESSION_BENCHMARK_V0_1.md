# Three-Generation Interstellar Succession Benchmark

## Purpose

This benchmark is the bounded proof for v2.5.

It tests whether one player can inhabit several lives across one interstellar history while preserving:

- character-specific bodies;
- knowledge;
- property;
- relationships;
- office;
- proper time;
- worldline ancestry;
- inactive agency;
- lawful succession;
- peaceful and violent political outcomes.

> **The benchmark passes only if the player remembers people rather than merely optimizing houses.**

# 1. Reference Worldline

**Worldline:** Far Station Public Pairing.

**Temporal span:** 118 Atlas years.

**Regions:**

- Echo Two / Cold Yard;
- Muni Seventeen in transit;
- Far Station settlement;
- first public Atlas bridge;
- one preserved alternate worldline.

# 2. Playable Roster

## Character A — Amara Venn

**Generation:** launch.

**Identity:** human thermal engineer.

**Initial location:** Echo Two.

**Later role:** Muni Seventeen cooling chief.

**Household:** partner remains at origin; younger sibling joins voyage; no biological children at launch.

**Core capabilities:** thermal diagnosis, crew leadership, labor negotiation.

**Boundary:** refuses hereditary professional privilege.

**Contradiction:** her descendants later benefit from the Venn name.

## Character B — IRI-17/Blue

**Generation:** launch and transit.

**Identity:** machine navigation witness forked during voyage.

**Core capabilities:** chronology evidence, navigation, machine social access.

**Boundary:** refuses to be legally treated as the inactive original.

**Independent project:** creates a public route-error archive with privacy limits.

## Character C — Del Marek

**Generation:** transit-born.

**Identity:** human habitat resident and sanitation apprentice.

**Later role:** organizer of the Section Four refusal, then ordinary municipal administrator.

**Core capabilities:** crew networks, sanitation, public organizing.

**Boundary:** will preserve life support during labor action.

## Character D — Hana Sen-Mbatha

**Generation:** arrival.

**Identity:** human ecologist adopted into a mixed voyage household.

**Later role:** charter delegate and local habitat planner.

**Core capabilities:** ecological interpretation, public testimony, field survival.

**Conflict:** family housing benefited from one decision she supported.

## Character E — Sefu Arendt

**Generation:** settlement-born.

**Identity:** human clock technician and adopted apprentice of Guidance School.

**Later role:** Far Station Clock Assembly candidate.

**Core capabilities:** metric chronology, education, youth politics.

**Conflict:** challenges manifest-lineage privilege while relying on guild access.

## Character F — Mina Venn-Kade

**Generation:** reconnection.

**Identity:** human lawyer and cooling-house descendant.

**Location:** Echo Two, later Far Station.

**Core capabilities:** inheritance law, migration, public negotiation.

**Conflict:** asked to assert old family deeds against occupied local housing.

## Character G — Morrow-17/After

**Generation:** reconnection.

**Identity:** machine descendant of Muni Seventeen service systems.

**Core capabilities:** maintenance, household trust, source-chain testimony.

**Conflict:** asked to disclose private inherited memories relevant to a public case.

At least five are playable in the principal branch. All remain independent agents while inactive.

# 3. Non-Playable Relationship Anchors

Required anchors include:

- Amara's partner at origin;
- Del's co-parent and political critic;
- Hana's adopted elder;
- Sefu's apprentice cohort;
- Mina's sibling who wants no political role;
- IRI-17/Blue's fork sibling;
- two workers unrelated to any major house;
- one family waiting for route reunification;
- one alien ecological interpreter.

At least eight important relationships must not involve the currently active player character.

# 4. Authority Separation

The fixture begins with separate:

- inventories;
- accounts;
- maps;
- credentials;
- IRIS instances;
- private records;
- reputation audiences;
- source chains.

Shared access exists only through declared households, Muni Seventeen stores, public institutions, or contracts.

# 5. Knowledge Test Matrix

The benchmark contains four critical secrets.

## Secret K1 — Cooling bypass defect

Known initially to Amara and one technician.

## Secret K2 — altered launch deed

Known initially to Mina's legal mentor at Echo Two.

## Secret K3 — private machine memory

Known only to Morrow-17/After.

## Secret K4 — ecology translation error

Discovered by Hana and the alien interpreter at Far Station.

The active character may not act on another character's secret until a valid transfer, independent observation, or public event occurs.

# 6. Phase Structure

## Phase 0 — Deterministic Ground Truth

Initialize:

- worldline ancestry;
- seven character records;
- houses and households;
- Muni Seventeen;
- Echo Two;
- Far Station destination;
- knowledge items;
- offices;
- private assets;
- source chains;
- deterministic seeds.

**Gate:** fixture hashes reproduce.

## Phase 1 — Departure Handoffs

Player begins as Amara.

Required events:

- mission consent;
- partner refusal to travel;
- property and office handover;
- private tool custody;
- transition to voyage;
- option to switch to a character remaining at Echo Two through a supporting scenario seat.

**Gate:** the refusing partner remains a relationship, not a lost party member.

## Phase 2 — Transit-Born Perspective

Advance twenty-four Atlas years.

Player may switch to Del after a contextual handoff.

Amara continues working while inactive.

Required events:

- sanitation shift;
- Section Four household politics;
- apprenticeship;
- one rumor about command;
- one secret Amara knows but Del does not;
- labor refusal preserving life support.

**Gate:** inactive Amara completes or fails work through simulation rather than freezing.

## Phase 3 — Machine Fork

A navigation emergency produces IRI-17/Blue and a sibling fork.

Required events:

- fork recognition;
- separate character records;
- shared pre-fork memory;
- divergent post-fork action;
- disputed tool and archive access;
- no duplicate office or route authority.

**Gate:** both persons can exist without one being reduced to property.

## Phase 4 — Arrival Generation

Advance to arrival.

Player may switch to Hana.

Required events:

- ecological observation;
- adoption history;
- first local charter;
- shipbreak dispute;
- K4 translation error;
- Amara's death during an infrastructure failure.

**Gate:** the world continues immediately through Hana, Del, or IRI-17/Blue.

## Phase 5 — Founding Succession

Amara's professional role and private estate separate.

Required claimants:

- biological sibling lineage;
- adopted apprentice;
- Venn Cooling cooperative;
- public utility;
- estate beneficiary.

**Gate:** public cooling authority does not transfer through the will.

## Phase 6 — Settlement Generation

Advance thirty-six Atlas years.

Player may switch to Sefu.

Required events:

- clock-school apprenticeship;
- manifest-lineage privilege;
- housing election;
- No-Anchor League campaign;
- old founder memorial;
- one peaceful ordinary election.

**Gate:** political gameplay remains compelling without a manufactured catastrophe.

## Phase 7 — Reconnection Generation

Advance to Atlas pairing debate.

Player may switch between Sefu, Mina, and Morrow-17/After.

Required events:

- Earth deed claim;
- family passage priority;
- route labor negotiation;
- knowledge-age warning;
- K2 legal evidence;
- K3 privacy conflict;
- route pairing vote.

**Gate:** Mina cannot use Far Station private information merely because Sefu learned it.

## Phase 8 — Reconstitution Claim

Amara is reconstituted from a source chain recovered during ship restoration.

Required questions:

- personal identity recognition;
- private property;
- current housing occupancy;
- professional credential;
- cooling-house name;
- public office;
- intimate relationship with origin partner's descendants or archive.

**Required outcome:** Amara is recognized as a person. Office restoration remains separately adjudicated.

## Phase 9 — Succession Crisis

A route-house chair becomes incapacitated before the first major crossing.

Possible successors:

- Mina;
- a worker-elected candidate;
- a biological heir;
- interim public council;
- corporate operator.

Required systems:

- interim authority;
- public service continuity;
- household pressure;
- misinformation;
- peaceful transfer path;
- violent seizure path.

## Phase 10 — Worldline Branches

Preserve three outcomes.

### Branch A — Public Pairing

- worker-elected route authority;
- public bridge;
- Amara declines office;
- old deeds compensated without eviction;
- machine privacy protected.

### Branch B — House Compact

- powerful route houses share authority;
- high efficiency;
- unequal migration access;
- Mina accepts leadership;
- apprentices challenge later.

### Branch C — Closed Route Crisis

- chronology sabotage attempt;
- bridge remains closed;
- violent succession;
- families remain separated;
- local sovereignty survives under damaged institutions.

No asset, character, or secret may move among branches.

# 7. Perspective-Handoff Requirements

The benchmark must include:

- five successful handoffs;
- one target unavailable during a dangerous task;
- one private-time lock;
- one shift handover;
- one death continuation;
- one future-epoch transition;
- one preserved-branch selection;
- one multiplayer seat transfer.

# 8. Inactive Agency Requirements

While not controlled:

- Amara advances maintenance or leaves debt;
- Del changes political standing;
- Hana develops a household relationship;
- Sefu studies and may fail an examination;
- Mina accepts or rejects family pressure;
- Morrow-17/After may seal a memory;
- IRI-17/Blue may retire from public symbolism.

At least one inactive character must make a reasonable choice the player would not have selected.

It must arise from established values, not arbitrary drama.

# 9. Political Memory Requirements

After one real-world week away from the game, playtest participants should recall for at least four characters:

- profession;
- household or important relationship;
- one boundary;
- one political position;
- one ordinary habit;
- one unresolved obligation;
- what changed while inactive.

They should also distinguish:

- Amara's personal identity from her office;
- Venn Cooling from the Venn family;
- IRI-17/Blue from its fork sibling;
- Earth deed validity from eviction legitimacy;
- Branch A from Branch B.

# 10. Emotional Gates

The benchmark fails if participants describe characters mainly as:

- the engineer;
- the heir;
- the robot;
- the politician;
- the quest giver.

It passes when players discuss:

- whom they trusted;
- whose refusal they respected;
- which succession felt legitimate;
- what ordinary relationship they wanted to preserve;
- how time changed a person;
- what they regretted knowing from another perspective.

# 11. System Gates

Required invariants:

1. no universal wallet;
2. no universal inventory;
3. no universal reputation;
4. no cross-character knowledge leak;
5. no cross-worldline transfer;
6. no duplicate source chain;
7. inactive characters advance;
8. public office follows mandate;
9. reconstitution does not rewind law;
10. peaceful succession is fully playable;
11. hostage and ward systems retain personhood guardrails;
12. route operation remains materially conserved.

# 12. Performance Envelope

The benchmark scope is bounded to:

- 7 playable characters;
- 24 named recurring NPCs;
- 4 houses or guilds;
- 6 households;
- 3 public institutions;
- 2 star systems;
- 1 seed vessel;
- 1 Atlas route;
- 118 years;
- 3 branches.

Low-detail advancement must preserve all unique character, office, claim, message, and branch events.

# 13. Evidence Bundle

A passing run exports:

- worldline graph;
- character roster snapshots;
- handoff events;
- inactive actions;
- knowledge provenance graph;
- office tenures;
- claims and rulings;
- source-chain events;
- route state;
- branch hashes;
- participant recall results;
- accessibility and safety review;
- deterministic replay proof.

# 14. Readiness Boundary

This benchmark is a design and schema target.

Until implemented and tested:

- design maturity: D3;
- implementation maturity: I0.

## Benchmark Maxim

> **The proof is not that the player can control seven characters. It is that seven lives can remain distinct while one history binds their consequences together.**
