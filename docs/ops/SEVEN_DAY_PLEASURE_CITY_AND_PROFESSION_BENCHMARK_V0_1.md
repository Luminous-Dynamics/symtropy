# Seven-Day Pleasure City and Profession Benchmark

## Purpose

This benchmark proves that a player-built “sin city” can function as a civilization and that profession depth produces distinct, consequential work rather than reskinned task chains.

## Reference District

Working legal name: **Municipal Entertainment and Night Economy District 6**.

Common names include **Six**, **Afterhours**, **the Stripworks**, and several venue-specific nicknames.

The district contains:

```text
1 mixed residential core
1 cooperative nightlife block
1 corporate casino-hotel
1 public clinic and harm-reduction station
1 worker-governed adult-only venue
1 informal market and transport network
1 theater and music hall
1 late-shift transit hub
1 laundry and sanitation yard
1 utility substation and cooling loop
1 licensing office
12 named profession anchors
```

The district must contain children, elders, households, religious or cultural institutions, and residents whose lives are unrelated to nightlife.

# Seven-Day Sequence

## Day 1 — Preparation

- festival deliveries arrive;
- tools and venues are inspected;
- a casino promotion increases expected volume;
- workers negotiate schedules;
- sanitation finds an unresolved drain fault;
- IRIS identifies conflicting capacity reports without deciding which institution is lying.

Proof focus: preparation, work orders, staffing, tool condition, and pressure forecasting.

## Day 2 — Opening Surge

- visitors arrive;
- transport and lodging approach capacity;
- a dealer notices unusual advantage play;
- the venue demands continued play;
- a performer’s equipment requires an uncertified repair;
- an adult venue refuses a politically powerful client.

Proof focus: professional judgment, authority, refusal, privacy, and partial operation.

## Day 3 — Product Alert

- several people show similar symptoms;
- the clinic receives incomplete product information;
- an informal tester provides a sample result;
- venue owners resist a public alert;
- transit workers reroute people to care;
- sanitation preserves discarded packaging as evidence.

Proof focus: cross-profession handover, uncertainty, privacy, and batch lineage.

## Day 4 — Corruption and Debt

- the casino reserve appears sufficient in public records;
- Kez-14 finds a custody inconsistency;
- a licensing inspection was selectively narrowed;
- an informal lender threatens a worker;
- the organization also provides the district’s only reliable dawn transport.

Proof focus: plural legitimacy, corruption evidence, service substitution, and financial conservation.

## Day 5 — Breakdown

- cooling and drainage fail during peak operation;
- closing venues would displace workers and visitors into unsafe streets;
- temporary repair is possible but not certifiable;
- the medic requests reduced capacity;
- the performer collective offers an outdoor alternative;
- residents oppose additional noise.

Proof focus: technical work becoming civic judgment.

## Day 6 — Festival Night

- the city chooses an operating plan;
- crowds, gambling, performance, substances, adult venues, transport, and public space interact;
- one serious incident occurs from prior causes, not scripted inevitability;
- several ordinary positive experiences also occur;
- workers act through their own authority and priorities.

Proof focus: combined load, culture, pleasure, harm reduction, and non-player agency.

## Day 7 — Monday Morning

- cleanup, laundry, waste, lost property, payment, medical follow-up, investigation, and family life continue;
- one venue faces insolvency;
- one worker files a complaint;
- one visitor expresses gratitude;
- one resident demands stricter limits;
- school, worship, meals, maintenance, and bad television return.

Proof focus: aftermath, ordinary life, compensation, handover, public memory, and consequence.

# Player Roles

The benchmark supports players acting as:

- city founder or administrator;
- venue owner or cooperative member;
- one of the professions;
- investigator or regulator;
- resident organizer;
- visitor with bounded influence.

No role grants omniscient control.

# Required Profession Proofs

Each of the twelve professions must show:

- a distinct sensory vocabulary;
- one preparation task;
- one moment of skilled execution;
- one shift conflict;
- one handover;
- one economic consequence;
- one public-responsibility decision;
- one follow-up after the visible task;
- one possible practice style;
- one failure not reducible to low skill.

# Player Absence

Run variants with:

- no absence;
- absence from Day 2 to Day 7;
- six-month absence after the festival;
- five-year absence after a chosen reform.

The district must continue through delegated authority and named agents.

# Worldline Branches

At minimum:

## Commons Branch

Worker governance, public health, transit, and housing protections expand. Growth slows and political conflict remains.

## Corporate Branch

A consortium refinances the district, improves visible operations, and increases surveillance, housing dependency, and behavioral targeting.

## Informal Federation Branch

Licensing collapses, worker and neighborhood networks maintain services unevenly, artistic variety increases, and corruption becomes harder to distinguish from mutual aid.

# Hard Failure Conditions

The benchmark fails if:

- money appears without stakes, visitors, or transactions;
- venues operate without labor, utilities, cleanup, or transport;
- adult consent is inferred or purchased;
- intoxication creates consent or automatic violence;
- private worker or health information leaks through ordinary telemetry;
- removing an informal group deletes its services without consequence;
- all professions expose the same interaction rhythm;
- documentation replaces competence;
- the player must personally resolve every event;
- the city freezes during absence;
- Monday morning is omitted;
- pleasure is depicted only as harm or corruption;
- vulnerable people exist only to demonstrate maturity.

# Performance Envelope

A representative local run targets:

```text
12 named professional anchors
40 additional named residents and workers
250 locally represented visitors
2,000 district-level visitor cohort members
25 active venues
60 concurrent work orders
20 material and utility networks
12 major event-journal decisions
```

Reduced-detail modes preserve named people, consent, custody, pay, injuries, incidents, utility conservation, projects, and public decisions.

# Evidence Bundle

A promotion bundle must contain:

- exact seed and content-package identity;
- authoritative event journal;
- shift and handover traces;
- material, financial, labor, and utility ledgers;
- redacted health and consent evidence;
- replay hashes;
- save/load and migration results;
- performance traces;
- accessibility and localization review;
- subject and exploitation-safety review;
- blind profession-distinctiveness playtest results;
- known failures and disabled components.

# Promotion Gate

The campaign remains D3 / I0 until the benchmark exists in code and a blind playtest can identify at least eight of twelve professions from interaction rhythm and information alone, without relying on job title or costume.
