# v1.6 Interstellar Threshold Campaign

## Purpose

The v1.5 corpus defined a lived solar system. v1.6 defines what changes when civilization crosses between stars and loses access to a shared practical present.

The campaign adds eight contracts/runtime specifications and one integrated benchmark:

```text
interstellar civilization and relativistic local sovereignty
relativistic navigation, proper time, and causal coordination
autonomous probes, arks, replication, and mission inheritance
deep-time ark ecology, population, skill, and cultural drift
interstellar contact, noninterference, and long-vow diplomacy
stellar relay, signal provenance, and contact latency
interstellar rescue, abandonment, and irrecoverable loss
infrastructure-locked transit and gate transaction safety
thousand-year interstellar benchmark
```

## Campaign Thesis

> **Across stars, history arrives before authority. Civilization survives by preserving evidence, local sovereignty, reviewable vows, and care for outcomes no living sender may witness.**

## Scope Boundary

This campaign is horizon architecture. It does not add interstellar implementation to Seedworks, and it does not make Atlas Gates a required product feature.

All capabilities remain `D3 / I0` until code, replay, conservation, rights, persistence, migration, performance, and human-comprehension evidence exists.

## Major Corrections

1. Interstellar distance eliminates shared immediacy rather than merely increasing travel time.
2. Relativistic travelers carry authentic history but not automatic command.
3. Descendants, residents, and machine participants can reconsider inherited missions.
4. Replication requires identity, lineage, ceilings, and shutdown rules.
5. Ancient signals and precursor systems preserve evidence, not permanent authority.
6. Impossible rescue still leaves obligations of truth, warning, refuge, memory, and repair.
7. Atlas Gates remain infrastructure-locked, uniqueness-sensitive, and horizon-only.
8. Worldline forks preserve message, mission, treaty, identity, and asset ancestry.
