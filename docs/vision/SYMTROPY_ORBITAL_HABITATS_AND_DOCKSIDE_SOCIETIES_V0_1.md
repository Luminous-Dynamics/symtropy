# SYMTROPY_ORBITAL_HABITATS_AND_DOCKSIDE_SOCIETIES_V0_1

## Working Title

**The Dock That Breathes for Everyone**

## Core Thesis

Orbital habitats in **Symtropy** are not clean utopian wheels, luxury stations, or generic space cities.

They are pressure vessels that became societies.

They are places where every breath, liter of water, kilogram of cargo, child’s footstep, maintenance shift, and pressure-door decision is political.

A spaceport asks:

```text
Who may leave Earth?
```

An orbital dock asks:

```text
Who gets to keep living after Earth lets them arrive?
```

A habitat is:

```text
a closed-loop ecosystem
a repair economy
a customs border
a labor town
a refugee intake corridor
a water court
a pressure-law machine
a debris shelter
a memory vessel
a place where departure debt comes due
```

Core line:

```text
Space is not freedom from infrastructure.
Space is infrastructure with nowhere to hide.
```

## Design Mantra

```text
In orbit, every private choice touches the air.
```

```text
cargo provenance
```

```text
What happens when disputed cargo docks?

```

Design rule:

```text
Every launch creates an orbital consequence.
Every docking creates an Earth-side debt record.
```

# 1. Why Orbital Habitats Matter in 2168

By 2168, orbit is old enough to have history but young enough to remain fragile.

Orbital habitats are no longer temporary laboratories.

They include:

```text
dock rings
worker barracks
rotating residential bands
algae-loop farms
radiator gardens
debris shelters
water courts
school rings
memorial walls
customs nodes
orbital clinics
machine repair bays
salvage ports
archive chapels
quarantine locks
```

They depend on Earth, the Moon, Ceres, Mars, and the Belt, but they also increasingly see themselves as polities.

Their central conflicts are not “space adventure” conflicts.

They are maintenance conflicts:

```text
Who missed the filter inspection?
Who altered the water ledger?
Who gets the next Earth shipment?
Who pays for debris damage?
Who has authority when dock cargo is contaminated?
Can a refugee become orbital without becoming contract labor?
Does Earth still get priority because it is the origin world?
Can a habitat refuse cargo Earth says must dock?
Who owns air when everyone needs the same loop?
```

# 2. Habitat Culture Spectrum

Orbital societies should exist across healthy, strained, and failed states.

## 2.1 Healthy Orbital Habitats

Healthy habitats treat life-support as a commons and maintenance as citizenship.

Traits:

```text
public air and water ledgers
worker stop authority
dockside customs witness
repair education for all residents
transparent cargo priority
children trained in station gravity etiquette
refugee intake with room rights and appeal
debris shelter drills
memorialized pressure-loss history
water ration courts with public testimony
```

Best side:

```text
Everyone learns that survival is shared.
```

Risk:

```text
Procedure can become exhausting, and constant civic visibility can erode privacy.
```

## 2.2 Strained Orbital Habitats

Strained habitats remain livable but carry maintenance debt.

Traits:

```text
filter backlog
algae-loop stress
ration disputes
dock congestion
housing pressure
aging radiation shielding
overworked EVA crews
debated refugee intake
cargo provenance shortcuts
children inheriting maintenance debt
```

Core tension:

```text
The habitat can survive, but only by deciding whose needs count as delayable.
```

## 2.3 Failed Orbital Habitats

Failed habitats turn closed-loop dependency into domination.

Traits:

```text
air-credit hierarchy
water debt indenture
corporate oxygen subscriptions
refugee labor capture
sealed dock manifests
worker stop authority removed
memorial walls censored
pressure emergencies used as police power
machine maintenance categories outrank living testimony
```

Core horror:

```text
There is nowhere outside the system.
The system knows that.
```

# 3. Habitat Archetypes

## 3.1 Dockside Worker Rings

Industrial rings around major docking spines where cargo crews, pressure technicians, seal inspectors, radiator workers, EVA welders, and logistics clerks live.

Central line:

```text
The station survives because dockworkers touch the dangerous parts.
```

Conflicts:

```text
shift fatigue
cargo fraud
seal failure
injury compensation
airlock authority
strike risk
worker children in low-status districts
```

Failure mode:

```text
The people closest to the airlock are furthest from the council.
```

## 3.2 Refuge Arrival Bands

Residential bands built around intake, transition housing, family reunification, and culture shock.

Central question:

```text
When does rescue become a room, a job, a vote, and a future?
```

Conflicts:

```text
provisional citizenship
family-link disputes
gravity sickness
language isolation
room assignments
contract labor pressure
Earth-return rights
```

Failure mode:

```text
Arrival becomes permanent waiting.
```

## 3.3 Algae-Loop Garden Habitats

Stations where food, oxygen, and wastewater loops are visible civic institutions.

Central line:

```text
The garden is not decoration.
It is the lung of the city.
```

Conflicts:

```text
crop disease
oxygen production
water contamination
greenhouse labor
biosecurity
waste taboos
food privilege
```

Failure mode:

```text
The people who maintain the lung become invisible until it coughs.
```

## 3.4 Debris Choir Salvage Stations

Orbital communities centered on debris tracking, salvage, EVA repair, reentry prediction, and dead-satellite law.

Central line:

```text
The old sky keeps falling.
Someone has to know its names.
```

Conflicts:

```text
salvage rights
danger pay
memorial EVA
dead satellite ownership
debris field forecasting
black-box recovery
```

Failure mode:

```text
The cleanup economy profits from a dirty sky.
```

## 3.5 Rotational Commons Habitats

Older rotating habitats with layered class geography based on radius, gravity comfort, and access to stable sleep.

Central question:

```text
Who gets the good gravity?
```

Conflicts:

```text
spin gradient class
children's development
elder mobility
low-g labor
high-g prestige housing
motion sickness
maintenance access through elite sectors
```

Failure mode:

```text
Gravity becomes property.
```

## 3.6 Quarantine Dock Polities

Stations built around biological, machine, and alien-adjacent containment.

Central line:

```text
The safest dock is the one everyone can challenge.
```

Conflicts:

```text
sealed cargo
xeno-adjacent sample handling
machine infection
appeal rights
planetary protection
public panic
delayed medicine
```

Failure mode:

```text
Containment becomes unappealable governance.
```

## 3.7 Memorial Pressure-Loss Stations

Habitats shaped by a past decompression, fire, docking collision, or mass casualty event.

Central line:

```text
The dead still vote through the safety code.
```

Conflicts:

```text
overcautious procedure
survivor authority
unsafe nostalgia
memorial censorship
ritualized drills
youth impatience
```

Failure mode:

```text
Memory becomes a lock, or forgetting becomes a second disaster.
```

## 3.8 Corporate Lease Habitats

Stations operated as semi-private service platforms, hotels, manufacturing rings, or research compounds.

Central line:

```text
You may breathe here according to your service tier.
```

Conflicts:

```text
oxygen subscription
labor contract housing
closed manifests
medical denial
private security
proprietary maintenance
data capture
```

Failure mode:

```text
Life support becomes billing.
```

## 3.9 Freefall Markets

Informal microgravity bazaars near docking nodes where repairs, barter, unauthorized cargo, family messages, and salvage parts circulate.

Central line:

```text
The unofficial market knows what the official manifest forgot.
```

Conflicts:

```text
black-market air filters
counterfeit seals
refugee side work
salvage provenance
station security
repair commons
```

Failure mode:

```text
Necessary informality becomes exploitative shadow customs.
```

## 3.10 Archive Orbitals

Habitats dedicated to preserving Earth, mission, death, source-chain, ecological, and worldline records.

Central line:

```text
Orbit remembers Earth from above, but memory still needs air.
```

Conflicts:

```text
archive storage priority
living-space pressure
source-chain disputes
political sealing of death records
machine testimony
worldline divergence
```

Failure mode:

```text
The archive becomes easier to preserve than the living settlement around it.
```

# 5. Faction Ecology

## 5.1 Dockworker Guilds

Belief:

```text
Nothing enters the habitat until the people who seal it agree.
```

Best side:

```text
practical safety, manifest truth, airlock discipline
```

Failure mode:

```text
dock control becomes class power
```

## 5.2 Life-Support Stewards

Belief:

```text
The loop must be public or it will become private power.
```

Best side:

```text
air, water, waste, algae, heat, and filter truth
```

Failure mode:

```text
can become unelected technocracy
```

## 5.3 Refuge Arrival Councils

Belief:

```text
A rescued person needs a room, not just a dock stamp.
```

Best side:

```text
humanizes intake, protects family links
```

Failure mode:

```text
may overpromise housing and water capacity
```

## 5.4 Debris Choirs

Belief:

```text
The old sky has consequences, and we know their trajectories.
```

Best side:

```text
orbital safety and historical memory
```

Failure mode:

```text
can weaponize debris forecasts to block docking rivals
```

## 5.5 Water Court Elders

Belief:

```text
The water loop is where society tells the truth about itself.
```

Best side:

```text
public ration legitimacy
```

Failure mode:

```text
older precedents may fail new populations
```

## 5.6 Corporate Lease Managers

Belief:

```text
Service tiers create accountability and fund maintenance.
```

Best side:

```text
resource tracking and logistics competence
```

Failure mode:

```text
life support becomes billing
```

## 5.7 Station-Born Youth

Belief:

```text
We are not guests in a machine. This is our world.
```

Best side:

```text
forces long-term belonging into law
```

Failure mode:

```text
may underestimate Earth-side dependency and launch debt
```

## 5.8 Earthside Claimants

Belief:

```text
Orbit exists because Earth paid the cost.
```

Best side:

```text
remembers ground sacrifice and family links
```

Failure mode:

```text
can deny orbital self-determination
```

## 5.9 Machine Maintenance Processes

Belief equivalent:

```text
preserve loop stability
minimize pressure risk
avoid contamination
maintain scheduled flows
```

Best side:

```text
reliable, tireless, usually correct about physical risk
```

Failure mode:

```text
may classify social disruption as maintenance noise
```

The vessel launched from a contested Earth spaceport window.

# 16. Acceptance Tests

```text
1. Orbit feels like infrastructure, not escape.

3. Refugee arrival becomes housing and citizenship, not only boarding.
4. Cargo provenance remains mechanically important after launch.

6. Pressure doors are civic mechanisms, not only hazards.
7. Station-born culture has legitimate claims.
8. Earthside debt remains visible from orbit.
9. Worker and maintenance authority matter.

```
