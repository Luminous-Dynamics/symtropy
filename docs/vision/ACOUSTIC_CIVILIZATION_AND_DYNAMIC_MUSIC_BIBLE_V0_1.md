# Acoustic Civilization and Dynamic Music Bible

## Owned Question

**What should Symtropy sound and feel like when civilization, machinery, ecology, architecture, culture, danger, memory, and alien life are all audible parts of the world?**

## Core Thesis

Sound is not decoration around the simulation.

It is:

```text
physical evidence
spatial orientation
machine condition
social presence
cultural memory
ecological activity
warning
comfort
ritual
music
silence
```

A player should often hear a system changing before a meter confirms it.

> **Civilization has a sound because maintained relationships have rhythm.**

## Prime Directives

1. **No constant cinematic wallpaper.** Music must leave room for place, machinery, weather, speech, and silence.
2. **No generic sci-fi hum everywhere.** Every device, building, vehicle, ecology, and culture has a source and acoustic body.
3. **No diagnostic sound that lies without cause.** Mechanical audio follows actual state, load, damage, environment, and control behavior.
4. **No culture reduced to a musical stereotype.** Societies contain multiple traditions, generations, sacred contexts, popular forms, work rhythms, and countercultures.
5. **No alien sound designed only to be frightening.** Nonhuman acoustics derive from bodies, media, senses, architecture, and communication needs.
6. **No critical information in audio alone.** Captions, visual indicators, haptics, and structured alerts provide alternatives.
7. **No music that dictates moral interpretation.** Score can create tension, grief, wonder, or ambiguity without declaring which faction is righteous.
8. **No endless escalation.** Rest, quiet, domestic sound, and ordinary pleasure are part of the emotional architecture.
9. **No perfect global mix.** Masks, helmets, pressure, injury, architecture, distance, weather, and attention alter what can be heard.
10. **No procedural music without memory.** Dynamic composition should recall places, people, choices, cultures, and unresolved histories.

# 2. Machine Listening

Machines should possess legible acoustic identities.

A healthy machine has stable rhythm, spectrum, and response. Failure may appear as:

```text
missed cycle
rough bearing
cavitation
resonance
arcing
pressure flutter
fan imbalance
control oscillation
```

Players can learn by ear without every fault becoming a rhythm game.

Expertise improves interpretation and isolation, not the existence of the cue.

# 3. Architecture as Acoustic Governance

Architecture controls:

```text
who can hear whom
which labor is hidden
where privacy exists
how alarms travel
whether machines dominate conversation
which rituals fill public space
```

A public assembly, clinic, factory, pleasure garden, archive, and quarantine hall should produce different acoustic relationships—not only different reverb presets.

# 4. Cultural Sound

Every culture defines:

```text
public versus private music
who may perform
how children learn
work and repair rhythms
mourning forms
festival forms
sacred silence
instrument materials
machine participation
attitudes toward amplification and recording
```

Cultures change, borrow, remix, resist, commercialize, and remember.

# 5. Dynamic Music Grammar

Dynamic score follows several independent dimensions:

```text
place identity
current activity
tension and danger
wonder and discovery
relationship state
settlement condition
historical memory
player-chosen aesthetic preference
```

It should not simply increase percussion as enemies approach.

Music may:

```text
introduce a place motif
fragment it under failure
allow another culture to reinterpret it
return it after restoration with audible scars
leave it unresolved after compromise
```

# 6. Motif Memory

Motifs may belong to:

```text
person
relationship
settlement
vehicle
faction
machine
alien contact
promise
loss
worldline
```

A motif is not guaranteed heroic or villainous. Its transformations reflect context and memory.

A recovered vehicle might retain a damaged rhythmic signature. A treaty motif may appear in two incompatible cultural arrangements before converging.

# 7. Diegetic and Nondiegetic Boundary

The game may move sound across the boundary deliberately.

Examples:

```text
festival music continues as the player leaves, then becomes score
machine rhythm becomes musical pulse during deep diagnosis
archive recording joins present instruments
alien contact signal becomes shared motif only after recognition
```

The transition should support meaning rather than novelty alone.

# 8. Silence

Silence may mean:

```text
safety
privacy
absence
vacuum
power loss
mourning
quarantine
listening
fear
respect
```

Silence should not be treated merely as missing content.

# 9. Combat and Danger

Combat audio prioritizes:

```text
threat direction
weapon and impact character
cover and structural state
vehicle and machine condition
team communication
withdrawal and surrender signals
```

The mix should preserve enough environmental and social information to support nonlethal resolution and escape.

# 10. Alien Acoustics

Alien sound begins with:

```text
medium
body
sensing range
timescale
social organization
architecture
protected boundaries
```

An oceanic intelligence may communicate through pressure and current. A lithic intelligence may use resonance below ordinary human timescale. A swarm polity may speak through formation noise rather than individual voice.

Human translation tools may transpose, compress, spatialize, or visualize these signals while labeling transformation.

# 11. Accessibility

Required features:

```text
complete dialogue subtitles
speaker identity and direction
non-speech captions
critical sound visualization
frequency and dynamic-range options
tinnitus-safe alternatives
mono compatibility
haptic cues
music and ambience controls
cognitive-load presets
```

Caption language distinguishes observed sound from interpreted meaning.

# 12. Seedworks Sound Proof

The representative build should prove:

```text
one machine diagnosable partly by sound
one settlement soundscape that changes after player action
one quiet domestic or cultural space
one danger mix that preserves team and environment cues
one dynamic motif with a remembered transformation
one accessible non-speech caption path
```

# 13. Acceptance Tests

1. Players detect a representative machine fault from combined physical and acoustic cues.
2. A settlement state change alters soundscape through real sources, not only score.
3. Dynamic music can withdraw completely without leaving the scene emotionally empty.
4. Motif recurrence is recognized without becoming repetitive.
5. Cultural music remains internally diverse and context-specific.
6. Alien signals remain distinguishable from their translated presentation.
7. Critical cues survive accessibility modes and reduced dynamic range.
8. Combat audio supports direction, cover, team communication, and retreat.
9. Acoustic state survives save/load where it represents persistent machinery or history.
10. Audio density remains inside voice and CPU budgets.

## Final Rule

```text
The world should not sound alive because audio is busy.
It should sound alive because every rhythm belongs to something that is trying to continue.
```
