# Symtropy Planetary World Types Design Bible

## Version 0.1 — Worlds as Moral Physics

## Core Thesis

Worlds in Symtropy are not backdrops.

They are systems that shape:

```text
bodies
cities
memory
law
movement
labor
care
repair
alien cognition
political conflict
```

A world type should not ask:

```text
What does this planet look like?
```

It should ask:

```text
What kind of civilization becomes possible here?
What kind of life does this world reward?
What kind of repair does this world make difficult?
Who is disabled by this environment?
Who is empowered by it?
```

# 2. New Primary World Type: Gravity-Bound Super-Earth

## Core fantasy

```text
The world is heavier than the body.
```

## Built around

```text
high gravity
dense atmosphere
low architecture
exo-suit dependency
mobility politics
massive native life
compressed ecosystems
```

## Visual language

```text
low cities
wide arches
thick columns
compression gardens
crawler roads
exo-suit commons
gravity rail corridors
rest terraces
tether plazas
deep foundations
```

## Native life

Life on these worlds tends toward:

```text
low bodies
many limbs
wide stances
heavy armor
thick tendons
slow powerful motion
ground-contact senses
short necks
dense support structures
```

Plants and fungi tend toward:

```text
mat forests
ground orchards
low canopy webs
thick root plates
wide fungal shelves
pressure moss
crawl-vine ecologies
```

## Gameplay

```text
exo-suit calibration
fall-risk management
crawler route repair
gravity rail access
mobility rights dispute
heavy-world rescue
native-body diplomacy
assist-gear rationing
```

## Core conflict

```text
movement as a public right versus movement as controlled infrastructure
```

## Rule

```text
Gravity can be assisted, but never ignored.
```

## Design principle

A Gravity-Bound Super-Earth should make the player feel that every step has cost.

The question is not:

```text
Can the player move?
```

The question is:

```text
Who built the systems that allow movement, and who do they exclude?
```

# 6. Example: Gravity-Bound Super-Earth

```text
world_type:
Gravity-Bound Super-Earth

gravity_band:
1.5g – 2.2g

atmosphere_type:
dense breathable or semi-breathable atmosphere

pressure_band:
high but survivable with adaptation

dominant_water_state:
surface rivers, heavy mist, pressure wetlands, deep basins

dominant_energy_source:
solar, geothermal, pressure-gradient power

native_body_logic:
low, wide, strong, many-limbed, ground-contact sensory organs

native_sense_logic:
vibration, pressure, low-frequency sound, chemical ground traces

architecture_logic:
low terraces, compression arches, crawl forums, gravity rail corridors

main_traversal_modes:
exo-suits, crawlers, gravity rails, low-profile rovers, tethers

human_survival_constraint:
fatigue, falls, cardiovascular stress, tool weight, suit dependency

alien_survival_constraint:
field-sled corridors may disrupt ground-sense ecology

primary_resource:
mobility support infrastructure

primary_civic_conflict:
who gets to move freely

first_contact_risk:
humans mistake low posture for submission; natives mistake upright movement for threat display

Field_Deck_bias:
SCAN highlights load, fall risk, body stress, safe posture, gravity gradient

failure_state:
mobility infrastructure privatized; disabled humans and native elders trapped by access law

Chronicle_event_hooks:
HighGravityWorldEntered
ExoSuitCalibrated
AssistGearGranted
AssistGearDenied
NativeBodyRouteRespected
GravityRailRestored
MobilityCommonsPrecedentCreated
```

# Final Principle

A Symtropy world should never be just a biome.

It should be a moral environment.

The terrain should ask:

```text
Who belongs here without assistance?
Who needs help?
Who controls that help?
What does the native life consider obvious?
What do humans dangerously misunderstand?
What kind of repair becomes sacred here?
```

The best worlds are not places the player conquers.

They are places the player must learn how to deserve.
