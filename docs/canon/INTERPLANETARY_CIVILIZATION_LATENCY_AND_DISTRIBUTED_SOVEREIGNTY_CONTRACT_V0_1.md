# Interplanetary Civilization, Latency, and Distributed Sovereignty Contract

## Owned Question

**How can people on planets, moons, orbital habitats, fleets, and long-duration vessels remain part of a shared civilization when messages, cargo, rescue, law, and political consent cannot arrive instantly?**

## Core Thesis

Interplanetary civilization is not planetary civilization with a larger map.

Distance changes the meaning of authority.

```text
A message can arrive after the crisis it concerns.
A law can be obsolete before a distant settlement receives it.
A rescue promise can be sincere and physically impossible.
A child can become an adult before a constitutional appeal returns.
A colony can remain culturally related while becoming politically independent.
```

The defining design rule is:

> **No institution may exercise immediate control across a distance it cannot immediately understand, supply, rescue, or answer.**

Interplanetary society therefore depends on:

```text
local autonomy
precommitted authority
bounded delegation
asynchronous consent
reliable clocks
verifiable messages
physical logistics
appealable emergency powers
cultural and political divergence
```

## Prime Directives

1. **Latency is gameplay, not loading time.** Delay changes planning, trust, command, markets, family life, and diplomacy.
2. **Local survival authority stays local.** Distant governments may set treaties and standards, but cannot safely micromanage urgent habitat operations.
3. **No remote omniscience.** A world acts on the information that has actually arrived.
4. **No instant interplanetary referendum.** Representation, mandates, delayed ballots, and ratification windows must exist.
5. **No colonial permanence by default.** Settlements that sustain themselves acquire real autonomy and a lawful path to renegotiate their relationship.
6. **No rescue fiction.** Rescue depends on trajectories, readiness, consumables, political permission, and time.
7. **No universal clock without local time.** Shared epochs support records; lived time remains tied to worlds, habitats, shifts, and bodies.
8. **No command without accountability.** Delayed command requires scope, expiry, evidence, dissent, and later review.
9. **No unity without divergence.** Shared civilization may preserve standards, rights, and mutual aid while languages, customs, laws, and identities change.
10. **No system-scale feature may erase embodied play.** Every strategic commitment must eventually become ships, cargo, work, risk, travel, repair, or human consequence.

# 1. Political Scales

Interplanetary civilization contains overlapping scales:

```text
household
ship or habitat
settlement
moon or regional compact
planetary federation
orbital network
interplanetary corridor
system compact
worldline
```

These are not strict levels in a hierarchy.

A habitat may depend economically on a planet while remaining legally autonomous. A fleet may operate under a system rescue compact while retaining local command. A mining settlement may belong to a planetary culture yet negotiate directly with another moon because the transfer window demands it.

## Subsidiarity Under Delay

A question should be decided at the smallest scale that can:

```text
observe the relevant facts
bear the material consequences
protect affected rights
coordinate required infrastructure
provide meaningful appeal
```

Delay strengthens the case for local authority because remote institutions see an older world.

# 2. Communication Classes

Not all messages carry the same authority.

```text
presence traffic       real-time local sensor and crew coordination
operational traffic    route, docking, life-support, cargo, and rescue data
civic traffic          votes, laws, credentials, court filings, testimony
scientific traffic     observations, models, warnings, replication results
personal traffic       family messages, private media, cultural exchange
chronicle traffic      signed historical outcomes and worldline ancestry
emergency traffic      hazard warnings, distress, quarantine, collision risk
```

Each class requires different:

```text
priority
bandwidth
privacy
authentication
expiry
replay behavior
legal effect
```

A delayed personal message is emotionally painful. A delayed collision warning is lethal. A delayed law may be invalid for current conditions. The game must preserve those differences.

# 3. Asynchronous Governance

## Mandates

Representatives carry bounded mandates containing:

```text
constituency
scope
validity interval
known-condition snapshot
nonnegotiable rights floor
negotiation latitude
reporting duty
recall or replacement process
```

A representative cannot claim fresh consent after circumstances have materially changed.

## Ratification Modes

Interplanetary agreements may use:

```text
advance mandate
provisional signature
local emergency accession
sequential ratification
delayed referendum
conditional activation
sunset pending confirmation
```

The player should see the tradeoff between speed and legitimacy.

## Forked Decisions

During long delay, different communities may make incompatible but locally legitimate decisions.

The system must support:

```text
parallel provisional policies
later reconciliation
recognized divergence
compensation for reliance
constitutional fork
peaceful separation
```

The game must not retroactively pretend only one timeline was valid.

# 4. Distant Authority and Command

Remote command is strongest when it provides:

```text
shared goals
resource commitments
known rules of engagement
contingency branches
rights protections
reporting formats
review after contact resumes
```

It is weakest when it attempts:

```text
minute-by-minute tactical control
unbounded emergency power
punishment based on stale data
resource requisition without current inventory
orders that assume unavailable rescue
```

## Command Latency Doctrine

Every delayed command must expose:

```text
issued_at
information_cutoff
expected_arrival_window
scope
expiry
contingency conditions
issuer authority
recipient discretion
review obligation
```

Recipients may lawfully deviate when conditions exceed the command's stated assumptions, but must preserve evidence and later explain the decision.

# 5. Interplanetary Rights Floor

The rights floor must include:

```text
right to breathable life-support minimum
right to emergency shelter when physically possible
right to truthful life-support and transit information
right to local emergency agency
right to refuse impossible or suicidal remote orders
right to communication privacy within safety constraints
right to lawful exit when transport exists
right to family and identity continuity across worlds
right to appeal delayed authority
right not to be abandoned as an expendable colony
right not to be forced into cultural or political sameness
```

Rights can be constrained by real physical scarcity, but never silently erased by distance.

# 6. Shared Standards Without Central Ownership

System-scale civilization may maintain shared standards for:

```text
docking and rescue interfaces
identity and source-chain exchange
medical and pressure data
hazard classification
navigation epochs
cargo provenance
quarantine evidence
machine testimony
Chronicle event envelopes
```

Compatibility does not imply political subordination.

A habitat may use the same rescue beacon standard as Earth while rejecting Earth's taxation, military authority, or social law.

# 7. Time, Calendars, and Lived Synchrony

Every event should distinguish:

```text
local civil time
ship or habitat operational time
shared system epoch
event observation time
message send time
message receive time
estimated physical occurrence interval
```

The player should encounter:

```text
birthdays celebrated before replies arrive
votes cast using months-old debate
contracts priced against uncertain arrival dates
mourning after distant confirmation
conflicting historical dates from different clocks
```

Timekeeping is both technical infrastructure and cultural practice.

# 8. Family, Culture, and Belonging Across Distance

Interplanetary life creates:

```text
asynchronous families
multi-world households
diaspora rituals
recorded mentorship
slow courtship and delayed separation
children who know relatives through archives
cultural movements that arrive after their source has changed
```

Personal communication must remain grounded in real send and receive times. NPCs may reply to an earlier version of the player, misunderstand silence, or learn about events after relationships have already changed.

# 9. Economic and Material Sovereignty

Political autonomy requires some control over:

```text
air and water
food production
energy
maintenance
medical care
manufacturing
communications
transport access
emergency reserves
```

A settlement dependent on one distant supplier is vulnerable even if legally sovereign.

The game should represent autonomy as a material capability profile, not a declaration alone.

# 10. Multi-World Conflict and Peace

Interplanetary conflict is shaped by:

```text
warning delay
transfer windows
fuel and mass
fixed habitats
civilian life support
communications infrastructure
rescue obligations
uncertain surrender status
```

The rules of war must protect life-support systems, rescue channels, medical traffic, and civilian transit.

A blockade is never just a colored boundary. It is an attempt to control oxygen, food, medicine, parts, information, and movement across time.

# 11. Worldline Relationship

A system-scale civilization may fork because of:

```text
political secession
communication isolation
contact interpretation
colony autonomy
war
failed migration
technology incompatibility
Chronicle disagreement
```

Worldline forks must preserve:

```text
identity ancestry
asset custody
message provenance
shared pre-fork history
unresolved obligations
rights claims
```

# 12. Player Experience Requirements

The system must create playable activities such as:

```text
carrying a representative with a bounded mandate
racing a medical cargo against a transfer window
choosing whether to trust a delayed warning
negotiating provisional authority aboard a habitat
repairing a relay that holds months of family messages
investigating an order issued on stale intelligence
escorting a colony delegation seeking autonomy
rescuing a vessel whose legal status is disputed
```

The player should feel that distance produces responsibility, uncertainty, longing, independence, and political change—not merely travel time.

# 13. Acceptance Tests

A representative implementation must prove:

1. Two settlements receive the same information at different times and make different locally valid decisions.
2. A delayed command exposes its information cutoff and may be lawfully overridden under changed conditions.
3. A vote or treaty cannot complete without real mandate and ratification rules.
4. A remote authority cannot directly operate local life support without delegated permission.
5. A personal message retains send and receive context across save/load.
6. Shared standards survive political separation.
7. A self-sustaining settlement can pursue lawful autonomy without deleting obligations or minority rights.
8. A worldline fork preserves pre-fork communication and asset ancestry.

# 14. Non-Goals

This contract does not require the first Seedworks release to simulate a populated solar system.

It exists to ensure that later space expansion grows from the same physical, civic, and historical grammar as the representative region.

## Final Line

> **A solar system becomes a civilization not when every world obeys the same center, but when distance can no longer excuse abandonment, domination, or forgetting.**
