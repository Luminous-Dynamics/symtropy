# Settlement Autonomy, Colonization Ethics, and Non-Extractive Expansion Contract

## Owned Question

**How can players establish new communities beyond Earth without reducing worlds to empty property, settlers to corporate instruments, or existing life and history to obstacles?**

## Core Thesis

Expansion is not morally neutral because distance is large and land appears unused.

A site may already contain:

```text
microbial ecology
nonhuman agency
seasonal migration
slow geological intelligence
prior human claims
abandoned workers
sacred or scientific value
machine stewardship
future ecological possibility
```

The core rule is:

> **No world is empty merely because no human government currently occupies it.**

Settlement is legitimate when it preserves life, evidence, consent, exit, local autonomy, and the capacity to repair its own consequences.

## Prime Directives

1. **Survey before claim.** Physical usability does not prove moral availability.
2. **Agency uncertainty creates restraint.** Unknown life or signal requires study, containment, and reversible action before irreversible transformation.
3. **No permanent parent ownership.** A settlement that bears its own risks and maintains its own life support gains political standing.
4. **No company-town air.** Employers cannot convert control of oxygen, water, transport, medicine, or identity into unlimited labor authority.
5. **No settlement by disposable labor.** Construction workers, migrants, prisoners, clones, machines, and modified bodies retain rights and future standing.
6. **No terraforming as default victory.** Adaptation, coexistence, limited zones, orbital habitation, and noncontact may be better outcomes.
7. **No claim without maintenance.** A map declaration does not create effective stewardship.
8. **No extraction without accounting.** Material removal must expose ecological, cultural, labor, and future-option costs.
9. **No failed settlement erasure.** Evacuation, abandonment, bodies, archives, debts, contamination, and rescue obligations persist.
10. **No frontier exemption from law.** Distance may require local discretion, not fewer rights.

# 1. Site Status

Candidate sites may be classified as:

```text
apparently abiotic
life suspected
life confirmed
agency uncertain
agency confirmed
historically occupied
actively claimed
contested
protected scientific or heritage zone
quarantined
hazardous commons
```

Classification records evidence and uncertainty. It is revisable.

# 2. Survey Sequence

Before irreversible settlement:

```text
remote observation
noninvasive sampling
hazard mapping
life and agency assessment
historical and claim search
reversible field camp
replicated findings
public review
contact or noncontact protocol where needed
bounded settlement authorization
```

Emergencies may justify temporary shelter, but not permanent ownership by fait accompli.

# 3. Territorial Claims

Claims are decomposed into:

```text
use right
habitation right
transit right
resource extraction right
scientific access
stewardship duty
exclusion authority
construction authority
future expansion option
```

No actor gains every right automatically.

## Effective Stewardship

A claim remains legitimate only when the claimant can:

```text
maintain safety
prevent uncontrolled contamination
respect protected boundaries
support residents
respond to incidents
preserve records
submit to review
```

Dormant speculative claims decay or become contestable.

# 4. Existing Life and Nonhuman Standing

Settlement planning must consider:

```text
habitat continuity
chemical and microbial contamination
noise and light
thermal change
water and atmosphere diversion
migration corridors
reproductive cycles
signal quiet
unknown agency
```

Possible outcomes:

```text
no settlement
remote orbital support
seasonal or limited presence
buffer zone
shared habitat treaty
relocation by consent where meaningful
restoration obligation
full settlement with monitored boundaries
```

# 5. Terraforming and Adaptation

The game distinguishes:

```text
human body adaptation
sealed habitats
local environmental modification
regional ecological engineering
planetary terraforming
```

Larger interventions require greater evidence, representation, reversibility, and long-term monitoring.

Terraforming cannot erase prior life or agency because the player prefers Earthlike aesthetics.

# 6. Settlement Founders and Residents

Founding actors may include:

```text
public expedition
worker cooperative
religious or cultural community
refugee compact
scientific mission
corporate venture
military outpost
machine collective
multi-species treaty mission
```

Their founding purpose influences but does not permanently own descendants.

Residents acquire standing through:

```text
risk borne
maintenance labor
care and family life
length of residence
civic participation
cultural creation
local knowledge
```

# 7. Parent Polity Relationship

Possible relationships:

```text
administrative district
associated settlement
chartered autonomous community
protectorate with expiry
independent polity
shared scientific preserve
multi-polity compact
```

A parent polity may retain narrow authority over declared matters such as shared defense or debt, but cannot claim indefinite total sovereignty merely because it financed launch.

## Autonomy Triggers

Autonomy review should occur when:

```text
local life-support becomes durable
population becomes multigenerational
communication delay makes direct rule impractical
local law and culture diverge
residents bear substantial risk without representation
founding contract expires
```

# 8. Labor, Debt, and Exit

Settlement labor systems must expose:

```text
contract terms
hazards
life-support dependency
wages or shares
housing and medical access
transport and exit rights
debt
collective bargaining
whistleblowing
```

Prohibited structures include:

```text
oxygen debt that prevents exit
reproductive obligations
identity revocation for labor refusal
inherited employment debt
unreviewable company courts
abandonment after injury
```

Exit may be physically delayed by transfer windows, but legal exit and transport priority remain real obligations.

# 9. Resource Extraction

Extraction proposals define:

```text
material and quantity
method
energy and water
labor
waste
site transformation
life and agency uncertainty
transport
beneficiary
local share
restoration or closure plan
```

## Local Benefit

Residents should not be left with:

```text
hazards
depleted reserves
maintenance debt
political dependency
```

while distant owners receive all durable value.

Possible benefit structures:

```text
public ownership
worker shares
royalty and restoration trust
local infrastructure
knowledge transfer
shared manufacturing
future-option reserve
```

# 10. Failed Settlements

Failure modes include:

```text
life-support collapse
financial abandonment
political conflict
ecological incompatibility
contact crisis
supply isolation
radiation or impact damage
population loss
```

Failure creates duties:

```text
rescue
evacuation
body and identity recovery
hazard containment
archive preservation
debt review
property and custody settlement
site restoration
memorialization
```

Abandoned sites remain causal historical locations, not free loot dungeons.

# 11. Cultural and Political Divergence

Off-world communities may develop different:

```text
languages
bodies
rituals
work patterns
family structures
law
timekeeping
identity
relationship to Earth or origin worlds
```

Divergence is not automatically degeneration, betrayal, or alienation.

The player may help preserve connection without demanding sameness.

# 12. Multi-Species Settlement

A multi-species settlement requires:

```text
environmental zones
transition interfaces
translation institutions
nonhuman representation
conflict and appeal procedures
habitat and territory boundaries
copy and memory rights
quarantine evidence
```

No species becomes the default owner merely by providing the transport technology.

# 13. Player Activities

```text
surveying a candidate site without destroying evidence
negotiating a limited-use claim
building a reversible field habitat
exposing a company-town life-support contract
supporting a settlement autonomy convention
choosing body adaptation instead of terraforming
closing and restoring an exhausted mine
rescuing an abandoned labor settlement
mediating conflict over microbial agency
preserving connection after political independence
```

# 14. Acceptance Tests

A representative implementation must prove:

1. A claim separates use, exclusion, extraction, and stewardship rights.
2. Agency uncertainty blocks irreversible development without adequate evidence.
3. A temporary emergency camp does not become automatic permanent ownership.
4. Residents can gain lawful political autonomy.
5. Labor refusal cannot revoke minimum life support or identity.
6. Extraction conserves material and creates waste, labor, and closure obligations.
7. A failed settlement produces rescue, archive, custody, and contamination consequences.
8. Cultural divergence persists without automatic hostility.
9. Settlement founding moves real people, cargo, knowledge, and institutions.
10. Worldline forks preserve claim and obligation ancestry.

# 15. Anti-Patterns

Reject:

```text
empty planet equals free property
colony as build menu
settlers as workforce count
corporate funding equals eternal sovereignty
terraforming as universal progress bar
abandoned settlement as consequence-free salvage
nonhuman life as biome modifier only
frontier lawlessness as player freedom
```

## Final Line

> **Expansion becomes civilization only when the new community can belong to its world without treating the world, its workers, or its descendants as property.**
