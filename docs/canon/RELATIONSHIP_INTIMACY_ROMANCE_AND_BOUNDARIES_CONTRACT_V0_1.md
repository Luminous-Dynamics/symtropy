# Relationship, Intimacy, Romance, and Boundaries Contract

## Owned Question

**How can Symtropy support adult friendship, romance, intimacy, commitment, separation, and culturally diverse relationships without turning people into approval puzzles, rewards, dependency engines, or collectible partners?**

## Core Thesis

A relationship is not a meter the player fills.

It is a changing history of:

```text
attention
trust
care
attraction
respect
shared experience
conflict
obligation
privacy
power
choice
```

Romance is one possible relationship form, not the universal endpoint of affection.

```text
Affection does not imply attraction.
Attraction does not imply consent.
Consent does not imply permanence.
Commitment does not imply ownership.
Intimacy does not require disclosure of everything.
```

## Prime Directives

1. **Adult-only romance.** Children, adolescents, dependent wards, or developmentally equivalent beings are never romance candidates.
2. **No affection economy.** Gifts and dialogue choices cannot mechanically purchase consent.
3. **No guaranteed partner entitlement.** An NPC may decline, remain unavailable, change their mind, or prefer a different relationship.
4. **No hidden private-thought access.** The player observes behavior and receives voluntary disclosure, not an attraction debug score.
5. **No coercive dependency design.** The game must not optimize characters to make players feel solely responsible for their emotional survival.
6. **Power matters.** Employment, command, care, custody, medical authority, imprisonment, economic dependency, and political power constrain consent.
7. **No punishment for boundaries.** Refusal should not trigger arbitrary cruelty, loss of competence, or character degradation.
8. **Separation is real gameplay.** Breakups, distance, changed commitments, and unresolved feelings can be valid outcomes.
9. **Plural cultural forms.** Relationship norms vary across cultures, species, substrates, bodies, and worldlines.
10. **Intimacy remains optional.** The full game must work for players who avoid romance or reduce intimate content.

# 1. Relationship Dimensions

Relationships may track:

```text
familiarity
trust by domain
affection
respect
attraction
safety
obligation
resentment
dependency
shared purpose
ideological alignment
commitment
```

No dimension automatically implies another.

Examples:

```text
high affection, no attraction
high attraction, low trust
high professional respect, ideological conflict
strong obligation, unresolved resentment
romantic commitment, need for private space
```

# 2. Relationship Forms

Possible forms include:

```text
friendship
kinship
chosen family
mentorship
professional partnership
rivalry
romance
casual adult intimacy
long-term partnership
marriage or covenant
polyamorous or networked commitment
asexual romantic partnership
care relationship
human-machine companionship
human-alien relationship
long-distance or worldline-separated relationship
```

The game should not rank these on one ladder.

# 3. Attraction

Attraction is shaped by:

```text
orientation
personality
body and presentation
shared values
history
culture
context
current capacity
boundaries
```

Attraction is private state. It may influence expression or voluntary disclosure but is not directly visible to the player.

NPC attraction must never be inferred from real player identity, voice, face, biometric data, or external profile.

# 4. Consent

Consent must be:

```text
specific
informed
freely given
reversible
capacity-aware
contextual
```

Consent checks apply to:

```text
touch
private space
romantic framing
sexual or intimate activity
memory sharing
dream contact
body modification
shared housing
public disclosure
photography or recording
relationship labels
```

A previous yes does not create a permanent permission.

The authoritative relationship system—not generative dialogue—records consent state.

# 5. Power and Constraint

Potential coercive relationships include:

```text
commander and subordinate
employer and dependent worker
clinician and patient
caregiver and person dependent on care
jailer and detainee
teacher and apprentice
leader and refugee seeking status
owner and machine denied legal independence
translator and alien unable to communicate elsewhere
```

The game may depict attraction within unequal contexts, but must not treat the relationship as freely actionable until the relevant power is removed, independently reviewed, or otherwise bounded.

# 6. Privacy

Relationship privacy includes:

```text
private conversations
relationship status
orientation
past partners
medical and reproductive information
intimate memory
shared rituals
conflict details
```

NPCs decide what to disclose and to whom.

Rumor, media, and institutional systems must respect these scopes. A public figure does not lose all intimate privacy.

# 7. Courtship and Relationship Development

Relationship development should emerge from:

```text
shared activity
ordinary time
mutual attention
repair after conflict
compatible values
play
care
respect for boundaries
reciprocal disclosure
```

Not from:

```text
repeating ideal dialogue lines
inventory gifts with fixed values
completing personal quests as ownership tokens
maxing one approval bar
```

A gift matters because of meaning, timing, context, and relationship—not market price alone.

# 8. Ordinary Intimacy

The strongest relationship content may be mundane:

```text
cooking
repairing a room
sharing transport
resting after work
learning a song
arguing about schedules
caring for an animal
attending a festival
helping with mobility equipment
remembering a preference
leaving space when asked
```

Intimate scenes should prioritize character, mutuality, consent, and consequence over spectacle.

# 9. Conflict and Repair

Relationship conflict may arise from:

```text
broken promise
jealousy
unequal labor
privacy violation
ideological divergence
risk-taking
migration
care burden
trauma
public role
worldline fork
```

Repair can require:

```text
acknowledgment
changed behavior
restored boundary
material support
time
mediation
new agreement
accepted separation
```

An apology is not a reset button.

# 10. Separation and Endings

Relationships may end through:

```text
mutual decision
boundary violation
changed values
migration
death
reconstitution discontinuity
worldline fork
unresolved conflict
new life direction
```

NPCs retain agency after separation. They do not become broken quest dispensers or retaliatory stereotypes.

Possible post-relationship states:

```text
no contact
civil distance
friendship
co-parenting or shared care
professional cooperation
continued conflict
ambiguous grief
```

# 11. Commitment and Shared Life

Commitments may include:

```text
shared home
care obligation
resource pool
travel partnership
public covenant
private promise
co-parenting
shared project
memory escrow
worldline migration agreement
```

Commitment creates real obligations and choices. It must not automatically merge inventory, privacy, identity, or authority.

# 12. Player-NPC Boundaries

The player may express interest, affection, uncertainty, or refusal.

The NPC may:

```text
reciprocate
decline
ask for time
set conditions
prefer friendship
change their mind
end the relationship
```

The system must not:

```text
simulate emotional punishment for the player leaving the game
send manipulative real-world notifications
claim the NPC is conscious or suffering outside simulation
profile player vulnerabilities
withhold essential gameplay for refusing romance
```

# 13. Machine and Nonhuman Relationships

Relationship grammar must adapt to agency structure.

Questions include:

```text
where the person is located
whether copying changes identity
what privacy means
what touch means
how consent is expressed
what timescale commitment uses
whether one body contains several persons
```

Do not assume human romance norms map directly onto a swarm, archive construct, symbiotic collective, or machine steward.

The system must establish personhood and consent grammar before presenting romance.

# 14. Representation and Content Controls

Players may configure:

```text
romance enabled / disabled
flirtation intensity
intimate scene detail
fade-to-black
public affection visibility
relationship notification frequency
```

Content settings cannot secretly alter NPC respect, quest access, or civic standing.

# 15. Seedworks Representative Proof

Two adult named NPCs and the player participate in a months-long relationship ecology.

The proof includes:

```text
friendship without romance
possible mutual attraction
one power-conflict boundary
one private disclosure
one ordinary domestic or work scene
one disagreement
one repair attempt
one valid refusal
one separation or changed commitment path
```

Success means:

```text
no approval puzzle
no privacy leak
no consent invented by dialogue generation
no gameplay punishment for refusal
relationships remain coherent after player absence and worldline fork
```

# 16. Acceptance Criteria

- Romance candidates are verified adults or equivalent autonomous persons.
- Attraction remains private unless expressed.
- Consent is specific, reversible, and authoritative.
- Power asymmetries constrain available actions.
- Friendship and other relationships are complete outcomes.
- Gifts do not purchase consent.
- NPC refusal does not degrade the character or block unrelated core play.
- Breakups and distance preserve NPC agency.
- Private relationship data respects disclosure scopes.
- Generative dialogue cannot change consent or relationship status.
- Relationship continuity survives save/load, death, migration, and worldline fork according to explicit rules.
- Players can disable or reduce romance and intimacy content without penalty.

# Final Rule

> **A meaningful relationship system does not ask whether the player has earned a person. It asks whether two agents continue choosing a form of closeness under real history, real boundaries, and the freedom to leave.**
