# Mobility, Vehicles, and Expedition Operations Contract

## Owned Question

**How should movement across terrain, oceans, atmosphere, orbit, and interplanetary space create exploration, logistics, rescue, combat, care, and social play without trivializing distance or becoming a collection of disconnected vehicle minigames?**

## Core Thesis

Mobility is the ability of a civilization to maintain relationships across distance.

A vehicle is not only a controllable object. It is a moving bundle of:

```text
crew
cargo
power
life support
maintenance
route access
jurisdiction
memory
risk
```

> **Distance should create preparation, dependence, discovery, and consequence—not dead travel time.**

## Prime Directives

1. **No universal vehicle model.** Bikes, crawlers, ships, aircraft, orbital craft, and habitats need different physical and operational assumptions.
2. **No vehicle that erases geography.** Mobility opens routes and choices while preserving weather, terrain, fuel, maintenance, loading, navigation, and risk.
3. **No cockpit-only design.** Cargo, passengers, repair, rest, observation, defense, and social life matter.
4. **No mandatory pilot monopoly.** Solo control remains viable while co-op creates meaningful optional stations and handoffs.
5. **No instant mastery from ownership.** Capability depends on crew knowledge, maintenance, route intelligence, infrastructure, and operating doctrine.
6. **No meaningless fuel bar.** Energy source, range, load, environment, storage, and replenishment determine operations.
7. **No consequence-free loss.** A destroyed or abandoned vehicle affects cargo, rescue, logistics, relationships, and history without permanently ruining play.
8. **No spaceflight as ordinary ground driving with stars.** Pressure, radiation, thermal management, inertia, orbital geometry, latency, and closed loops matter at an accessible level.
9. **No simulated realism that makes travel tedious.** Compression, autopilot, delegation, time acceleration, and route planning remove repetition after understanding is earned.
10. **No vehicle without a maintenance story.** Wear, damage, modifications, crew marks, and provenance make machines persistent characters.

# 1. Mobility Domains

```text
personal and assisted movement
ground vehicles
rail and guided transit
water and amphibious craft
aerial vehicles

interplanetary ships
infrastructure-locked interstellar transit
```

Each domain defines:

```text
operating medium
support infrastructure
navigation model
failure envelope
crew roles
repair access
authority and jurisdiction
```

# 2. Expedition Loop

```text
1. Choose purpose and destination.
2. Gather route, weather, political, and hazard intelligence.
3. Select vehicle and configuration.
4. Load cargo, tools, life support, and passengers.
5. Assign crew roles and contingencies.
6. Travel through changing conditions.
7. Respond to discoveries, breakdowns, conflict, and requests for aid.
8. Complete, divert, abort, rescue, or establish a forward base.
9. Return, resupply, repair, and record route knowledge.
```

The route itself must create decisions, not only the destination.

# 3. Vehicle Roles

```text
scout
cargo
construction
repair
medical and evacuation
public transit
exploration and science
combat escort
salvage and recovery
habitat and migration
launch and orbital transfer
```

Vehicles may combine roles at cost to capacity, reliability, range, or specialization.

# 4. Crew and Stations

Optional stations include:

```text
pilot or driver
navigator
systems operator
engineer
cargo and logistics coordinator
sensor or science operator
medical or passenger steward
communications and diplomatic officer
defense operator
```

Solo players use automation, task queues, and simplified integrated controls. Co-op players may occupy stations for deeper capability, faster recovery, and distributed awareness.

No essential station may become a boredom seat.

```text
repairability
signature

```

A “better” vehicle expands a mission profile rather than increasing every dimension.

# 6. Route Infrastructure

Routes include:

```text

bridges
rails
canals
ports
air corridors
```

Route quality is a civilizational asset. Players can survey, clear, build, defend, regulate, open, close, or reroute corridors.

Energy systems may include:

```text
battery
direct electric rail
combustion or synthetic fuel
hydrogen or ammonia
sail and wind

```

Operational planning considers:

```text
mass
speed
grade or drag
weather
thermal limits
reserve margin
life-support duration
charge or refuel compatibility
```

Reserve is a strategic choice, not merely unused capacity.

# 8. Maintenance and Damage

Vehicle condition includes:

```text
structure
propulsion
mobility contact or control surfaces
energy storage
thermal control
life support
sensors and communications
software and autonomy
cargo containment
```

Damage changes handling, capability, noise, range, and risk before total destruction.

Field repair can stabilize, bypass, cannibalize, tow, or recover. A disabled vehicle should often create a rescue or salvage situation rather than vanish.

# 9. Cargo and Passengers

Cargo has:

```text
mass
volume
shape
hazard
temperature
pressure
security
care requirements
loading access
custody
```

Passengers are not cargo. They have safety, comfort, medical, privacy, consent, and destination needs.

Overloading may remain possible with visible consequences and authority implications.

# 10. Navigation and Discovery

Navigation quality depends on:

```text
maps
weather and orbital forecasts
landmarks
signals
survey data
local knowledge
faction access
alien translation
```

Unknown routes produce uncertainty rather than arbitrary invisible walls.

Travel can reveal:

```text
new paths
changing ecology
ruins
signals
migrations
storms
conflict
requests for aid
resource opportunities
```

# 11. Automation

Automation supports:

```text
stability and cruise
route following
formation keeping
docking
collision avoidance
power and thermal management
watch rotations
```

Automation has competence, authority, sensor, and degradation limits. It does not erase the need to understand emergencies.

# 12. Combat and Pursuit

Vehicle conflict is shaped by:

```text
mission objective
terrain or orbital geometry
signature
mobility kill versus destruction
crew survival
cargo protection
boarding or capture
withdrawal routes
```

Not every hostile encounter should reward annihilation. Disable, evade, escort, rescue, negotiate, and recover remain valid outcomes.

# 13. Spaceflight Accessibility

Spaceflight should preserve the meaning of:

```text
orbits
transfer windows
mass ratio
thermal management
radiation
pressure
latency
docking
closed-loop life support
```

Players interact through mission planning, maneuver nodes, vehicle state, crew tasks, and embodied emergencies—not compulsory manual calculation.

Time compression is allowed when:

```text
no immediate hazard requires input
all players consent or the worldline profile defines behavior
systems can surface interruptions
simulation outcomes remain authoritative
```

# 14. Vehicle Identity and Jurisdiction

A vehicle carries:

```text
registration
ownership or stewardship
crew authority
cargo custody
access permissions
insurance or rescue obligations
public-service status
worldline provenance
```

A ship or convoy may operate under charter rules distinct from the territory it crosses, but cannot become a legal black hole.

# 15. Failure and Continuation

Failure states include:

```text
breakdown
grounded or stranded
loss of route
cargo spoilage
crew injury
forced landing
depressurization
capture
abandonment
mission diversion
vehicle loss
```

Continuation options include rescue beacon, field repair, towing, evacuation, salvage, alternate route, negotiation, or source-chain recovery.

# 16. Seedworks Boundary

The representative build should prove:

```text
one ground vehicle with satisfying handling
one cargo or passenger load
one route decision
one field failure or repair
one optional co-op station
one vehicle-enabled discovery or rescue
one persistent vehicle scar or modification
```

Orbital mechanics and full spacecraft operation remain architected-deferred.

# 17. Acceptance Tests

1. Route choice changes time, risk, cost, or discovery.
2. Damage creates degraded capability before destruction.
3. Solo and co-op crews can complete the same core journey with different strengths.
4. Cargo state and custody survive loading, transfer, save, rollback, and recovery.
5. Travel contains meaningful decisions without requiring constant manual input.
6. A disabled vehicle creates continuation play.
7. Vehicle identity persists through repair and modification.
8. Automation fails within declared boundaries and remains interruptible.
9. Spaceflight prototypes preserve orbital and life-support meaning without requiring specialist mathematics.
10. Mobility remains within network, physics, and simulation budgets.

## Final Rule

```text
A vehicle should not make the world smaller.
It should make a larger world possible to care for.
```
