# Atlas Metric Engineering, FTL, and Causality Contract

## Owned Question

**How can Symtropy let players personally travel among many star systems without turning the galaxy into a set of adjacent levels, erasing seed ships, breaking time logic, or permitting travel into the past?**

## Core Thesis

Symtropy permits faster-than-light travel only as a constrained, future-directed application of civilization-scale metric engineering.

FTL is not an ordinary engine fitted to any ship. It is not a free aim-and-jump ability. It is not teleportation. It is not a license to rewrite history.

Most interstellar expansion begins through probes, relativistic craft, arks, seed ships, inherited missions, and generations of local development. Mature civilizations may later build Atlas Anchors and negotiate bounded routes through engineered spacetime geometry.

> **Distance creates history. Atlas engineering may shorten a journey, but it may not erase the history created before the road existed.**

# 1. Fictional Scientific Premise

Symtropy adopts one explicit speculative discovery: controlled metric systems can establish a preferred navigable causal ordering called **Atlas Time**.

Atlas Time is not a universal Newtonian clock. Local proper time, gravitational time dilation, velocity time dilation, civic calendars, and observation delay remain real. Atlas Time exists to constrain engineered superluminal paths so they remain future-directed within one declared worldline.

An Atlas transit may arrive sooner than light traveling through ordinary space would arrive. It may not arrive at an Atlas timestamp earlier than departure.

The implementation must never imply that this premise is established contemporary science. It is a declared fictional mechanism chosen because it creates coherent gameplay and preserves consequences.

# 2. Hard Causality Laws

The following are non-negotiable simulation invariants.

1. **No arrival before departure.** Arrival Atlas Time must be greater than departure Atlas Time.
2. **No negative-time route cycle.** A sequence of Atlas routes may not return matter or information to an Atlas timestamp at or before the initiating departure.
3. **No arbitrary ship FTL.** Interstellar FTL requires prepared metric infrastructure or an explicitly bounded alien equivalent.
4. **No route to historical state.** A destination is a current descendant state in a declared worldline, never a saved earlier version of the same place.
5. **No silent worldline merge.** Divergent worldlines do not combine people, objects, claims, or histories by convenience.
6. **No unique-object duplication.** Transit cannot create duplicate persons, cargo, currency, offices, source chains, ships, or legal claims.
7. **No causality inference from interface convenience.** A loading transition does not imply zero elapsed time.
8. **No remote authority through mere connectivity.** Opening a route does not restore an origin government’s expired jurisdiction over a seed civilization.
9. **No forced reconnection.** A destination civilization may refuse construction, pairing, traffic, migration, trade, or reopening.
10. **No scientific uncertainty laundering.** IRIS and technical interfaces must distinguish measured route state, predicted route state, and unverified theory.

Any system that cannot prove these laws must fail closed.

# 3. Travel Families

Symtropy contains several legitimate interstellar travel fantasies. They coexist rather than replacing one another.

## 3.1 Conventional Sublight Travel

Includes chemical, nuclear, fusion, beamed, sail, antimatter-assisted, and other momentum-carrying craft.

These vessels retain:

- acceleration and braking;
- reaction mass or external momentum exchange;
- energy and heat rejection;
- radiation and collision risk;
- habitat metabolism;
- navigation uncertainty;
- relativistic time dilation;
- ordinary signal delay.

## 3.2 Metric-Trim Travel

Local metric control may shape gravity, inertia exposure, tidal stress, geodesics, and high-acceleration survivability without exceeding light speed.

Metric trim is useful for:

- orbital insertion;
- gravcraft;
- station keeping;
- high-gravity construction;
- crew acceleration protection;
- debris and radiation path management;
- relativistic mission support.

Metric trim does not create free energy or remove the need for navigation and braking.

## 3.3 Relativistic and Seed Voyages

A civilization reaching an unpaired star must cross the original distance physically unless it encounters a destination society with independently compatible infrastructure.

Seed ships may carry:

- people and households;
- embryos and ecological archives;
- machines and autonomous industry;
- constitutional and institutional tools;
- source-chain and Chronicle infrastructure;
- metric-anchor seed components;
- the knowledge required to build—but not the authority to compel—a future route.

## 3.4 Atlas Corridors

Two mature endpoints may negotiate a temporary future-directed metric corridor.

A corridor:

- reduces effective route length;
- has positive transit latency;
- requires both endpoints;
- has mass, energy, thermal, coherence, and traffic limits;
- follows a prepared route solution;
- cannot be redirected casually;
- may carry vessels, cargo, and bounded communications;
- can close, drift, degrade, or become politically unavailable.

## 3.5 Atlas Bridges and Gates

A persistent high-capacity route is an Atlas Bridge. The inhabited civic and industrial complex surrounding an endpoint is commonly called an Atlas Gate.

The gate is not one ring-shaped object. It is a coupled system of:

- metric field infrastructure;
- power production and storage;
- heat rejection;
- clocks and navigation references;
- approach and departure volumes;
- traffic control;
- quarantine;
- customs and custody;
- public authority;
- emergency shutdown;
- housing and labor;
- rescue capacity;
- archives and worldline records.

# 4. Player Access to New Worlds

Players must be able to reach new worlds through two distinct modes.

## 4.1 Pioneer Access

The player travels on the first physical expedition, ark, seed vessel, alien migration, or recovered precursor route.

Pioneer access preserves:

- elapsed time;
- onboard life;
- mission inheritance;
- social and political change;
- risk;
- arrival uncertainty;
- local founding;
- possible permanent separation from the origin epoch.

It is the only ordinary human-controlled path to a star with no receiving infrastructure.

## 4.2 Reconnection Access

A destination already possesses or completes a compatible endpoint.

The player helps open, repair, negotiate, or use the route. The destination is not a blank colony level. It has its own history, government, households, conflicts, ecology, and interpretation of the original mission.

The first reconnection may be more politically consequential than the original launch.

# 5. Seed-Ship Priority

Atlas technology must make seed ships more meaningful, not obsolete.

A seed ship carries the possibility of future reconnection, not a guaranteed claim over the destination.

The destination generation may:

- complete the inherited anchor plan;
- redesign it;
- delay it;
- repurpose it locally;
- pair with another civilization;
- impose conditions;
- reject it permanently;
- treat it as dangerous heritage;
- lack the resources to finish it;
- discover that the origin endpoint no longer exists.

The game must preserve the political fact that descendants did the work of surviving and building the receiving civilization.

# 6. Four Time Domains

All interstellar systems must expose four different notions of time.

## 6.1 Atlas Time

Orders route departures, arrivals, worldline ancestry, and causality validation.

## 6.2 Local Civic Time

Represents a settlement’s calendars, seasons, administrative terms, festivals, legal deadlines, and historical eras.

## 6.3 Proper Time

Measures experienced physical duration for a person, ship, machine, biological process, or local system.

## 6.4 Knowledge Time

Represents the newest confirmed state an observer has for another place.

Interfaces must not present these as interchangeable.

A player may arrive in Atlas year 2381 after experiencing four proper years while possessing reliable knowledge of the destination only through 2378.

# 7. Information and Observation

FTL transit does not grant omniscience.

Knowledge may travel through:

- ordinary light-speed signals;
- courier vessels;
- active Atlas communication channels;
- trusted relays;
- alien systems with declared constraints.

Every remote claim must carry:

- observation time;
- reception time;
- route or channel;
- provenance;
- confidence;
- possible branch mismatch;
- censorship or custody notes.

A map may know that a system exists while knowing almost nothing current about it.

# 8. Authority and Consent

An Atlas route is simultaneously physical infrastructure and a political relationship.

Opening a route requires valid authority for:

- endpoint operation;
- destination reception;
- traffic capacity;
- quarantine;
- migration;
- cargo custody;
- emergency intervention;
- environmental impact;
- public expenditure;
- route closure.

These authorities may belong to different institutions.

No military, corporation, player, host, companion, or IRIS instance may compress them into one master permission.

# 9. Ecology and Planetary Safety

Metric infrastructure may affect:

- orbital stability;
- radiation fields;
- magnetospheres;
- tidal environments;
- migrating species;
- alien communication media;
- machine and archive systems;
- local spacetime-sensitive ecologies.

Endpoint construction therefore requires environmental and nonhuman claim review.

A technically stable route may still be ecologically or politically unacceptable.

# 10. Economy and Logistics

Atlas routes have finite throughput.

They create strategic differences among:

- trunk routes;
- low-capacity local routes;
- courier-only links;
- seasonal corridors;
- politically restricted bridges;
- dormant anchors;
- one-way alien routes;
- isolated seed worlds.

Interstellar markets remain shaped by:

- route access;
- latency;
- mass limits;
- reservation priority;
- insurance;
- quarantine;
- energy prices;
- labor;
- maintenance debt;
- interruption risk.

A connected galaxy is not one frictionless market.

# 11. FTL Communication

FTL communication is allowed only through functioning Atlas or equivalent infrastructure.

It has:

- bandwidth;
- queueing;
- energy and thermal cost;
- access control;
- encryption;
- custody;
- outage states;
- interception and censorship risk;
- positive Atlas latency.

When a route closes, connected societies may return to ordinary light-delay or courier communication.

# 12. Multiplayer Worldlines

Players may occupy different future horizons without duplicating one history.

A player undertaking a long pioneer voyage migrates forward. Players remaining at origin continue in their own current epoch. The traveler cannot return to origin’s past.

Reconnection may occur when:

- origin time naturally advances;
- another player undertakes a causally valid journey;
- an Atlas corridor opens between compatible future states;
- the server creates an explicit descendant branch.

Host authority never becomes worldline authority.

# 13. Death, Reconstitution, and Transmission

Physical travel is the default.

If future technologies transmit cognitive or biological state rather than transporting a body, the event must use the source-chain system and answer:

- Was the origin destroyed?
- Was a new body created?
- Does another active instance remain?
- Which memories were transmitted?
- Which instance has legal standing?
- Which IRIS continuity traveled?
- Are both instances now persons?

The game may not conceal duplication or death behind a harmless teleport effect.

# 14. Failure and Irrecoverable Loss

Atlas failures include:

- route abort before commitment;
- stranding in ordinary space;
- destination loss;
- endpoint desynchronization;
- worldline mismatch;
- field collapse;
- quarantine breach;
- political closure;
- navigation corruption;
- thermal runaway;
- mass-envelope violation;
- an Atlas Scar that remains after the route is gone.

Some failures are recoverable. Some require years of repair. Some permanently alter the network. Some kill people.

The system must not guarantee rescue merely because the player is involved.

# 15. Player-Facing Legibility

Before any transit, the player must be able to inspect:

- departure Atlas Time;
- minimum arrival Atlas Time;
- expected proper time;
- latest confirmed destination state;
- prediction uncertainty;
- route capacity;
- route weather;
- quarantine status;
- authority status;
- abort windows;
- consequences of cancellation;
- worldline and uniqueness warnings.

IRIS may summarize these conditions but may not certify safety beyond available evidence.

# 16. Forbidden Shortcuts

The following are explicitly non-canonical:

- a universal FTL drive installed on ordinary personal craft;
- selecting any visible star and warping there;
- instantaneous zero-cost player teleportation between systems;
- returning to an earlier save as an in-world time-travel action;
- treating route opening as automatic conquest;
- ignoring the lives of seed-ship descendants;
- duplicating companions or cargo during server migration;
- making every alien civilization use human Atlas engineering;
- using FTL to make rescue, logistics, or war consequence-free;
- allowing an old origin government to reclaim a destination by historical paperwork alone.

# 17. Acceptance Gates

The contract is not satisfied until a representative implementation proves:

1. a sublight voyage with divergent proper and Atlas time;
2. a destination whose current state is newer than the player’s knowledge;
3. a seed civilization that may refuse reconnection;
4. an Atlas route with positive latency and finite capacity;
5. rejection of a negative-time route cycle;
6. preserved uniqueness across transit and crash recovery;
7. route closure without deleting either endpoint civilization;
8. multiplayer forward migration without duplication;
9. one ecological or nonhuman objection that can block an otherwise stable route;
10. one arrival where the player discovers that historical distance mattered more than travel time.

# Production Maxim

> **Symtropy may let the player cross the galaxy. It must never make the galaxy feel small.**
