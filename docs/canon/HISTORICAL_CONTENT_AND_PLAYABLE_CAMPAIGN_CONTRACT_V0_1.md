# Historical Content and Playable Campaign Contract

## Purpose

Symtropy now contains enough named history to become memorable and enough simulation contracts to remain causal. The next risk is that lore becomes one of three weak things:

```text
encyclopedia text
quest-window decoration
fixed cinematic continuity
```

This contract defines how authored history enters play without replacing systemic agency, turning people into content dispensers, or forcing every worldline toward one canonical ending.

> **History becomes playable when the player enters an unfinished consequence rather than receiving a completed story.**

## Canonical Boundary

Historical content may establish:

- that a documented event occurred in a worldline;
- which physical, legal, ecological, demographic, and cultural traces survived;
- which institutions and people claim authority;
- which evidence exists and who can access it;
- which tensions are active at campaign start;
- which outcomes are physically and socially reachable.

Historical content may not establish in advance:

- what the player believes;
- which interpretation is morally mandatory;
- that a named faction must survive;
- that one NPC is secretly correct without supporting evidence;
- that a campaign resolves because a dialogue node was selected;
- that an institution disappears when its leader is defeated;
- that ordinary people stop living while the campaign waits;
- that all worldlines converge on the authored ending.

## The Playable-History Unit

The canonical unit is a **campaign packet**, not a quest chain.

A campaign packet contains:

```text
historical wound or inheritance
current material system
people and institutions already acting
competing interpretations
bounded pressures and clocks
player entry vectors
activities and encounters
ordinary-life contexts
failure and partial-success states
revisit transformations
worldline branch consequences
```

A packet may generate missions, contracts, hearings, expeditions, battles, care work, construction, research, rituals, negotiations, or quiet domestic scenes. The packet itself persists beyond any one activity.

## Required Causal Spine

Every campaign packet must prove this chain:

```text
past event
→ surviving trace
→ present dependency or dispute
→ people making decisions
→ player-visible opportunity
→ validated action
→ material and social consequence
→ remembered interpretation
→ later revisit
```

A packet fails review if a step is replaced by narration alone.

## Historical Layers

Playable history keeps five layers separate.

### 1. Physical History

Examples:

- ruined seawalls;
- mismatched habitat rings;
- contaminated soil;
- patched firmware;
- abandoned rail corridors;
- memorial architecture;
- altered migration routes.

Physical traces are authoritative only within measurement uncertainty. A ruin proves that something happened, not every claim made about it.

### 2. Documentary History

Examples:

- signed manifests;
- maintenance logs;
- court records;
- sensor archives;
- personal letters;
- corporate filings;
- Chronicle entries.

Documents preserve provenance, custody, access scope, correction history, and known gaps.

### 3. Living Memory

People remember, misremember, conceal, reinterpret, inherit, and dispute.

Living memory belongs to agents and communities. It does not automatically become public world truth.

### 4. Institutional Inheritance

Past events survive through:

- laws;
- debts;
- ownership claims;
- safety standards;
- taboos;
- service obligations;
- emergency powers;
- professional credentials;
- diplomatic vows.

Institutional inheritance can remain active even when almost nobody remembers why it began.

### 5. Cultural Response

Songs, meals, clothing, jokes, architecture, festivals, insults, rituals, and art may carry history more durably than formal records.

Cultural response requires people, teaching, media, and material practice. It may drift across generations and jurisdictions.

## Player Entry Vectors

A packet must support at least three substantially different entry roles where the setting permits them.

Possible vectors include:

- resident with prior relationships;
- worker or apprentice;
- investigator or witness;
- repair contractor;
- elected or delegated representative;
- migrant or returning exile;
- trader or convoy operator;
- medic or caregiver;
- archivist or field researcher;
- security or rescue worker;
- corporate employee;
- dissident or informal-network member;
- person restored through disputed reconstitution.

Entry vectors alter knowledge, access, obligations, trust, and risk. They do not merely change introductory dialogue.

## Ordinary Life Requirement

Every major campaign must contain ordinary-life scenes that remain valuable outside crisis.

At minimum, a packet must identify:

- where people eat;
- where they rest;
- how children, elders, machines, animals, and visitors fit into daily space;
- one routine form of work;
- one form of play or art;
- one disagreement unrelated to the central crisis;
- one service the contested institution genuinely provides.

Without this layer, stakes become abstract and factions become slogans.

## Faction and NPC Initiative

No major faction waits indefinitely for the player.

Each faction or institution must have:

- a current objective;
- available resources;
- internal disagreement;
- prohibited or costly actions;
- at least one initiative it can perform without the player;
- a response to absence;
- a response to partial failure;
- a possible successor or splinter state.

Named NPCs must retain personal projects and relationships outside the campaign’s central conflict.

## Choice and Control

The player may influence history without becoming its sole author.

Campaigns should include:

- choices under incomplete knowledge;
- outcomes that require coalition or institutional capacity;
- consequences that cannot be repaired immediately;
- people who refuse the player;
- systems that continue after the player leaves;
- values that cannot be maximized simultaneously;
- situations where abstaining is itself consequential.

A campaign may include heroic action. It must not imply that social reality exists only to validate the protagonist.

## Resolution Vectors

A packet should offer several categories of resolution rather than a binary success state:

```text
repair
reform
replacement
mutualization
secession
containment
adaptation
truth recovery
restitution
evacuation
coexistence
refusal
managed failure
```

Some vectors may be incompatible. None should erase the material work required after the dramatic decision.

## Failure and Partial Success

Failure may produce:

- service degradation;
- displacement;
- institutional capture;
- loss of evidence;
- ecological damage;
- relationship rupture;
- a stronger informal network;
- a safer but less free system;
- a more legitimate but less capable system;
- postponed rather than resolved conflict.

Campaign failure does not mean content deletion. It creates a different revisit state.

## Worldline Variation

Authored history is a source graph, not a mandatory chronology.

A worldline variant may alter:

- which institution survived;
- who controls a site;
- which evidence remains;
- whether a catastrophe was prevented or transformed;
- whether a corporation reformed, fragmented, or became Null;
- whether a diaspora returned, integrated, or divided;
- which historical school dominates public memory.

Variation must preserve causal ancestry. Unique people, artifacts, assets, and source chains may not duplicate across a fork.

## Knowledge and Spoiler Boundaries

Campaign interfaces must distinguish:

```text
observed
reliably documented
claimed
inferred
predicted
unknown
withheld
classified
private
```

A content packet may know every authored possibility. The player-facing interface may expose only what the character could know.

## Violence and Nonviolent Capacity

Combat may be central when physical conflict is real, but every major campaign must identify noncombat capabilities that matter:

- logistics;
- evidence preservation;
- evacuation;
- repair;
- medical care;
- negotiation;
- labor organization;
- standards work;
- media and correction;
- ecological monitoring;
- witness protection;
- ritual and mourning.

Nonviolent play must change authoritative state, not merely award flavor text.

## Scope and Production Discipline

A campaign packet must declare:

- intended play duration;
- required systems;
- named-character budget;
- site and environment budget;
- dialogue and voice budget;
- simulation cadence;
- cinematic or bespoke-animation budget;
- localization burden;
- accessibility requirements;
- minimum viable version;
- cut order.

A packet with no cut line is not production-ready.

## Acceptance Invariants

A playable-history implementation must demonstrate:

1. the conflict has a cause before player arrival;
2. at least two sides provide real social value or protection;
3. ordinary life is visible;
4. people act without player prompting;
5. knowledge follows evidence and transmission;
6. material services do not vanish with faction defeat;
7. partial success remains playable;
8. player absence advances bounded state;
9. revisit presentation reflects physical and social consequences;
10. worldline branches preserve ancestry and uniqueness;
11. the campaign can degrade to deterministic authored content;
12. at least one outcome remains outside direct player control.

## Governing Principle

> **A Symtropy campaign is not a story told at the player. It is a historical system the player must inhabit.**
