# Player Experience and Session Rhythm Contract

## Owned Question

**What must Symtropy feel like minute by minute, hour by hour, and across a long-lived worldline?**

This document does not define every mechanic. It defines the experiential rhythm those mechanics must jointly produce.

## Core Thesis

A Symtropy session should move between:

```text
agency
tension
work
wonder
connection
consequence
rest
```

The game should not remain in one emotional register for too long.

A civilization sandbox needs intensity and contemplation, difficult work and effortless beauty, private mastery and collective consequence.

## The Seven Experience Modes

### 1. Agency

The player makes a meaningful choice or performs a skillful action.

Examples:

```text
choosing a route
landing a vehicle
placing a bridge span
reconfiguring a machine
committing to a tactical flank
selecting a research sample
negotiating a cargo exchange
```

Agency requires clear affordances and observable response.

### 2. Tension

The player faces danger, uncertainty, scarcity, time pressure, pursuit, or a difficult tradeoff.

Tension should sharpen attention, not merely interrupt work.

### 3. Work

The player performs sustained, legible activity toward a transformation.

Good work contains learning, judgment, coordination, or expressive craft.

Bad work is repetition whose outcome was already decided.

### 4. Wonder

The player encounters something whose value is not fully instrumental.

Wonder may reveal a horizon, organism, machine, city, weather system, memory, or cultural practice.

### 5. Connection

The player relates to another person, machine, animal, ecology, community, or team.

Connection includes cooperation, intimacy, rivalry, teaching, dependence, recognition, and shared celebration.

### 6. Consequence

The player sees that prior action changed a system, place, relationship, custom, route, threat, or historical record.

Consequences should appear in the world before they appear in summary screens whenever possible.

### 7. Rest and Play

The player chooses low-pressure activity, personal expression, observation, social presence, or experimentation.

Rest is not merely a pause between “real” activities. It is part of inhabiting a civilization.

## Session Shapes

A session may emphasize one shape while borrowing beats from others.

### Expedition Session

```text
prepare → travel → notice → investigate → risk → discover → extract or remain → return
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
explorer
scientist
scout
salvager
archivist
```

Required payoff:

A discovery changes knowledge, opportunity, route planning, or interpretation.

### Construction Session

```text
conceive → survey → source → transport → assemble → initialize → test → inhabit
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
builder
engineer
architect
programmer
logistician
```

Required payoff:

The construction changes capability, movement, safety, beauty, production, or social life.

### Convoy and Logistics Session

```text
contract → load → route → coordinate → encounter disruption → adapt → deliver → settle consequences
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
driver
pilot
trader
escort
logistician
```

Required payoff:

Cargo movement alters availability, relationships, construction, or regional resilience.

### Defense or Raid Session

```text
read threat → prepare → approach → contact → adapt → break objective → withdraw or hold → aftermath
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
fighter
medic
scout
engineer
commander
```

Required payoff:

The result changes threat posture, territory, resources, intelligence, or relationships.

### Research and Translation Session

```text
observe → form hypothesis → gather evidence → compare → test → revise → communicate → unlock possibility
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
scientist
ecologist
linguist
archivist
systems engineer
```

Required payoff:

Knowledge enables action or prevents category error. It may also reveal uncertainty rather than merely unlock a technology.

### Social and Cultural Session

```text
arrive → participate → express → relate → improvise → remember
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
artist
performer
host
teacher
friend
leader
```

Required payoff:

The player creates belonging, reputation, memory, style, or shared practice without needing an emergency.

### Civic Session

```text
issue becomes visible → positions form → evidence circulates → players influence → decision occurs → implementation is contested
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
organizer
leader
diplomat
advocate
witness
```

Required payoff:

A rule, allocation, authority, or relationship changes.

Civic sessions must be optional in frequency and varied in interaction. A player should not need to attend a meeting after every mission.

### Recovery Session

```text
loss → triage → trace → recover or reconstruct → accept scar → re-enter world
```

Primary fantasies:

```text
rescuer
medic
investigator
friend
survivor
```

Required payoff:

The world continues with a meaningful scar rather than becoming a simple reload.

## The Session Weave

A strong 60–90 minute session usually contains:

```text
1 primary session shape
1 secondary session shape
2–4 transitions between experience modes
1 clear world change
1 moment of unforced curiosity or delight
1 closure beat
```

Example:

```text
Convoy preparation        [agency + work]
Storm route diversion     [tension]
Unexpected glass forest   [wonder]
Vehicle repair with crew  [work + connection]
Ambush at old relay       [tension + agency]
Delivery opens clinic     [consequence]
Night market celebration  [rest + connection]
```

## Tempo Bands

### High Tempo

Combat, pursuit, collapse, emergency landing, breach, rescue.

Recommended duration:

Short enough that players can understand changing conditions; long enough to express skill.

High tempo should not be interrupted by dense lore, inventory sorting, or governance prompts.

### Active Tempo

Traversal, construction, salvage, operation, coordination, investigation.

This is the dominant band of most sessions.

### Reflective Tempo

Conversation, observation, planning, comparison, cultural participation, Chronicle review.

Reflective tempo should be chosen or earned, not imposed during urgent play.

### Rest Tempo

Customization, music, cooking, social presence, housing, scenic travel, collecting, pets, games.

Rest tempo must offer real enjoyment rather than functioning only as recovery chores.

## Attention Budget

The game may ask the player to actively track only a limited number of immediate concerns.

During high tempo:

```text
one primary objective
one immediate threat picture
one body or equipment condition
one team signal layer
```

During active work:

```text
one transformation goal
up to three relevant constraints
clear next physical action
optional deeper context
```

During reflective play:

More information may be available, but progressive disclosure remains mandatory.

## Friction Budget

Friction categories include:

```text
travel
inventory
maintenance
resource handling
permissions
coordination
interface navigation
repetition
waiting
```

Each activity must identify its **meaning-bearing friction**.

Examples:

```text
Convoy travel: route choice, terrain, weather, threat, vehicle condition.
Construction: staging, alignment, quality, safety, collaboration.
Civic action: evidence, coalition, tradeoff, implementation.
```

The remaining friction should be compressed, automated, delegated, or removed.

### Repetition Rule

After a player has demonstrated a routine several times, progression should allow:

```text
faster execution
better tools
batching
delegation
automation
specialization
```

Mastery should reduce tedium while preserving important decisions.

## Delight Density

Every authored region should contain recurring non-obligatory rewards:

```text
vistas
ambient behaviors
micro-stories
strange materials
music
wildlife
humor
small social invitations
beautiful machines
collectible knowledge
playful traversal
```

A practical target for early playtests:

```text
The player encounters a curiosity, expressive choice, social beat, or beautiful reveal at least every 10–15 minutes during ordinary play.
```

This is a test target, not a mandate for constant spectacle.

## Optional Depth Contract

A player should be able to complete core activities through an accessible surface.

Advanced surfaces provide:

```text
optimization
novel solutions
finer control
additional evidence
higher-quality outcomes
new forms of expression
```

They must not be required merely to understand what happened.

Examples:

```text
A player can route power physically.
A visual automation user can define control logic.
A terminal expert can inspect, script, and audit the system.
```

## Role Fairness in Cooperative Play

Every supported role should have:

```text
something useful to notice
something skillful to do
something consequential to decide
something visible to contribute
```

No role should become an assistant watching another player use the “real” interface.

### Shared Spotlight Rule

Long activities should create parallel work where practical.

Example construction:

```text
one player surveys and anchors
one transports material
one configures power or software
one manages safety or threat
```

Roles may overlap. They should not become rigid classes.

## Solo Viability

Solo play may use:

```text
NPC help
bounded automation
slower threat escalation
smaller cargo units
pause-capable planning surfaces
multi-stage objectives
```

Solo viability must preserve the same causal world rather than replacing it with unrelated simplified mechanics.

## Failure Continuation

Failure should usually produce one of:

```text
loss of time or position
resource damage
changed threat posture
injury
relationship cost
partial objective
new recovery activity
historical scar
```

Use hard reset when simulation integrity is lost or when a competitive mode requires it—not as the default answer to every mistake.

## Session Closure

A session should create a natural stopping point without requiring the world to stop.

Closure may be:

```text
returning to shelter
delivering cargo
finishing a construction stage
reaching a safe orbital state
committing a Chronicle event
sharing a meal or ritual
saving an expedition camp
handing responsibility to NPCs or automation
```

The player should understand:

```text
what changed
what remains unresolved
what opportunity is now visible
```

## Time-Horizon Promises

### First 10 Minutes

The player moves, touches a real system, makes one useful choice, and sees a horizon beyond the immediate task.

### First Hour

The player experiences traversal, danger or pressure, transformation, another living agent, and a persistent consequence.

### First 5 Hours

The player identifies a preferred role, uses a vehicle or major mobility transition, opens at least one regional possibility, and forms a relationship worth remembering.

### First 20 Hours

The player has created a durable capability, participated in a multi-system event, seen the basin change, and encountered a mystery larger than the region.

### Long Horizon

The player’s settlement, network, city, planet, or worldline reflects accumulated choices without invalidating new players or making all old spaces irrelevant.

## Experience Anti-Patterns

Reject or redesign activities where:

```text
meaning exists only in text after the action
one player performs while everyone else waits
a system creates chores faster than mastery can remove them
every consequence is a punishment
every calm moment becomes exposition
every cultural activity is only a stat buff
travel contains no decisions, discovery, or social possibility
combat ignores the world around it
success restores the world to an unchanged default
```

## Playtest Measures

Measure both behavior and interpretation.

### Behavioral

```text
time to first voluntary detour
number of distinct verbs used
percentage of session in menus
time without meaningful decision
role participation balance
frequency of repeated low-decision actions
number of world changes noticed without prompting
```

### Interpretive

Ask players:

```text
What did you choose?
What changed because of it?
What did another player contribute?
What made you curious?
What felt like work in a good way?
What felt like a chore?
What would you do next if no quest directed you?
```

## Acceptance Gates

A playable slice should not be called representative of Symtropy until:

```text
Players voluntarily explore beyond the objective.
At least three player fantasies report meaningful contribution.
Players can describe a causal world change without reading a summary.
High-tempo play is readable.
At least one calm activity is enjoyable without a reward multiplier.
The Field Deck does not dominate total attention.
Routine work has an evident path toward mastery or automation.
Most players can identify a natural stopping point and a compelling return goal.
```

## Final Rule

```text
A Symtropy session should leave the player with a story of what they did,
a place they changed, and a future they want to revisit.
```

# v0.2 Integration Addendum — Activity Closure and Revisit Rhythm

A satisfying session should normally contain:

```text
one legible opportunity or self-declared project
one change in method, risk, or understanding
one consequence that becomes visible before exit
one natural closure point
one reason to care about the world after closure
```

Activities may remain unresolved, but the player should understand the current state and next available commitment.

Revisits should not be mandatory cleanup tours. They should reveal new function, people, ecology, culture, conflict, or knowledge.
