# Firstlight Core Action, Danger, Combat, Rescue, and Destruction Contract

## Owned Question

**How does Firstlight prove that Symtropy is physically exciting, tactically expressive, and dangerous without making violence consequence-free or replacing action with ethical exposition?**

## Core Thesis

Symtropy must feel good before its consequences can matter.

The player should experience:

- responsive movement;
- readable tools and weapons;
- material impact;
- tense rescue;
- destructible and repairable environments;
- tactical coordination;
- meaningful withdrawal;
- aftermath that preserves rather than neutralizes the excitement.

> **Consequences follow exciting action. They do not excuse weak action.**

# 1. Opening Action Promise

Within the first three hours the player must encounter a sequence that proves:

- movement under pressure;
- physical interaction;
- environmental reading;
- rescue or protection;
- tactical choice;
- possible combat;
- persistent damage;
- a noncombat aftermath.

No single scripted set piece may fake all of these through cinematics.

# 2. Core Physical Verbs

The vertical slice must support production-quality versions of:

## Movement

- walk;
- run;
- crouch;
- mantle;
- ladder climb;
- controlled drop;
- brace;
- squeeze through;
- swim or wade in bounded water;
- drag or carry under constraint;
- enter and exit a compact vehicle.

## Manipulation

- grip;
- turn;
- pull;
- cut;
- pry;
- weld;
- fasten;
- connect;
- isolate;
- scan;
- stabilize;
- place;
- throw;
- operate mounted controls.

## Protection

- take cover;
- deploy barrier;
- close bulkhead;
- brace structure;
- suppress fire;
- shield another person;
- create safe route;
- extinguish;
- de-energize;
- retreat.

## Combat

- aim;
- fire;
- strike;
- shove;
- restrain where lawful and feasible;
- use nonlethal tools;
- use environmental systems;
- coordinate;
- surrender;
- accept surrender;
- disengage.

No verb requires universal animation perfection at first, but every included verb must have reliable feedback and state consequence.

# 3. Movement Feel

Movement should communicate an embodied adult in a material world.

Required properties:

- immediate input response;
- low camera latency;
- configurable head motion;
- weight without sluggishness;
- momentum that does not steal control;
- readable footing changes;
- surface-specific audio;
- accessible alternatives for repeated dexterity;
- reliable interaction while carrying or supporting another person;
- no arbitrary inability to step over small obstacles.

The game should not imitate military shooters when the current body, equipment, injury, gravity, or environment differs.

# 4. Tools and Weapons

## 4.1 Tools Are Physical Capabilities

A tool defines:

- contact geometry;
- operating range;
- energy or consumable needs;
- calibration;
- heat;
- contamination;
- wear;
- safety envelope;
- sound;
- compatible procedures.

Tools should not be colored keys used on matching objects.

## 4.2 Weapons Are Material Systems

Weapons require:

- ammunition or energy;
- heat or recoil management;
- maintenance;
- safe storage;
- legal and social context;
- distinct effect on materials and bodies;
- audio and visual signatures;
- evidence and custody.

The slice may include:

- one compact firearm or projectile weapon;
- one industrial or directed-energy defensive tool;
- one less-lethal option;
- improvised environmental defenses;
- companion capabilities.

Do not build a large weapon catalogue before one weapon feels excellent.

# 5. Damage Model

Damage is layered.

## People

Possible states include:

- shock;
- bleeding;
- fracture;
- burn;
- inhalation injury;
- pain;
- impaired movement;
- sensory impairment;
- unconsciousness;
- death.

The model must remain legible without fetishizing anatomy.

## Machines

Possible states include:

- power loss;
- control damage;
- sensor failure;
- actuator failure;
- thermal runaway;
- seal breach;
- structural distortion;
- corrupted authority;
- partial autonomous operation.

## Structures

Possible states include:

- superficial damage;
- loss of cover;
- deformation;
- severed utility;
- fire;
- local collapse;
- progressive instability;
- blocked or opened route.

## Ecology

Possible states include:

- contamination;
- sediment disturbance;
- habitat destruction;
- fire;
- noise and migration disruption;
- water-flow change.

# 6. Destruction Principles

## 6.1 Selective, Not Universal

Firstlight does not need every object voxelized.

Destructible classes should include:

- doors and access panels;
- light partitions;
- barriers;
- pipes and cables;
- machinery components;
- supports in authored structural systems;
- vegetation and wetland edges where meaningful;
- vehicles and cargo;
- cover objects;
- specific floors or walls in the Lower Works.

## 6.2 Destruction Creates State

Destruction may:

- open a route;
- remove cover;
- release water, gas, heat, or contamination;
- disable a service;
- create evidence;
- injure people;
- cause later repair work;
- alter ownership or liability;
- change how NPCs use a place.

## 6.3 Repair Is Not Reset

Repairs preserve:

- changed materials;
- visible patches;
- altered tolerances;
- cost;
- downtime;
- records;
- blame or pride;
- future failure modes.

# 7. Rescue

Rescue is a primary action fantasy, not a context prompt.

A rescue can require:

- locating a person;
- assessing hazards;
- communicating;
- isolating power;
- moving debris;
- stabilizing injury;
- carrying or dragging;
- route planning;
- calling support;
- accepting that another person must be prioritized.

The player may not be able to save everyone.

The game must distinguish:

- failed attempt;
- unsafe attempt;
- delayed rescue;
- successful extraction with injury;
- successful rescue because another character acted elsewhere.

# 8. Combat Philosophy

Combat should be:

- dangerous;
- readable;
- fast enough to create excitement;
- slow enough for judgment;
- shaped by terrain and systems;
- compatible with retreat;
- affected by training and equipment;
- capable of harming infrastructure and bystanders;
- followed by custody, care, repair, and memory where appropriate.

Combat should not be:

- a mandatory solution to every hostile state;
- an abstract damage-per-second race;
- immune to social and legal context;
- perfectly clean because the player used a nominally nonlethal tool;
- interrupted constantly by moral commentary.

# 9. Hostile Behavior

Hostile actors have objectives beyond “attack the player.”

They may seek to:

- steal equipment;
- seize medicine;
- open a route;
- delay responders;
- rescue their own people;
- destroy evidence;
- enforce a claim;
- escape;
- sabotage infrastructure;
- secure food or shelter;
- follow a broken machine procedure.

Hostile state changes through:

- casualties;
- objective progress;
- route closure;
- surrender;
- communication;
- leader loss;
- environmental danger;
- evidence that their premise is wrong;
- arrival of other factions.

# 10. Firstlight Threat Set

The vertical slice should prove a small but varied ecology of danger.

## Industrial Failure

- live power;
- floodwater;
- structural instability;
- fire;
- toxic release;
- moving machinery.

## Human or Human-Adjacent Hostility

One group with a credible material objective and internal difference.

## Null Procedure

A machine or institutional system continuing an expired protective, custody, or evacuation logic.

## Ecological Danger

A local animal, swarm, pathogen risk, or environmental process that is not evil.

## Weather

Storm, heat, low visibility, or flood conditions that alter every other threat.

# 11. Companion Coordination

Companions contribute through:

- covering routes;
- operating controls;
- stabilizing people;
- carrying equipment;
- reading profession-specific cues;
- communicating with locals;
- challenging unsafe action;
- pursuing another rescue priority;
- refusing unlawful or suicidal orders.

The player issues requests and coordination calls, not omnipotent squad commands.

# 12. IRIS During Danger

IRIS should:

- prioritize immediate hazards;
- communicate confidence;
- reduce verbal density;
- avoid repeating visible facts;
- distinguish sensor loss from safety;
- support accessibility;
- record evidence when authorized;
- remain silent during concentration when configured.

Example:

> “Water is rising. The west cable remains energized. I cannot confirm the lower room is empty.”

Not:

> “Warning! Objective updated! Rescue all civilians!”

# 13. Failure and Recovery

Possible failure consequences include:

- injury;
- lost equipment;
- damaged route;
- escaped hostile actors;
- incomplete evidence;
- public criticism;
- companion distrust of a method;
- delayed project;
- changed institutional policy;
- death and source-chain recovery.

Reload remains available according to player settings, but the authored path should support continued play after imperfect outcomes.

# 14. Action Pacing

The opening requires:

- short danger within three hours;
- one optional medium expedition before hour fifteen;
- one major event between hours twenty and thirty;
- quiet recovery after each;
- no assumption that action players want constant combat.

# 15. Accessibility

Action systems must support:

- aim assistance;
- input remapping;
- hold/toggle options;
- timing-window adjustment;
- motion reduction;
- haptic, visual, and audio alternatives;
- simplified carry and stabilization interactions;
- pause or slow-time where compatible with multiplayer mode;
- content controls for injury presentation.

Assistance modifies interface and execution burden, not causal stakes.

# 16. Proof Metrics

The action slice passes when players report and demonstrate:

- satisfying movement;
- clear material impact;
- readable threats;
- at least two viable approaches;
- understanding of why damage occurred;
- willingness to retreat when appropriate;
- memory of someone protected or lost;
- awareness that the place remained changed;
- desire to replay because the action itself was enjoyable.

# 17. Failure Conditions

The action proof fails if:

- combat feels like a weaker version of another shooter;
- rescue is a progress bar;
- destruction is cosmetic;
- every danger becomes a moral lecture;
- companions wait for orders while people die;
- nonlethal tools are magic morality buttons;
- aftermath is only dialogue;
- IRIS narrates every threat;
- failure requires reload to remain interesting;
- physical interaction has high latency or unclear contact.

## Contract Maxim

> **Let the player move fast, act decisively, and feel impact. Then let the world remember exactly what that action touched.**
