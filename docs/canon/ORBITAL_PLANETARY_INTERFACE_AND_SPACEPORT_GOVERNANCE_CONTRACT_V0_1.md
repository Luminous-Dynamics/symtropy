# Orbital-Planetary Interface and Spaceport Governance Contract

## Owned Question

**How do planets, orbital habitats, fleets, launch settlements, and travelers share access to orbit when every launch, landing, cargo transfer, rescue, quarantine, and debris decision creates physical and political consequences?**

## Core Thesis

A spaceport is not a fast-travel terminal.

It is the physical and legal interface between two different civilizations:

```text
surface society
orbital society
```

The surface owns land, atmosphere, communities, ecological exposure, and much of the launch infrastructure.

Orbit controls scarce trajectories, docks, rescue windows, debris risk, life-support intake, and access to the wider system.

Neither side may treat the other as merely a supplier or destination.

```text
A launch window is a shared atmospheric event.
A dock slot is a life-support commitment.
A rescue is a jurisdictional act.
A cargo manifest can carry an ecology.
```

## Prime Directives

1. **No abstract launch.** Vehicles, fuel or energy, weather, range safety, crew, cargo, trajectory, and destination capacity must exist.
2. **No surface sacrifice zone by default.** Noise, plume, debris, contamination, displacement, and water use remain visible.
3. **No orbital ownership of the sky.** Orbital traffic authority coordinates safety; it does not automatically own planetary access.
4. **No planetary monopoly on departure.** Surface states cannot make exit impossible merely by controlling launch infrastructure.
5. **Rescue precedes ordinary commercial priority.** Rescue still has physical constraints, risk, and later accountability.
6. **Air, water, pressure, and mass are political.** Every arrival imposes a habitat burden.
7. **Quarantine must be evidence-bound and appealable.** Unknown biology or machine state may justify caution, not indefinite disappearance.
8. **Heritage and science matter.** Launch plumes, mining, and construction can damage irreplaceable sites and observations.
9. **Dock labor has rights.** The people who load, clean, inspect, repair, and receive ships are not invisible automation.
10. **Space access must create play beyond bureaucracy.** Piloting, repair, cargo, rescue, exploration, construction, diplomacy, and social life remain central.

# 1. Interface Institutions

A mature interface may contain:

```text
surface launch authority
spaceport operator
orbital traffic coordination
range safety
customs and biosecurity
rescue coordination
habitat intake authority
dock labor institutions
planetary environmental review
archive and identity services
```

These may be one institution in a small frontier settlement or many institutions in a mature system. Their authority remains decomposed.

# 2. Access Rights

Separate rights include:

```text
right to approach
right to request launch
right to request landing
right to dock
right to refuel or recharge
right to emergency shelter
right to transit cargo
right to immigrate
right to inspect
right to quarantine
right to refuse service
right to leave
```

A person may have a right to emergency docking without a right to permanent residence.

A habitat may refuse hazardous cargo while still owing rescue to the crew.

# 3. Launch Allocation

Launch resources include:

```text
pad or rail time
range safety
energy or propellant
weather window
tracking
crew support
cargo handling
orbital slot
destination acceptance
```

Allocation methods may include:

```text
public schedule
auction
mission priority
scientific reservation
mutual-aid treaty
emergency requisition
military control
community lottery
```

The method is political and creates different failures.

A public schedule can be slow. An auction can exclude refugees. Emergency priority can become permanent. Military control can protect traffic while suppressing exit.

# 4. Orbital Traffic

Traffic coordination tracks:

```text
trajectories
conjunction risk
debris
communications
navigation standards
vehicle capability
abort options
rescue coverage
```

Coordination messages are recommendations or binding orders according to scoped authority.

The runtime must distinguish:

```text
unsafe trajectory
unrecognized vehicle
politically denied vehicle
unknown vehicle
quarantine concern
communication failure
```

# 5. Surface Environmental Burden

Launch and landing can produce:

```text
noise
heat
plume chemistry
dust
water consumption
wildlife disruption
fall zones
debris
land seizure
infrastructure strain
```

Spaceport governance must define who monitors, who is compensated, who may halt operations, and how cumulative burden is limited.

A spaceport can enrich a region while turning neighboring communities into a sacrifice zone.

# 6. Dock and Habitat Capacity

An orbital destination must account for:

```text
pressure volume
oxygen and atmosphere processing
water
food
waste
radiation shelter
medical capacity
sleeping space
docking hardware
maintenance labor
social intake capacity
```

Arrival approval is not a symbolic immigration flag. It reserves actual capability.

# 7. Cargo and Customs

Surface-orbit cargo may include:

```text
people
water
food
industrial material
fuel
biological samples
species
body modifications
machine cores
archives
cultural artifacts
weapons
alien technology
```

Cargo requires custody, mass accounting, hazard classification, provenance, and destination acceptance.

Biosecurity must account for two-way contamination. Earth organisms may threaten alien or closed habitats; orbital microbes may threaten surface ecologies.

# 8. Rescue Law

Rescue situations include:

```text
vehicle failure
missed orbit
decompression
radiation event
debris strike
launch abort
stranded surface crew
lost communication
medical emergency
```

A rescue duty defines:

```text
who must respond
within what risk limits
what costs may be recovered
what evidence is preserved
what happens to cargo and passengers afterward
what quarantine applies
```

No rescued person becomes property or debt-bound labor because of rescue cost.

# 9. Quarantine and Contact

Quarantine states:

```text
observation
restricted contact
controlled transfer
medical isolation
ecological isolation
machine-network isolation
full refusal with remote aid
```

Every state requires evidence, review, humane conditions, communication, and appeal where cognitively possible.

Unknown alien agency may require noncontact protocols. The system must not treat uncertainty as permission for sterilization.

# 10. Labor and Automation

Spaceports and docks depend on:

```text
loaders
inspectors
mechanics
traffic coordinators
medics
cleaning crews
life-support workers
rescue crews
translators
archive and identity staff
```

Automation changes labor but does not erase responsibility.

Workers may organize around:

```text
hazard pay
radiation exposure
shift limits
rescue obligations
automation displacement
surveillance
right to refuse unsafe launch
```

# 11. Exit and Migration

Leaving a planet may be restricted by physics, capacity, quarantine, criminal process, debt, or political control.

Legitimate restrictions are narrow, evidence-bound, reviewable, and time-limited.

Illegitimate restrictions include:

```text
debt bondage
employer exit veto
identity confiscation
political loyalty tests
hereditary surface status
indefinite emergency closure
```

Orbital societies likewise cannot expel people into lethal transit without destination and life-support guarantees.

# 12. Planetary-Orbital Representation

A planetary federation and orbital habitats may create shared forums for:

```text
traffic
rescue
debris
environmental burden
migration
customs
planetary defense
first contact
standards
```

Representation cannot be based only on population. An orbital habitat with few people may control critical rescue or debris infrastructure; a surface region may carry most launch harm.

# 13. Conflict and Blockade

Spaceports are strategic targets.

Possible conflict states:

```text
inspection dispute
commercial denial
sanctions
military blockade
launch suppression
orbital bombardment threat
rescue corridor
neutral port
```

The war contract governs force. The interface contract preserves humanitarian passage, rescue, civilian exit, and evidence.

A blockade that prevents food or life-support supplies produces civilian consequences and political memory.

# 14. Spaceport Growth

Spaceports create settlement change:

```text
housing
labor migration
markets
pollution
infrastructure
class divisions
international districts
religious and cultural exchange
smuggling
technical education
```

A mature spaceport is a city, not merely a pad.

# 15. Player Activities

```text

escort a quarantine transfer
mediate dockworker action

```

# 16. Seedworks and Progression Boundary

Seedworks foreshadows the interface through:

```text
visible orbital wreckage
one functioning or ruined tracking station
one cargo or identity link to orbit
one offworld NPC or returned origin
one launch-related environmental scar
```

Full operational spaceport play belongs to later progression.

# 17. Acceptance Tests

- launch consumes real vehicle, energy, crew, and schedule capacity;
- destination acceptance reserves actual life-support capacity;
- rescue can override ordinary priority without erasing later review;
- quarantine has evidence, humane conditions, communication, and expiry review;
- launch burden appears in nearby ecology and communities;
- dock labor can stop unsafe operations through valid procedure or direct action;
- cargo remains conserved through surface-orbit LOD transitions;
- a political denial is not mislabeled as a safety denial;
- planetary and orbital authorities can disagree without all traffic becoming impossible;
- players can revisit a growing spaceport and read its labor, ecological, cultural, and political history.

## Final Line

```text
A planet and its orbit become one civilization only when departure, arrival,
rescue, and burden are governed as shared acts rather than separate markets.
```
