# Mobile Continuance Base, Home, Vehicle, and Crew Sovereignty Contract

## Owned Question

**How can the player's first enduring base be simultaneously a satisfying vehicle, workshop, home, survivor community, and historical object without turning its occupants into inventory or allowing one moving platform to replace every settlement?**

## Core Thesis

The Continuance Crawler is not a reward chest with wheels.

It is a damaged public vehicle repurposed during the Breaking of Firstlight and inhabited by people who have rights, needs, projects, memories, disagreements, and claims.

> **The player may become steward, operator, owner of particular modules, elected coordinator, debtor, or passenger. They do not automatically own the people, institutions, records, or public assets carried aboard.**

# 1. Why the Mobile Base Exists

The crawler provides continuity after the loss of a stable home.

It supports:

- travel;
- shelter;
- storage;
- workshop practice;
- medical support;
- water and sanitation;
- power;
- communication;
- survivor relationships;
- mobile projects;
- camp deployment;
- exploration;
- later embarkation onto larger planetary and space transport.

Its limitations create reasons to visit, negotiate with, depend upon, and eventually help found settlements.

# 2. Origin and Custody

The first crawler begins as one of:

- municipal emergency vehicle;
- transit maintenance carrier;
- public-works crawler;
- cooperative fabrication rig;
- private vehicle under repossession;
- mixed salvage assembled from several lawful claims.

The opening must record:

- legal owner or owners;
- operator authority;
- public-service obligations;
- debts;
- modifications;
- stolen or disputed components;
- survivor claims;
- emergency authority;
- title and custody evidence.

The player may begin with operational control without full property ownership.

Later disputes may involve:

- a municipality demanding return;
- a cooperative claiming worker ownership;
- a corporation asserting a lien;
- survivors voting against sale;
- a companion claiming a module they built;
- a public archive that cannot be privatized;
- a medical bay subject to care obligations;
- a machine person whose body is integrated into vehicle systems.

# 3. Crew and Resident Rights

Every resident aboard retains:

- bodily autonomy;
- movement and exit rights subject to immediate safety;
- privacy appropriate to available space;
- consent regarding care and modification;
- property;
- communication rights;
- political voice in shared conditions;
- right to refuse unlawful or unsafe work;
- right to seek another household or convoy;
- protection from being jettisoned as an optimization decision;
- recognition of dependencies the base creates.

The game must never phrase a resident as `+1 medic module` or `crew bonus`.

Their skills expand possible action through actual work, availability, trust, equipment, and authority.

# 4. Governance Modes

The crawler may develop several legitimate governance forms.

## 4.1 Emergency Stewardship

A bounded temporary coordinator holds declared authority during immediate evacuation.

## 4.2 Household Consensus

Residents negotiate shared space, route, supplies, and work through household procedures.

## 4.3 Cooperative Crew

Workers and residents hold membership shares, elect coordinators, and assign shifts.

## 4.4 Public-Service Charter

The crawler remains a mobile clinic, repair service, archive, or relief unit with obligations beyond its residents.

## 4.5 Private Expedition

Specific owners fund and operate the vehicle under contracts with crew and passengers.

## 4.6 Convoy Compact

Several vehicles coordinate route, defense, rescue, supply, and camp duties without merging ownership.

The player may influence or hold office in these forms. No mode grants permanent unilateral authority.

# 5. Base Is Not Settlement

A mobile base can deploy temporary systems but does not automatically create:

- legitimate land claims;
- civic authority;
- permanent housing rights;
- ecological permission;
- local resource ownership;
- public jurisdiction;
- citizenship.

When the crawler arrives somewhere, it becomes a visitor, customer, refugee vehicle, public-service unit, contractor, occupier, or invited partner according to actual relationships.

# 6. Physical Constraints

Every module consumes and produces real constraints:

- mass;
- volume;
- center of mass;
- axle or suspension load;
- power;
- heat;
- water;
- waste;
- maintenance;
- noise;
- vibration;
- crew attention;
- route clearance;
- spare parts;
- fire and contamination zones.

No upgrade is purely additive.

Examples:

- armor protects occupants but damages roads and increases braking distance;
- a larger clinic displaces bunks and needs privacy and sterilization;
- a greenhouse preserves culture and food but consumes water and heat rejection;
- a heavy workshop enables repair but limits bridge access;
- additional batteries improve silent operation but create thermal and fire risk;
- a powerful transmitter reveals location;
- a memorial compartment consumes space yet may be central to survivor identity.

# 7. Initial Continuance Crawler

The reference chassis contains:

- cockpit and navigation station;
- propulsion and power bay;
- compact water and sanitation system;
- shared sleeping space;
- medical berth;
- small workshop;
- cargo deck;
- Field Deck and IRIS node;
- archive or evidence locker;
- exterior tool mounts;
- roof access;
- one damaged or unfinished module.

The opening load determines which systems are functional, crowded, absent, or improvised.

# 8. Home Development

The crawler becomes home through use, not a decoration score.

Home state may emerge from:

- assigned or negotiated sleeping places;
- cooking practices;
- lighting;
- sound;
- privacy partitions;
- memorials;
- repairs;
- personal objects;
- recurring arguments;
- shared maintenance;
- rituals of arrival and departure;
- who holds keys;
- who can invite guests;
- where children, animals, or machine bodies can safely exist.

Decoration is supported, but residents may move, reject, borrow, repair, or reinterpret shared objects.

# 9. Personal Space

The player should receive some bounded personal space early, such as:

- bunk alcove;
- tool cabinet;
- seat and shelf;
- small room;
- exterior locker;
- workstation.

The space may be altered through:

- objects;
- materials;
- photographs or records;
- lighting;
- sound;
- body-support equipment;
- religious or cultural practice;
- tools;
- clothing;
- trophies and failures.

The player may not appropriate another resident's space without consequence.

# 10. Module Ownership

The crawler is composed of modules with potentially different claims.

Examples:

- municipal chassis;
- player-owned tool bench;
- cooperative battery bank;
- public medical bay;
- companion-owned drone rack;
- household greenhouse;
- protected archive;
- machine-integrated processor;
- rented cargo module.

A module can be removed, transferred, damaged, inherited, seized, or jointly governed according to evidence and agreements.

# 11. Work and Shifts

Operating the crawler requires real labor:

- driving;
- navigation;
- maintenance;
- energy management;
- water and sanitation;
- cooking;
- cleaning;
- medical support;
- communications;
- watch;
- cargo restraint;
- route scouting;
- diplomacy;
- childcare and dependent care.

Automation may reduce work but has limits, maintenance needs, authority, and possible personhood implications.

No resident is permanently available.

# 12. Conflict Aboard

Conflict may arise from:

- route choice;
- risk tolerance;
- cargo priority;
- privacy;
- work distribution;
- rationing;
- medical isolation;
- debt;
- guest admission;
- weapons;
- institutional mission;
- personal relationships;
- whether to return to Firstlight;
- whether the crawler should become private property or public service.

Resolution forms include:

- conversation;
- vote;
- work refusal;
- mediation;
- departure;
- module separation;
- charter change;
- emergency authority;
- legal adjudication;
- unresolved coexistence.

# 13. Mobile Progression Stages

## 13.1 Evacuation Crawler

Overloaded, damaged, improvised, and focused on immediate survival.

## 13.2 Expedition Home

Repaired enough for planned travel, professional work, and personal space.

## 13.3 Modular Convoy

Additional vehicles and trailers join through independent ownership and a convoy compact.

## 13.4 Deployable Mobile Settlement

The convoy can temporarily establish water, power, clinic, workshops, markets, communications, and habitation.

## 13.5 Planetary Transport Component

Modules can embark on trains, ships, heavy airlift, orbital carriers, or landing craft.

## 13.6 Seed or Spacefaring Continuity

Selected modules, practices, people, and objects may become part of a spacecraft or seed vessel.

The original crawler core should remain visible or archivally traceable. Progression must not erase history through a clean replacement screen.

# 14. Vehicles Beyond the Crawler

The crawler is not the only mobile base design.

Later forms include:

- tracked or wheeled continuance rigs;
- walking heavy carriers;
- river and ocean flotillas;
- rail-based mobile settlements;
- heavy airships;
- subterranean boring habitats;
- ice crawlers;
- alien symbiotic caravans;
- machine migratory bodies;
- orbital transfer habitats;
- modular landing settlements.

Each form must express terrain, resource, cultural, labor, and governance differences.

# 15. Destruction and Loss

The base may be:

- damaged;
- immobilized;
- boarded;
- partially burned;
- contaminated;
- stripped;
- abandoned;
- divided;
- sold;
- repossessed;
- inherited;
- transformed into a permanent structure.

The game must preserve resident continuity and property claims through these changes.

Destroying the base should be possible but not casually resettable.

# 16. Combat and Defense

Defensive systems may include:

- armor;
- sensors;
- smoke and concealment;
- drones;
- electronic countermeasures;
- nonlethal tools;
- weapons;
- hardened compartments;
- emergency mobility.

Weapons impose:

- mass;
- ammunition;
- legal constraints;
- maintenance;
- escalation;
- resident consent or conflict;
- civilian risk;
- target-identification requirements.

A mobile home must never become combat-immune because the player sleeps there.

# 17. Economy

The crawler can earn or consume resources through:

- repair services;
- medical work;
- transport;
- salvage;
- research;
- trade;
- cultural performance;
- public contracts;
- relief work;
- escort;
- exploration.

Residents and institutions may have separate accounts and claims.

The player account does not receive all revenue by default.

# 18. Return and Memory

The crawler carries material memory of Firstlight:

- bent panels;
- signs;
- tools;
- archived voices;
- seeds;
- recipes;
- music;
- damage;
- repair styles;
- names;
- disputes over what deserves preservation.

When the player returns to Firstlight, the crawler is itself evidence of what left.

# 19. Accessibility

The base must support:

- multiple navigation interfaces;
- route and maintenance assistance;
- configurable physical interaction burden;
- visual, auditory, and haptic diagnostics;
- body and mobility accommodations;
- safe rest;
- adjustable camera and vehicle motion;
- clear spatial organization;
- IRIS memory support;
- reduced clutter modes without deleting physical constraints.

# 20. Promotion Gates

The mobile-base design passes only if:

- players identify it as home, not inventory;
- residents are remembered as occupants with agency;
- upgrades create understandable tradeoffs;
- spatial layout is learnable;
- damage changes operation;
- legal and social custody remain legible;
- the crawler creates reasons to engage settlements rather than replacing them;
- convoy play preserves independent vehicles and people;
- personal expression is visible;
- at least one object from the opening remains emotionally salient after twenty hours.

## Contract Maxim

> **A mobile base is a home because lives accumulate inside it, a vehicle because matter limits it, and a polity because shared survival never makes one occupant the owner of everyone else.**
