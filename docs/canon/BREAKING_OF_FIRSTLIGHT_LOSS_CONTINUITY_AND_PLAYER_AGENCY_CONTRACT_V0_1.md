# Breaking of Firstlight Loss, Continuity, and Player Agency Contract

## Owned Question

**How may Symtropy destroy the player's first stable home without making attachment feel fraudulent, reducing unique residents to tutorial casualties, or proving that early choices never mattered?**

## Core Thesis

Firstlight is not destroyed so the game may discard its tutorial.

Firstlight is broken so that the player enters the larger world carrying specific people, objects, debts, practices, evidence, wounds, and unfinished histories.

> **The player cannot save Firstlight as a stable civic whole. The player can materially alter who escapes, what survives, what becomes known, what remains recoverable, and what kind of community leaves the ruins.**

The catastrophe is a fixed historical pressure with variable causal expression. Its occurrence is part of the campaign premise. Its human, ecological, institutional, and material outcome is not predetermined.

# 1. The Opening Promise

Within roughly two hours, the player must:

- inhabit a unique Firstlight worldline;
- learn enough of the place to recognize its ordinary life;
- form at least one unforced preference about a person, place, practice, or object;
- become useful through embodied action;
- glimpse a wider cosmic mystery;
- make one personal creation or modification;
- experience escalating warnings;
- act during a systemic evacuation;
- make constrained loading and rescue decisions;
- depart in a mobile continuance base;
- retain clear reasons to return, search, investigate, rebuild, or grieve.

The title reveal may occur after departure, when the player understands that the prologue was already part of the persistent world.

# 2. What "Destroyed" Means

`Destroyed` is not a single Boolean.

The Breaking may render Firstlight:

- unable to sustain its previous population;
- physically fragmented;
- partially flooded, burned, collapsed, quarantined, occupied, displaced, or metric-unstable;
- cut off from normal transport;
- politically dissolved;
- ecologically transformed;
- legally contested;
- unsafe for immediate return;
- dependent on outside rescue;
- reduced to distributed enclaves;
- reconstituted later under a different name and charter.

The following must remain possible:

- ruins with recoverable interiors;
- surviving districts;
- isolated residents;
- autonomous machines following last lawful instructions;
- archives with incomplete custody;
- hostile salvage and occupation;
- memorialization and misinformation;
- later resettlement;
- return campaigns;
- source-chain recovery;
- discovery that the catastrophe's public explanation was incomplete.

The event ends Firstlight's role as the player's secure starting settlement. It does not erase Firstlight from the simulation.

# 3. People Are Not Tutorial Fuel

No generated resident exists only to die for emotional motivation.

Every named or high-salience resident must have, before the Breaking:

- a present-tense life;
- at least one relationship not involving the player;
- a practical obligation;
- a preference or boundary;
- an intended future;
- a plausible evacuation behavior;
- a state transition if separated from the player.

The cast must include people who:

- evacuate with the player;
- evacuate by another route;
- refuse evacuation for understandable reasons;
- remain because they are performing essential work;
- are missing;
- are confirmed dead;
- are injured or disabled by the event;
- survive and later disagree about what happened;
- use the disaster to gain power;
- help others without becoming companions;
- leave Firstlight and never rejoin the player.

A generated person may die, but death must arise from the event state, their position, choices, available support, and player action—not from a hidden quota requiring a sad outcome.

# 4. Survivor State Vocabulary

The authoritative runtime must distinguish at least:

## 4.1 Confirmed Safe

The player or trusted evidence confirms arrival at a viable refuge.

## 4.2 Departed on Another Route

The person left Firstlight, but their later location may be unknown.

## 4.3 Remaining by Choice

The person knowingly stayed because of duty, kinship, political resistance, faith, property, care, or identity.

## 4.4 Unable to Evacuate

The person lacked transport, access, mobility support, documentation, medical stability, or sufficient warning.

This state must expose institutional failure rather than implying personal fault.

## 4.5 Missing

No authoritative evidence resolves their outcome.

Missing is not secretly equivalent to dead.

## 4.6 Confirmed Dead

Death has adequate evidence under the source-chain and public-record systems.

## 4.7 Recovery Possible

Biological death occurred, but lawful reconstitution may remain technically possible, disputed, delayed, partial, or controlled by another institution.

## 4.8 Forked or Duplicated Claim

A machine, uploaded continuity, or reconstituted person produces more than one legitimate claimant. The game must not collapse them into one collectible identity.

# 5. Fixed Event, Variable Outcome

The Breaking may be inevitable at campaign start, but the following must vary:

- initial warning quality;
- whether authorities act early;
- which infrastructure fails first;
- which routes remain viable;
- weather and ecological amplification;
- hostile intervention;
- public trust;
- companion availability;
- vehicle condition;
- medical capacity;
- archive custody;
- destination capacity;
- whether the player identified the hidden metric anomaly;
- which neighborhoods believe the player;
- what cargo can be loaded;
- who receives the final available seats;
- what evidence survives.

The player must be able to produce outcomes that are materially better or worse without ever receiving a perfect-save solution.

# 6. Causal Layers of the Breaking

The catastrophe must have at least four interacting layers.

## 6.1 Physical Trigger

Examples include:

- extreme storm and watershed overload;
- power-grid resonance;
- structural cascade;
- subsurface failure;
- industrial release;
- hostile attack;
- Null interference;
- metric-field instability.

## 6.2 Hidden Amplifier

The physical trigger interacts with something not fully understood:

- a deep-time alien structure;
- a dormant Atlas scar;
- an abandoned human metric experiment;
- incompatible field geometries;
- an ecological process misclassified as inert;
- a machine continuity system operating under obsolete authority.

## 6.3 Institutional Failure

At least one authority contributes through:

- suppression;
- delay;
- conflicting jurisdiction;
- proprietary secrecy;
- underfunded maintenance;
- evacuation discrimination;
- false reassurance;
- seizure of transport;
- broken custody;
- fear of political embarrassment.

## 6.4 Irreversible Transition

The combined system crosses a threshold after which Firstlight cannot continue as before.

The player should be able to recognize the threshold retrospectively, even if they could not prevent it.

# 7. Player Agency Requirements

During the Breaking, player action must affect at least five persistent categories.

## 7.1 Human Continuity

Who escapes, who receives care, who remains connected, and who becomes separated.

## 7.2 Material Continuity

Which tools, medicines, cultures, archives, components, vehicles, and personal objects leave Firstlight.

## 7.3 Institutional Continuity

Which charters, credentials, records, authority tokens, public-service procedures, and evidence chains remain usable.

## 7.4 Cultural Continuity

Which songs, foods, rituals, jokes, craft practices, stories, and memorial objects are carried forward.

## 7.5 Ecological Continuity

Which seeds, microbes, water cultures, animals, habitat data, and repair knowledge survive.

## 7.6 Epistemic Continuity

What is known about the cause, what remains disputed, and who controls the surviving evidence.

# 8. No Perfect Evacuation

The player cannot carry everyone and everything.

This limitation must arise from legible constraints:

- mass;
- volume;
- seats;
- medical stability;
- route time;
- power;
- cooling;
- terrain;
- access;
- loading labor;
- authority;
- destination capacity.

The game must not create a hidden correct inventory puzzle.

Different viable continuities include:

- saving more people with fewer tools;
- carrying a clinic at the expense of industrial capacity;
- preserving records but losing food cultures;
- taking a dangerous specialist who refuses subordinate status;
- preserving a machine body or preserving several biological passengers;
- leaving with minimal cargo to keep a route open for another convoy.

The player should understand why a choice mattered, even when later consequences are surprising.

# 9. Emotional Guardrails

The Breaking must not rely on:

- a montage of every named resident dying;
- killing the first friendly character solely for motivation;
- forced pet death;
- unskippable suffering used as prestige;
- graphic imagery beyond selected content settings;
- accusing the player of abandonment for logging off;
- faking an avoidable death after the player succeeded;
- secretly negating rescue decisions to preserve a sequel plot;
- making all survivors worship the player;
- making every survivor blame the player.

Grief may be intense. Manipulation may not be dishonest.

# 10. Replay and Worldline Variation

Separate worldlines may differ in:

- cast identities;
- household networks;
- district names;
- initial infrastructure state;
- weather;
- institutional leadership;
- anomaly expression;
- evacuation routes;
- available mobile-base chassis;
- rival involvement;
- survivor outcomes;
- final Firstlight state.

The event must remain recognizable enough for production testing while being personal enough that players do not all report the same dead friend and identical escape.

# 11. Multiplayer

Players sharing one worldline share:

- one cast;
- one Firstlight state;
- one catastrophe timeline;
- one survivor ledger;
- one set of physical objects;
- one mobile-base capacity envelope.

Players may disagree and split effort.

No player privately sees a different generated resident occupying the same social role.

If players create separate worldlines, their casts and outcomes may diverge. Assets and people may not be transferred between branches.

# 12. Return Is Mandatory as a Possibility, Not a Schedule

The larger game must preserve at least one later route back toward Firstlight.

Return may reveal:

- a surviving enclave;
- an occupied ruin;
- a rebuilt settlement;
- a closed quarantine zone;
- an ecological transformation;
- a propaganda monument;
- a machine-maintained ghost district;
- a functioning community that does not want the player to reclaim authority;
- evidence that the public cause was incomplete.

The player must never be told that returning is the only morally valid path.

Some players will build a new life elsewhere. That choice is legitimate.

# 13. Production Gates

The Breaking may not be promoted unless playtests show:

- players formed attachment before the catastrophe;
- at least two residents were remembered after seven days;
- players understood that outcomes varied with action;
- players did not describe the first hour as fake because the place was doomed;
- loading choices felt constrained but not arbitrary;
- players wanted to learn at least one survivor's fate;
- the mobile base felt inherited from Firstlight rather than awarded by the game;
- accessibility variants preserved agency;
- repeat players observed meaningful variation.

## Failure Conditions

The opening fails if players say:

- “Everyone was obviously going to die.”
- “Nothing I did mattered.”
- “The game punished me for caring.”
- “The survivors were just party members selected by the script.”
- “The base appeared because the tutorial ended.”
- “I never learned enough about the place to miss it.”

# Canonical Decision

Firstlight's stable civic form ends during the opening campaign.

Its people, evidence, objects, ecology, institutions, ruins, and contested memory persist.

> **Firstlight is broken, not deleted. The player's first great power is not preventing all loss. It is deciding what kind of continuity remains possible when a world can no longer stay whole.**
