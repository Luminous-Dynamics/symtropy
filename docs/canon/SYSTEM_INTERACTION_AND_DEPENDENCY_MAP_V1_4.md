# System Interaction and Dependency Map

## v2.1 Causal Spine

```text
resident and visitor demand
→ venue schedules and staffing
→ utilities, transport, housing, sanitation, care, and public-space load
→ professional work and handovers
→ transactions, wagering, substances, performance, and adult-only activity
→ incidents, delight, fatigue, debt, evidence, and reputation
→ regulation, informal response, corruption, organizing, and public decision
→ cleanup, follow-up, career history, city change, and worldline memory
```

No stage may skip directly from venue construction to revenue.

# 1. Pleasure-City Authority Boundaries

## Economy

Owns transactions, wages, tips, stakes, reserves, credit, debt, taxes, and custody.

Does not own consent, health, licensing legitimacy, or private identity.

## Venue Operations

Own schedules, capacity, staffing, access, and site policy.

Does not own worker bodies, private records, civic authority, or truth.

## Health

Owns clinical state, care, public alerts, and protected records.

Does not own criminal guilt, moral worth, or consent.

## Adult Consent

Owns authoritative adult participation boundaries.

Cannot be inferred from economy, romance, employment, or generated language.

## Regulation and Justice

Own authority, investigation, evidence, orders, appeal, and accountability.

Does not make every licensed venue safe or every informal institution criminal.

## Information Ecology

Owns claim transmission and public interpretation.

Does not overwrite world truth or protected private state.

## IRIS

Owns bounded assistance to its user.

Does not become regulator, medic, professional, judge, or morality score.

# 2. Profession Authority Boundaries

## Practitioner

May perceive, judge, propose, execute, stop, document, and hand over within competence and authority.

## Tool

Provides capability and evidence. It does not guarantee correct interpretation.

## Credential

Provides scoped institutional authorization. It does not create competence.

## Crew

Owns assigned work and coordination. It does not erase individual refusal or responsibility.

## Institution

Owns policy, resources, supervision, and public obligations. It may share responsibility for failure.

## Project

Owns milestones, dependencies, commitments, and risk. It does not transform future labor into an invisible resource.

# 4. Conservation

The combined system conserves:

- money and staked assets;
- goods and product batches;
- water, power, cooling, food, and waste;
- worker time;
- tool and equipment state;
- housing and transport capacity;
- medical supplies;
- unique identities and source chains.

Reputation, confidence, and cultural meaning are not conserved quantities, but their changes require causal events.

# 5. Privacy

Protected domains include:

- consent;
- adult-work identity;
- client history;
- health and substance use;
- self-exclusion;
- immigration and household status;
- worker complaints;
- IRIS private memory;
- source-chain information.

Aggregated simulation must preserve privacy boundaries.

# 6. Simulation Levels of Detail

Local detail may simulate named visitors and individual interactions.

District and regional modes may aggregate ordinary visitors and transactions while preserving:

- named residents and workers;
- serious incidents;
- consent boundaries;
- pay and debt;
- utility conservation;
- venue ownership;
- projects;
- corruption and public decisions;
- career and injury state.

# 7. Failure Propagation

Examples:

```text
understaffed sanitation
→ drain blockage
→ venue closure pressure
→ hidden temporary repair
→ festival load
→ contamination and public hearing
```

```text
predatory credit
→ household debt
→ worker schedule pressure
→ fatigue
→ game-integrity error
→ operator concealment
→ auditor investigation
```

```text
prohibition
→ informal market growth
→ trusted transport network
→ extortion
→ targeted enforcement
→ service gap
→ political reform or succession conflict
```

# 8. Integration Gates

The v2.1 benchmark cannot promote until it proves:

- material and financial conservation;
- adulthood, consent, and privacy;
- twelve distinct profession rhythms;
- shift and handover continuity;
- non-player initiative;
- long-absence progression;
- deterministic replay;
- graceful degradation;
- player content controls;
- ordinary delight and aftermath.

## Canonical Summary

Pleasure-city and profession systems meet at the shift: bodies, tools, institutions, money, infrastructure, culture, and public responsibility become one lived causal event.
