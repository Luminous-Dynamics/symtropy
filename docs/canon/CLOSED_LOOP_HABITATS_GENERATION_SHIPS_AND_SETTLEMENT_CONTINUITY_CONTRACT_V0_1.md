# Closed-Loop Habitats, Generation Ships, and Settlement Continuity Contract

## Owned Question

**What makes a sealed habitat, long-duration vessel, or generation ship a society rather than a container full of passengers?**

## Core Thesis

In space, the environment is not background.

It is maintained every minute.

```text
Air is an institution.
Water is a lineage.
Heat is a budget.
Waste is unfinished food.
Privacy consumes volume.
A birth changes decades of capacity planning.
A death changes both grief and material balance.
```

The core rule is:

> **A habitat is legitimate only when the people whose lives depend on it can understand, maintain, question, and eventually transform the systems that keep them alive.**

## Prime Directives

1. **No invisible life support.** Air, water, food, heat, radiation protection, waste, and maintenance must have physical and social owners.
2. **No population as cargo.** People remain households, bodies, cultures, obligations, and rights-bearing agents.
3. **No permanent passenger caste.** Long-duration residents gain standing in decisions that govern survival.
4. **No reproductive coercion.** Capacity constraints may create difficult politics, but bodies and parenthood remain protected by consent and rights.
5. **No expertise monopoly without succession.** Every critical system requires training, redundancy, documentation, and supervised practice.
6. **No closed loop without imports and losses.** Even mature habitats experience leakage, degradation, rare-material dependence, and ecological uncertainty.
7. **No safety without exit and appeal.** Sealed environments make authority powerful; emergency control must be scoped and reviewed.
8. **No generational mission without generational consent.** Descendants may reinterpret, continue, redirect, settle, or reject inherited purpose.
9. **No sterile survival fantasy.** Homes, rituals, gardens, art, play, privacy, intimacy, grief, conflict, and beauty are part of habitability.
10. **No colony founded by menu.** Settlement requires people, equipment, knowledge, legitimacy, and a viable transition plan.

# 1. Habitat Types

```text
orbital station
rotating habitat
surface pressure settlement
subsurface habitat
industrial outpost
research station
rescue refuge
mobile fleet habitat
generation ship
sleeper ark
alien-compatible contact habitat
```

Each type has different gravity, radiation, supply, governance, expansion, and evacuation constraints.

# 2. Habitability Domains

A habitat must maintain:

```text
atmospheric composition
pressure
water quantity and quality
food and nutrient continuity
thermal balance
radiation exposure
waste processing
microbial ecology
medical capacity
structural integrity
power and computation
private and communal space
social and cultural continuity
```

The player should be able to see when one domain is borrowing stability from another.

Examples:

- Emergency cooling raises water demand.
- Food sterilization damages microbial resilience.
- Radiation sheltering reduces usable living volume.
- Overcrowding increases conflict, disease risk, and maintenance obstruction.

# 3. The Habitat Social Contract

Every mature habitat must answer:

```text
Who owns the hull?
Who operates life support?
Who can issue emergency commands?
Who receives scarce repair parts?
Who may inspect environmental data?
Who trains successors?
Who may leave?
Who may arrive?
Who decides expansion?
What happens when mission and living community diverge?
```

## Required Rights

```text
access to truthful environmental data
minimum breathable and thermal safety
medical privacy
safe refusal of abusive labor
representation in survival policy
appeal of quarantine and confinement
protected private space proportional to reality
education in habitat systems
family and identity continuity
lawful exit when transport and destination exist
```

# 4. Crew, Residents, and Citizenship

The game distinguishes:

```text
transit passenger
contract worker
temporary resident
permanent resident
citizen or member
protected guest
refugee
crew under command
minor or dependent person
machine or nonhuman resident
```

These statuses may overlap. Operational role does not erase civic standing.

A captain may command a burn without owning the lives aboard. A life-support technician may have emergency authority without permanent political superiority.

# 5. Generation Ships

A generation ship carries an inherited project across lives.

Its design must include:

```text
mission purpose
constitutional renewal intervals
education and apprenticeship
population and genetic diversity safeguards
cultural memory
technical succession
archive correction
resource reserves
possible destination failure
right to redirect
right to settle early
right to abandon mission under defined conditions
```

## Inherited Mission Problem

Founders may consent to launch. Descendants did not.

Therefore:

```text
mission legitimacy must be renewed
children cannot be treated as mission assets
archives must preserve dissent
alternative futures must remain thinkable
command cannot justify itself solely through founder intent
```

# 6. Reproduction, Childhood, and Care

Population planning may consider:

```text
care capacity
living volume
food and water
medical capability
genetic and cultural diversity
future workforce and elder support
travel duration
settlement destination capacity
```

But the game must never represent reproduction as a state-owned production queue.

Gameplay may involve:

```text
expanding care and education before population growth
negotiating migration instead of coercion
supporting parents and guardians
resolving inherited mission conflict
protecting children during life-support crisis
```

# 7. Labor and Maintenance

Closed habitats make maintenance unavoidable, but not all residents should become identical technicians.

Required systems:

```text
universal safety literacy
specialist apprenticeship
cross-training
maintenance rotations
fatigue limits
hazard compensation
protected whistleblowing
automation oversight
public incident review
```

Maintenance failure can arise from:

```text
material shortage
skill loss
administrative capture
exhaustion
hidden design defect
proprietary lock
cultural taboo
conflict
Null drift
```

# 8. Food, Microbiology, and Ecological Continuity

Habitat ecology includes:

```text
crops
microbial communities
pollinators or equivalents
waste decomposers
water biota
pathogens
stored genetic libraries
companion species
alien symbioses
```

Sterility is not a permanent solution. Ecological management must balance biosecurity with resilience.

# 9. Space, Privacy, and Dignity

Living volume has political meaning.

The system tracks:

```text
private volume
noise exposure
light and circadian control
shared sanitation
accessible routes
quiet and mourning spaces
children's spaces
intimacy boundaries
cultural and ritual spaces
```

A materially successful habitat can still become unlivable through surveillance, crowding, forced schedules, or denial of private life.

# 10. Crisis and Emergency Authority

Habitat emergencies include:

```text
pressure loss
fire
radiation storm
life-support contamination
power failure
collision
epidemic
violent conflict
navigation failure
food-system collapse
```

Emergency authority must specify:

```text
trigger evidence
scope
command roles
protected rights
communication duties
expiry
review

```

Confinement, sedation, rationing, or reproductive control are never automatic consequences of an emergency.

# 11. Settlement Founding

A vessel becomes a settlement only when it establishes:

```text
viable life-support horizon
repair capacity
local resource access
shelter and care
political standing
household continuity
education
archive and identity continuity
transport contingency
relationship with existing life or claims
```

The transition may be:

```text
planned
provisional
emergency
contested
seasonal
failed
```

A settlement charter cannot create oxygen or competence. A functioning base without rights is not legitimate settlement.

# 12. Alien and Multi-Body Habitats

Habitats may support beings with different:

```text
pressure
temperature
atmosphere
gravity
radiation tolerance
light
sound
chemistry
privacy
agency structure
time scale
```

Shared habitats require negotiated environmental zones, transition spaces, consent boundaries, and life-support accounting that does not define one body as the neutral default.

# 13. Player Activities

```text
commissioning a new habitat ring
tracking a slow oxygen imbalance
teaching an apprentice during routine work
mediating a mission-renewal assembly
expanding a school before accepting migrants
restoring a microbial loop after over-sterilization
repairing pressure damage under distributed crew roles
investigating falsified environmental reports
choosing whether a generation ship redirects
founding a settlement without erasing prior life or claims
```

# 14. Acceptance Tests

A representative implementation must prove:

1. Life-support failure has physical cause, delayed indicators, and multiple repair paths.
2. Residents can inspect meaningful environmental data without omniscient diagnostics.
3. A specialist loss degrades capability unless succession exists.
4. Emergency authority expires and produces review evidence.
5. Household privacy survives normal telemetry and save/load.
6. Population change affects care, space, food, education, and future maintenance.
7. A generation-ship mission can be lawfully renewed, redirected, or rejected.
8. Founding a settlement requires conserved people, cargo, knowledge, and life-support capacity.
9. An alien-compatible zone preserves different environmental requirements.

# 15. Anti-Patterns

Reject:

```text
oxygen as one decorative bar
crew as interchangeable labor points
children as growth bonuses
captain as permanent dictator
instant colony deployment
perfect recycling
unlimited private space
sterility as ecological mastery
founder mission as eternal law
```

## Final Line

> **A closed habitat becomes a home when survival knowledge is shared, power can be questioned, children inherit choices rather than orders, and the hull protects lives without owning them.**
