# Disaster Preparedness, Continuity of Operations, and Recovery Contract

## Owned Question

How should Symtropy represent disasters as foreseeable, unequal, infrastructural, ecological, social, and recoverable events rather than spectacular interruptions that reset after a mission?

## Core Thesis

A disaster is not only a hazard.

It is the collision between a hazard and a society's exposure, preparedness, infrastructure, inequality, information, care capacity, and authority.

> **The storm is natural. The casualty pattern is historical. The recovery becomes political.**

Disaster gameplay must include:

```text
risk reduction
preparedness
warning
continuity planning
evacuation
shelter
rescue
care
service triage
relief logistics
accountability
recovery
reconstruction
memory
adaptation
```

# 1. Hazard Is Not Disaster

Hazards include:

- storms;
- floods;
- wildfire;
- heat;
- cold;
- drought;
- earthquake;
- landslide;
- epidemic;
- radiation release;
- industrial contamination;
- habitat breach;
- orbital debris;
- reactor failure;
- network collapse;
- war damage;
- alien ecological reaction;
- Null cascade.

Disaster severity depends on:

```text
hazard intensity
exposure
vulnerability
preparedness
warning time
mobility
care capacity
infrastructure redundancy
social trust
institutional competence
ongoing conflict
```

# 2. Preparedness Is Playable Infrastructure

Preparedness may include:

- risk maps;
- shelters;
- evacuation routes;
- drills;
- stockpiles;
- backup power;
- water reserves;
- redundant communications;
- mutual-aid agreements;
- trained volunteers;
- accessible transport;
- continuity runbooks;
- emergency credentials;
- archive and data backups;
- seed, medicine, and tool caches;
- public education;
- early-warning systems.

Preparedness consumes resources before a visible crisis. That creates real political tension.

The player should see both:

- the opportunity cost of preparation;
- the avoided harm when preparation works.

# 3. Continuity of Essential Functions

Every settlement defines a bounded continuity floor.

Typical essential functions:

```text
water
air and pressure
emergency power
medical care
food storage and distribution
shelter
fire suppression
communication
identity and source-chain recovery
sanitation
transport and evacuation
public safety
archive continuity
```

A continuity plan must specify:

- minimum service level;
- priority recipients based on need and system survival;
- backup sites;
- alternate staff;
- manual fallback;
- resource duration;
- authority scope;
- dependency graph;
- restoration sequence;
- communication plan;
- expiry of emergency powers.

# 4. Warning and Uncertainty

Warnings must carry:

- source;
- confidence;
- geographic and temporal scope;
- expected impacts;
- recommended actions;
- known uncertainty;
- update channel;
- accessibility formats.

False alarms, missed warnings, conflicting models, damaged sensors, propaganda, and public distrust are legitimate gameplay states.

The game must not punish people merely for failing to obey an opaque warning.

# 5. Evacuation Is a Logistics and Rights Problem

Evacuation must account for:

- transport capacity;
- route safety;
- household separation;
- children and dependents;
- disabled people;
- medical equipment;
- animals and nonhuman kin;
- machine bodies;
- language access;
- identity documents;
- cultural and sacred items;
- detention or border risks;
- fear of property loss;
- distrust of authorities;
- return conditions.

Evacuation options include:

- voluntary departure;
- supported evacuation;
- mandatory evacuation under bounded emergency authority;
- shelter in place;
- vertical evacuation;
- staged relocation;
- convoy evacuation;
- off-world or orbital refuge;
- nonhuman habitat movement.

Mandatory evacuation must have a necessity basis, limits, review, and protection from confiscation or permanent exclusion.

# 6. Shelter and Relief

Shelter is not a bed count.

It requires:

- safety;
- water and sanitation;
- privacy;
- accessibility;
- medical support;
- family integrity;
- information;
- cultural and religious accommodation;
- safe spaces for children;
- machine and nonhuman requirements;
- protection from exploitation and violence;
- complaint and appeal channels.

Relief systems should track distribution, custody, spoilage, transport, staffing, trust, and exclusion.

# 7. Incident Coordination

Emergency coordination may use temporary command structures, but the following remain mandatory:

- explicit authority scopes;
- role clarity;
- information sharing with privacy;
- public notice;
- rights-floor constraints;
- recordkeeping;
- dissent and escalation channels;
- transition back to ordinary governance;
- after-action review.

The most competent emergency actor is not automatically entitled to govern after the emergency.

# 8. Unequal Exposure and Disaster Justice

Disasters reveal prior decisions.

Different groups may face higher risk because of:

- settlement location;
- inaccessible infrastructure;
- poor housing;
- occupational exposure;
- exclusion from warnings;
- lack of transport;
- citizenship status;
- debt or employment control;
- environmental racism or caste;
- historic disinvestment;
- proprietary body-maintenance dependencies;
- machine or nonhuman non-recognition.

Recovery must be able to expose and repair these patterns rather than restoring the exact vulnerability that existed before.

# 9. Recovery Stages

## 9.1 Stabilization

Stop immediate cascading harm.

## 9.2 Relief

Meet urgent bodily and social needs.

## 9.3 Service Restoration

Restore essential functions under safe temporary arrangements.

## 9.4 Return or Relocation

Support informed return, permanent relocation, or contested decisions about unsafe places.

## 9.5 Reconstruction

Rebuild physical, ecological, economic, and institutional systems.

## 9.6 Adaptation and Accountability

Change standards, routes, institutions, charters, ecosystems, and preparedness based on evidence.

Recovery may take days, years, or generations.

# 10. Build Back Differently

The game should avoid "build back better" as a universal moral slogan.

Reconstruction questions include:

- who decides what returns;
- who receives compensation;
- whether residents can return;
- whether a hazardous industry remains;
- whether a memorial or scar is preserved;
- whether land is restored ecologically;
- whether emergency housing becomes permanent dignity or permanent neglect;
- whether outside aid creates dependency or extraction;
- whether displaced communities retain political voice.

# 11. Disaster Memory

A disaster leaves:

- deaths and injuries;
- changed bodies;
- changed households;
- repaired or abandoned places;
- new routes;
- debt;
- mutual-aid networks;
- institutional reforms;
- mistrust;
- rituals and memorials;
- preparedness culture;
- myths and disputed blame;
- ecological succession;
- Chronicle events.

The world should remember both heroism and preventable failure.

# 12. Player Roles

Players may act as:

- scout;
- rescuer;
- driver or pilot;
- medic;
- engineer;
- dispatcher;
- shelter worker;
- communicator;
- archive recovery specialist;
- ecological steward;
- public official;
- investigator;
- affected resident;
- organizer;
- builder.

No role should make all others decorative.

# 13. Solo and Co-op

Solo play requires delegable plans, competent NPC teams, pause or planning support where appropriate, and bounded automation.

Co-op should create meaningful roles without forcing permanent specialization.

A player who chooses care, logistics, communication, or evacuation must contribute as materially as one fighting hazards.

# 14. Seedworks Representative Scope

A representative disaster proof should include:

- one forecast hazard with uncertainty;
- one preparation choice before impact;
- one warning problem;
- one household requiring accessible evacuation;
- one service continuity decision;
- one shelter or refuge site;
- one logistics constraint;
- one preventable failure or avoided loss;
- one recovery project;
- one after-action reform;
- one revisit weeks later.

The water system may participate, but it may not define the entire event.

# 15. Acceptance Tests

The disaster system fails if:

1. hazard intensity alone determines casualties;
2. evacuation ignores bodies, households, and transport;
3. emergency powers lack scope or expiry;
4. preparation has no visible benefit;
5. relief items appear without logistics;
6. recovery restores all state instantly;
7. a mission completion clears trauma, displacement, and infrastructure damage;
8. vulnerable groups are only helpless objectives;
9. player combat contribution dominates all other roles;
10. the after-action review has no ability to change future risk.

# 16. Non-Goals

This contract does not require:

- real-world disaster forecasting accuracy;
- graphic suffering;
- management of every relief item;
- one correct emergency doctrine;
- disaster as constant background misery;
- punishment for players who do not enjoy administration.

## Final Rule

> **A disaster should reveal what a civilization prepared, neglected, protected, sacrificed, and learned—not merely how dramatic its ruins look.**
