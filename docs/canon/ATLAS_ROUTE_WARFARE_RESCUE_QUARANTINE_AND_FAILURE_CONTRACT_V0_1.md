# Atlas Route Warfare, Rescue, Quarantine, and Failure Contract

## Owned Question

**How can Atlas infrastructure become strategically important without reducing interstellar war to portal capture, making civilian routes disposable, or guaranteeing rescue from every failed transit?**

## Core Thesis

Atlas infrastructure concentrates power, vulnerability, labor, migration, medicine, archives, and military access. Conflict around it must therefore be treated as infrastructure warfare with civilian consequences, professional duties, and long aftermath.

> **A route is never only a military objective. It is also somebody's road home, hospital supply line, workplace, quarantine boundary, and last remaining contact with another world.**

# 1. Protected Functions

Atlas endpoints may contain protected or specially governed functions:

- civilian passenger reception;
- medical evacuation;
- refugee processing;
- quarantine;
- rescue coordination;
- public communications;
- archives and source-chain custody;
- critical life-support imports;
- nonhuman diplomatic interfaces;
- route-weather observatories.

Military use does not automatically erase these functions.

# 2. Strategic Actions

Conflict may involve:

- route blockade;
- throughput denial;
- traffic priority seizure;
- anchor isolation;
- clock-array sabotage;
- false route-weather reports;
- thermal-field attack;
- mass-reservation flooding;
- quarantine manipulation;
- communication censorship;
- endpoint occupation;
- seizure of repair supply chains;
- forced one-way evacuation;
- destruction of an unpaired destination's reconnection project;
- closure to prevent invasion.

Not every action requires destroying the field lattice.

# 3. Civilian Consequences

Route conflict can cause:

- stranded travelers;
- medication shortages;
- family separation;
- food and industrial scarcity;
- refugee accumulation;
- dangerous informal routes;
- collapse of remote public services;
- censorship and historical isolation;
- stranded seed or rescue vessels;
- loss of source-chain evidence;
- ecological harm from emergency operation;
- employment collapse in route settlements.

These consequences must be named, simulated, and revisited.

# 4. Military Transit

Military use requires declared authority and route class.

The system must model:

- mass and capacity consumed by military traffic;
- displacement of civilian traffic;
- destination consent or coercion;
- quarantine and biological risk;
- rules of engagement;
- custody of weapons;
- emergency abort;
- downstream retaliation;
- worker refusal;
- route-law challenges.

A player cannot silently turn a civic bridge into a personal invasion device.

# 5. Worker Refusal

Route workers may lawfully or unlawfully refuse:

- unsafe operation;
- invalid authority;
- civilian displacement;
- chronology violations;
- quarantine bypass;
- concealed military cargo;
- operation above thermal or coherence limits;
- transport into likely atrocity.

Their refusal may save lives, trigger prosecution, create mutiny accusations, or expose divided institutions.

# 6. Rescue Duties

Rescue is constrained by:

- route state;
- available mass and energy;
- destination capacity;
- rescue craft;
- hazard uncertainty;
- quarantine;
- time;
- competing victims;
- whether intervention creates a larger failure.

The game must permit tragic triage without presenting cruelty as efficiency.

# 7. No Guaranteed Player Priority

The player and companions do not automatically outrank:

- children;
- critical patients;
- essential repair crews;
- a vessel with more recoverable lives;
- people already promised evacuation;
- nonhuman populations dependent on the same route.

Rescue priority must be governed by declared policy and contestable decisions.

# 8. Quarantine

Quarantine is neither an automatic villain nor automatic justification.

It requires:

- a defined hazard claim;
- evidence and uncertainty;
- scope;
- review;
- living conditions;
- medical and legal access;
- communication rights;
- release conditions;
- compensation where appropriate;
- protections against indefinite administrative renewal.

Atlas routes amplify quarantine stakes because hazards and people can move across ecological boundaries rapidly.

# 9. Route Closure

Legitimate closure reasons may include:

- chronology uncertainty;
- field instability;
- epidemic or ecological hazard;
- invasion risk;
- destination request;
- worker safety;
- treaty requirement;
- unknown alien interference;
- thermal or structural failure.

Closure procedures require:

- public reason or justified classified record;
- effective time;
- treatment of committed transits;
- stranded-person plans;
- medical exceptions;
- review and appeal;
- reopening criteria;
- evidence preservation.

# 10. Siege and Blockade

A blockade is not abstract pressure.

The simulation tracks:

- blocked goods and people;
- substitute routes;
- local inventories;
- rationing;
- black markets;
- public-health effects;
- military necessity claims;
- distributional burden;
- duration;
- attempts at negotiation or relief.

A blockade that knowingly destroys civilian survival systems enters atrocity and accountability systems.

# 11. Sabotage and Espionage

Sabotage gameplay may target:

- clocks;
- sensor arrays;
- cooling;
- software;
- field materials;
- route data;
- manifests;
- authority tokens;
- quarantine evidence;
- repair stock.

The player should often face choices between:

- disabling capacity;
- preserving civilian use;
- exposing evidence;
- delaying operation;
- rescuing workers;
- accepting strategic disadvantage.

# 12. Atlas Scars

Failed or weaponized metric systems can leave long-term scars.

Scars may be:

- physical;
- orbital;
- ecological;
- sensory;
- computational;
- legal;
- cultural;
- diplomatic.

A scar can remain after the war, the government, and even the original route have disappeared.

# 13. Post-Conflict Duties

After conflict, affected institutions may owe:

- route stabilization;
- rescue and body recovery;
- family notification;
- archive reconstruction;
- decontamination;
- environmental repair;
- worker healthcare;
- refugee return or settlement;
- compensation;
- public inquiry;
- prosecution;
- memorialization;
- revised authority and safety rules.

Reopening quickly is not always the most just or safest outcome.

# 14. Occupation

Occupying an endpoint does not automatically provide:

- valid clock custody;
- worker cooperation;
- destination consent;
- route solution knowledge;
- quarantine legitimacy;
- functioning repair chains;
- public trust;
- authority over the paired system.

A captured gate may be unusable or dangerous.

# 15. Interstellar Rescue Campaigns

Representative rescue situations:

## 15.1 Committed Transit, Lost Destination

A vessel is already in route custody when the receiving anchor fails.

## 15.2 Thermal Field Evacuation

The endpoint must choose between saving the route core and evacuating nearby worker housing.

## 15.3 Atlas Silence

A paired endpoint stops responding, but evidence cannot distinguish attack, quarantine, and deliberate closure.

## 15.4 Seed Ship Too Early

A seed vessel arrives before the destination is prepared and cannot brake safely without local assistance.

## 15.5 One-Way Alien Rescue

An alien route can evacuate victims but offers no return path for rescuers.

## 15.6 Scar Settlement

A community lives inside the economic and physical residue of a failed route others want to reactivate.

# 16. Player Power Boundaries

The player may:

- command a ship or unit under valid authority;
- sabotage or defend infrastructure;
- organize rescue;
- refuse an unlawful order;
- expose false evidence;
- negotiate closure or reopening;
- help workers strike;
- build alternatives;
- testify after conflict.

The player may not:

- erase civilian consequences through mission completion;
- override route workers because they are companions;
- use IRIS as unquestionable targeting authority;
- transport duplicated armies through rollback;
- reopen a route while ignoring committed quarantine.

# 17. Multiplayer Safety

Multiplayer route conflict must prevent:

- host deletion of committed transits;
- duplicate cargo through reconnect;
- endless route denial without world governance response;
- trapping offline players without declared disconnect policy;
- hidden chronology edits;
- weaponizing private mature-content or medical data at quarantine;
- griefing through irreversible future migration invitations.

# 18. Acceptance Gates

1. Route seizure changes access but not automatic paired-endpoint authority.
2. A military transit displaces real civilian capacity.
3. A worker refusal can halt an unsafe operation.
4. A rescue policy may prioritize someone other than the player.
5. A blockade produces traced civilian effects.
6. A quarantine is reviewable and provides humane conditions.
7. A committed transit survives endpoint failure without duplication.
8. An Atlas Scar remains after the immediate crisis.
9. Postwar reopening requires repair and political process.
10. A technically successful military action can remain morally and strategically disastrous.

# Production Maxim

> **The most powerful road in the galaxy should also be one of the hardest things to use without becoming responsible for everyone depending on it.**
