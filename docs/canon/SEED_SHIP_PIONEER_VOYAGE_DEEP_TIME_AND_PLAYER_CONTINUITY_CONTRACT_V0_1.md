# Seed Ship, Pioneer Voyage, Deep Time, and Player Continuity Contract

## Owned Question

**How can a player personally reach a star before an Atlas route exists without turning decades of travel into either tedious chores or an inconsequential loading screen?**

## Core Thesis

A seed voyage is a moving civilization, not a vehicle sequence between two maps.

The player may cross the original distance through relativistic flight, biological suspension, generation travel, reconstitution, machine continuity, or a mixture of these. Whatever method is used, time passes, institutions change, people live and die, the mission may be amended, and the destination remains entitled to its own future.

> **The first road to another star is the society that survives long enough to build the far end.**

# 1. Why Seed Voyages Remain Necessary

A new Atlas route normally requires compatible infrastructure at both endpoints.

Therefore an unpaired destination can be reached through:

- autonomous probe;
- crewed relativistic expedition;
- generation ship;
- sleeper or suspended-biological mission;
- reconstitution ark;
- machine-led seed mission;
- alien vessel or recovered route;
- independently developed destination contact.

An origin civilization may carry plans for an Atlas Seed. It may not assume that the destination must build or pair it.

# 2. The Ship Is a Society Under Acceleration

A seed ship requires more than propulsion and payload.

It contains:

- households;
- children, elders, and dependents;
- habitat ecology;
- work and maintenance;
- education and apprenticeship;
- healthcare;
- governance;
- records and source-chain continuity;
- conflict resolution;
- worship, art, sport, and ordinary leisure;
- waste, sanitation, and death care;
- mission inheritance;
- exit and dissent rules where physically possible.

The ship must be able to answer:

```text
Who decides mission changes?
Who owns essential infrastructure?
Who performs dangerous work?
What happens when a profession loses all qualified workers?
Can descendants refuse the mission?
How are births and reproductive decisions governed?
Who may awaken archived people?
What obligations survive a founder's death?
What happens if arrival becomes impossible?
```

# 3. Voyage Temporal Modes

The player may move among three presentation modes without changing authoritative simulation truth.

## 3.1 Active Voyage

First-person or close operational play during:

- launch;
- acceleration and braking;
- repairs;
- medical emergencies;
- relationship events;
- elections and constitutional crises;
- births, deaths, and reconstitution;
- contact and signals;
- ecological failures;
- mutiny, refusal, or mission amendment;
- arrival preparation.

## 3.2 Accelerated Continuity

Days, months, or years advance while the player remains able to inspect:

- habitat trends;
- projects;
- crew and household changes;
- births and deaths;
- education and apprenticeship;
- incoming messages;
- resource margins;
- maintenance debt;
- political proposals;
- IRIS summaries;
- personal aging and health.

The player may interrupt accelerated continuity when a declared threshold is crossed.

## 3.3 Deep-Time Passage

The simulation advances to the next historically significant event through deterministic low-detail stepping.

Interruptions are mandatory for:

- irreversible mission changes;
- loss of life-support margin;
- major companion or household transition;
- office succession involving the player;
- contact;
- source-chain discontinuity;
- destination viability change;
- arrival;
- events explicitly promised to the player.

Deep-time passage may compress quiet life. It may not delete it from history.

# 4. Player Continuity Modes

## 4.1 Continuous Biological Life

The player ages through the voyage according to proper time.

Benefits:

- direct continuity;
- strong relationship persistence;
- ordinary embodied life.

Costs:

- aging;
- health risk;
- limited journey duration;
- possible inability to return to old relationships.

## 4.2 Biological Suspension

The player alternates between active periods and protected low-metabolism states.

Suspension requires:

- medical capacity;
- monitoring;
- consent;
- wake criteria;
- failure handling;
- care labor;
- legal rules for a sleeping office-holder or dependent.

Suspension is not paused existence. The rest of the ship continues.

## 4.3 Reconstitution Continuity

The player may die or deliberately transition through preserved source-chain data and later reconstitution.

The system must preserve:

- source provenance;
- missing memories;
- legal authority expiry;
- companion recognition disputes;
- bodily difference;
- competing continuity claims;
- grief for the original death.

Reconstitution does not permit the player to remain continuously in command while absent.

## 4.4 Cybernetic or Machine-Supported Longevity

Body modification may extend active life but creates:

- maintenance dependency;
- firmware and part supply;
- bodily sovereignty questions;
- uneven access;
- identity and class effects;
- adaptation to destination conditions.

## 4.5 Playable Successor

A campaign may continue through a descendant, apprentice, companion, machine, or institutionally chosen successor.

This is not a hidden character replacement. The transition must be authored, legible, and consensual at the player-experience level.

The original player's history remains part of the world.

# 5. Mission Inheritance

A mission objective is not eternally binding because an ancestor wrote it.

Every long voyage requires:

- founding mission statement;
- protected rights floor;
- amendment process;
- emergency powers;
- review intervals;
- archive access;
- dissent representation;
- destination reassessment;
- authority succession.

Possible amendments:

- change destination;
- delay settlement;
- become a mobile habitat;
- abandon Atlas construction;
- respond to distress;
- refuse origin authority;
- establish noncontact boundaries;
- join another polity;
- divide the mission into multiple settlements.

# 6. Crew and Descendant Autonomy

People born aboard did not personally consent to launch.

The ship owes them:

- education that is not only mission indoctrination;
- meaningful civic participation;
- reproductive autonomy;
- professional choice within physical constraints;
- ability to challenge inherited authority;
- access to true mission history;
- recognition of disability and care needs;
- a credible voice in settlement or continued voyage.

A mission that can survive only through coercive reproduction or permanent caste assignment is a political failure, not an optimization success.

# 7. Work and Professional Continuity

The voyage must model professions across generations:

- propulsion and metric engineering;
- ecology;
- medicine;
- education;
- fabrication;
- sanitation;
- governance;
- archive custody;
- navigation;
- food production;
- structural maintenance;
- childcare and elder care;
- mental health;
- art and social life.

Critical knowledge must have:

- multiple practitioners;
- apprenticeship;
- records;
- drills;
- cross-training;
- succession;
- recovery plans after death or conflict.

IRIS may support learning but cannot replace embodied professional communities.

# 8. Ordinary Life

Seed ships need ordinary life to prevent every voyage from becoming crisis montage.

Include:

- meals and food disputes;
- sports adapted to gravity schedules;
- celebrations of maintenance milestones;
- private relationships;
- unpopular school subjects;
- terrible entertainment;
- fashion built from limited materials;
- jokes about mission founders;
- quiet places;
- pets or companion ecologies where viable;
- arguments over noise, space, and privacy;
- rituals for people who will never see the origin again.

Players become invested when they understand what the mission is preserving, not only what threatens it.

# 9. Messages from Origin

Messages arrive according to valid channels and delays.

They may report:

- political change;
- deaths and births;
- new technology;
- war;
- revised mission requests;
- misinformation;
- apologies received decades late;
- an Atlas program beginning or collapsing;
- the origin endpoint moving or being destroyed.

The ship is not obligated to obey a newer origin government merely because a message arrives.

# 10. Destination Change During Voyage

The destination may change through:

- stellar evolution or activity;
- ecological discovery;
- prior inhabitants;
- alien contact;
- another expedition arriving first;
- probe error;
- terraforming failure;
- political closure;
- emerging Atlas signal;
- catastrophic origin assumptions.

Arrival planning must use current evidence, not launch-era certainty.

# 11. Arrival Is a Campaign

Arrival phases:

1. remote observation;
2. orbital and system survey;
3. communication and claim review;
4. hazard and ecology assessment;
5. landing or habitat placement;
6. temporary survival;
7. prior-claim negotiation;
8. local industry;
9. household continuity;
10. settlement founding;
11. Atlas Seed evaluation;
12. possible reconnection.

The destination is never automatically empty because no human city is visible.

# 12. Atlas Seed Authority

The Seed package may contain technical knowledge and cryptographic lineage.

It does not contain permanent political authority.

Activation requires local decisions about:

- ecological effect;
- public cost;
- labor;
- migration;
- quarantine;
- route ownership;
- military risk;
- cultural influence;
- whether reconnection is desirable.

The destination may dismantle, archive, or repurpose the Seed.

# 13. Return and Separation

A pioneer may eventually return through a later Atlas route.

Return may reveal:

- the origin society is centuries older in Atlas Time;
- the player's property and office no longer exist;
- companions aged differently;
- descendants remember the player mythically;
- the origin uses a different IRIS lineage;
- the player's mission is politically embarrassing;
- the destination now feels more like home.

Return must not restore expired authority or relationships automatically.

# 14. Multiplayer Voyages

A player joining a seed voyage chooses a forward temporal commitment.

The system must disclose:

- expected Atlas advance;
- expected proper time;
- separation from players remaining behind;
- likely communication delay;
- whether return infrastructure exists;
- whether the voyage creates or enters a future worldline horizon.

Players remaining at origin are not forced to skip time.

The traveler cannot reappear in their past because another player opens a menu.

# 15. Failure and Abandonment

Possible outcomes include:

- successful settlement;
- mobile civilization;
- mission redirection;
- rescue integration;
- divided settlement;
- generation-ship continuity;
- failed ecology;
- arrival at an occupied world;
- permanent isolation;
- extinction;
- surviving archive without living crew;
- destination rejection of the ship;
- return without settlement.

Failure remains historical and may become a later rescue, archive, ruin, or contact campaign.

# 16. Player-Facing Controls

Before deep-time advance, the player must be able to inspect:

- projected Atlas and proper-time change;
- current resource margins;
- scheduled wake or interruption rules;
- unresolved commitments;
- household and companion risks;
- office succession;
- known incoming messages;
- likely destination changes;
- source-chain state;
- multiplayer separation.

No irreversible decade-scale advance occurs through an unlabeled ordinary sleep command.

# 17. Anti-Grind Rules

The voyage must not require repetitive manual completion of every:

- meal;
- cleaning cycle;
- calibration;
- school day;
- medical check;
- crop harvest;
- routine shift.

Routine becomes active play when:

- the player chooses professional immersion;
- a system becomes uncertain;
- a relationship matters;
- a handover fails;
- a resource margin changes;
- a cultural practice forms;
- a crisis or irreversible decision emerges.

# 18. Acceptance Gates

1. A voyage advances Atlas Time and proper time differently.
2. At least one onboard child reaches adulthood and changes the mission politically.
3. One critical profession survives through apprenticeship rather than IRIS substitution.
4. One quiet multi-year period passes without arbitrary crisis generation.
5. A message from origin arrives late enough to change its meaning.
6. The destination invalidates one launch-era assumption.
7. Descendants may reject Atlas construction.
8. Player death and reconstitution do not preserve expired command.
9. Multiplayer travelers move forward without duplicating themselves at origin.
10. Arrival produces a settlement and claim campaign rather than an instant colony placement screen.

# Production Maxim

> **The voyage is not dead time between worlds. It is where one world becomes another.**
