# Multi-Character, Worldline, and Perspective Authority Contract

## Owned Question

**How can one player inhabit many lives across decades, planets, institutions, species, and divergent histories without becoming an invisible immortal ruler who owns every character's possessions, knowledge, loyalties, or authority?**

## Core Thesis

A Symtropy account may contain many playable characters.

Some characters coexist in one worldline. Others inhabit descendants of a shared history that have diverged and may never be silently merged. The account preserves access to perspectives and Chronicle records. It does not possess one universal body, inventory, wallet, reputation, map, office, household, source chain, or field of knowledge.

> **The player may inhabit many lives. The account does not become a life above them.**

# 1. Three Distinct Layers

## 1.1 Account

The account owns only:

- authentication and player settings;
- accessibility preferences;
- content controls;
- Chronicle navigation rights;
- proof that the player may resume particular characters;
- worldline save ancestry;
- out-of-world achievements where appropriate;
- optional authoring and moderation permissions.

The account does not own diegetic money, land, office, equipment, memory, reputation, or political legitimacy.

## 1.2 Chronicle

The Chronicle is the player-facing historical interface that organizes:

- playable characters;
- worldline ancestry;
- known relationships;
- dates and proper-time differences;
- current locations;
- unresolved obligations;
- last confirmed remote states;
- branch divergence;
- source-chain continuity;
- retired, dead, missing, or inaccessible characters.

The Chronicle may display truths available to the player as a save-management interface while clearly separating them from what the current character knows.

## 1.3 Embodied Character

The embodied character is the currently controlled person, machine, distributed agent, alien being, or other valid playable subject.

The character owns or participates in:

- a body or declared embodiment;
- proper time;
- a source chain;
- memories and uncertainty;
- relationships;
- credentials;
- citizenship and legal standing;
- possessions and access;
- obligations;
- current location;
- sensory and professional capabilities;
- a bounded knowledge envelope.

Only the embodied character may act through their body and authority.

# 2. Same-Worldline Characters

Several playable characters may exist simultaneously in one worldline.

Examples include:

- a municipal engineer on Earth;
- a seed-vessel navigator in transit;
- the navigator's adult child at the destination;
- a route worker at an Atlas endpoint;
- a machine fork with disputed household standing;
- an alien diplomat participating in the same regional conflict;
- a former office-holder living privately after retirement.

Non-active playable characters remain simulated agents. They may:

- work;
- travel;
- rest;
- refuse requests;
- change relationships;
- vote;
- age;
- become injured;
- complete projects;
- migrate;
- lose office;
- gain office;
- die;
- be reconstituted;
- become temporarily or permanently unavailable.

Making a character playable does not freeze them until selected.

# 3. Divergent Worldline Characters

A player may retain characters in multiple worldlines created by:

- explicit branch preservation;
- multiplayer fork;
- incompatible campaign decisions;
- deterministic scenario replay;
- research or authoring mode;
- restoration from a prior worldline checkpoint;
- migration into a future epoch that no longer shares mutable present state.

Characters in different worldlines are historically separate.

They may not exchange:

- items;
- currency;
- offices;
- votes;
- military units;
- evidence;
- source-chain authority;
- living relationships;
- institutional ownership;
- causal messages.

The Chronicle may compare them. The characters may not know that comparison unless a later system explicitly creates a causally valid observation of alternate worldlines. Such technology is outside current canon.

# 4. Character Switching

Switching perspective must correspond to a declared transition rather than instantaneous possession.

Valid transitions include:

- sleep or safe rest;
- shift handover;
- a Chronicle chapter boundary;
- retirement;
- imprisonment or incapacity;
- election or appointment;
- death and continuation selection;
- long voyage;
- route transit;
- receipt of an authenticated message;
- a household or institutional request;
- explicit time advance;
- multiplayer seat transfer;
- beginning a separately preserved worldline.

A switch may be immediate in interface time while still recording a real in-world transition.

The game must not imply that the departing character ceased to exist merely because control moved elsewhere.

# 5. Perspective Authority

The active perspective determines:

- sensory access;
- visible interface layers;
- language comprehension;
- professional diagnostics;
- private records;
- legal powers;
- social invitations;
- trusted testimony;
- physical reach;
- risk;
- emotional context.

A sanitation worker, governor, machine witness, child, alien intermediary, and Atlas engineer may perceive the same district differently without one view being the complete truth.

IRIS may help organize the active character's evidence. It may not import facts learned by another character unless a valid transfer occurred.

# 6. No Universal Player Wallet

Resources belong to people and institutions.

Characters may share access through:

- a household account;
- cooperative membership;
- employment;
- a public budget;
- inheritance;
- trust or guardianship;
- military or expedition issue;
- ship stores;
- corporate credentials;
- explicit loan or gift.

Switching characters does not transfer private assets.

A shared account must identify:

- legal owner;
- authorized users;
- limits;
- revocation rules;
- audit trail;
- obligations;
- succession.

# 7. No Universal Reputation

Reputation belongs to relationships, audiences, professions, and institutions.

One account may control characters who are:

- allies;
- strangers;
- family;
- political rivals;
- professional competitors;
- debtor and creditor;
- claimant and judge;
- original and fork;
- citizen and foreign official;
- members of opposing wartime factions.

The game must not average these into one account morality score.

# 8. No Universal Map or Knowledge

A discovered location is not automatically known to every character.

The Chronicle may show that another playable character visited a place while the active character's map displays:

- no record;
- a rumor;
- an old map;
- a censored route;
- a professional plan;
- an inherited description;
- a message awaiting authentication.

Likewise, a player who personally remembers a secret must still act through what the current character could plausibly know.

The runtime should support roleplay without requiring the player to forget. It must prevent impossible actions rather than relying only on self-restraint.

# 9. Playable Character Eligibility

Not every NPC must become playable.

A character becomes eligible when the game can preserve:

- a stable identity or declared continuity;
- embodiment and location;
- a knowledge envelope;
- relationships and obligations;
- meaningful agency;
- safe control boundaries;
- persistence;
- a reason for perspective transfer.

Children, vulnerable adults, detainees, patients, and people under coercion require additional review. Playability must not convert vulnerability into spectacle or grant the player authority the character does not possess.

# 10. Death, Reconstitution, and Forks

Death may produce several distinct playable possibilities:

- continue as another living character;
- resume a legally accepted reconstitution;
- resume a disputed reconstitution;
- play a fork as a new person;
- follow a household member or successor;
- remain in the same epoch through another perspective;
- advance to later history.

No continuation inherits all previous authority automatically.

A returned office-holder may have:

- personal continuity;
- disputed property claims;
- no current mandate;
- incomplete memories;
- a successor who is lawfully serving;
- family members who do not recognize the relationship as unchanged.

# 11. Interstellar and Deep-Time Play

Multi-character play is the primary way Symtropy can preserve human-scale continuity across galactic time.

The player may:

- begin as a seed-vessel founder;
- later inhabit a child born during transit;
- play an arrival-generation engineer;
- return to an Earth character decades later;
- inhabit a route worker during reconnection;
- revisit the founder through reconstitution;
- continue as an alien or machine participant affected by the route.

This does not require every character to exist in a different timeline. Most of the strongest political stories occur because several playable lives coexist within one causally valid history.

# 12. Multiplayer

In multiplayer:

- different players may control different characters in one household or institution;
- one player may transfer a character seat only through explicit rules;
- host authority does not grant character authority;
- a disconnected player does not freeze the character unless the scenario declares stasis;
- consent is required before another player assumes a private character;
- worldline branches preserve ancestry and do not duplicate unique state back into the parent;
- PvP conflict does not expose private account data or cross-character knowledge.

# 13. Political Epic Without Universal Cynicism

The multi-character system may support dynastic conflict, route politics, succession crises, betrayal, war, and contested inheritance.

It must also support:

- orderly succession;
- durable friendship;
- competent administration;
- voluntary retirement;
- successful institutions;
- peaceful adoption;
- reconciliation;
- workers preventing elite conflict;
- families uninterested in ruling;
- quiet generations in which ordinary life improves.

The setting should not imply that cruelty is inherently more realistic than care.

# 14. Hard Invariants

1. A player account is not a diegetic sovereign.
2. Characters do not share knowledge without a valid transfer.
3. Characters do not share assets without a valid institutional relationship.
4. Same-worldline characters continue living when inactive.
5. Divergent-worldline characters cannot exchange causal state.
6. Perspective switching never duplicates a unique person.
7. Reconstitution does not automatically restore office.
8. Playability does not erase refusal, privacy, or dependency.
9. Chronicle truth and character knowledge remain visibly distinct.
10. The game must preserve the possibility that history continues without any currently controlled character.

## Production Maxim

> **A great political chronicle is not one immortal protagonist wearing many bodies. It is many people inheriting a world none of them fully owns or understands.**
