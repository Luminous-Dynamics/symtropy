# Symtropy Game Constitution

## One-Sentence Promise

**Symtropy is a first-person civilization sandbox in which players explore living worlds, survive danger, invent physical systems, build societies, fight over possible futures, and leave histories that remain visible after they are gone.**

## The Center of the Game

Symtropy is about **lived civilizational agency**.

```text
The player enters a world that already has causes.
They learn enough to act without ever knowing everything.
They survive, discover, create, contend, cooperate, and belong.
Their actions change what people and places can become.
The world remembers the consequences that matter.
```

The governing sequence is:

```text
enter → perceive → choose → act → transform → inhabit → remember
```

No single subsystem is the center of Symtropy.

Not water.
Not governance.
Not factories.
Not combat.
Not aliens.
Not simulation.
Not decentralization.
Not the Field Deck.

The game exists in the **interaction** among them.

## The Nine Product Pillars

### 1. Embodied Adventure

The player exists through a body in a material place.

Terrain, weather, gravity, visibility, weight, wounds, tools, vehicles, heat, pressure, distance, sound, and physical condition matter. Menus may help coordinate action, but they may not replace the sensation of doing it.

The player should enjoy moving, handling, aiming, driving, climbing, carrying, building, and operating machines before understanding every system behind them.

### 2. Exploration, Discovery, and Awe

The world must reward curiosity that is not reducible to quest completion.

A discovery can be:

```text
a route that changes logistics
a hidden machine process
a surviving community
a dangerous weather pattern
a forgotten art form
a species with an unexpected role
a physical anomaly
a deep-time archive
a ruined ship
a nonhuman form of agency
a view that makes the player stop moving
```

Discovery must create some combination of wonder, possibility, danger, knowledge, or future consequence.

### 3. Construction, Invention, and Mastery

Players do not only restore an inherited world. They create capabilities that did not exist before.

They may build:

```text
tools
weapons
vehicles
habitats
farms
factories
roads
networks
robots
institutions
ships
cities
cultural spaces
```

Construction should change how the world can be traversed, supplied, defended, understood, inhabited, and imagined.

Mastery comes from understanding material, energetic, computational, ecological, and social constraints—not from climbing a flat numerical ladder.

### 4. Living Worlds

Settlements, factions, ecologies, economies, machines, and cultures respond over time.

The game is not a static map decorated with scripted quests. Causes propagate. People interpret events. Resources move. Habitats recover or degrade. Institutions learn or harden. Routes open and close. Repeated choices become customs, resentments, technologies, and precedents.

Simulation must remain legible enough that players can form useful models without seeing every variable.

### 5. Conflict, Risk, and Courage

Symtropy must support dangerous, skillful, exciting play.

Combat, pursuit, defense, sabotage, rescue, hostile weather, hazardous industry, raids, war, containment failure, and first-contact escalation are valid parts of the game.

Moral depth may complicate conflict, but it may not make conflict mechanically dull.

Threats should create readable tactical problems. Victory should involve skill, preparation, teamwork, and sometimes restraint.

### 6. Society, Culture, and Belonging

Players build more than bases and supply chains.

They participate in:

```text
friendship
rivalry
labor
trade
care
education
art
music
belief
ritual
fashion
sport
pleasure
mourning
celebration
governance
family and chosen kin
```

Civilization must answer not only how life survives, but what life is for.

Players who do not want formal political office must still be able to shape society through craft, trade, care, art, teaching, defense, discovery, and everyday presence.

### 7. Delight, Play, and Self-Expression

Symtropy cannot be only worthy, difficult, or responsible.

It must contain joy that is not merely a productivity buff:

```text
racing a rover across salt flats
building a beautiful room
playing music at a repair festival
collecting strange minerals
raising an animal companion
cooking a regional meal
customizing a ship
watching an alien weather event
sharing a joke after danger
visiting a city because it is alive
```

The world should give players reasons to remain after the emergency is solved.

### 8. Planetary and Cosmic Horizon

The first game begins locally, but the horizon includes regions, continents, oceans, orbital habitats, moons, planets, interplanetary logistics, alien contact, worldline diplomacy, and interstellar seed civilizations.

The horizon must be perceptible early through skies, ruins, signals, vehicles, institutions, stories, and technologies.

It must not become an excuse to implement every scale at once.

### 9. Durable History

Most actions remain ephemeral. Meaningful consequences become history.

The Chronicle may preserve:

```text
foundings
inventions
discoveries
restorations
betrayals
wars
treaties
migrations
extinctions
liberations
public failures
cultural works
deaths
worldline forks
```

The player should be able to point at a changed landscape, route, settlement, machine, relationship, or custom and say:

```text
That exists because something happened here.
```

No single verb is the game.

A central feature must connect several verbs and produce a visible change.

## The Scale Ladder

### Hand Scale

Tools, weapons, samples, cables, wounds, doors, controls, components, gestures.

### Site Scale

Ruins, caves, factories, habitats, ships, laboratories, gardens, temples, dungeons.

### Settlement Scale

Homes, workshops, farms, markets, care, culture, utilities, defense, public spaces.

### Regional Scale

Roads, rivers, convoys, ecosystems, trade, migration, weather, warfare, communication.

### Planetary Scale

Climate, biospheres, orbital access, continental politics, large infrastructure, planetary history.

### Worldline Scale

Forks, migrations, confluence, portable identity, cross-world treaties, historical ancestry.

Every scale must create distinctive decisions. Larger scales may not erase the tactile reality of smaller ones.

## Player Fantasies

Symtropy should support players who primarily want to be:

```text
explorer
builder
engineer
mechanic
driver
pilot
fighter
logistician
scientist
ecologist
medic
trader
leader
diplomat
archivist
artist
performer
farmer
teacher
storyteller
```

A healthy cooperative session lets several fantasies contribute without requiring every participant to enjoy every system.

Solo play must remain viable through delegation, automation, NPC collaboration, pacing changes, and reduced coordination burden—not by flattening the world into a simpler game.

## The Player Freedom Rule

Depth is valuable only when players can approach it through multiple surfaces.

For major systems, prefer:

```text
physical interaction
visual or assisted interaction
expert terminal or planning interaction
```

The player should not be forced to become a programmer, politician, economist, or systems engineer to enjoy the game.

Those roles should provide additional agency and mastery, not basic permission to participate.

## Action Before Explanation

The player should first:

```text
see a problem
feel its stakes
try an action
observe a response
```

Then the game may reveal deeper political, historical, ecological, or technical meaning.

Lore may deepen satisfying action. It may not substitute for it.

## The First Game: Seedworks

Seedworks is the first regional proof that Symtropy’s systems can coexist.

Firstlight Basin should contain:

```text
a settlement worth returning to
terrain worth crossing
multiple systems worth transforming
danger worth preparing for
people with lives beyond the crisis
a machine ecology with tactical presence
resource movement with real geography
construction that opens new possibility
culture that is already forming
visible orbital and planetary horizons
more than one viable future
```

Water remains important because life depends on it, not because it defines the product.

The Old Waterworks remains a strong site. It is not the thesis of the game.

## The Regional Proof Rule

Do not build the whole galaxy first.

**Build one region where the whole game is already trying to emerge.**

The first regional proof must demonstrate:

```text
embodied traversal
curiosity-driven discovery
combat or meaningful danger
physical transformation
resource movement
mobility transition
living NPC response
cultural life
player expression
persistent consequence
cosmic horizon
```

Not every feature must appear at full depth. Every pillar must be visible in credible seed form.

## The Anti-Monopoly Rule

No subsystem may monopolize:

```text
screen time
progression
meaning
production budget
required player attention
```

A slice fails if one system can be removed only by making the rest of the game incoherent, unless that system is the embodied world itself.

Examples of unhealthy monopoly:

```text
every mission ends in a vote
every discovery becomes a repair job
every conflict becomes combat
every progression gate requires factory throughput
every social bond becomes a reputation meter
every mystery is solved by the Field Deck
every consequence becomes Chronicle bureaucracy
```

## Optional Depth, Mandatory Legibility

Players may ignore many advanced systems temporarily.

They may not be confused about:

```text
what they can do
why an immediate result occurred
what danger is present
what changed in the world
where deeper information can be found
```

The game may preserve uncertainty about causes and motives. It must not preserve uncertainty about basic controls and feedback.

## Friction Has a Budget

Materiality, travel, maintenance, law, and logistics create meaning, but all friction must justify itself.

Friction is valid when it creates:

```text
skill
planning
cooperation
tension
place
tradeoffs
story
```

Friction should be reduced or abstracted when it creates only:

```text
repetition
waiting
menu churn
inventory housekeeping
unreadable punishment
travel with no decisions
```

## Consequence Without Paralysis

Choices should matter, but players must still feel free to experiment.

Prefer:

```text
repairable damage
debt
changed relationships
new obligations
scarred but continuing worlds
```

Use irreversible loss sparingly, clearly, and at moments with enough context to make responsibility meaningful.

## Moral Texture Rule

Every serious conflict may contain understandable motives, but not every scene needs to become a symmetrical ethical seminar.

The world must contain:

```text
kindness without a catch
cruelty without sophistication
petty conflict
humor
error
ambition
cowardice
beauty
ordinary generosity
```

People are not only embodiments of philosophical positions.

## Production Truth Rule

A design claim is not proven by prose.

It becomes product truth only when supported by some combination of:

```text
playable implementation
measured prototype
validated content pipeline
performance evidence
playtest evidence
```

Horizon concepts remain valuable, but may not silently become milestone requirements.

## North-Star Experience Tests

Symtropy is moving toward its promise when players can truthfully say:

```text
I wanted to see what was beyond the ridge.
I learned how this place worked by acting in it.
I made something that changed what was possible.
My role mattered without making everyone play like me.
The danger was exciting and readable.
The settlement felt alive when I returned.
I had something enjoyable to do after the crisis.
The world remembered an outcome I cared about.
I could glimpse a future larger than the current map.
```

## Final Rule

```text
Symtropy is not the sum of its systems.
It is the experience of living among their consequences.
```
