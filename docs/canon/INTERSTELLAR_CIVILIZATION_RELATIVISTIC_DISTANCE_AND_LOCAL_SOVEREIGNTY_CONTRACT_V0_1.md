# Interstellar Civilization, Relativistic Distance, and Local Sovereignty Contract

## Owned Question

**What does civilization mean when communities can no longer share a practical present, rescue may be impossible, and every journey can separate travelers from the political, biological, and cultural world that launched them?**

## Core Thesis

Interstellar space is not a larger map.

It is a change in the meaning of coordination.

```text
planetary distance delays action
interplanetary distance fragments knowledge
interstellar distance fragments historical time
relativistic travel fragments lived chronology
```

A star-faring civilization cannot remain one continuously commanding polity merely because its descendants share a name, archive, or origin.

> **No authority may claim a shared present across stars when physics has made that present unavailable.**

Interstellar civilization therefore consists of related but locally sovereign societies connected through inherited evidence, delayed messages, negotiated standards, autonomous missions, cultural ancestry, and occasional physical crossings.

# 1. Scope Boundary

This contract governs the horizon beyond ordinary solar-system civilization:

```text
sub-light interstellar probes
slow arks and generation vessels
relativistic crewed or machine missions
stellar relay chains
long-delay diplomacy
precursor artifacts and dormant infrastructure
rare infrastructure-locked transit
interstellar worldline ancestry
```

It does not make interstellar travel part of the Seedworks representative build.

All capabilities remain `D3 / I0` until bounded fixtures demonstrate physical conservation, causal messaging, persistence, rights, migration, replay, and player comprehension.

# 2. No Shared Immediate Present

Two settlements separated by light-years cannot participate in one ordinary real-time government.

They may share:

```text
constitutional ancestry
standards
archives
mutual-recognition rules
long vows
delayed treaties
scientific observations
identity and source-chain proofs
cultural memory
```

They do not automatically share:

```text
current law
current emergency conditions
current leadership
current resource state
current consent
current military authority
current public opinion
current interpretation of old promises
```

A message from another star is historical evidence by the time it arrives.

## Authority Rule

Remote authority must declare:

```text
knowledge cutoff
assumed conditions
scope
expiry
local deviation rights
review path
```

A distant order that cannot observe current survival conditions is advice, mandate history, or treaty input. It is not self-executing authority.

# 3. Relativistic Chronology

Travelers moving at relativistic velocity may experience less time than the communities they leave behind.

The game must distinguish:

```text
proper time experienced by a traveler
coordinate time used by a reference frame
local civic time at origin
local civic time at destination
message creation and receipt times
worldline branch ancestry
```

## Design Consequences

A crew may arrive to find:

- their sponsor polity dissolved;
- their mission repudiated;
- their language archaic;
- their family line centuries older;
- their destination already inhabited;
- their legal credentials historically authentic but no longer current;
- their ship treated as a protected archive, dangerous relic, migrant polity, or military uncertainty.

Relativistic travel never grants superior legitimacy.

> **Arriving from the past does not make the traveler sovereign over the future.**

# 4. Local Sovereignty Under Causal Isolation

Every enduring interstellar settlement must possess enough authority to preserve life without waiting for another star.

Minimum local powers include:

```text
life-support control
emergency response
care and public health
resource allocation
labor and exit protections
local justice
contact and quarantine decisions
settlement succession
archive interpretation
mission reconsideration
```

These powers cannot be permanently leased back to a financing planet, launch authority, corporation, fleet, or archive institution.

## Parent-Polity Standing

A launching polity may retain:

```text
historical claim to origin
contractual repayment rights above the rights floor
archive and rescue obligations
representation in pre-arrival governance
shared intellectual-property or commons duties
negotiated diplomatic standing
```

It does not automatically retain:

```text
ownership of descendants
control of minimum life support
permanent labor claims
exclusive territorial sovereignty
power to prevent constitutional renewal
right to classify local dissent as mutiny forever
```

# 5. Forms of Interstellar Community

Interstellar civilization is not one institutional form.

## 5.1 Ancestral Commonwealth

Independent systems preserve mutual recognition, cultural ancestry, archives, standards, and voluntary aid.

Strength:

```text
shared identity without central command
```

Failure mode:

```text
nostalgia presented as authority
```

## 5.2 Long-Vow Network

Communities inherit commitments intended to survive centuries:

```text
protect a biosphere
maintain a relay
preserve a language corpus
warn others of a threat
provide refuge to future arrivals
avoid a contact zone
```

Every generation must be able to review the vow's meaning, burden, and legitimacy.

Failure mode:

```text
dead purpose enforced through sacred continuity
```

## 5.3 Relay Confederation

Societies coordinate through a chain of observatories, archives, couriers, and signal relays.

Strength:

```text
knowledge continuity and contact resilience
```

Failure mode:

```text
relay custodians become gatekeepers of history
```

## 5.4 Ark Diaspora

Multiple vessels or settlements share ancestry but develop divergent cultures and laws.

Strength:

```text
adaptation and plurality
```

Failure mode:

```text
founder mission used to deny descendants' autonomy
```

## 5.5 Contact Compact

Multiple human and nonhuman societies coordinate scoped contact, quarantine, evidence, noninterference, or sanctuary rules.

Strength:

```text
shared restraint under uncertainty
```

Failure mode:

```text
ancient quarantine becomes permanent domination
```

## 5.6 Gate Commonwealth

Rare societies coordinate access to infrastructure capable of shortening interstellar transit.

This is a horizon form only. Gate operation cannot become ordinary personal travel.

Strength:

```text
reconnection of isolated civilizations
```

Failure mode:

```text
control of distance becomes control of political existence
```

# 6. Interstellar Rights Floor

Distance never suspends fundamental protections.

Minimum rights include:

```text
right to local survival authority
right not to inherit compulsory labor or debt through ancestry alone
right to mission reconsideration
right to truthful transit and latency information
right to refuse irreversible launch when consent is not valid
right to minimum life support independent of political loyalty
right to local constitutional renewal
right to preserve or relinquish inherited identity
right to receive historical messages without treating them as present commands
right to noncontact and quarantine appeal where physically possible
right to archive one's own divergence
```

For generation ships and arks:

```text
children are not mission equipment
future descendants are not pre-consenting passengers
maintenance necessity does not erase labor rights
closed habitat dependence does not permit ownership of persons
```

# 7. Interstellar Identity and Source Chains

A source chain can prove continuity of authorship and ancestry.

It cannot prove that an old authority remains legitimate.

Interstellar identity must separate:

```text
cryptographic continuity
biographical continuity
body continuity
legal standing
local citizenship
mission role
archive significance
worldline ancestry
```

An arriving traveler may be:

- cryptographically authentic;
- biologically continuous;
- historically important;
- legally obsolete;
- locally untrusted;
- civically entitled to due process but not command.

# 8. Cultural and Linguistic Divergence

Interstellar separation should create real cultural distance.

Possible outcomes:

```text
language drift
ritual reinterpretation
new kinship systems
adapted bodies
machine-human co-governance
mission myths
calendar divergence
new ecological ethics
old Earth identities becoming historical rather than immediate
```

The game must not treat divergence as corruption by default.

Shared ancestry can coexist with genuine difference.

# 9. Interstellar Economy

An interstellar economy cannot operate as one synchronous market.

Most exchange consists of:

```text
information
scientific observations
blueprints
cultural works
verified warnings
identity attestations
slow physical cargo
rare migrants
long-term commitments
```

Prices, currencies, laws, and institutions may change before delivery.

No interstellar contract may assume that the recipient society remains politically unchanged when the cargo or message arrives.

# 10. Security and War

Interstellar conflict is constrained by distance but not made harmless.

Threats include:

```text
self-replicating probes
relativistic impact threats
dormant sabotage
hostile mission inheritance
signal deception
archive poisoning
gate seizure
quarantine absolutism
slow colonial encroachment
```

The design must resist turning these threats into convenient total-war permission.

Unknown origin, silence, age, machine substrate, or unusual trajectory is not sufficient proof of hostility.

# 11. Worldline Divergence

Interstellar societies will diverge both historically and mechanically.

Worldline relationships may be:

```text
ancestral branch
independent continuation
message-only relation
ark descendant
contact-zone neighbor
gate-reconnected polity
archive reconstruction
claimed but disputed continuity
```

Forks must preserve:

```text
message ancestry
asset ancestry
mission ancestry
identity ancestry
treaty ancestry
known uncertainty
```

A fork cannot duplicate the same physical vessel, gate, cargo, person, or unique source chain into multiple transferable realities without explicit worldline rules.

# 12. Player Experience

Interstellar play should produce:

```text
wonder at scale
responsibility before departure
grieving of unreachable outcomes
uncertainty about what survived
reunion with changed descendants
translation across historical distance
care for systems one may never revisit
```

It should not primarily produce:

```text
star-map checklist progression
routine warp commuting
instant empire management
planetary reskins
remote omniscience
```

# 13. Representative Proof

The first proof is deliberately small:

```text
one origin habitat
one destination habitat
one autonomous probe
one delayed constitutional message
one relativistic traveler
one inherited mission conflict
one local decision that contradicts the launch plan
```

The proof succeeds when players understand why the destination has the right to differ and why the traveler remains a person without becoming an automatic ruler.

# 14. Hard Invariants

```text
no shared immediate truth across light-years
no remote command without knowledge cutoff and local deviation rights
no founder authority made permanent by distance
no descendants treated as mission property
no relativistic traveler granted automatic sovereignty
no source-chain authenticity confused with current legitimacy
no synchronous interstellar market
no physical transit without conserved mass, energy, time, and capacity
no worldline fork without ancestry and uniqueness rules
no casual FTL in ordinary vehicles
```

# Acceptance Gate

Interstellar civilization is ready for implementation only when a bounded fixture demonstrates:

- causal message delay;
- distinct local knowledge;
- local survival authority;
- relativistic chronology preservation;
- mission reconsideration;
- identity without automatic command;
- worldline ancestry;
- player comprehension of why distance changes legitimacy.
