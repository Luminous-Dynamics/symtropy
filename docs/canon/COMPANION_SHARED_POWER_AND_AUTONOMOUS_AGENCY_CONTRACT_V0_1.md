# Companion Shared Power and Autonomous Agency Contract

## Owned Question

**How can companions make the player more capable and emotionally invested without becoming obedient equipment, approval meters, or portable institutional keys?**

## Core Thesis

A companion is an inhabitant whose life intersects with the player's strongly enough to create repeated coordination, mutual knowledge, obligation, conflict, and history.

Companion power is relational and situated.

It may arise through:

```text
embodied skill
perception
social trust
institutional standing
historical knowledge
shared procedure
mutual prediction
access to places and people
willingness to accept responsibility
```

It does not arise because the player filled an affection bar.

> **A companion expands what the player can responsibly attempt. The player does not acquire the companion as an ability.**

## Prime Directives

1. **Companions retain independent agency.** They may refuse, reinterpret, delay, negotiate, withdraw, or leave.
2. **No approval economy.** Gifts, praise, or dialogue optimization cannot purchase loyalty, intimacy, consent, or political obedience.
3. **Power remains scoped.** A companion's office, credentials, contacts, body, memories, and relationships remain theirs.
4. **Coordination must be learned.** Strong partnerships emerge through repeated shared practice, not one cinematic choice.
5. **Disagreement is compatible with closeness.** A companion may love, trust, or respect the player while opposing a plan.
6. **No universal combat party.** Companions may be medics, technicians, witnesses, organizers, artists, caregivers, drivers, diplomats, machines, or nonhuman agents.
7. **Absence is real.** Companions continue work, relationships, illness, projects, and political life while not traveling with the player.
8. **Dependency must remain visible.** Care needs, life support, housing, legal status, family obligations, and bodily limits cannot be erased for convenience.
9. **Death and separation are not interchangeable content resets.** Loss must preserve consequences, records, grief, succession, and possible source-chain uncertainty.
10. **No companion exists only for player validation.** Each flagship companion requires a life that can remain meaningful without the player.

# 1. Companion Eligibility

An NPC becomes a companion candidate when all of the following are true:

- they possess an authored identity and independent project;
- their embodied or social capabilities create meaningful coordination;
- their relationship with the player can change through history;
- they can operate under companion and non-companion simulation modes;
- their refusal and departure states are authored;
- their household, institution, or community remains represented;
- their inclusion does not imply romance;
- their absence from the party does not erase their relevance.

Companion status is not a metaphysical category. It is a gameplay relationship state.

A person may travel with the player temporarily without becoming a persistent companion. A close friend may never join expeditions. A former companion may remain one of the most important people in the player's life.

# 2. Forms of Shared Power

## 2.1 Operational Power

A companion may perform a concurrent physical or procedural role:

- stabilize a patient while the player intervenes;
- hold isolation boundaries during repair;
- drive while the player operates a station;
- maintain a crowd corridor;
- inspect a second subsystem;
- carry and protect evidence;
- preserve life support while the player explores;
- operate tools requiring another body or viewpoint.

Operational power is limited by location, tools, fatigue, willingness, authority, and risk.

## 2.2 Perceptual Power

Companions notice different worlds.

A mechanic hears failing harmonics. A watershed steward recognizes altered flow. A machine witness notices log discontinuity. A performer reads crowd timing. A person raised in a corporate habitat recognizes a clause others treat as ordinary signage.

Perceptual insight appears as grounded observations, questions, warnings, or optional actions. It must not become omniscient highlighting.

## 2.3 Social Power

Companions carry relationships the player does not own:

```text
family ties
union standing
religious credibility
professional trust
criminal or informal contacts
migrant networks
machine lineage
cultural fluency
personal debts
historical resentment
```

A companion may introduce the player, testify, translate, request help, or open negotiation. They may also refuse to expose a contact or spend social standing for a player objective.

## 2.4 Institutional Power

Some companions hold office, licenses, custody authority, command, clinical responsibility, or witness standing.

These powers remain governed by law and role. A companion cannot be treated as a portable permit. Misusing office may create removal, liability, scandal, injury, or relationship rupture.

## 2.5 Historical Power

Companions may know:

- prior versions of a place;
- a deceased worker's habits;
- an older dialect;
- a hidden maintenance path;
- how a public story changed;
- what the player was like before reconstitution;
- why an institution fears a particular failure.

Historical knowledge may be incomplete, biased, private, or contested.

## 2.6 Relational Power

Repeated shared work creates coordination not available to strangers:

- silent hand signals;
- trusted handovers;
- anticipatory tool preparation;
- known de-escalation roles;
- protected vulnerability disclosures;
- accurate challenge under stress;
- recognition of fatigue, pain, fear, or tunnel vision;
- recovery after conflict.

This is the primary progression vector.

# 3. Relationship Progression

Companion progression records **shared practices and negotiated permissions**, not a universal level.

Examples:

```text
trusted clinical handover
shared workshop shorthand
coordinated evacuation protocol
mutual custody authority for one evidence class
agreed emergency override limits
known sensory accommodations
established disagreement procedure
family introduction
shared mourning ritual
permission to access one private archive
```

Progression must remain domain-specific.

A companion may trust the player medically and distrust them politically. They may coordinate perfectly in a vehicle while refusing to discuss family. They may grant public testimony but not private memory access.

# 4. Commands, Requests, and Delegation

The player does not issue universal commands.

Interactions fall into four classes:

```text
request — asks the companion to choose whether and how to act
delegation — grants scoped authority and resources
coordination call — invokes an agreed shared procedure
emergency direction — time-critical instruction under a recognized authority context
```

Every request is evaluated through:

- current relationship and trust by domain;
- values and obligations;
- competence and tools;
- risk to self and others;
- legal or institutional authority;
- fatigue, injury, fear, and capacity;
- competing commitments;
- uncertainty;
- prior promises and failures.

A refused request should expose a reason when the companion can safely express it. Refusal is not a malfunction.

# 5. Companion Initiative

Companions may:

- propose plans;
- begin ordinary work;
- assist another NPC;
- investigate something within their role;
- protect a boundary;
- spend their own resources;
- call for help;
- leave the immediate area;
- preserve evidence;
- challenge the player;
- negotiate with other companions;
- make mistakes.

Initiative must remain bounded by perceived state and authored action classes. It may not silently create facts, items, permissions, or completed work.

# 6. Party Composition

There is no assumption that every expedition supports the same party size or roles.

Possible arrangements include:

- solo with IRIS;
- one close companion;
- a temporary professional crew;
- a household traveling together;
- a vehicle team with station roles;
- a diplomatic delegation;
- a rescue group;
- a machine-human work unit;
- a nonhuman contact ensemble.

Large groups should be represented through crew and command systems rather than every member behaving like a conventional RPG follower.

# 7. Romance and Intimacy

Romance is independent from companion utility.

A companion may be:

- unavailable;
- uninterested;
- a friend;
- chosen family;
- a rival;
- an ex-partner;
- a professional collaborator;
- a romantic partner who does not travel;
- a traveling partner with no romantic interest.

Companion usefulness must never increase pressure toward romance. Romantic refusal cannot remove professional competence or friendship content.

# 8. Conflict and Repair

Relationship conflict may arise from:

- broken promises;
- unsafe methods;
- political disagreement;
- divided loyalties;
- misuse of authority;
- privacy violations;
- neglect;
- unequal sacrifice;
- public humiliation;
- grief;
- incompatible future plans.

Repair may require:

```text
acknowledgment
material restitution
changed procedure
time
third-party mediation
public correction
boundary respect
acceptance that closeness will not be restored
```

A successful repair does not necessarily return the relationship to its prior form.

# 9. Departure

Companions may leave because they:

- complete the shared objective;
- return to family or work;
- accept office;
- become injured or exhausted;
- disagree with the player's direction;
- fear the player;
- pursue a different project;
- enter a relationship elsewhere;
- migrate;
- require care;
- refuse a worldline commitment.

Departure must not always be framed as betrayal.

A companion who leaves may remain emotionally, politically, or historically central.

# 10. Failure Conditions

The companion system fails if:

- friendship functions as a currency;
- companions become stat sticks;
- every disagreement is solved by persuasion;
- companions abandon households without consequence;
- player orders override law, consent, or professional duty;
- companion initiative invents authoritative state;
- romance gates the strongest abilities;
- refusal is mechanically punished;
- death is replaced by a generic successor with identical function;
- IRIS reveals private companion cognition;
- every companion exists to admire, challenge, or heal the player.

## Canonical Summary

```text
Companions are not equipment.
Trust is domain-specific.
Coordination is learned.
Authority remains scoped.
Refusal is valid.
Absence preserves life.
Power creates responsibility.
```
