# Worldline Long-Horizon and Endgame Contract

## Owned Question

**What does Symtropy become after players can survive, build, automate, govern, and travel—and how does a mature worldline remain meaningful without invalidating early play?**

## Core Thesis

Symtropy does not end when the player reaches a final technology tier.

Late play shifts from personal scarcity toward **stewardship under scale**.

```text
survival asks whether life continues
construction asks what becomes possible
governance asks who controls possibility
long-horizon play asks what deserves to persist across generations and worldlines
```

Endgame is not infinite numerical growth. It is the ability to undertake larger commitments while facing wider consequences.

# 1. Long-Horizon Phases

These are capability regimes, not mandatory linear chapters.

## 1.1 Local Viability

The player can survive, contribute, and understand one settlement or crew.

## 1.2 Regional Interdependence

Multiple settlements, ecosystems, routes, and factions become mutually dependent.

## 1.3 Planetary Institution

Players can shape watershed agreements, transit systems, climate adaptation, biosphere policy, planetary defense, or shared archives.

## 1.4 Orbital and Interplanetary Society

Habitats, launch systems, fleets, rescue law, delayed communication, and off-world labor become normal concerns.

## 1.5 Xeno-Political Maturity

The worldline can recognize and negotiate with nonhuman agents without treating every difference as resource or threat.

## 1.6 Worldline Stewardship

Players can fork, migrate, preserve, compare, federate, or attempt Confluence with other histories.

# 2. Endgame Pillars

A mature worldline should support several simultaneous ambitions.

```text
planetary repair
industrial or logistical reach
cultural flourishing
scientific discovery
political federation
military deterrence or demilitarization
species and habitat stewardship
interstellar infrastructure
archive continuity
worldline translation
```

No single ambition is the universal win condition.

# 3. Non-Terminal Victory

A worldline may achieve a declared victory state without ending play.

Victory families:

```text
Resilient Planet
Open Federation
Living Biosphere Compact
Interplanetary Commons
Archive Continuity
First Contact Peace
Machine-Human Covenant
Great Work Completed
Null Ecology Contained
Confluence Attempted
```

Each victory must declare:

```text
what was achieved
what remains unresolved
who paid the cost
what institutions maintain it
what new vulnerabilities were created
```

# 4. Great Works

Great Works are long projects with visible intermediate states.

Examples:

```text
continental transit spine
planetary climate observatory
orbital debris commons
public shipyard network
biosphere restoration corridor
multi-species translation city
worldline archive lattice
deep-space rescue chain
planetary energy ring
```

Great Works require:

```text
material supply
knowledge
labor
maintenance institutions
political standing
site access
risk governance
intergenerational commitment
```

They should unlock new forms of play rather than merely produce a completion cinematic.

# 5. Mature-World Pressure

Success changes problems rather than eliminating them.

Examples:

```text
abundance increases migration and ecological draw
safe routes create dependency and strategic targets
a powerful federation must preserve local autonomy
a restored biosphere conflicts with industrial expansion
advanced automation risks deskilling and authority drift
interstellar contact creates quarantine and translation pressure
```

The game must avoid manufacturing arbitrary disasters solely to punish success. New pressures should arise from scale, dependency, external events, and real tradeoffs.

# 6. Legacy Without Veteran Monopoly

Mature players and institutions may possess enormous capability. New players must still have meaningful agency.

Rules:

1. Core embodied play remains useful at every scale.
2. Mature infrastructure creates accessible roles, training, and expeditions.
3. New players can discover local truths veterans overlooked.
4. No veteran should own permanent monopoly over identity, water, air, spawn access, or essential tutorial space.
5. Worldline charters must define newcomer standing and exit rights.
6. Legacy power requires maintenance and public exposure.

# 7. Succession and Institutional Memory

Long-lived worlds need continuity beyond individual attendance.

Systems:

```text
delegated stewardship
machine and NPC operations
public maintenance schedules
succession charters
archive handoff
role rotation
emergency recovery keys
abandonment procedures
```

A worldline should degrade gracefully when leaders disappear. It should not become permanently hostage to inactive accounts.

# 8. Worldline Forks

Forking is legitimate when communities cannot reconcile:

```text
rules
history interpretation
mod set
risk tolerance
PvP policy
resource reset
major political decision
```

A fork records:

```text
ancestry
shared history cutoff
portable identities and inventories by policy
copied or excluded infrastructure
charter differences
archive claims
debts and obligations
```

Forking should not be frictionless duplication of scarce cross-world assets.

# 9. Migration

Players, NPCs, machines, blueprints, knowledge, and cultural practices may migrate under explicit rules.

Migration questions:

```text
what is portable
what remains site-bound
what credentials transfer
what quarantine applies
what obligations follow
what memories and scars persist
```

# 10. Confluence

Confluence is a negotiated relationship among divergent histories, not a magical server merge.

Possible forms:

```text
archive comparison
trade corridor
mutual recognition
shared expedition
partial institutional federation
blueprint exchange
historical reconciliation
full state translation attempt
```

Full Confluence requires conflict resolution for incompatible terrain, deaths, laws, ownership, and ecological states. Many worldlines should choose limited relationship instead.

# 11. Seasonal and Live Operation Boundaries

Symtropy may host evolving worldlines, but must not use destructive resets as the default content engine.

Allowed:

```text
new regions
external pressures
seasonal climate patterns
migrations
festivals
new expeditions
worldline forks
optional challenge worlds
```

Avoid:

```text
routine inventory wipes
mandatory narrative expiration
fear-of-missing-out power
world changes that ignore local player history
```

# 12. Late-Game Conflict

Conflict may operate at:

```text
expedition
route
settlement
regional
orbital
planetary
worldline
```

Higher scale should emphasize logistics, intelligence, diplomacy, public support, repair, and recovery—not simply larger hit-point pools.

Planetary destruction, biosphere sterilization, permanent population captivity, and irreversible worldline deletion require exceptional consensual rules and should not be ordinary PvP tools.

# 13. Endgame Economy

Late economies should shift toward:

```text
maintenance
coordination
rare environmental conditions
precision capacity
trusted labor
knowledge
transport windows
political legitimacy
risk absorption
```

Raw material abundance does not remove bottlenecks in care, time, habitat, translation, or trust.

# 14. Chronicle and Legacy

Mature worldlines produce readable eras.

```text
Founding
Expansion
Crisis
Reform
Contact
Federation
Great Work
Fork or Confluence
```

The Chronicle should summarize causal arcs while preserving access to evidence and dissenting interpretations.

# 15. Endgame Acceptance Evidence

The contract succeeds when:

```text
players can name several worthwhile ambitions after basic survival is solved
early tools and local spaces remain relevant
new players find meaningful roles in mature worlds
major projects create intermediate play and visible change
success generates understandable responsibilities rather than arbitrary punishment
worldline forks preserve ancestry and difference
victory states feel consequential without forcing the community to stop playing
```
