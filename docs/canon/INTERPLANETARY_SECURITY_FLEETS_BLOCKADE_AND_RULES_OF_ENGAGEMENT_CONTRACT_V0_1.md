# Interplanetary Security, Fleets, Blockade, and Rules of Engagement Contract

## Owned Question

**How does organized force operate across a solar system without turning every ship into a warship, every port into a target, and every communication delay into permission for atrocity?**

## Core Thesis

A fleet is not a stack of combat power.

It is a moving arrangement of:

```text
people
fuel and energy
life support
sensors
weapons
repair
rescue
law
communications
political intent
```

In interplanetary space, violence is unusually dangerous because habitats cannot retreat from vacuum, civilian and military logistics overlap, and help may be months away.

The core rule is:

> **The ability to destroy life support creates a greater duty of restraint, not a broader right to command.**

## Prime Directives

1. **Security is broader than combat.** Patrol, rescue, navigation, debris control, escort, inspection, and de-escalation are core fleet roles.
2. **No instant strategic command.** Local commanders operate under bounded mandates and stale information.
3. **No total-war default.** Civilian habitats, life-support systems, rescue traffic, medical vessels, and departure corridors receive heightened protection.
4. **No invisible blockade.** A blockade is physical control over routes, mass, communications, and survival supplies.
5. **No guilt by hull class.** Cargo ships, habitat tugs, salvage vessels, and armed escorts remain distinct legal and tactical entities.
6. **No surrender without survivability.** Accepting surrender creates obligations for air, water, medical care, custody, and communication.
7. **No boarding as consequence-free loot.** Boarding changes jurisdiction, evidence, body risk, custody, and escalation.
8. **No rescue exception for enemies.** Rescue duties continue after combat when physically feasible and safe enough to attempt.
9. **No military ownership of politics.** Fleets may execute lawful policy; they may not define permanent civilian order by operational necessity alone.
10. **No victory without demobilization.** Weapons, emergency authority, seized ports, and detention must have accountable end states.

# 1. Fleet Roles

System-scale fleet functions include:

```text
search and rescue
convoy escort
traffic safety
debris and hazard control
anti-piracy patrol
quarantine support
customs support
scientific protection
humanitarian logistics
territorial defense
deterrence
interdiction
combat
peacekeeping under treaty
```

A healthy civilization does not require every role to be owned by one military institution.

# 2. Force Composition

A fleet may contain:

```text
rescue cutters
patrol craft
cargo tenders
repair ships
medical vessels
sensor and relay craft
escort craft
interceptors
tugs
habitat ships
ammunition or energy support
command vessels
uncrewed or machine platforms
```

Combat capability depends on logistics and information, not only weapon count.

# 3. Strategic Authority Under Delay

Every deployment mandate defines:

```text
political objective
area and time scope
rules of engagement
protected traffic
inspection authority
detention authority
rescue obligations
escalation thresholds
local discretion
reporting and review
expiry and recall conditions
```

Orders arriving after material change may be adapted or rejected under the interplanetary latency contract.

## Commander's Duty

A commander must consider:

```text
current evidence
civilian consequence
crew and vessel survival
mission legality
feasible alternatives
future rescue and accountability
```

Obedience to stale command is not an automatic defense.

# 4. Detection, Identification, and Uncertainty

The system distinguishes:

```text
unknown contact
unresponsive contact
spoofed identity
armed contact
hostile act
hostile intent inferred
navigation hazard
distress behavior
quarantine concern
```

Weapons cannot lawfully engage based on a generic red marker alone.

Identification confidence has sources, uncertainty, and possible deception.

# 5. Rules of Engagement

Rules may authorize:

```text
observe
hail
shadow
warn
escort
inspect
interdict
jam within scope
disable
board
detain
use lethal force
```

Each step requires evidence and proportionality appropriate to context.

## Positive Identification

Positive identification is claim-specific:

```text
identity
cargo
weapon state
hostile action
authority
quarantine status
```

Knowing one does not prove the others.

# 6. Boarding

Boarding requires:

```text
legal basis
safe approach
pressure and atmosphere compatibility
crew and passenger estimate
medical and detention capacity
evidence plan
rules for private spaces and data
withdrawal contingency
```

Possible bases include:

```text
consent
distress rescue
customs authority
warrant
treaty inspection
active hostile action
abandoned hazard
```

Boarding outcomes include compliance, negotiation, rescue, seizure of specific contraband, detention, failed boarding, or escalation.

# 7. Capture, Detention, and Surrender

Surrender status must be communicated, received, and physically actionable.

A surrendering vessel retains:

```text
life-support rights
medical care
protection from summary violence
identity and property records
communication rights within safety limits
review of detention
```

Captor responsibilities include:

```text
stabilizing the vessel
accounting for people
separating evidence from plunder
preserving private records
providing care
arranging lawful custody or release
```

A blockade is represented through:

```text
patrol coverage
sensor confidence
intercept capability
route geometry
rules of engagement
port cooperation
communications control
resupply and maintenance
```

It affects:

```text
food
medicine
air and water consumables
parts
migration
information
insurance and markets
neutral traffic
```

## Blockade Legitimacy

The system evaluates:

```text
declared objective
scope
notice
neutral access
humanitarian exceptions
inspection process
appeal
civilian burden
duration
feasible alternatives
```

A blockade that makes civilian survival impossible becomes a mass-harm policy, not clean strategic pressure.

# 9. Protected Systems and Traffic

Heightened protection applies to:

```text
rescue beacons
medical traffic
civilian evacuation
life-support infrastructure
water and air processing
radiation shelters
unarmed archive and identity relays
noncombatant habitats
humanitarian cargo
```

Protection may be lost through specific misuse, but the system requires evidence and proportional response rather than category-wide revocation.

# 10. Weapons and Damage

Space combat should emphasize:

```text
sensors
position and timing
heat
power
communications
propulsion
radiators
life support
crew safety
mission kill versus destruction
```

Destruction is rarely the only or best objective.

Combat options include:

```text
force withdrawal
disable propulsion
disrupt weapons
separate hostile boarding party
protect convoy
break sensor lock
open escape corridor
compel inspection
```

# 11. Autonomous and Machine Weapons

Autonomous systems require:

```text
bounded target classes
confidence thresholds
abort channels
communication-loss behavior
protected-object logic
recorded decisions
post-action review
```

No autonomy may infer species, faction, or legal guilt from substrate alone.

Lost-contact behavior should prefer safety, withdrawal, defense, or precommitted narrow action over open-ended hunting.

# 12. Piracy, Privateering, and Irregular Conflict

The game distinguishes:

```text
survival theft
organized piracy
political interdiction
state privateering
salvage dispute
mutual-aid seizure
fraudulent rescue
```

Irregular actors have motives, supply, refuge, markets, and political relationships. They are not random encounter generators.

# 13. Fleet Morale and Internal Politics

Crew and machine participants track:

```text
mission confidence
fatigue
fear
trust in command
perceived legality
loss and grief
supply confidence
home-world politics
```

Mutiny, refusal, whistleblowing, desertion, and conscientious objection arise from causes and procedures, not random morale collapse.

# 14. Peace, Ceasefire, and Demobilization

Peace requires:

```text
verified ceasefire messages
safe separation
rescue and casualty exchange
prisoner accounting
mine or autonomous-weapon deactivation
blockade lifting
port restoration
reparations or restitution
command review
force demobilization or reassignment
```

Weapons and emergency powers do not vanish when a treaty flag changes.

# 15. Player Activities

```text
escorting a civilian convoy through disputed space
investigating a false hostile-identification chain
boarding a vessel whose distress call may be coerced
choosing between pursuit and rescue
negotiating inspection of protected medical cargo
maintaining a blockade while opening a humanitarian corridor
refusing an expired attack order
recovering autonomous weapons after ceasefire
mediating surrender between delayed authorities
```

# 16. Acceptance Tests

A representative implementation must prove:

1. Unknown contact is not automatically hostile.
2. A delayed order can expire before engagement.
3. Mission-kill and de-escalation options are viable.
4. Surrender creates conserved life-support and custody obligations.
5. Rescue traffic receives protected priority after battle.
6. A blockade is enforced through real routes and can leak or fail.
7. Humanitarian exemptions move actual cargo.
8. Boarding preserves evidence and private-data boundaries.
9. Autonomous defense fails safe under lost communication.
10. Ceasefire produces physical demobilization tasks.

# 17. Anti-Patterns

Reject:

```text
fleet strength as one number
instant command across planets
red contact equals lawful target
civilian habitat as ordinary hit-point object
boarding as loot-room transition
blockade as economic debuff only
surrender without care burden
war ending when last enemy ship explodes
```

## Final Line

> **A fleet proves its civilization not by how far it can project violence, but by how carefully it carries law, rescue, restraint, and responsibility into places where no one can intervene quickly.**
