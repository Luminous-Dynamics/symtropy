# Canon Registry and Document Governance

## Purpose

This document identifies the current canonical decision spine and integrates the v2.4 Atlas metric-engineering campaign without confusing speculative worldbuilding, implementation-ready design, scientific truth, and verified software capability.

# Status Vocabulary

- `canonical` — current binding design authority;
- `canonical-draft` — binding direction pending implementation evidence or final review;
- `implementation-spec` — concrete runtime or production design, not proof of code;
- `lore-canonical-draft` — approved worldbuilding direction subject to content validation;
- `supporting` — useful context that does not override the decision spine;
- `superseded` — retained for history but no longer current authority;
- `archived` — preserved artifact outside active design.

# Authority Order

When documents disagree, use:

1. Game Constitution and player-experience invariants;
2. authoritative world-state and multiplayer truth rules;
3. current canonical contracts;
4. current implementation specifications;
5. production standards and benchmarks;
6. lore and content bibles;
7. supporting and historical documents.

A newer version supersedes an older version only through explicit metadata or registry decision.

# v2.4 Scientific and Fictional Boundary

## Real Physics Is Not Claimed

The corpus may discuss relativity, proper time, horizons, energy, and metric geometry. It does not claim that practical FTL or Atlas engineering exists in contemporary science.

The fictional premise is explicit:

> Controlled metric systems can produce future-directed superluminal routes constrained by Atlas Time.

All further design derives from that premise.

## Fiction Does Not Remove Conservation

Atlas travel remains subject to declared:

- chronology;
- positive latency;
- mass and uniqueness;
- energy and heat;
- infrastructure;
- authority;
- ecology;
- labor;
- quarantine;
- failure.

# v2.4 Authority Boundaries

## Atlas Time Owns Causal Order

It does not replace proper time, local calendars, or character knowledge.

## Proper Time Owns Experienced Physical Duration

It does not decide political legitimacy or event ancestry.

## Knowledge Envelopes Own Confirmed Remote Information

Server truth does not automatically become character knowledge.

## Metric Runtime Owns Physical Route State

It does not issue migration, military, quarantine, or civic authority.

## Route Authority Owns Bounded Operation

It does not own destination sovereignty, private consent, or source-chain identity.

## Seed Missions Carry Knowledge, Not Permanent Rule

The Atlas Seed may contain plans and cryptographic ancestry. Local descendants decide whether to build and pair it.

## Multiplayer Hosts Serve State

They do not own chronology, routes, offices, or worldline ancestry.

# Relationship to Earlier Interstellar Canon

The v1.6 interstellar-threshold documents remain active where not explicitly superseded.

v2.4 narrows and specifies:

- the fictional FTL mechanism;
- chronology protection;
- multiplayer epoch handling;
- player access to new worlds;
- metric-engineering tiers;
- ordinary route culture;
- route warfare and failure.

It does not remove:

- relativistic travel;
- arks and seed ships;
- local sovereignty;
- light-delay outside active infrastructure;
- mission inheritance;
- noncontact;
- irrecoverable loss;
- long-horizon worldlines.

# Canonical Hard Rules

```text
No arrival before departure.
No nonpositive Atlas route cycle.
No arbitrary personal interstellar warp.
No destination without a receiving society or prior physical crossing.
No duplicate people, cargo, authority, or source chains.
No remote origin sovereignty through reconnection.
No forced seed-world pairing.
No FTL omniscience.
No host-owned chronology.
No route failure without persistent consequences.
```

# Implementation Honesty

All v2.4 capabilities are **D3 / I0** unless evidence says otherwise.

Documents, schemas, pseudocode, fixtures, and exact patch reconstruction prove only that the design corpus is coherent and reproducible.

They do not prove:

- a working relativity engine;
- a route graph validator;
- multiplayer epoch migration;
- deterministic century simulation;
- FTL transit;
- production networking;
- playability;
- scientific feasibility.

# Promotion Evidence

Promotion requires:

- deterministic chronology tests;
- proper-time integration tests;
- knowledge-latency tests;
- route-cycle rejection;
- unique transit crash recovery;
- asynchronous region replay;
- host migration;
- accessibility review;
- player-comprehension research;
- full benchmark evidence.

# Production Maxim

> **The documentation may define a road through spacetime. Only evidence may claim that the game can safely drive on it.**
