# First Contact, Translation, and Xenotechnics Contract

## Owned Question

**How can Symtropy make first contact a sustained, playable process of observation, uncertainty, boundary formation, negotiation, and mutual transformation rather than a cutscene, reputation meter, combat faction reveal, or instant universal translation?**

## Core Thesis

First contact begins before either side knows what kind of event is occurring.

```text
A signal may be speech, weather, repair, warning, reproduction, law, mourning, or attack.
A body may be an individual, organ, vehicle, colony, sensor, or disposable expression of a larger agent.
A gift may be food, contamination, contract, offspring, memory, or surrender.
```

The core fantasy is not decoding a puzzle box. It is earning a shared reality without forcing the other into human categories.

> **Translation is the construction of enough mutual correction that neither side must guess alone.**

## Prime Directives

1. **No universal translator.** Tools produce hypotheses, correspondences, and confidence—not immediate authoritative meaning.
2. **No species-alignment shortcut.** Contact state belongs to specific agents, polities, ecologies, histories, and contexts.
3. **No assumption that visible bodies are persons.** Agency location remains an empirical and ethical question.
4. **No first contact that defaults to combat because interpretation is difficult.** Containment, retreat, observation, signaling, mediation, and refusal must be supported.
5. **No moral immunity for alien civilizations.** Nonhumanity, age, power, or mystery does not make coercion legitimate.
6. **No human innocence by default.** Mining, scanning, sterilization, mapping, terraforming, and rescue may be experienced as invasion.
7. **No clean technology loot.** Xenotechnology carries environmental assumptions, dependencies, values, interfaces, and possible agency.
8. **No translation without cost.** Time, risk, distortion, exposure, instrumentation, and political disagreement shape contact.
9. **No forced contact as the only path.** Some entities or societies may choose distance, quarantine, limited exchange, or silence.
10. **No single ending.** Contact may become coexistence, treaty, rivalry, quarantine, mutual incomprehension, migration, symbiosis, or separation.

# 1. Contact Phases

```text
0. Unrecognized Presence
1. Anomaly and Competing Explanations
2. Evidence of Responsive Pattern
3. Boundary Testing
4. Provisional Signal Correspondence
5. Agency Recognition
6. Limited Contact Protocol
7. Negotiation and Reciprocal Commitments
8. Institutionalized Relationship
9. Transformation, Schism, or Breakdown
```

Phases are not strictly linear. New actors, contradictory evidence, violence, or translation failure may regress or fork the process.

# 2. The Translation Ladder

## 2.1 Correlation

A signal reliably co-occurs with a state or event.

Example:

```text
pressure rhythm appears before corridor closes
```

## 2.2 Response

The other system changes behavior after a controlled signal.

## 2.3 Discrimination

Different signals produce different responses.

## 2.4 Reference

A signal appears to indicate something beyond immediate stimulus.

## 2.5 Intent and Boundary

Evidence suggests warning, request, refusal, permission, or commitment.

## 2.6 Grammar or Protocol

Signals combine according to repeatable structure.

## 2.7 Shared Correction

Both parties can indicate misunderstanding and revise it.

Only shared correction supports high-confidence negotiation.

# 3. Translation Domains

```text
sensory calibration
spatial reference
time and sequence
body and agency
resource and metabolism
harm and safety
boundary and consent
memory and identity
exchange and obligation
law and authority
grief, reproduction, and care
```

Competence in one domain does not imply competence in another.

A civilization may exchange navigational data while remaining unable to express individual consent in mutually recognized form.

# 4. Category Violence

Category violence occurs when an interpretation becomes operational power before uncertainty is resolved.

Examples:

```text
polity → infestation
biosphere → resource field
quarantine boundary → hostile blockade
memory structure → raw data
migratory nursery → empty territory
repair organism → infection
silence → surrender
```

The game should expose when a category is provisional, contested, or dangerous.

# 5. Contact Rights Floor

Under agency uncertainty, the minimum protections are:

```text
proportional containment before extermination when viable
recorded evidence for irreversible action
right to withdraw from contact where withdrawal is legible
protection of habitat and memory from casual sampling
no coerced copying, disassembly, or translation of persons
no technology transfer that creates undisclosed dependency
no treaty claim beyond demonstrated mutual understanding
```

These protections do not prohibit defense against immediate harm.


A successful contact sequence must involve choices, risks, and revisitable consequences rather than only collecting enough scan points.

# 7. Contact Roles

Co-op roles may include:

```text
field observer
sensor and signal operator
ecologist or body specialist
historian and archivist
security and containment operator
diplomatic witness
engineer
pilot or route keeper
```

Solo play integrates these through bounded tools, NPC assistance, and slower contact pacing.

# 8. Xenotechnology

Xenotechnology is any reliable nonhuman method of changing conditions.

It may be:

```text
material
ecological
social
memory-based
biological
field-based
ritual or procedural
architectural
```

Before use, the player must establish:

```text
what the system changes
what it consumes
what environment it assumes
what boundaries it crosses
whether it contains or expresses agency
how it fails
whether it can be repaired or stopped
what obligations accompany transfer
```

# 9. Translation Is Bidirectional

Humans do not merely decode aliens. Aliens build models of humans.

Player actions become evidence about:

```text
human agency
truthfulness
violence
repair
individuality
ownership
machine relations
time horizon
capacity to honor commitments
```

Different human factions may be translated as distinct polities rather than one species.

# 10. Misunderstanding and Failure

Failure states include:

```text
false confidence
translation capture by one faction
accidental contamination
boundary violation
signal mimicry by hostile systems
internal alien schism
human political disagreement
unreadable timescale
contact fatigue
violence
```

Continuation may produce:

```text
quarantine
a long silence
partial protocol
a taboo
an apology project
a damaged but recoverable relationship
a rival interpreter faction
a Chronicle precedent
```

# 11. Contact and Combat

Combat may occur, but the game distinguishes:

```text
predation
territorial defense
containment
misread warning
polity conflict
machine enforcement
intentional conquest
```

Nonlethal tools, withdrawal, shielding, and negotiation windows remain meaningful where physically plausible.

# 12. Contact and Science

Scientific progress requires:

```text
repeatable observations
controlled signals
independent interpretations
instrument calibration
negative evidence
recorded uncertainty
```

Political need may force action before certainty. The game must show the gap rather than silently converting inference into fact.

# 13. Seedworks Boundary

Seedworks should foreshadow first contact through one bounded nonhuman or machine-ecology encounter that proves:

```text
one ambiguous signal
at least two plausible interpretations
one controlled test
one boundary or refusal
one consequence for acting too quickly
one noncombat resolution
```

It does not require a fully translated alien civilization.

# 14. Acceptance Tests

1. Players can distinguish observation, hypothesis, and accepted meaning.
2. At least two plausible interpretations remain live during an encounter.
3. Contact state changes through responsive experiments, not scan accumulation alone.
4. A mistaken classification creates concrete but recoverable consequences.
5. Xenotechnology has explicit inputs, dependencies, failure, and authority.
6. The other party changes its model of the player based on behavior.
7. Solo and co-op contact remain viable.
8. Combat, containment, retreat, and negotiation use the same underlying contact state.
9. Persistence preserves translation evidence and disputed meanings.
10. The system never assigns universal species morality from local contact.

## Final Rule

```text
The first proof of intelligence is not that the other understands us.
It is that our model changes when the other corrects it.
```
