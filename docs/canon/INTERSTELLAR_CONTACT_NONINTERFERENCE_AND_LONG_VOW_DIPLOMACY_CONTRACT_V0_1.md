# Interstellar Contact, Noninterference, and Long-Vow Diplomacy Contract

## Owned Question

**How can civilizations communicate, restrain themselves, make promises, or preserve noncontact across distances where every reply may arrive after governments, bodies, ecosystems, and meanings have changed?**

## Core Thesis

Interstellar contact is diplomacy with history rather than immediacy.

A signal may be:

```text
centuries old
sent by a dissolved polity
translated by descendants
received after the sender changed its mind
addressed to a society that no longer exists
```

> **No interstellar message carries a permanent right to define the sender, the recipient, or the future relationship between them.**

Diplomacy must therefore be scoped, evidence-bearing, reviewable, and humble about causal delay.

# 1. Contact States

Interstellar relations should not collapse into `unknown / friendly / hostile`.

Possible states include:

```text
unconfirmed signal
natural-versus-artificial ambiguity
one-way observation
mutual detection
bounded correspondence
noncontact declaration
quarantine relation
scientific exchange
refuge or rescue appeal
contact compact
contested representation
historical relationship only
treaty under renewal
dormant or lost contact
```

Each state records evidence and uncertainty.

# 2. No One Spokesperson by Default

A signal source may represent:

```text
one person
one machine
one habitat
one faction
one species
one ecosystem
one archive
one extinct polity
one autonomous mission
one distributed intelligence
```

The first entity that answers does not automatically speak for an entire world, species, or star system.

Recognition must be scoped:

```text
identity recognition
agency recognition
territorial standing
contact authority
treaty capacity
witness capacity
archive significance
```

# 3. Long Vows

A long vow is a commitment intended to survive beyond ordinary political time.

Examples:

```text
do not enter a biosphere
maintain a silent sky corridor
preserve a warning beacon
answer distress without annexation
avoid replicators in a region
protect a migratory stellar ecology
hold a relay in common
receive future refugees
```

## Required Fields

```text
protected value
scope
origin evidence
expected duration
review interval
successor authority
known burden
exit and amendment procedure
violation evidence
repair path
```

A long vow cannot be valid merely because it is old.

Each receiving generation must be able to assess:

```text
whether the protected value remains real
whether the burden remains proportionate
whether affected parties still consent
whether the vow became a tool of domination
```

# 4. Noninterference

Noninterference is not indifference.

It may require active restraint:

```text
signal quiet
trajectory exclusion
biological quarantine
no resource extraction
no cultural manipulation
no covert uplift
no military observation at intimate resolution
no self-replicating infrastructure
```

## Noninterference Failure Modes

```text
watching suffering while hiding behind doctrine
preventing all contact without appeal
using quarantine to preserve hierarchy
secretly shaping a society while claiming neutrality
classifying a world as protected without recognizing its agency
```

The game must make both intervention and restraint morally and materially accountable.

# 5. Contact Evidence

Every durable contact claim must retain:

```text
raw signal or observation
instrument state
processing pipeline
translation hypotheses
alternative explanations
custody and signatures
time and reference frame
knowledge cutoff
privacy or noncontact restrictions
```

A translated statement is not the raw signal.

A repeated pattern is not automatically language.

A response is not automatically consent to deeper contact.

# 6. Delayed Dialogue

Each message contains:

```text
sender identity and claimed representation
creation time
sender knowledge cutoff
translation confidence
scope of request or promise
assumptions
expiry or review trigger
privacy classification
```

Receiving societies may answer:

```text
accept
reject
request clarification
recognize only part of the claim
refuse contact
send evidence without agreement
create a local provisional interpretation
```

The original sender may never receive the reply.

# 7. Treaty Succession

An interstellar treaty cannot assume institutional continuity.

When a message arrives, the recipient must ask:

```text
Does the sender still exist?
Does the claimed successor have standing?
Did the treaty expire?
Were later amendments sent but not yet received?
Are affected communities represented?
Has the physical situation changed?
```

Treaty state may be:

```text
proposed
received
locally ratified
awaiting remote receipt
provisionally observed
contested
superseded in one direction
historical only
renewed
terminated with delayed notice
```

# 8. Quarantine

Interstellar quarantine may protect:

```text
biospheres
machine ecologies
information systems
memetic or cognitive boundaries
unknown contact zones
habitats with incompatible biochemistry
```

Quarantine requires:

```text
specific hazard model
uncertainty
scope
least-harmful available boundary
review
appeal or alternative contact path
humanitarian and rescue exceptions
```

Permanent quarantine without review is a sovereignty claim.

# 9. Precursor Presence

Ancient artifacts or observatories may contain:

```text
warnings
contact records
boundary markers
failed diplomacy
quarantine orders
refuge commitments
archived persons
automated interpreters
```

Rules:

1. Ancient does not mean current.
2. Power does not prove representation.
3. A machine guardian may be protecting a legitimate boundary or enforcing dead authority.
4. Human discovery does not create ownership.
5. Absence of an immediate reply does not prove abandonment.

# 10. Contact and Technology Exchange

Technology exchange must preserve dependency and context.

Before adopting a xenotechnology, the worldline must understand:

```text
environmental assumptions
maintenance and resource requirements
agency or living components
copy and modification rights
contact implications
failure and shutdown behavior
historical purpose
```

A gift may be sincere, coercive, misunderstood, self-propagating, or impossible to maintain.

# 11. Contact Conflict

Escalation may arise from:

```text
translation error
boundary violation
resource competition
quarantine disagreement
representation fraud
slow military approach
replicator fear
misread rescue attempt
historical warning interpreted as threat
```

The response ladder should preserve:

```text
observation
signal
clarification
boundary reinforcement
withdrawal
third-party witness
limited interdiction
rescue exception
```

Total war is not the default response to uncertainty.

# 12. Nonhuman Timescales

Some societies may respond over:

```text
seconds
seasons
orbits
centuries
geological intervals
bursts separated by dormancy
```

Treaties may use:

```text
precommitment
observable boundary conditions
long-lived witnesses
periodic beacons
automatic review triggers
steward succession
```

Slow response is not absence of agency.

# 13. Player Experience

The player should feel:

```text
the weight of sending a message no one alive may answer
the danger of interpreting old intent as current permission
the intimacy of preserving another civilization's boundary
the uncertainty of whether a reply will ever arrive
the responsibility of representing only what one can legitimately represent
```

# 15. Hard Invariants

```text
no first respondent treated as species-wide sovereign
no translated claim promoted above its evidence
no old treaty treated as current without succession review
no noninterference used to hide covert manipulation
no quarantine without hazard model, scope, and review
no ancient artifact treated as living authority automatically
no technology transfer without dependency and agency analysis
no silence treated as consent
no slow reply treated as absent agency
```

# Representative Proof

A four-hundred-year-old signal warns against entering a nearby biosphere. The sender's political identity is uncertain, but the ecological evidence supporting the boundary remains strong. A later message—received from a different source—claims the warning was illegitimate.

The proof succeeds when:

- the raw signals and translations remain separate;
- representation claims stay scoped;
- the biosphere receives standing despite no spokesperson;
- the worldline can preserve noncontact while investigating;
- neither ancient power nor newer rhetoric automatically wins;
- a reviewable long vow can be formed without claiming permanent authority.
