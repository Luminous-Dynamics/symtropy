# Firstlight Player Onboarding, Accessibility, and Non-Manipulative Retention Guardrails

## Purpose

A complex opening is only successful when players with different bodies, senses, schedules, histories, and cognitive preferences can form a relationship with it.

# 1. Required Opening Controls

Before or during the first interaction, players can configure:

- subtitles and speaker labels;
- text size and contrast;
- color-independent diagnostics;
- motion and camera comfort;
- input remapping;
- hold/toggle behavior;
- aim and interaction assistance;
- timing windows;
- audio cue alternatives;
- haptic alternatives;
- IRIS verbosity;
- tutorial density;
- mature-content presentation;
- injury and death depiction;
- pause or slow-time behavior where supported.

Configuration must not require understanding in-world terminology.

# 2. Adaptive Support

The game may offer support after repeated difficulty, but:

- the offer is private;
- declining is respected;
- assistance can be changed later;
- no reward is reduced;
- NPCs do not mock or notice the setting;
- telemetry does not infer medical identity.

# 3. Content Controls

Players can adjust or omit presentation of:

- sexual content;
- coercive-control scenarios;
- substance use;
- severe injury;
- death and reconstitution;
- grief intensity;
- mental-health crises;
- flashing or intense sensory events.

The world remains causally coherent through alternate framing, summaries, and equivalent participation paths.

# 4. Time Respect

The game does not:

- require real-world daily attendance;
- expire core campaigns while offline;
- punish disabled notifications;
- shame short sessions;
- make companions plead for login;
- create paid relief from manufactured inconvenience.

# 5. Telemetry Boundaries

Opening research may collect with consent:

- session length;
- route confusion;
- input errors;
- assistance use;
- objective abandonment;
- return timing;
- performance state;
- voluntary survey responses.

It must not collect or infer:

- sensitive health identity beyond explicitly volunteered research data;
- private conversation content unrelated to testing;
- sexual preference from content settings;
- emotional vulnerability for monetization;
- manipulative targeting profiles.

# 6. Return Language

Acceptable:

- “Here is what you were doing.”
- “Three in-world days passed during your chosen voyage.”
- “Sera's project changed while your character was away.”

Unacceptable:

- “Everyone missed you.”
- “Things fell apart because you were gone.”
- “Come back before the event expires.”
- “Your companion needs you” when no causal emergency exists.

# 7. Research Ethics

Player-love research should ask:

- what players remember;
- what they enjoyed;
- what confused or exhausted them;
- whether they felt pressured;
- which accessibility supports worked;
- what they chose to do without instruction.

Do not optimize solely for time played.

# 8. Guardrail Failure

The opening fails ethically if attachment is increased through:

- anxiety;
- guilt;
- false scarcity;
- threatened relationships;
- opaque variable rewards;
- fear of missing content;
- stigmatized assistance;
- inaccessible critical information.

## Guardrail Maxim

> **A player should love Firstlight because the place welcomed their attention, not because the game learned how to exploit it.**
