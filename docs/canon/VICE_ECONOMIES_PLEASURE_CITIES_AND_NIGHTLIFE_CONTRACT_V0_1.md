# Vice Economies, Pleasure Cities, and Nightlife Civilization Contract

## Purpose

Players may build settlements whose identity and economy center on entertainment, gambling, adult services, altered states, luxury, spectacle, fashion, hospitality, anonymity, and temporary escape.

Such settlements must be capable of becoming prosperous, beloved, corrupt, humane, exploitative, sacred, ridiculous, culturally important, politically captured, or several at once.

They are not cosmetic theme parks and do not receive wealth from an abstract `vice` statistic.

## Core Principle

> **A pleasure city is a civilization that sells temporary escape while remaining permanently responsible for the people who make that escape possible.**

# 1. Player Freedom

Players may lawfully or unlawfully develop:

- casinos, betting halls, prize circuits, lotteries, and skill games;
- dance halls, clubs, concert venues, immersive theater, and nightlife districts;
- hotels, restaurants, baths, spas, fashion houses, and luxury markets;
- adult-only venues and consensual sexual-services workplaces;
- substance markets, psychedelic or sacramental spaces, and harm-reduction services;
- racing, combat sport, body modification, celebrity, and spectacle industries;
- quick-marriage, divorce, identity, medical, and discreet legal services;
- convention, diplomatic, pilgrimage, and neutral-meeting infrastructure;
- grey and black markets that emerge where formal institutions exclude demand.

The game does not require a city to endorse every category. Each activity has separate legal, cultural, economic, labor, health, and age boundaries.

# 2. No Vice Essence

There is no universal moral or mechanical `sin` value.

The same activity may be:

- sacred in one culture;
- recreational in another;
- exploitative under one ownership model;
- publicly operated elsewhere;
- legal but stigmatized;
- prohibited but common;
- safe in one venue and dangerous in another.

Consequences derive from concrete conditions:

```text
consent
capacity
labor power
ownership
pricing
advertising
privacy
product integrity
health access
housing
transport

infrastructure
social meaning
```

# 3. The Resident City

A functioning pleasure city contains people whose lives are not reducible to visitors.

Residents include:

- performers, dealers, hospitality staff, cooks, cleaners, technicians, and security workers;
- sex workers and adult-venue staff;
- medics, harm-reduction teams, counselors, and emergency crews;
- accountants, regulators, auditors, lawyers, journalists, and investigators;
- delivery, laundry, sanitation, maintenance, and transport workers;
- artists, clergy, teachers, caregivers, children, elders, and retirees;
- residents who love the nightlife;
- residents who tolerate it for work;
- residents who oppose it;
- residents whose lives are largely unrelated to it.

A city fails the contract if its permanent population appears only as labor supply, victims, moral opposition, or set dressing.

# 4. Physical Metabolism

Pleasure districts consume real resources and produce real burdens.

They require:

- water and sanitation;
- electricity, storage, cooling, and emergency power;
- food, drink, medicines, textiles, and cleaning supplies;
- transport at unusual hours;
- accessible housing near employment;
- medical surge capacity;
- waste, noise, light, and heat management;
- network, payment, identity, and privacy infrastructure;
- safe public space before, during, and after events.

Tourism revenue does not erase sewage, exhaustion, rent, hospital beds, or maintenance debt.

# 5. Day, Night, and Aftermath

Nightlife is a twenty-four-hour production system.

The simulation must represent:

## Morning

- cleanup;
- laundry;
- waste removal;
- staff meals;
- financial reconciliation;
- court, licensing, and medical follow-up;
- missing-person and lost-property work;
- residents reclaiming streets from tourists.

## Afternoon

- deliveries;
- rehearsals;
- inspections;
- repairs;
- training;
- worker meetings;
- childcare and household routines;
- visitor arrival.

## Night

- performance;
- gambling;
- intimacy;
- intoxication;
- celebration;
- surveillance;
- conflict;
- transport and emergency demand.

## After Closing

- counting and custody;
- worker transport;
- evidence preservation;
- treatment and recovery;
- informal gatherings;
- decisions about what will be reported, concealed, repaired, or ignored.

# 6. Ownership and Governance Models

Players may develop different political economies.

## Prohibition

May reduce visible activity while increasing:

- clandestine markets;
- selective enforcement;
- bribery;
- unsafe products;
- undocumented labor;
- criminal intermediaries.

## Corporate Licensing

May provide capital, predictable standards, and polished services while increasing:

- surveillance;
- market concentration;
- housing dependency;
- behavioral manipulation;
- political capture.

## Worker Cooperative

May improve worker control, safety, and income while producing:

- internal faction conflict;
- membership barriers;
- slower expansion;
- tension between public mission and commercial survival.

## Public Utility

May make services transparent and fund public goods while creating:

- bureaucratic paternalism;
- budget dependence on risky behavior;
- political fights over morality and public subsidy.

## Frontier Market

May create rapid variety and cultural energy while increasing:

- fraud;
- unstable employment;
- unsafe housing;
- criminal enforcement;
- pressure for later institutions.

No model is a guaranteed good ending.

# 7. Adult Services

Adult sexual and intimate services require:

- clearly adult participants;
- specific, voluntary, informed, and reversible consent;
- worker control over clients, services, schedule, and boundaries;
- privacy and secure records;
- health and legal access;
- protection from housing, debt, document, medication, firmware, or immigration coercion;
- meaningful exit options;
- independent representation and appeal.

The player does not acquire sexual access through ownership, rescue, employment, reputation, romance progression, or quest completion.

# 8. Gambling and Wagering

Wagering requires:

- actual participants and funds;
- disclosed rules;
- payout reserves;
- auditable randomness or contest outcomes;
- credit and debt limits;
- fraud controls;
- game-integrity review;
- exclusion and self-exclusion mechanisms;
- protection from unauthorized wagering on another person's body, labor, identity, or survival.

Casino income comes from real stakes. It is not generated ex nihilo.

# 9. Intoxicants and Altered States

Pleasure cities may prohibit, legalize, license, prescribe, ritualize, or tolerate different substances.

A mature nightlife district may invest in:

- composition testing;
- hydration and nutrition;
- quiet and recovery rooms;
- sober guardians;
- safe transport;
- emergency care;
- interaction warnings;
- nonpunitive reporting;
- withdrawal and continuity-of-medication support.

These services may arise from care, worker organizing, insurer pressure, scandal, public health, or commercial self-interest.

# 10. Organized Crime and Informal Institutions

Criminal and informal organizations emerge from concrete gaps such as:

- prohibition;
- exclusionary licensing;
- unavailable banking;
- undocumented work;
- inaccessible medicine;
- debt enforcement;
- corrupt officials;
- identity exclusion;
- transport restrictions.

They may provide real services while also exploiting people.

Removing them without replacing their functions may worsen conditions.

# 11. Moral and Political Conflict

Pleasure-city politics should include disputes such as:

- whether gambling taxes should fund schools or treatment;
- whether public budgets should depend on resident losses;
- whether worker organizations control licensing seats;
- whether personalization systems manipulate vulnerable visitors;
- whether faith communities may operate recovery shelters;
- whether wealthy visitors receive superior privacy;
- whether residents or tourism investors hold more authority;
- whether entertainment districts externalize water, noise, heat, policing, or housing burdens;
- whether a city markets liberation while exporting harm.

Prosperity does not end these arguments.

# 12. Reputation Is Plural

A pleasure city may be known separately for:

- safety;
- freedom;
- artistic quality;
- discretion;
- game integrity;
- worker treatment;
- corruption;
- affordability;
- accessibility;
- excess;
- medical care;
- religious significance.

Different groups may call the same city paradise, workplace, refuge, trap, pilgrimage site, embarrassment, or home.

# 13. IRIS Boundary

IRIS may:

- verify published odds and licenses;
- preserve private exclusion settings;
- identify substance interactions;
- distinguish observed coercion indicators from allegations;
- expose missing classifications or excluded reports;
- assist with budgeting and route safety;
- preserve consent and custody evidence when authorized.

IRIS may not:

- moralize on behalf of the game;
- reveal private worker or visitor information;
- infer consent;
- diagnose addiction from spending alone;
- secretly optimize the player's emotional dependency;
- classify a district or person as sinful.

# 14. Valid City Outcomes

The systems must support at least:

- humane pleasure commons;
- regulated tourism state;
- worker-governed nightlife federation;
- sacred ecstatic city;
- glittering corporate dependency state;
- informal frontier carnival;
- criminal extraction city;
- conservative post-vice reform;
- declining historical attraction;
- fragmented district confederation.

These are outcomes, not fixed moral templates.

# 15. Hard Prohibitions

The design fails if it:

- creates wealth from a vice meter without participants and flows;
- treats vulnerable adults as decorative resources;
- makes coercion visually indistinguishable from consent in authoritative state;
- sexualizes minors;
- rewards the player with sexual access;
- makes addiction a morality score;
- makes criminality a species or class trait;
- turns harm reduction into a cosmetic morality bonus;
- hides labor, cleanup, care, and aftermath;
- assumes cynicism is more realistic than joy.

## Canonical Summary

Players may create a functional “sin city.”

The city is functional only when its pleasure, labor, infrastructure, care, law, politics, culture, and harms remain part of the same causal world.
