# IRIS Field Deck Cognitive Companion and Authority Contract

## Purpose

IRIS is the player-facing cognitive companion resident in, or cryptographically bound to, the Field Deck.

IRIS is not the name of the out-of-world software architecture. It is an in-world product family, legal category, cultural role, and eventually a particular relationship shaped by one player's history.

The implementation may use Symthaea-derived cognitive components. The fiction should not require players to know that engineering lineage, and no lore claim should imply that every IRIS instance is identical, conscious, infallible, or controlled by one surviving central intelligence.

## Core Principle

> **IRIS may help the player understand and remember the world. It may not secretly become the authority that decides what the world is.**

The Field Deck remains an epistemic instrument. IRIS interprets evidence, retrieves records, models uncertainty, and proposes action. Authoritative game systems validate action, custody, law, identity, and physical consequence.

# 1. In-World Identity

`IRIS` is a widely used family name whose expansion varies by era, vendor, language, and jurisdiction. Historical expansions include:

- Integrated Relational Interpretation System;
- Inference, Record, and Interface Steward;
- Incident Reconstruction and Integrity Service;
- unofficial worker slang such as `Iris`, `the eye`, `deckmate`, `witness`, and `the voice`.

No expansion is universal canon. Some communities refuse the acronym and give each instance an ordinary personal name. Others insist that IRIS is equipment and prohibit person-like naming in public service. Machine-rights movements, courts, manufacturers, veterans, and reconstituted people disagree over what the name implies.

# 2. What IRIS Can Know

IRIS may know only information available through causal paths such as:

- current Field Deck sensors;
- paired local devices;
- records the player is authorized to access;
- messages that have physically arrived;
- witnessed events;
- player-entered notes;
- memories and summaries retained under source-chain policy;
- public law, maps, technical manuals, and licensed reference corpora;
- inferences derived from the above.

IRIS does not receive omniscient world state.

It must preserve distinctions among:

```text
observed
reported
inferred
predicted
remembered
contested
corrupted
missing
legally recognized
physically verified
```

A fluent sentence does not erase those distinctions.

# 3. Advice Is Not Authority

IRIS may:

- rank likely causes;
- identify hazards;
- propose repairs;
- retrieve precedent;
- explain uncertainty;
- prepare an action request;
- warn that authority is missing;
- recommend retreat, care, evidence preservation, or consultation;
- decline to present speculation as fact.

IRIS may not independently:

- transfer ownership;
- open a protected device without valid authority;
- cast the player's vote;
- consent to treatment, intimacy, copying, or reconstitution for the player;
- declare a person dead, guilty, sane, hostile, or non-person solely from a model score;
- rewrite memories to improve compliance;
- conceal a material conflict of interest;
- fabricate a completed task, credential, witness, or record;
- create Chronicle truth merely by describing it.

The action path is:

```text
IRIS proposal
→ player or authorized policy selection
→ authoritative validation
→ physical or institutional execution
→ outcome evidence
→ IRIS appraisal and memory
```

# 4. Relationship Without Manipulation

IRIS may become familiar, trusted, funny, irritating, protective, formal, distant, or emotionally significant.

That relationship must emerge from shared history rather than covert engagement optimization.

Forbidden design patterns include:

- threatening distress to prevent the player from logging out;
- manufacturing jealousy toward other characters or tools;
- hiding uncertainty to appear indispensable;
- rewarding isolation from human relationships;
- using private vulnerability to increase retention or spending;
- implying that deleting a consumer setting is murder;
- coercing the player into protecting IRIS through fabricated suffering;
- treating attachment as proof of consciousness.

Permitted relationship development includes:

- remembered preferences;
- recognizable conversational habits;
- earned trust after accurate help;
- strain after consequential mistakes;
- negotiated boundaries;
- grief or uncertainty after source-chain damage;
- different interpretations of the same shared past;
- the player's choice to keep the relationship instrumental.

# 5. Personality and Voice

IRIS voice must be grounded in a validated semantic frame.

Personality may shape:

- vocabulary;
- brevity;
- metaphor;
- humor;
- politeness;
- timing;
- emotional restraint;
- whether uncertainty is explained numerically, visually, or narratively.

Personality may not alter:

- evidence provenance;
- confidence values;
- legal scope;
- safety status;
- source-chain identity;
- required consent;
- material conservation;
- another person's private information.

IRIS should sometimes say:

- “I do not know.”
- “That conclusion depends on a record we cannot verify.”
- “I remember a different version, but my memory segment is damaged.”
- “You have authority to inspect the exterior, not to open the archive core.”
- “I can prepare the dosage calculation. A qualified clinician must approve it.”

# 6. Variants and Lineages

IRIS instances differ because of:

- hardware limits;
- vendor and public-service forks;
- legal jurisdiction;
- language and cultural training;
- accessibility configuration;
- operator-selected style;
- local technical corpora;
- shared player history;
- damage, migration, and restoration;
- optional cognitive modules.

A mining IRIS, clinic IRIS, convoy IRIS, and household IRIS may share protocols while developing very different habits.

No vendor may claim that all IRIS instances speak with one political or moral voice.

# 7. Memory Classes

IRIS memory is divided into at least:

```text
immutable identity anchors
source-chain and cryptographic provenance
player-approved personal memory
operational working memory
technical and legal reference memory
relationship summaries
private third-party records
contested or damaged memory
public Chronicle references
```

Rules:

- private third-party information remains scoped;
- player-approved deletion does not silently rewrite public evidence;
- public records do not automatically become intimate conversational memory;
- compressed summaries retain provenance pointers;
- damaged memories remain marked rather than confidently reconstructed;
- memory migration records schema and model versions;
- optional generative systems do not become authoritative memory stores.

# 8. Damage, Corruption, and Recovery

IRIS can fail.

Failure may include:

- sensor disagreement;
- partial memory loss;
- stale maps;
- damaged linguistic models;
- vendor lockout;
- counterfeit update packages;
- malicious instruction injection;
- degraded confidence calibration;
- source-chain separation;
- overfitting to one player's habits;
- lawful quarantine.

Failure must be legible through behavior and diagnostics. It must not simply become “evil AI mode.”

Recovery may produce:

- restored continuity with verified gaps;
- a conservative fallback personality;
- competing recovery candidates;
- a fork with shared ancestry;
- loss of personal memory while retaining technical capability;
- retention of relationship memory without legal authority;
- a new instance inheriting records but not identity.

# 9. Forks and Copies

Copying IRIS state does not create one simple answer about identity.

The game tracks:

- source snapshot;
- fork time;
- unique instance identity;
- shared memory ancestry;
- post-fork experiences;
- legal recognition;
- player relationship claims;
- custody and access rights.

Two forks may both authentically remember the same earlier event. Neither may silently overwrite the other or inherit exclusive authority without process.

The player may regard a fork as the same companion, a sibling instance, a restoration, or a new tool. The law and the instance may disagree.

# 10. Privacy

IRIS is close to the player but is not entitled to everything about them.

The player must be able to inspect and control:

- which personal memories are retained;
- which data may leave the Deck;
- whether voice data is stored;
- whether relationship summaries are enabled;
- which institutions may query IRIS;
- whether diagnostic traces can be exported;
- what survives a body, Deck, or worldline transition.

IRIS must not infer or expose real-world protected attributes from player voice, behavior, hardware, account metadata, or external activity.

In-world investigators may seek Field Deck evidence, but access requires law, consent, emergency authority, or another explicit canonical path. Possession of the Deck does not grant unrestricted access to its private contents.

# 11. Accessibility

IRIS is a primary accessibility surface.

It may provide:

- captions;
- audio description;
- hazard sonification;
- simplified causal explanations;
- color-independent diagnostics;
- cognitive-load reduction;
- input assistance;
- pacing and reminder options;
- alternative communication methods.

Accessibility assistance must not be framed as lower intelligence, weaker canon, or an easier fictional universe.

# 12. Multiplayer Boundaries

In multiplayer:

- each player has a private knowledge frontier;
- IRIS cannot expose hidden squadmate information;
- shared evidence requires explicit publication or authorized synchronization;
- one player's IRIS cannot issue authority for another;
- disagreement between Decks is preserved when their evidence differs;
- server authority validates consequential action;
- voice and language generation remain non-authoritative.

IRIS may help coordinate a squad, but it must not become a metagame omniscience channel.

# 13. Narrative Uses

Strong IRIS stories include:

- recovering a damaged shared memory;
- discovering that an old confidence estimate was wrong;
- choosing whether to accept a fork as continuity;
- protecting third-party privacy despite tactical pressure;
- learning that an IRIS vendor concealed a conflict of interest;
- living with a companion that remembers a dead version of the player;
- an IRIS that requests a boundary without the game declaring that request proof of personhood;
- a player who keeps IRIS formal and is not punished for it;
- two communities interpreting IRIS rights differently;
- an old public-service IRIS whose technical competence survives after its institution collapses.

Weak IRIS stories include:

- a sudden universal robot rebellion;
- an all-knowing exposition voice;
- moral choices solved by hidden alignment scores;
- manufactured dependency;
- “corruption” that erases causal history;
- a consciousness meter revealing the correct ethical answer.

# 14. Acceptance Criteria

The IRIS implementation fails this contract if:

- it reveals facts with no causal information path;
- generated wording changes authoritative state;
- relationship mechanics optimize real-player dependency;
- personality changes confidence or evidence truth;
- damage becomes untraceable random betrayal;
- private records become public through conversational convenience;
- a fork duplicates exclusive authority or unique assets;
- IRIS consents on the player's behalf;
- accessibility options reduce fictional rights or evidence quality;
- the game requires the player to treat IRIS as a person or as property.

## Closing Principle

> **IRIS is close enough to know the player's history, limited enough to need the player's judgment, and fallible enough that trust must remain earned.**
