# Companion Consent, Refusal, Departure, and Dependency Contract

## Purpose

Persistent companions create unusual power risks. The player may control transport, housing, medicine, pay, immigration sponsorship, body maintenance, source-chain recovery, or access to the only safe route.

A companion system is not humane merely because it contains a refusal dialogue line.

> **A refusal is meaningful only when the companion can survive making it.**

# 1. Participation Consent

Companion participation is scoped to:

- a journey;
- a task;
- a role;
- a risk envelope;
- a time window;
- a destination;
- a disclosure level;
- an authority context.

Agreeing to travel does not imply consent to combat, illegal entry, intimate access, memory inspection, body modification, or indefinite service.

# 2. Refusal

Companions may refuse because of:

```text
risk
values
professional duty
family obligation
injury or fatigue
lack of competence
lack of evidence
legal limits
privacy
fear
trauma
political disagreement
religious commitment
noncontact boundary
```

Refusal must not automatically lower affection or trigger a punishment state.

The player may:

- ask why;
- offer alternatives;
- supply missing resources;
- change the plan;
- seek another worker;
- proceed alone;
- invoke legitimate emergency authority when applicable;
- accept the refusal.

# 3. Coercive Dependencies

The system must detect whether the player or their institution controls:

- air, water, heat, shelter, charging, or life support;
- medication, prosthetic maintenance, or body firmware;
- wages or debt;
- transport;
- legal status or identity records;
- source-chain recovery;
- family reunification;
- professional credentials;
- protection from violence;
- sole translation access.

A companion's apparent agreement under severe dependency cannot be treated as ordinary free consent.

The game may depict ethically difficult arrangements, but must preserve appeal, alternatives, outside witnesses, and visible material conditions.

# 4. Dismissal

The player may end active travel, but dismissal cannot erase employment law, housing, care, pay, custody, or promises.

Possible dismissal forms:

- end expedition and return safely;
- suspend role pending review;
- terminate contract with pay and transport;
- separate after conflict;
- emergency evacuation;
- abandon the companion.

Abandonment is a world action with physical and relational consequences, not a menu cleanup operation.

# 5. Departure

A companion may initiate departure.

The system should distinguish:

```text
ordinary return
career opportunity
family duty
political office
migration
medical leave
relationship separation
fear-based escape
principled resignation
institutional reassignment
missing or captured status
death
source-chain uncertainty
```

Departure may create grief without creating villainy.

# 6. Care Relationships

A companion who requires care remains an agent.

Care may include:

- medication;
- mobility assistance;
- sensory accommodation;
- charging;
- thermal or atmospheric support;
- translation;
- memory orientation;
- recovery after injury;
- dependent household care.

Care cannot be exchanged for obedience, intimacy, confession, or permanent companionship.

# 7. Command Relationships

Military, emergency, clinical, or workplace command must remain scoped.

A lawful order requires:

- legitimate authority;
- relevant jurisdiction;
- sufficient information;
- feasible action;
- protected refusal for unlawful or grossly unsafe orders;
- review and evidence.

Companion closeness does not expand command authority.

# 8. Fear and Safety

A companion can fear the player.

Fear may arise through:

- violence;
- threats;
- reckless decisions;
- privacy invasion;
- control of necessities;
- repeated disregard of boundaries;
- unpredictable behavior;
- faction reputation.

Fear must not be hidden behind a normal follower interface. Behavior, distance, allies, safe houses, and institutional response should change.

# 9. Grief and Continuing Bonds

When a companion dies, disappears, migrates, or becomes unrecoverable, the relationship does not become zero.

Continuing consequences may include:

- unfinished work;
- household changes;
- inherited tools;
- debts;
- testimony;
- memorials;
- anger;
- songs and jokes;
- IRIS memories;
- disputed recovery;
- successors who reject comparison.

A replacement worker cannot inherit the same emotional role automatically.

# 10. Source-Chain and Fork Cases

If a companion is restored or forked:

- each continuity has separate agency;
- legal standing follows source-chain evidence and local law;
- relationships require renewed negotiation;
- private memories are not automatically shared;
- a partner, child, crew, or player may accept one continuity, several, or none;
- no version is required to continue traveling.

# 11. Player Safety and Content Controls

Companion coercion, abuse, abandonment, death, and grief require:

- content descriptors;
- adjustable presentation;
- skip or summary paths where feasible;
- no loss of critical mechanical understanding;
- subject review for high-risk authored arcs;
- privacy-respecting telemetry.

# 12. Failure Conditions

Fail if:

- companions cannot leave;
- refusal causes automatic hostility;
- player-controlled necessities silently force consent;
- dismissal teleports a companion home;
- care creates obedience bonuses;
- a restored companion resumes intimacy automatically;
- every companion forgives after one apology;
- a dead companion's abilities transfer unchanged to a replacement;
- abuse is framed as proof of passion;
- the player can inspect private dependency or fear meters directly.
