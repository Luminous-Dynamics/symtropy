# First 40 Hours Player Love and Retention Contract

## Owned Question

**How does Symtropy make players love its first place, people, tools, dangers, and possibilities deeply enough that the first ten to forty hours feel unforgettable rather than merely impressive?**

## v2.7 Opening Amendment

The standards in this contract remain active, but the stationary Firstlight assumption is replaced by the Breaking of Firstlight structure. The player spends roughly two hours loving a unique community, survives a variable evacuation, and carries survivors and material continuity into the first mobile base. The remaining first forty hours unfold through diaspora, travel, rebuilding, profession mastery, and possible return.

## Core Thesis

The opening is not a shortened tour of every system.

It is a deliberately concentrated life in one region where the player:

- becomes physically fluent;
- learns to read one place;
- becomes useful to several people;
- suffers one meaningful setback;
- experiences danger and delight;
- develops one professional identity;
- establishes a personal relationship with IRIS;
- sees consequences survive beyond a completed objective;
- ends with desires that belong to the player rather than a checklist.

> **The first forty hours must make Firstlight feel less like a tutorial zone and more like a place the player is already reluctant to leave.**

# 1. Love Is the Target, Not Compulsion

This contract uses `retention` to mean voluntary return created by attachment, curiosity, mastery, and unfinished responsibility.

It does not authorize:

- fear-of-missing-out schedules;
- manipulative streaks;
- hidden variable-ratio rewards;
- artificial daily chores;
- punishment for taking breaks;
- companion distress triggered solely to pull the player back;
- opaque scarcity designed to create anxiety;
- notifications implying that fictional people will suffer because the player logged off;
- monetized convenience around fatigue, inventory, death, intimacy, or access.

A player should return because they remember Sera, want to hear whether a repaired pump remains quiet, miss the night market, want to master a difficult procedure, or remain curious about a sealed tunnel.

# 2. The Seven Opening Promises

The first forty hours must prove seven promises in play.

## 2.1 The World Is Physically Satisfying

Within the first ten minutes the player must perform at least one action with readable material response:

- open a jammed enclosure;
- route power;
- brace a failing object;
- move through difficult terrain;
- use a medical or repair instrument;
- rescue someone;
- drive or ride a vehicle.

The action must communicate weight, contact, sound, resistance, and consequence.

## 2.2 People Continue Existing

Within the first hour the player must observe an NPC doing something unrelated to serving the player.

Within the first four hours:

- one person should remember a prior interaction;
- one person should be unavailable because of their own obligation;
- one relationship between NPCs should change without player ownership;
- one person should disagree without becoming an enemy.

## 2.3 Skill Can Be Learned

Within the first two hours, the player must encounter a task that initially feels difficult but becomes more legible through practice rather than stat inflation.

Within ten hours, the player should be able to identify at least one cue they could not read at the beginning.

## 2.4 Danger Is Real and Exciting

Within the first three hours the game must demonstrate that danger involves movement, judgment, tools, and uncertainty—not only dialogue or resource loss.

The opening must include at least one sequence where:

- the player can act decisively;
- several approaches remain possible;
- failure changes the situation without simply ending the save;
- consequences continue after the adrenaline stops.

## 2.5 Quiet Life Is Worth Protecting

Before the first major crisis, the player must experience ordinary life worth remembering:

- food;
- music;
- a joke;
- a view;
- a shared task;
- a local routine;
- a place where people relax.

The game must not demand that the player care about a place it has shown only during catastrophe.

## 2.6 Choices Become History

By hour five, at least one early action must return in altered form.

Examples:

- a temporary repair becomes someone else's routine;
- an injured person changes work assignment;
- a rumor changes after testimony;
- a route opens or closes;
- a tool is modified by another worker;
- a promise becomes inconvenient;
- a private kindness remains private rather than producing public reputation.

## 2.7 The Horizon Is Larger Than the Tutorial

By hour ten, the player should understand that the region connects to:

- larger history;
- other societies;
- orbital and planetary systems;
- machine and alien questions;
- eventual settlement founding and interstellar travel.

The opening should reveal this through credible traces, people, infrastructure, sky, and conversation—not a catalogue of future features.

# 3. Emotional Anchors

The opening must establish at least six kinds of anchor.

## 3.1 Person

Someone the player recognizes, understands partially, and expects to meet again.

## 3.2 Place

A location with sensory identity, routine, and changing use.

## 3.3 Practice

A profession or repeated skilled activity that becomes personally satisfying.

## 3.4 Object

A tool, vehicle, Field Deck, garment, repair, or keepsake with persistent history.

## 3.5 Promise

A commitment that is neither a quest marker nor a hidden morality score.

## 3.6 Mystery

A question that can remain unresolved without blocking ordinary play.

No single anchor may carry the entire emotional load.

# 4. The Opening Shape

## 4.1 First Ten Minutes — Contact

The player should:

- gain embodied control quickly;
- hear IRIS in a bounded, useful role;
- encounter one person under practical pressure;
- perform one tactile action;
- see one striking but inhabited vista;
- understand one immediate objective;
- retain freedom to look away from it.

No cinematic longer than ninety seconds should precede meaningful control unless required for accessibility or explicit player choice.

## 4.2 First Hour — Competence and Welcome

The player should:

- reach a recurring home base;
- meet three people in different contexts;
- receive one useful tool;
- fail safely at one learnable task;
- make one low-stakes choice;
- encounter one private or humorous detail;
- understand how to rest, save, and return.

## 4.3 First Five Hours — Belonging and Pressure

The player should:

- begin one profession;
- revisit at least four places;
- share one practiced task with a companion;
- face one dangerous event;
- witness one institutional disagreement;
- attend or pass through one ordinary social activity;
- cause one delayed consequence.

## 4.4 First Ten Hours — Identity

The player should be able to answer:

- Where do I feel safe?
- Who do I trust for what?
- What am I learning to do?
- What have I changed?
- What do I still misunderstand?
- What do I want to try next?

The answer must not be dictated by a class-selection screen.

## 4.5 Hours Ten to Twenty — Complication

The opening expands through:

- cross-profession dependencies;
- companion refusal or independent action;
- one significant region-wide event;
- one moral or political conflict grounded in material systems;
- one optional dangerous expedition;
- one celebration or cultural event;
- one early assumption proven incomplete.

## 4.6 Hours Twenty to Forty — Ownership Without Possession

The player should experience:

- a project that takes several sessions;
- an institution that changes through participation;
- a relationship that survives disagreement;
- a consequence that cannot be optimized away;
- one absence or time passage;
- one return to a changed place;
- a credible decision about staying, leaving, founding, or pursuing a wider horizon.

By hour forty, the player may have influence and responsibility. They do not own Firstlight or its people.

# 5. Firstlight Density Requirements

The opening region should prioritize depth over area.

Required minimums:

- 6 flagship companions or relationship anchors;
- 24 to 36 memorable recurring residents;
- 12 persistent landmarks;
- 3 deeply playable professions;
- 1 service robot with independent standing;
- 1 compact vehicle loop;
- 1 dangerous ruin or underground complex;
- 1 ecological dependency;
- 1 corporate or external pressure;
- 1 public institution with real procedure;
- 1 nightlife, festival, market, or cultural rhythm;
- 1 historical mystery;
- 1 crisis with physical, social, and civic aftermath.

The opening must not require hundreds of interchangeable NPCs to appear alive.

# 6. Player Archetypes

The first forty hours must support several legitimate motivations.

## Builder

Wants tools, materials, repair, construction, and visible improvement.

## Explorer

Wants routes, ruins, ecology, danger, and hidden history.

## Social Player

Wants people, households, intimacy, culture, and political life.

## Professional Mastery Player

Wants difficult procedures, equipment, apprenticeships, and peer recognition.

## Action Player

Wants danger, movement, combat, rescue, and tactical choice.

## Systems Player

Wants resource chains, institutions, logistics, and causal experimentation.

No archetype should spend more than forty-five minutes without encountering a meaningful expression of its motivation during the first five hours.

# 7. Pacing Rights

Players have the right to:

- pause between crises;
- ignore nonurgent opportunities;
- revisit instructions;
- reduce or increase IRIS assistance;
- choose shorter or longer sessions;
- leave the region without completing every campaign;
- return after an absence without being shamed;
- experience difficult content through configured presentation controls.

The simulation may continue, but offline progression must not weaponize attachment.

# 8. Tutorial Philosophy

The world teaches through:

- situated need;
- safe practice;
- companion demonstration;
- tool behavior;
- visible causality;
- IRIS explanation on request;
- failure with diagnosis;
- repeated use in altered contexts.

Avoid:

- modal tutorials during danger;
- every tool explained before use;
- terminology dumps;
- forced mastery gates disguised as chores;
- instructions that contradict physical affordances;
- companion dialogue written only as tutorial text.

# 9. Love Without Universal Approval

Emotional investment does not require every player to love every character or institution.

The target is meaningful specificity:

- a person may be respected but disliked;
- a district may feel unsafe but fascinating;
- a companion may leave;
- a beloved institution may be flawed;
- a successful repair may enable a policy the player opposes;
- the player may choose to depart while still caring what happens.

# 10. Measurement

The opening may not be promoted solely because completion and session length are high.

Required research measures include:

- unaided recall of people, places, practices, promises, and mysteries;
- desire to return stated in the player's own words;
- observed physical fluency;
- comprehension of consequences;
- perceived agency;
- emotional attachment without guilt pressure;
- pacing fatigue;
- cognitive overload;
- accessibility barriers;
- number of memorable self-directed moments;
- whether players discuss what happened rather than only what systems exist.

A player who stops after ten satisfying hours is not a failed player.

# 11. Failure Conditions

The first forty hours fail if:

- the main attraction is feature quantity;
- players remember UI systems but not people or places;
- every problem is presented as a crisis;
- the first settlement feels disposable;
- professions are menus rather than practices;
- companions are tutorials or combat perks;
- IRIS talks more than the world;
- combat has no tactile excitement;
- ordinary life exists only as exposition;
- failure mainly causes reloads;
- the player cannot articulate a personal next desire;
- retention depends on anxiety, obligation spam, or manipulation.

## Contract Maxim

> **Players should not stay for forty hours because the game withholds its real game. They should stay because each hour makes a place, practice, relationship, or possibility more worth returning to.**
