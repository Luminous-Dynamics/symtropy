# Interstellar Rescue, Abandonment, and Irrecoverable Loss Contract

## Owned Question

**What obligations remain when a distress signal arrives years or centuries late, rescue may be physically impossible, and the only available actions are warning, witness, diversion, refuge preparation, archive preservation, or care for descendants?**

## Core Thesis

Interstellar rescue must not promise what physics cannot deliver.

But impossibility does not erase responsibility.

> **When a body cannot be reached, civilization may still owe truth, warning, refuge, memory, reparative action, and protection of what remains.**

The system must distinguish failure to save from refusal to care.

# 1. Distress States

A distress case may be:

```text
live and reachable
live but delayed
reachable only through high-risk diversion
reachable only by supplies or information
unlikely to remain alive at intercept
historical by the time received
uncertain or possibly forged
multi-generational
planetary or ecological rather than vessel-scale
```

The interface must not present all distress signals as ordinary timed quests.

# 2. Rescue Feasibility

Feasibility depends on:

```text
signal age and uncertainty
relative position and trajectory
available responders
propulsion and transfer geometry
life-support endurance
repair and medical needs
intercept risk
responder obligations
potential refuge capacity
```

A feasibility assessment produces:

```text
possible intercepts
arrival distributions
survival assumptions
resource requirements
risks to responders and bystanders
partial-aid options
uncertainty
```

No economic value score may replace this assessment.

# 3. Duty to Respond

A responding polity should evaluate:

```text
ability to reduce harm
urgency
number and vulnerability of lives
prior obligations
risk to responders
risk created by inaction
available partial aid
fairness among simultaneous cases
```

Wealth, citizenship, cargo value, faction prestige, species, body type, or political loyalty must not define rescue priority.

# 4. Forms of Interstellar Aid

## 4.1 Direct Intercept

A responder changes trajectory to reach the distressed mission.

## 4.2 Supply Intercept

Uncrewed packages deliver:

```text
power
propellant
parts
medicine
food
communication
navigation correction
```

## 4.3 Information Rescue

Transmit:

```text
repair procedure
medical guidance
trajectory correction
hazard warning
legal refuge guarantee
contact translation
software patch with provenance
```

## 4.4 Refuge Preparation

A destination prepares capacity for survivors, descendants, embryos, archives, machines, or ecological material.

## 4.5 Relay and Witness

Preserve the signal, warn others, verify what happened, and prevent disappearance.

## 4.6 Descendant Repair

The original people may be unreachable, but their descendants, settlements, claims, debts, or damaged worlds may still be helped later.

# 5. Abandonment

Abandonment is not identical to inability.

A case becomes abandonment when an actor with meaningful capacity:

```text
conceals the distress
refuses aid for discriminatory reasons
prioritizes recoverable property over reachable lives
blocks other responders
uses life support or rescue as leverage
falsifies feasibility
fails to preserve warning or evidence
```

The Chronicle should distinguish:

```text
physically impossible
unreasonably dangerous
resource-constrained triage
negligent delay
political refusal
profit-based refusal
deliberate concealment
```

# 6. Survivor Standing

Survivors do not become property, debt collateral, intelligence assets, or permanent wards of rescuers.

They retain rights to:

```text
medical care
identity and source-chain recovery
privacy
family and household continuity
political voice
refusal of invasive study
local due process
exit when feasible
cultural and mission continuity or change
```

Rescue costs may create public obligations only above the rights floor and through reviewable rules.

# 7. Historical Distress

A centuries-old distress signal may still require action.

Possible obligations:

```text
search for descendants
protect a wreck or grave site
recover source chains
warn of the cause
correct a concealed history
provide reparations
honor a refuge promise
contain an ongoing hazard
```

The game should avoid turning historical tragedy into salvage loot alone.

# 8. Irrecoverable Loss

Some losses cannot be reversed.

The design must allow:

```text
people die beyond recovery
messages never arrive
arks vanish
archives remain partial
species or cultures are lost
mission outcomes remain unknown
```

The player may preserve evidence, create memorials, support descendants, change institutions, or prevent repetition.

No late-game technology should erase every meaningful loss by default.

# 9. Bodies, Wrecks, and Archives

A derelict may contain:

```text
living survivors
reconstitutable persons
bodies
private archives
public mission records
hazardous systems
cultural heritage
unique machines
salvageable matter
```

Order of operations:

```text
stabilize hazard
search for life
establish identity and privacy boundaries
preserve evidence
notify or seek representatives
assess salvage rights
recover material with witness
```

Salvage authority never silently includes bodies, identities, or private memory.

# 10. Interstellar Missing Persons

A person may remain missing across generations.

Status options:

```text
in transit
communication lost
overdue
presumed dead
historically unresolved
continuity recovered
source chain recovered without body
body recovered without source chain
```

Legal and relational systems must preserve uncertainty rather than forcing immediate closure.

# 11. Rescue Compacts

A long-lived rescue compact defines:

```text
coverage region
available assets
priority rules
funding and contribution
relay and watch duties
refuge capacity
salvage boundaries
privacy and evidence handling
review and succession
```

It must not promise universal rescue where none is physically possible.

# 12. Nonhuman Rescue

Rescue may apply to:

```text
machine persons
swarm or distributed agencies
biospheric fragments
archive constructs
alien vessels
migratory ecologies
```

The responder must determine where agency and harm are located.

A “disabled probe” may be a tool, a person, a habitat, a quarantine system, or a dangerous replicator.

# 13. Player Experience

Interstellar rescue should create:

```text
uncertainty
hard but legible triage
sacrifice without automatic martyrdom
care through preparation
mourning without nihilism
institutional learning
```

It should not create:

```text
fake timers detached from physics
wealth-ranked morality
free rescue teleportation
salvage-first treatment of bodies
punishment for accepting physical impossibility
```

# 14. Chronicle Events

```text
DistressSignalReceivedLate
RescueFeasibilityPublished
ResponderDiverted
SupplyInterceptCommitted
RefugeCapacityReserved
DistressConcealed
HistoricalWreckFound
SourceChainRecovered
IrrecoverableLossAcknowledged
MemorialAndReparationEstablished
RescueCompactReformed
```

# 15. Hard Invariants

```text
no rescue priority based on wealth or cargo value
no responder teleportation
no false certainty about survival
no salvage claim over bodies or private memory
no survivor ownership through rescue debt
no historical distress discarded as irrelevant automatically
no impossible rescue presented as moral failure by itself
no worldline fork duplicating unique survivors, wrecks, or source chains accidentally
```

# Representative Proof

A distress signal from an ark arrives 86 years after emission. Direct rescue is impossible. Evidence suggests descendants may reach a different star system in another century, while a hazardous autonomous factory remains on their original route.

The proof succeeds when the worldline can:

- publish honest impossibility;
- send useful information and warnings;
- prepare future refuge;
- contain the ongoing hazard;
- preserve identity and private evidence;
- distinguish care from rescue fantasy;
- carry the obligation across generations and forks.
