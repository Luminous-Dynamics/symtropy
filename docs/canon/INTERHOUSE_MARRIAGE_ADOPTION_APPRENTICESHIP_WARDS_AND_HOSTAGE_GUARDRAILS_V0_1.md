# Interhouse Marriage, Adoption, Apprenticeship, Wards, and Hostage Guardrails

## Owned Question

**How can Symtropy portray relationships used in political alliance, succession, education, diplomacy, and coercion without treating people—especially children, dependents, companions, machines, or alien persons—as tradable resources?**

## Core Thesis

Relationships may have political consequences. They are not political inventory.

Marriage, partnership, adoption, apprenticeship, fosterage, wardship, diplomatic residence, and hostage-taking must be modeled as distinct social and legal relationships with different consent, care, dependency, and exit conditions.

> **A treaty may depend on a relationship. The treaty does not own the people inside it.**

# 1. Relationship Types

## 1.1 Adult Marriage or Partnership

A voluntary adult relationship that may carry:

- intimacy;
- household formation;
- care;
- property arrangements;
- migration status;
- inheritance;
- political symbolism;
- alliance expectations.

Its validity requires the consent of the participants, not only their houses.

## 1.2 Adoption

A legal and social creation of kinship and care responsibility.

Adoption may involve:

- children;
- adults;
- machine persons;
- alien-equivalent kinship;
- households;
- ceremonial lineages.

Adoption is not recruitment into political obedience.

## 1.3 Apprenticeship

A professional relationship involving:

- teaching;
- supervised work;
- tools;
- credentials;
- compensation;
- safety;
- professional succession.

Apprentices are workers and learners, not household property.

## 1.4 Fosterage

Temporary or long-term care without necessarily changing permanent kinship.

It requires:

- care standards;
- review;
- contact rules;
- education;
- health access;
- safe complaint channels;
- an exit or permanency plan.

## 1.5 Wardship

A person lives under another household or institution's protection, education, or guardianship because of:

- diplomatic exchange;
- family death;
- migration;
- legal incapacity;
- professional training;
- political agreement.

Wardship can be legitimate or coercive depending on material reality.

## 1.6 Diplomatic Guest

A person resides with another polity or house for:

- negotiation;
- education;
- cultural exchange;
- observation;
- confidence-building;
- route coordination.

A diplomatic guest retains personhood, communication, and departure rights subject to explicit lawful limits.

## 1.7 Hostage

A person is held to compel another actor.

This is coercive even when disguised as honor, fosterage, exchange, protective custody, or prestigious education.

The game may portray hostage systems. It must name and simulate the coercion rather than accepting elite euphemism as truth.

# 2. Adult Political Relationships

Adult characters may enter relationships that affect:

- citizenship;
- property;
- inheritance;
- alliance;
- migration;
- public image;
- access to institutions;
- route politics.

The relationship must still allow:

- refusal;
- negotiation;
- privacy;
- separation;
- divorce or equivalent;
- independent political opinion;
- protection from retaliation;
- continued personhood after the alliance fails.

No romance progression unlocks another house as a faction bonus.

# 3. Arranged and Negotiated Marriage

Symtropy may portray arranged relationships among consenting adults.

The simulation distinguishes:

- family introduction;
- community matchmaking;
- negotiated household compact;
- economic pressure;
- immigration dependence;
- coercive arrangement;
- forced marriage.

Consent is not proven by public ceremony, silence, family approval, or later affection.

A participant may sincerely value family involvement and still retain the right to refuse.

# 4. Relationship Agreements

Adult partners may negotiate:

- residence;
- property;
- income;
- care;
- privacy;
- children;
- public duties;
- political appearance;
- source-chain access;
- reconstitution preferences;
- separation;
- inheritance;
- machine or alien kinship translation.

These agreements cannot waive fundamental bodily autonomy or create ownership of a person.

# 5. Adoption and Succession

Adopted kin may hold real inheritance, household, ceremonial, or succession rights.

Biological descendants may challenge those rights, but the game does not treat biological claims as inherently superior.

Adoption decisions should consider:

- the adoptee's age and agency;
- existing relationships;
- culture;
- care capacity;
- political motivation;
- privacy;
- possible coercion;
- continuity across route separation.

An adoption performed solely to capture property or office may be legally valid, invalid, contested, or culturally accepted depending on the society. The game should show the people affected.

# 6. Children

Children must never function as alliance tokens in the player interface.

Political systems may impose harmful expectations upon them, but the simulation must preserve:

- age-appropriate agency;
- education;
- play;
- privacy;
- stable care;
- communication with trusted people;
- protection from sexual content;
- protection from political retaliation;
- the ability to grow into disagreement.

A child may inherit a famous name without becoming a playable political pawn.

Scenes involving child separation, coercion, or danger require restrained presentation and subject review.

# 7. Apprenticeship

An apprenticeship contract should specify:

```text
teacher_or_institution
apprentice
skill_domain
learning goals
work duties
compensation
hours
safety
housing if any
tool custody
assessment
complaint path
termination
credential outcome
```

Failure modes include:

- unpaid labor;
- dangerous work;
- credential withholding;
- debt bondage;
- hereditary exclusion;
- harassment;
- political recruitment disguised as training.

A successful apprenticeship may create a stronger succession claim than biological descent for professional roles.

# 8. Machine Apprentices and Kin

Machine persons may learn through:

- copied training;
- supervised practice;
- memory exchange;
- simulation;
- embodied work;
- social adoption.

Copying a teacher's memory does not automatically make the new person property or heir.

A machine lineage may recognize:

- source ancestor;
- teacher;
- maintainer;
- fork sibling;
- household parent;
- professional descendant.

These relationships can overlap without collapsing into one ownership relation.

# 9. Alien Kinship Translation

Alien relationships may not map to human marriage, adoption, wardship, or hostage categories.

Examples:

- temporary symbiotic joining;
- shared migration custody;
- memory-host relationships;
- metamorphic stage guardianship;
- collective offspring;
- habitat-based personhood;
- copied continuity.

The interface should display translation confidence and avoid using emotionally loaded human terms as settled fact.

# 10. Wards and Diplomatic Residents

A ward or diplomatic resident must have:

- named legal status;
- reason for placement;
- living conditions;
- education or role;
- communication access;
- healthcare;
- private space;
- trusted advocate;
- review schedule;
- return or exit conditions;
- protection from collective punishment.

The receiving house gains responsibility, not ownership.

# 11. Hostage Systems

Hostage-taking may occur through:

- detention of relatives;
- diplomatic “guests” unable to leave;
- children exchanged as security;
- machine backups held under threat;
- body-firmware access control;
- source-chain custody;
- withheld transit;
- hostage populations at route endpoints.

The game must represent:

- fear;
- isolation;
- bargaining;
- household consequences;
- legal status;
- escape and rescue risks;
- possible complicity of prestigious institutions;
- long aftermath.

It must not provide a clean `hostage leverage +20` resource detached from human cost.

# 12. Consent Under Dependency

Consent becomes unreliable when refusal threatens:

- air;
- housing;
- medicine;
- charging;
- immigration status;
- route passage;
- family contact;
- professional credential;
- source-chain recovery;
- safety.

The runtime should expose dependency structure rather than merely asking whether a dialogue node was accepted.

# 13. Breakup, Exit, and Political Consequences

An adult relationship may end while:

- the treaty continues;
- the treaty ends;
- the houses remain allied;
- public opinion shifts;
- property must be divided;
- children require care arrangements;
- one partner changes citizenship;
- one partner retains office;
- private information becomes politically dangerous.

The player should not be punished mechanically for respecting an NPC's refusal or separation.

# 14. Public and Private Identity

A political relationship has both public and private dimensions.

The game must not assume public affection reflects private safety.

Possible realities include:

- sincere intimacy under political scrutiny;
- private estrangement with cooperative public duty;
- public scandal around a healthy unconventional household;
- celebrated alliance concealing coercion;
- political rivals remaining loving partners;
- former partners becoming competent co-parents or colleagues.

# 15. Player Actions

Players may:

- enter a voluntary relationship;
- refuse a political match;
- negotiate a household compact;
- adopt or be adopted where appropriate;
- become or train an apprentice;
- host a diplomatic guest;
- expose concealed hostage conditions;
- help someone exit safely;
- mediate interhouse consequences;
- respect privacy;
- abuse power and face systemic consequences.

The player cannot:

- purchase affection;
- assign partners to NPCs;
- transfer children as resources;
- force adoption;
- command a companion to remain in a relationship;
- erase coercion through a charisma check.

# 16. Content Presentation

Mature relationship content requires:

- age verification;
- consent framing;
- content controls;
- privacy;
- nonvoyeuristic camera direction;
- alternative paths;
- subject-matter review;
- localization review;
- no sexualization of minors;
- no romantic reward for rescue.

Political pressure can be explicit without making intimate scenes explicit.

# 17. Representative Scenarios

## The Route Marriage

Two adult route heirs genuinely love each other but refuse to let their households define the relationship as a merger.

## The Adopted Engineer

An adopted apprentice is the most competent successor to a cooling house. Biological relatives challenge the appointment.

## Guest Without a Door

A prestigious diplomatic resident discovers that every exit requires permission. The host government calls it protection.

## The Fork in Custody

A machine lineage holds an inactive backup as “family property.” The backup is awakened and rejects the lineage's authority.

## The Child of Two Calendars

A child of separated-world parents becomes a public symbol while their actual needs are ignored.

## The Peace Ward

A young adult voluntarily studies in a rival polity and becomes trusted there, undermining factions that expected a hostage narrative.

# 18. Hard Invariants

1. Relationships do not become inventory.
2. Adult intimacy requires participant consent.
3. Children cannot consent to political trade.
4. Adoption creates care and kinship, not ownership.
5. Apprenticeship requires education, compensation, safety, and exit.
6. Wardship requires review and advocacy.
7. Hostage conditions must be named through material reality.
8. Dependency affects consent.
9. Breakup does not erase personhood or automatically dissolve every alliance.
10. Political drama never overrides the mature-content safety contract.

## Canon Maxim

> **The most meaningful alliance is not the one that binds two houses. It is the one whose people remain free enough to choose what the bond becomes.**
