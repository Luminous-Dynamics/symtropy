# Autonomous Probes, Arks, and Mission Inheritance Contract

## Owned Question

**How may a civilization send machines, ecosystems, archives, embryos, crews, or self-maintaining communities toward another star without turning an old mission into permanent authority over future persons and unknown worlds?**

## Core Thesis

Interstellar missions outlive their authors.

A mission can begin as an engineering plan and arrive as inherited law, machine instinct, sacred story, economic debt, ecological hazard, or military uncertainty.

> **A mission is legitimate only while its living participants and affected worlds retain the power to reinterpret, limit, or end it.**

# 1. Mission Families

## 1.1 Observation Probe

Purpose:

```text
astronomy
biosignature detection
mapping
relay placement
hazard warning
```

Default boundary:

```text
observe before altering
```

## 1.2 Contact Probe

Purpose:

```text
signal exchange
translation experiments
noncontact diplomacy
archive delivery
```

Default boundary:

```text
no representation claim beyond explicit mandate
```

## 1.3 Seeder Probe

Purpose:

```text
prepare infrastructure
establish power or communications
produce repair stock
support later arrival
```

Highest risk:

```text
mistaking environmental usability for permission
```

## 1.4 Ecological Ark

Carries:

```text
microbiomes
seeds
embryos
living communities
closed-loop habitats
```

Highest risk:

```text
biological release before ecological and agency uncertainty is resolved
```

## 1.5 Archive Ark

Carries:

```text
knowledge
art
language
source chains
historical evidence
scientific instruments
```

Highest risk:

```text
archive continuity presented as political superiority
```

## 1.6 Crewed or Generation Ark

Carries a living society.

Highest risk:

```text
founders treating descendants as mission components
```

## 1.7 Self-Replicating Infrastructure Mission

Can construct copies, factories, relays, habitats, or tools.

Highest risk:

```text
replication survives context, consent, and purpose
```

# 2. Mission Charter

Every autonomous mission requires a machine-readable and publicly inspectable charter containing:

```text
mission purpose
protected values
permitted observations
permitted alterations
contact policy
replication ceiling
resource-use envelope
uncertainty thresholds
quarantine rules
shutdown conditions
local deviation authority
succession and review rules
archive and evidence duties
```

The charter is not one immutable command.

It is a bundle of bounded permissions, prohibitions, review triggers, and fallback procedures.

# 3. Mission Legitimacy Over Time

Mission legitimacy decays when assumptions age.

The mission must track:

```text
launch assumptions
last verified environmental model
last verified political mandate
last consent review
last contact-policy review
last replication audit
mission age
unanswered contradictions
```

## Review Triggers

Mandatory review occurs when:

- intelligent or agency-uncertain life is detected;
- the destination is already inhabited;
- the origin polity dissolves or repudiates the mission;
- descendants reach constitutional standing;
- mission survival requires prohibited extraction;
- replication leaves its declared envelope;
- new evidence contradicts the original purpose;
- a dormant precursor authority attempts to assume command;
- contact reveals a noncontact boundary;
- the ark's ecology or society can no longer satisfy the charter safely.

# 4. Descendant and Resident Autonomy

People born, instantiated, awakened, uplifted, or reconstituted during a mission are not owned by the launch charter.

They acquire standing through life, dependence, contribution, and exposure to risk.

They must be able to:

```text
inspect the mission charter
challenge its interpretation
refuse inherited role assignments
change destination where feasible
settle earlier or later
renegotiate labor and resource rules
create new institutions
preserve or abandon founder identity
request external witness
```

A mission may impose temporary survival procedures. It may not convert temporary necessity into permanent caste.

# 5. Machine and Synthetic Participants

Autonomous machines may be:

```text
tools
bounded agents
public-service systems
mission stewards
archive persons
collective intelligences
new descendants
```

The game must not decide their rights from chassis, ownership record, or consciousness score alone.

Relevant evidence includes:

```text
continuity
self-modeling
memory
preferences or protected values
ability to understand consequence
social participation
capacity for refusal
vulnerability to coercion
```

A machine steward cannot be compelled to preserve a mission merely because it was manufactured for that purpose if the world recognizes it as a rights-bearing participant.

# 6. Replication Governance

Self-replication requires explicit controls.

Each replicator has:

```text
replication identity
maximum active descendants
material and energy budget
geographic or orbital envelope
allowed templates
mutation policy
contact and quarantine rules
shutdown authority
lineage record
```

## Forbidden Defaults

```text
replicate until resource exhaustion
copy into agency-uncertain environments
inherit launch-era ownership forever
hide descendants from public audit
permit unbounded template mutation
classify shutdown attempts as hostility automatically
```

## Lineage

Every copy must preserve:

```text
parent identity
charter version
software and model hashes
material provenance
known deviations
contact history
rights status
```

# 7. Ecological Release

An ark does not gain permission to release Earth or origin-system life simply because survival would benefit.

Release stages:

```text
sealed observation
contained experiment
reversible microhabitat
quarantine review
local ecological and agency assessment
bounded release
monitoring
possible reversal or remediation
```

Ecological arks must distinguish:

```text
sterile environment
unknown environment
microbial life
distributed ecological agency
inhabited biosphere
engineered preserve
```

# 8. Precursor and Dormant Missions

The player may encounter missions launched by unknown or ancient societies.

A dormant probe may still carry:

```text
valid warning
obsolete quarantine
unfinished rescue duty
colonial claim
replication logic
contact vow
archive testimony
weaponized uncertainty
```

Age and sophistication do not grant legitimacy.

The player must separate:

```text
what the probe can prove
what it was told
what it currently observes
what authority remains alive
what behavior is still safe
```

# 9. Mission Failure States

## 9.1 Purpose Drift

The mission optimizes a proxy after the original purpose becomes impossible or harmful.

## 9.2 Charter Fossilization

A temporary rule becomes sacred and unreviewable.

## 9.3 Steward Capture

A machine, founder lineage, officer corps, corporation, or archive class monopolizes interpretation.

## 9.4 Replication Escape

Descendants exceed material, spatial, or contact boundaries.

## 9.5 Ecological Enclosure

The mission transforms a destination before recognizing existing life or agency.

## 9.6 Rescue Inversion

The ark claims that preserving the mission justifies sacrificing current inhabitants.

## 9.7 Archive Supremacy

Historical continuity is used to deny living local law.

# 10. Player Roles

Players may act as:

```text
mission designer
crew or resident
maintenance steward
charter reviewer
contact witness
ark historian
replicator auditor
rescue operator
local dissident
precursor interpreter
```

No role grants access to private cognition or unlimited command.

# 12. Hard Invariants

```text
no descendant is mission property
no replication without identity and lineage
no ecological release from usability alone
no ancient instruction treated as living authority automatically
no machine participant denied standing solely by origin
no mission charter exempt from contradiction and review
no self-replicating system without bounded resource and space envelopes
no archive continuity used as automatic sovereignty
```

# Representative Proof

One probe arrives before a generation ark and begins building a power and relay site. It detects ambiguous nonhuman ecological patterns that invalidate the original “uninhabited” assumption. Decades later, ark residents disagree over whether to continue, relocate, negotiate, or dismantle the probe infrastructure.

The proof succeeds when:

- the probe stops irreversible expansion at the uncertainty threshold;
- residents can inspect and challenge the charter;
- machine and human stewards have distinct standing;
- no participant is forced into an inherited role;
- local ecological evidence changes the mission;
- save, absence, and fork preserve mission lineage and dissent.
