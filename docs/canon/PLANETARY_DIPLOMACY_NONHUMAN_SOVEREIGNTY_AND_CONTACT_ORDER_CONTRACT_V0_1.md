# Planetary Diplomacy, Nonhuman Sovereignty, and Contact Order Contract

## Owned Question

**Who may speak for a planet, recognize another intelligence, establish contact rules, negotiate coexistence, or claim sovereignty when agency may be distributed across settlements, habitats, ecosystems, machines, swarms, oceans, or deep time?**

## Core Thesis

First contact does not end when translation begins.

The harder phase starts afterward:

```text
Who has standing?
Who is represented?
What territory or habitat is being negotiated?
What commitments can each side actually make?
What remains unknowable?
```

A planetary federation may coordinate contact, but it cannot truthfully claim that every human, machine, settlement, species, or ecosystem speaks with one voice.

An alien polity may likewise be plural, contested, seasonal, distributed, or unable to make the kinds of commitments humans expect.

```text
Diplomacy begins when both sides stop pretending that recognition makes the other simple.
```

## Prime Directives

1. **No universal spokesperson by default.** The first entity understood is not automatically sovereign over its species or world.
2. **Agency location remains an open question.** A body, swarm, habitat, archive, ritual, or ecological process may carry only part of the relevant agency.
3. **Recognition is scoped.** Recognizing personhood, territorial standing, treaty capacity, ecological interest, or machine testimony are separate acts.
4. **Translation does not erase uncertainty.** Diplomatic language preserves confidence, ambiguity, and disputed mappings.
5. **No planetary treaty without affected representation.** Surface settlements, orbital habitats, nonhuman communities, and biospheric interests may all have standing.
6. **No contact authority by military possession.** The actor with the strongest weapons or first sensor contact does not automatically own diplomacy.
7. **No containment without review.** Quarantine may be necessary, but indefinite noncontact must remain evidence-bound and appealable where possible.
8. **No technology transfer as simple loot.** Tools carry environmental assumptions, dependencies, authority, and possible agency.
9. **No forced integration.** Coexistence may require distance, seasonal access, layered territory, or permanent noncontact zones.
10. **No ancient authority by age alone.** Deep-time civilizations may deserve attention, not obedience.

# 1. Recognition Types

Recognition may include:

```text
biological life
sentience uncertainty
individual personhood
collective personhood
ecological agency
territorial standing
treaty-making capacity
witness capacity
custody rights
noncontact rights
```

These statuses can differ.

Example:

```text
A planetary wetland may receive protected ecological standing
before humans can establish whether it has treaty-making agency.
```

# 3. Contact Authority

Planetary contact authority may own:

```text
shared observation
signal containment
planetary risk coordination
translation commons
initial nonaggression protocol
cross-region warning
orbital traffic response
```

It may not automatically own:

```text
local habitat access
resource concessions
cultural representation
religious interpretation
permanent territorial transfer
body or memory access
```

# 4. Representation of Human Plurality

A contact delegation may include:

```text
planetary federation representatives
contact-region residents
orbital representatives
scientists and translators
care and quarantine experts
machine persons
indigenous or local knowledge holders
rights guardians
military observers without command supremacy
```

The delegation must disclose its limits. It cannot promise compliance from polities outside its authority.

# 5. Representation of Nonhuman Plurality

Humans must not infer one alien polity from one signal pattern.

Possible internal structures:

```text
seasonal councils
competing swarms
life-stage authority
geographic minds
ritual assemblies
machine mediators
slow and fast cognition layers
colonies with distinct memory lineages
```

The contact runtime should preserve hypotheses about who speaks for whom.

# 8. Diplomatic Evidence

Evidence may include:

```text
repeated signal-response patterns
behavior under controlled conditions
machine or archive testimony
ecological changes
material exchange
boundary respect
translated commitments
long-term compliance
```

No single impressive translation proves full understanding.

Diplomatic records distinguish:

```text
raw observation
translation candidate
accepted correspondence
party declaration
binding commitment

```

# 9. Treaties Across Different Timescales

A party may deliberate over seconds, seasons, decades, or centuries.

Tools include:

```text
provisional boundaries
precommitment charters
slow-response beacons
caretaker institutions
successor obligations
reversible actions
long review windows
```

Silence is not automatically refusal. Delay also cannot indefinitely freeze urgent human survival. The parties may negotiate precautionary temporary arrangements.

# 10. Quarantine and Noncontact

Legitimate quarantine protects both sides from:

```text
pathogens
invasive biology
machine infection
signal hazards
cognitive intrusion
translation harm
ecological collapse
```

Noncontact can be a right, not only a punishment.

A noncontact zone requires:

```text
boundary legibility
safe observation limits
rescue exceptions
monitoring
review
anti-exploitation enforcement
```

# 11. Xenotechnology Diplomacy

Technology exchange requires agreement about:

```text
copy rights
repair rights
embedded agency
maintenance dependencies
environmental compatibility
military conversion
cultural or sacred restrictions
return or deletion
```

A translated tool may still contain a relationship or living process that cannot be owned as ordinary property.

# 12. Planetary Defense and Contact

Defense systems may be necessary when intent is unknown or hostile.

Rules:

```text
defensive readiness does not prove hostility
weapons do not own diplomacy
contact channels remain open where safe
civilian and nonhuman habitats receive protection
false certainty and escalation are monitored
```

A faction may exploit contact fear to centralize authority. That pressure must remain visible.

# 13. Interspecies Disputes

Disputes may concern:

```text
habitat damage
resource extraction
sampling
signal interference
migration
quarantine
translation privacy
machine copies
accidental harm
historical contact wounds
```

Resolution may involve restitution, restoration, boundary redesign, observation, noncontact, shared infrastructure, or withdrawal.

Human-style trial procedure is not always appropriate, but evidence, protection, accountability, and appeal remain goals.

# 14. Planetary Contact Failure Modes

## Contact Monopoly

One settlement or corporation controls all translation and trade.

## Species Essentialism

One faction's behavior defines the whole alien population.

## Benevolent Ancient Submission

Humans surrender judgment because the other civilization is older.

## Quarantine Empire

Risk authority becomes permanent planetary rule.

## Translation Colonialism

Human categories become mandatory conditions for recognition.

## Extraction Diplomacy

Contact exists mainly to secure resources or technology.

## Representation Fiction

A delegation signs commitments it cannot enforce.

# 16. Worldline and Confluence

Contact outcomes may fork worldlines:

```text
noncontact
coexistence
trade
shared research
conflict
quarantine
translation partnership
planetary alliance
```

Forks preserve shared pre-contact evidence and ancestry. A Confluence may later compare or reconcile divergent contact histories without declaring one automatically true.

# 17. Acceptance Tests

- the first understood entity is not automatically treated as sovereign;
- recognition types are separate and reviewable;
- a contact order exposes evidence, uncertainty, limits, and expiry;
- human delegates cannot commit outside their mandate;
- alien territorial claims can be represented without reducing them to land parcels;
- noncontact can be a legitimate negotiated outcome;
- xenotechnology transfer includes dependencies and rights;
- military readiness cannot silently replace diplomatic authority;
- worldline forks preserve contact evidence while allowing different outcomes;
- players can explain who was represented, what was promised, and what remained uncertain.

## Final Line

```text
A planet is ready for diplomacy when it can meet another world
without pretending either world speaks with one mouth.
```
