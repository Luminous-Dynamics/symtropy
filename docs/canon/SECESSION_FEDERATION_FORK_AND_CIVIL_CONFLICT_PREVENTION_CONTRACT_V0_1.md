# Secession, Federation Fork, and Civil Conflict Prevention Contract

## Owned Question

**How can a polity leave, divide, renegotiate, or dissolve a federation without making unity permanent by force or turning every constitutional dispute into war?**

## Core Thesis

A federation that cannot be left is an empire, even when it began by consent.

A secession process that ignores shared infrastructure, minorities, debt, ecosystems, and movement can also become coercion.

```text
Exit must be possible.
Exit must be consequential.
Exit must not erase the people who disagree with it.
```

Secession in Symtropy is not a single vote bar. It is a long process of evidence, deliberation, representation, negotiation, material settlement, continuity planning, and possible worldline divergence.

## Prime Directives

1. **No permanent union without review.** Federal membership must have amendment and exit procedures.
2. **No surprise secession.** Durable exit requires visible mandates, notice, deliberation, and transition planning except under immediate oppression or collapse.
3. **No majority ownership of minorities.** Residents who oppose exit retain rights, identity, property, movement, and representation.
4. **No hostage infrastructure.** Shared grids, water, archives, routes, habitats, and care systems require continuity plans.
5. **No debt as eternal chains.** Obligations are settled, restructured, compensated, or contested; they do not justify indefinite political captivity.
6. **No asset duplication.** Worldline or server forks must preserve ancestry and prevent duplicated transferable value.
7. **No constitutional disagreement as automatic treason.** Advocacy for reform, exit, autonomy, or dissolution is not sabotage by default.
8. **No forced conflict through multiplayer governance.** Worldline profiles and PvP consent remain authoritative.
9. **No peace without dissent.** Agreements preserve minority testimony and unresolved claims.
10. **No clean break fiction.** Families, labor, culture, routes, ecology, and memory continue across new borders.

# 1. Exit Pathways

## 1.1 Renegotiated Autonomy

A member remains inside the federation under revised authority, contribution, standards, or representation.

## 1.2 Associated Membership

A full member becomes a limited participant in selected systems.

## 1.3 Lawful Secession

A polity exits according to the constitutional procedure and negotiated transition.

## 1.4 Emergency Withdrawal

A polity suspends obligations because the federation violates the rights floor, loses legitimacy, or creates immediate existential danger.

## 1.5 Negotiated Dissolution

The federation winds down and distributes or transforms shared institutions.

## 1.6 Federation Fork

Two or more successor federations claim legitimate continuity from the same ancestor.

## 1.7 Collapse and De Facto Separation

Shared institutions fail before legal settlement. Later play concerns recognition, reconstruction, and claims.

# 2. Secession Mandate

A mandate must define:

```text
which territory or community is acting
who is eligible to participate
what status is proposed
what alternatives were considered
what transition period applies
what shared systems are affected
what minority and mobility protections apply
what threshold makes the result valid
```

Possible mandate sources:

```text
referendum
constitutional assembly
settlement council plus public ratification
member-polity consensus
worldline-owner fork with member opt-in
emergency popular convention
```

No one mechanism is universally legitimate. The charter must specify the ordinary path and emergency exceptions.

# 3. Referendum Integrity

A referendum is valid only when:

```text
question wording is legible
options are materially possible
voter identity is protected
information access is broad enough
coercion is monitored
opposition can organize
results are auditable
appeals are available
transition consequences are disclosed
```

The game should model:

```text
turnout
boycott
intimidation
media capture
diaspora participation
displaced residents
machine or nonhuman standing
contested districts
```

A numeric majority does not automatically solve legitimacy disputes.

# 4. Affected Populations

The process must account for:

```text
residents supporting exit
residents opposing exit
mixed households
mobile workers
refugees
diaspora
seasonal users
machine persons
nonhuman communities
people whose identity records are disputed
```

Protections may include:

```text
residency choice
dual membership
movement rights
property continuity
language and school rights
religious and cultural protection
access to care and pensions
identity and source-chain recognition
```

# 5. Shared Asset Settlement

Shared assets are classified by:

```text
location
ownership
operation
maintenance contribution
public-good function
debt
strategic importance
replacement difficulty
```

Settlement options:

```text
transfer
joint ownership
shared operation
lease
access treaty
compensation
neutral authority
division
managed decommissioning
```

Critical systems should not be switched off while title is disputed unless continued operation creates greater danger.

# 6. Debt and Obligation Settlement

Obligations include:

```text
financial debt
mutual-aid commitments
pension and care obligations
maintenance debt
environmental remediation
archive custody
defense commitments
unfinished projects
```

The parties negotiate allocation based on benefit, capacity, responsibility, and continuity.

A successor inherits neither all guilt nor no obligation. Evidence and institutional ancestry matter.

# 7. Borders and Movement

New borders create gameplay through:

```text
checkpoints
customs
commuting
mixed infrastructure
family visits
trade
migration
security
smuggling
ecological corridors
```

The default should not be immediate hard borders. Possible regimes include:

```text
open movement
common travel area
permit system
customs without immigration control
security-limited crossing
temporary protected corridor
```

# 8. Civil Conflict Prevention

Prevention mechanisms include:

```text
mediation
constitutional convention
shared-system guarantees
neutral monitoring
mutual stand-down
protected organizing
independent media
minority safeguards
staged autonomy
asset escrow
peacekeeping by consent
```

The system tracks escalation signals:

```text
militia formation
weapon movement
identity purges
infrastructure seizure
hate propaganda
emergency-law expansion
political detention
communications shutdown
```

Signals create warnings and opportunities; they do not make war inevitable.

# 9. Constitutional Crisis

Crisis occurs when actors disagree about who has authority to decide the future.

Possible states:

```text
dual governments
invalidated election
deadlocked chambers
source-chain compromise
emergency authority refusing expiry
member boycott
court nonrecognition
military or machine intervention
```

The runtime must preserve competing claims and evidence. It must not select a winner through hidden faction strength alone.

# 10. Force and Protection

If violence begins, the war and social-safety contracts govern.

Special protections include:

```text
neutral care facilities
archive protection
civilian exit routes
shared utility continuity
prisoner exchange
ceasefire monitoring
prohibition on identity destruction
```

Infrastructure seizure is a strategic act with civilian and legitimacy consequences.

# 11. Federation Fork and Worldlines

A federation fork creates:

```text
shared ancestor charter
shared pre-fork history
separate post-fork institutions
asset-allocation rules
identity recognition rules
migration choices
cross-fork treaties
```

A game worldline fork may let a community preserve an alternate political outcome without forcing all players into it.

Hard rules:

```text
unique transferable assets cannot exist as spendable originals in both forks
personal identity retains ancestry
Chronicle history before the fork remains shared
post-fork claims remain branch-specific
return or merge requires explicit reconciliation
```

# 12. Reunification and Confederation

Former members may later create:

```text
customs union
mutual-aid compact
confederation
shared infrastructure authority
full reunification
```

Reunification is not the moral default. Peaceful coexistence may be the better outcome.

# 13. Player Roles

Players may:

```text
organize for autonomy or union
protect an honest referendum
repair infrastructure during transition
negotiate asset sharing
escort displaced families
expose intimidation
mediate a ceasefire
choose a worldline fork
build a shared institution after political separation
```

The game does not dictate which constitutional outcome is correct.

# 14. Failure Modes

## Coercive Union

The federation uses shared systems to make exit impossible.

## Elite Secession

Leaders seek exit to evade labor, environmental, or justice obligations.

## Majoritarian Expulsion

Secession becomes a project to remove minorities or redefine them as outsiders.

## Infrastructure Blackmail

Either side threatens water, power, air, archives, or rescue.

## Frozen Partition

Temporary barriers become permanent without review.

## Duplicate Sovereignty

Both sides claim the same assets and identities without a conflict-resolution model.

## Worldline Escape Abuse

Players fork to evade debts, bans, or accountability while attempting to carry duplicated value.

# 15. Seedworks Proof Boundary

The representative region may prove this contract through:

```text
one member threatening to leave a regional compact
one shared route or service at risk
one minority or mixed household affected
one negotiated autonomy or association option
one transition agreement
```

Full planetary secession belongs to long-horizon play.

# 16. Acceptance Tests

- advocacy for exit does not automatically create criminal or hostile status;
- a vote preserves question, eligibility, evidence, turnout, and appeal;
- minorities retain rights and mobility after the result;
- shared assets receive explicit continuity plans;
- emergency withdrawal can occur under rights-floor violation;
- a worldline fork preserves ancestry without duplicating transferable assets;
- political separation does not erase families, debts, records, or ecological dependencies;
- ceasefire and mediation can prevent war without forcing reunion;
- an inactive federation owner cannot lock all members into a dead union;
- players can understand why union held, changed, or ended.

## Final Line

```text
A union proves its legitimacy not only by what it can build together,
but by whether it can survive disagreement without making departure a crime.
```
