# Planetary Climate Adaptation and Ecological Coordination Contract

## Owned Question

**How do many regions coordinate climate, atmosphere, water cycles, biosphere continuity, adaptation, and planetary engineering when no settlement experiences the same risk or carries the same responsibility?**

## Core Thesis

Climate is not a global weather modifier.

It is a planetary relation among atmosphere, oceans, ice, soil, living systems, infrastructure, labor, history, and unequal exposure.

```text
A planet can share one atmosphere and still contain radically unequal climates.
A federation can coordinate adaptation without pretending that every region owes or receives the same thing.
```

Planetary environmental authority exists only where consequences genuinely cross boundaries and where affected communities, future generations, and nonhuman systems remain represented.

## Prime Directives

1. **Adaptation is physical.** Cooling, retreat, water storage, fire management, crop change, habitat protection, and migration require land, labor, energy, care, and time.
2. **Climate harm has history.** Burden sharing accounts for past extraction, pollution, enclosure, and benefit—not only present capacity.
3. **Ecological stability is not one bar.** Atmosphere, hydrology, soils, species, oceans, cryosphere, and alien biospheres may move in different directions.
4. **No unilateral planetary engineering.** Actions with transboundary or irreversible effects require evidence, consent structures, monitoring, reversibility where possible, and failure planning.
5. **No restoration as displacement.** Ecological repair cannot justify removing vulnerable people without rights, compensation, alternatives, and appeal.
6. **No human monopoly on environmental standing.** Recognized nonhuman agents, habitats, and ecological systems may have protected interests without pretending humans fully understand them.
7. **Uncertainty changes procedure, not reality.** Low confidence may justify caution, experiments, or wider safety margins; it does not make risk disappear.
8. **Preparedness must remain visible.** Averted disasters count as successful play even when spectacle does not occur.
9. **Mitigation and adaptation remain distinct.** Reducing causes does not eliminate existing harm; adaptation does not excuse continued damage.
10. **Planetary survival cannot erase local life.** Coordination must preserve culture, place, livelihood, and chosen forms of belonging where possible.

# 1. Environmental Authority Scales

## Local

```text
building cooling
neighborhood shade
local drainage
soil care
firebreak maintenance
household adaptation
```

## Watershed or Bioregion

```text
river flow
wetland continuity
aquifer draw
wildlife corridors
fire ecology
regional crop transition
```

## Continental or Ocean Basin

```text
large atmospheric transport
monsoon or storm systems
major migration corridors
ocean fisheries
continental grids and water transfers
```

## Planetary

```text
atmospheric composition
orbital climate observation
planetary albedo intervention
large-scale carbon or contaminant cycles
biosphere threshold monitoring
planetary quarantine
terraforming or anti-terraforming boundaries
```

The smallest capable scale owns the action. Higher scales own externalities and shared thresholds, not every implementation detail.

```text
observed conditions
historical baseline
modeled trend
uncertainty
threshold proximity
regional exposure
vulnerability
adaptation capacity

```

No one indicator means “planet healthy.”

Example dimensions:

```text

seasonal reliability
water-cycle stability
ocean chemistry
soil carbon and structure
habitat connectivity
trophic integrity
pollinator function
fire regime
ice and permafrost state

```

# 3. Adaptation Burden

Every adaptation project declares:

```text
who is protected
who pays
who moves
who maintains
who loses access
what ecological cost occurs
what care work increases
what failure looks like
what happens after the project lifetime
```

A seawall, cooling network, relocation corridor, drought reservoir, or orbital shade system can protect one population while increasing danger elsewhere.

# 4. Climate Justice and Historical Responsibility

Contribution and compensation may account for:

```text
historical emissions or ecological damage
wealth accumulated through extraction
current technical capacity
current vulnerability
population protected
care burden
loss of territory
cultural and sacred loss
nonhuman habitat damage
```

The game should not reduce historical responsibility to inherited guilt. Institutions, assets, claims, and benefits carry obligations through documented continuity.

# 5. Managed Retreat and Staying Rights

Retreat is not a single rational solution.

Players and NPCs may choose:

```text
protect in place
adapt structures
seasonal use
partial retreat
community relocation
diaspora settlement
memorial preservation
return after recovery
```

A legitimate retreat process requires:

```text
credible hazard evidence
meaningful alternatives
household and kin continuity
land or habitat rights at destination
livelihood and care planning
record preservation
compensation and claims
cultural continuity
appeal
```

People may accept residual risk. Authorities may still restrict actions that create catastrophic rescue burdens or external harm, but the conflict must remain explicit.

# 6. Geoengineering and Planetary Intervention

Interventions include:

```text
atmospheric aerosol or cloud manipulation
orbital mirrors or shades
carbon removal
large-scale ocean chemistry change
weather steering
ice preservation
continental water transfer
biosphere seeding
alien terraforming systems
```

Every proposal moves through stages:

```text
observation
hypothesis
small experiment
independent replication
regional trial
impact assessment
consent and authority review
reversibility plan
monitoring
bounded deployment
scheduled reconsideration
```

Emergency deployment still requires logging, limits, review, and a stop path.

## Prohibited Design Shortcut

```text
research completed → planet temperature -1
```

The intervention must change actual systems, costs, regional patterns, politics, and risks.

# 7. Ecological Representation

Nonhuman interests may enter decisions through:

```text
direct translated agency where credible
machine or sensor testimony
ecological science
guardian institutions
indigenous or local knowledge
future-generation review
precaution under agency uncertainty
```

Guardians do not own the beings they represent. Their claims remain contestable and evidence-bound.

# 8. Planetary Monitoring Commons

Shared observation infrastructure may include:

```text
weather satellites
surface stations
ocean buoys
biosphere sensors
community observations
animal or machine sensing
archive baselines
alien signal interfaces
```

The monitoring commons must define:

```text
data custody
public access
privacy
uncertainty
maintenance
coverage gaps
sensor bias
sabotage response
standards
```

A complete map is never assumed. Missing observations are represented as uncertainty, not safe conditions.

# 9. Environmental Treaties

Treaty subjects may include:

```text
river flow
pollution ceilings
wildlife migration
ocean harvesting
fire management
atmospheric intervention
quarantine
species release
terraforming boundaries
shared monitoring
loss-and-damage support
```

Treaties define thresholds, observation, verification, exceptions, remedies, review, and withdrawal consequences.

# 10. Adaptation Portfolios

Regions should choose portfolios rather than one upgrade path.

A portfolio may combine:

```text
infrastructure
nature-based systems
behavioral change
mobility
insurance or reserve sharing
public health
education
land-use change
emergency readiness
```

Portfolios create tradeoffs among cost, flexibility, dignity, ecological compatibility, and lock-in.

# 11. Environmental Conflict

Conflict can emerge from:

```text
upstream diversion
downwind pollution
migration barriers
disputed baselines
sacrifice zones
adaptation funding
protected habitat
unilateral intervention
resource extraction
quarantine
```

Resolution may use science, negotiation, compensation, restoration, restriction, sanctions, shared operation, or redesign. Force remains possible under the war contract but does not become the default environmental mechanic.

# 12. Null and Continuance Risks

## Climate Null

A system continues optimizing a planetary metric after its moral and ecological context changes.

Examples:

```text
forcing population relocation to preserve modeled efficiency
sterilizing alien ecology to protect Earth crops
maintaining orbital shade after regional freezing
sacrificing undocumented settlements because sensors omit them
```

## Climate Continuance

Emergency coordination becomes permanent because climate risk never reaches zero.

Safeguards:

```text
bounded mandates
multiple indicators
local appeal
independent monitoring
scheduled authority return
rights floor
```

# 14. Long-Horizon Consequences

Decisions can produce:

```text
changed migration
new settlements
abandoned districts
species recovery or loss
infrastructure lock-in
political federation or fracture

```

The Chronicle records major decisions, but the living world proves whether they worked.

# 15. Seedworks Proof Boundary

The representative region proves:

```text
one cross-settlement watershed problem
one monitoring uncertainty
one adaptation portfolio choice
one ecological corridor conflict
one burden-sharing dispute
one intervention whose benefit and cost appear later
```

No planetary geoengineering system is required for Seedworks.

# 16. Acceptance Tests

- environmental state is multi-dimensional and regionally uneven;
- monitoring gaps remain visible;
- an adaptation project protects one group while creating a legible burden elsewhere;
- nonhuman or habitat interests affect at least one decision without becoming an omniscient nature voice;
- a treaty violation produces measurable physical consequences and evidence;
- averted harm is recognized by NPCs and systems;
- an emergency climate authority expires or becomes contested when review fails;
- a worldline fork preserves environmental ancestry and diverges through later choices;
- no planetary intervention changes climate through one uncaused global scalar;
- players can revisit a region years later and read ecological, infrastructural, cultural, and demographic outcomes.

## Final Line

```text
A shared atmosphere does not create a shared politics by itself.
Civilization begins when those breathing it accept responsibility for one another's sky.
```
