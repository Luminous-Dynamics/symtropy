# Living Worlds, Ecology, and Terraforming Contract

## Owned Question

**How does Symtropy make ecosystems and planets into living, legible, transformable participants in play without reducing ecology to decoration, a single health bar, or a universal Earth-normal optimization problem?**

## What This Document Does Not Own

This contract does not define individual species catalogs, first-contact diplomacy, settlement economy, or the exact simulation algorithm. It owns the player-facing meaning, rights boundaries, transformation rules, and product constraints for living worlds.

## Core Thesis

A living world is not scenery around civilization.

It is a set of coupled metabolisms with memory, thresholds, dependencies, wounds, and possible forms of agency.

```text
Players do not merely extract from planets.
They learn how worlds continue.
They decide which continuities to protect, change, join, or end.
```

Restoration is not returning every place to an imagined pristine baseline. Terraforming is not filling a progress bar until a planet resembles Earth. Ecology is not a penalty system attached to industry.

The core fantasy is:

> **Understand a living system deeply enough that intervention becomes relationship rather than domination.**

## Ecological Prime Directives

1. **No universal ecology score.** Soil, water, atmosphere, trophic integrity, habitat continuity, evolutionary novelty, contamination, and agency recognition remain distinct.
2. **No single correct baseline.** Historical conditions, current inhabitants, future viability, and deliberate cultural choices may conflict.
3. **No instant terraforming.** Planetary transformation requires infrastructure, energy, organisms, time, monitoring, governance, and acceptance of uncertainty.
4. **No species as inventory buffs.** Visible organisms have habitat requirements, interactions, welfare, reproduction, disease, and political meaning.
5. **No Earth exceptionalism.** Earth species may restore Earth-derived systems and catastrophically invade alien ones.
6. **No ecological harm without readable causes.** Players must be able to observe, infer, test, and revisit the mechanisms behind change.
7. **No irreversible surprise as routine design.** Large interventions require forecasts, staged deployment, monitoring windows, and abort or containment paths unless the uncertainty itself is clearly telegraphed.
8. **No ecology detached from civilization.** Food, health, construction, culture, transport, warfare, law, and settlement design all affect and depend on living systems.
9. **No assumption that agency requires a face.** A reef, wetland, fungal network, ocean, climate pattern, or biosphere may become a negotiating party when evidence supports it.
10. **No simulation complexity without gameplay return.** Every modeled ecological variable must change observable behavior, available choices, or future risk.

# 1. The Five Ecological Scales

## 1.1 Organism Scale

Individual or colony-level bodies the player can see, handle, treat, track, protect, cultivate, or harm.

Examples:

```text
companion animals
pollinators
keystone plants
microbial cultures
alien symbionts
uplift candidates
```

## 1.2 Habitat-Patch Scale

A grove, pond, soil bed, reef section, cave chamber, rooftop garden, or atmospheric pocket.

This is the smallest scale at which composition, shelter, reproduction, contamination, and local feedback loops become meaningful.

## 1.3 Landscape and Watershed Scale

Connected habitats linked by water, migration, fire, wind, nutrient flow, roads, industry, and settlements.

This scale owns:

```text
corridors
fragmentation
floodplains
pollution plumes
migration routes
invasive spread
regional restoration
```

## 1.4 Biome and Continental Scale

Climate-linked ecological regimes whose stability depends on broad circulation, large migrations, disturbance patterns, and land use.

## 1.5 Planetary Biosphere Scale

Atmospheric chemistry, ocean circulation, planetary energy balance, biogeochemical cycles, evolutionary reservoirs, global extinctions, and possible biospheric intelligence.

No ordinary player action directly edits planetary state. Planetary change emerges from networks of interventions, institutions, infrastructures, and long time horizons.

# 2. Ecological State Is a Vector

A living region tracks multiple conditions rather than one green meter.

```text
primary productivity
water-cycle integrity
soil or substrate function
habitat connectivity
trophic regulation
genetic and functional diversity
contaminant burden
invasive pressure
disease pressure
climate fit
resilience and redundancy
extinction debt
adaptation debt
agency uncertainty
```

Two places can have similar productivity and radically different futures.

A plantation may produce abundant biomass while possessing low diversity, poor resilience, high chemical dependency, and no habitat continuity. A damaged wetland may appear unproductive while recovering key filtration, flood-buffering, and nursery functions.

Ecological play must include discovery and restraint, not only deployment.

# 4. Restoration, Adaptation, and Terraforming

## 4.1 Repair

Restore a damaged function using local knowledge and bounded intervention.

Example:

```text
reconnect a pollinator corridor
remove a toxic discharge
reopen a tidal channel
stabilize a spawning substrate
```

## 4.2 Restoration

Move a system toward an earlier or agreed reference condition while acknowledging that climate, species, and communities have changed.

Restoration must state:

```text
reference condition
beneficiaries
excluded or displaced life
maintenance burden
uncertainty
reversal path
```

## 4.3 Assisted Adaptation

Help organisms or communities survive new conditions through migration, habitat modification, selective breeding, symbiosis, or biological modification.

## 4.4 Ecological Construction

Build new living systems without pretending they are historical restoration.

Examples:

```text
constructed wetlands
orbital closed ecologies
novel soil guilds
engineered reef breakwaters
alien-human buffer habitats
```

## 4.5 Terraforming

Deliberately alter planetary conditions across generations.

Terraforming requires:

```text
planetary observation network
energy and material infrastructure
staged models and experiments
biosphere governance
nonhuman-agency review
intergenerational commitments
abort and containment doctrines
Chronicle-scale accountability
```

Terraforming is always plural. Different settlements, species, and generations may desire different planets.

# 5. Ecological Rights and Authority

Ecological decisions may involve:

```text
human residents
future residents
animals and uplifted clades
alien organisms
biospheric or distributed intelligences
machine ecologies
watershed or habitat stewards
scientific institutions
settlement charters
```

Rights do not require romanticizing nature. Predation, disease, invasion, and ecological conflict remain real.

The minimum rights floor under agency uncertainty includes:

```text
no extermination before adequate classification when containment is viable
no irreversible habitat destruction without recorded necessity
no forced translation through military categories alone
no release of Earth life into alien ecologies without staged evidence
no ownership claim over an intelligent ecology merely because it lacks human institutions
```

# 6. Industry and Ecology Must Remain Coupled

Industry changes:

```text
land cover
noise
light
water flow
heat
chemical load
migration
harvest pressure
fire regimes
atmospheric composition
```

Ecology changes:

```text
food reliability
water quality
flood risk
soil productivity
disease
construction stability
route access
energy yield
settlement health
cultural life
```

The game must surface both directions.

A factory should not merely create an “ecology penalty.” Its inputs, emissions, heat, transport, and land use produce concrete pathways. Ecological recovery can reduce costs, create new materials, alter routes, or make life more desirable.

# 7. Ecological Conflict

Legitimate conflicts include:

```text
food production versus habitat continuity
flood safety versus wetland expansion
invasive removal versus recognized alien agency
predator restoration versus livestock safety
quarantine versus migration
heritage landscape versus climate adaptation
planetary habitability versus native biosphere sovereignty
```

No side should be generated as automatically correct. The world model must represent actual consequences and uncertainty.

# 8. Failure and Continuation

Ecological failure should change the future rather than always forcing reload.

Possible continuations:

```text
local extinction
new invasive equilibrium
contaminated but productive landscape
forced settlement relocation
expensive artificial life support
changed species relationships
political schism
quarantine zone
restoration movement
new scientific doctrine
biospheric hostility or withdrawal
```

Recovery may remain possible, but not necessarily as restoration of the previous state.

# 9. Progression

Ecological mastery grows through:

```text
better observation
longer baselines
replicated experiments
trusted ecological relationships
improved containment
species care knowledge
regional coordination
institutional memory
planetary sensing
```

It must not be represented solely by unlocking stronger organisms.

# 10. Seedworks Boundary

The representative build should prove:

```text
one habitat patch with visible cause and response
one trophic or species interaction
one industrial or settlement pressure
one reversible intervention
one delayed consequence
one ecological disagreement among NPCs
one Field Deck uncertainty state
one reason to revisit after several in-game days
```

Seedworks does not require a planetary biosphere simulation or terraforming.

# 11. Acceptance Tests

A living-world feature is not ready unless:

1. The player can identify at least one cause of ecological change without reading a design document.
2. Two interventions with similar short-term output create meaningfully different long-term states.
3. A system can degrade gradually and cross a threshold without relying on random punishment.
4. Ecological state affects at least two non-ecology systems.
5. The player can monitor uncertainty and revise an intervention.
6. Off-screen simulation preserves causal outcomes without simulating every organism.
7. Alien or distributed agency remains uncertain until evidence supports recognition.
8. The feature has explicit performance, persistence, and multiplayer authority boundaries.

## Final Rule

```text
A planet is not conquered when it becomes useful.
It becomes known when its continuities can correct the player’s plans.
```
