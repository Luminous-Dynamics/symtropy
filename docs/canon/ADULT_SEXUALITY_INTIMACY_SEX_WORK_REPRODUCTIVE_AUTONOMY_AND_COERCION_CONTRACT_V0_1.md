# Adult Sexuality, Intimacy, Sex Work, Reproductive Autonomy, and Coercion Contract

## Purpose

Symtropy may portray adult desire, sex, romance, rejection, jealousy, sex work, reproductive decisions, and coercion as parts of lived society.

The purpose is not to maximize explicitness. It is to represent adult life without pretending that desire, vulnerability, bodies, labor, and power disappear in a serious civilization simulation.

## Core Principle

> **Intimacy is meaningful because participants retain the ability to choose, refuse, negotiate, change, leave, and remain more than the relationship.**

# 1. Adult Scope

Romantic and sexual systems apply only to clearly established adults under the relevant species, culture, and legal framework.

The game must not:

- sexualize minors;
- allow adult–minor romance paths;
- use ambiguous age coding for sexualized characters;
- treat accelerated growth, cloning, artificial bodies, or reconstitution as loopholes;
- frame guardianship, teaching, medical care, captivity, or dependency as consent;
- make sexual access a reward for completing care or rescue.

When alien or artificial life stages are unfamiliar, content remains nonsexual until adulthood and decision capacity are clearly established.

# 2. Consent State

Consent is:

- specific;
- informed;
- voluntary;
- reversible;
- current;
- scoped to an act or relationship boundary;
- independent from attraction or prior agreement.

Consent is not inferred solely from:

- reputation;
- romance score;
- marriage;
- intoxication;
- employment contract;
- debt;
- rescue;
- previous intimacy;
- generated dialogue;
- hidden NPC state.

The authoritative consent state lives in validated relationship and action systems, not language generation.

# 3. Player Experience

Intimate content must be:

- optional;
- clearly signaled;
- interruptible;
- skippable;
- adjustable by content settings;
- available in nonvisual or summarized forms;
- equivalent in relationship consequence when skipped.

No major faction, progression, combat, economic, or identity advantage may require sexual or romantic participation.

Friendship, celibacy, aromanticism, asexuality, separation, and non-participation are complete lives rather than failed routes.

# 4. Desire and Attraction

Attraction may be:

- mutual;
- one-sided;
- temporary;
- inconvenient;
- culturally mediated;
- shaped by embodiment;
- absent;
- changed by history.

Attraction is private unless expressed or otherwise causally observed.

IRIS and other tools must not reveal hidden attraction as objective fact.

A player may misread signals. NPCs may misread the player. Misunderstanding should create awkwardness or conversation, not automatic punishment or entitlement.

# 5. Relationship Variety

Adult relationships may include:

- casual intimacy;
- long-term partnership;
- marriage or local equivalents;
- queer relationships;
- polyamorous or networked arrangements;
- monogamy;
- celibate partnership;
- long-distance relationships;
- interspecies or human–machine relationships where adulthood and consent are established;
- relationships that remain loving but end;
- culturally formal or informal bonds.

No structure is automatically enlightened, primitive, stable, exploitative, or universal.

Each institution has material and political context such as housing, inheritance, care, migration, reproduction, privacy, and legal recognition.

# 6. Power Asymmetry

Power affects the credibility of consent.

Relevant asymmetries include:

- employer and worker;
- commander and subordinate;
- clinician and patient;
- teacher and student;
- jailer and detainee;
- creditor and debtor;
- property or life-support controller and dependent resident;
- immigration sponsor and migrant;
- caregiver and care recipient;
- famous person and vulnerable admirer;
- high-status machine administrator and person whose body depends on its service.

The game must represent conflicts of interest, cooling-off periods, alternative access, reporting channels, and retaliation risk where appropriate.

An NPC saying “yes” under threat of losing air, medication, housing, legal status, or employment is not free consent.

# 7. Sex Work

Sex work may exist under different legal and economic systems.

It must be represented through:

- adult worker agency;
- boundaries and service negotiation;
- privacy;
- health and safety;
- labor organization;
- discrimination;
- client power;
- venue ownership;
- migration status;
- digital and embodied forms;
- exit options;
- exploitation and coercion risks;
- ordinary livelihoods and relationships outside work.

The game must distinguish:

- consensual adult sex work;
- survival sex under constrained options;
- trafficking;
- institutional coercion;
- fraudulent recruitment;
- intimate companionship work;
- nonsexual adult entertainment.

Workers are not environmental decoration, collectible partners, or automatic informants.

The player cannot purchase affection, testimony, political loyalty, or personhood.

# 8. Reproductive Autonomy

Reproductive decisions belong to the affected adults under body-sovereignty and care rules.

The game may depict conflict around:

- contraception;
- pregnancy;
- abortion;
- fertility treatment;
- genetic modification;
- artificial gestation;
- cloning;
- symbiotic reproduction;
- machine copying;
- inheritance;
- population policy.

Institutions may attempt coercion, but the game does not normalize bodies as demographic assets.

Reproductive coercion includes:

- sabotaging contraception;
- forced pregnancy or termination;
- denying treatment as punishment;
- tying body autonomy to citizenship or debt;
- compulsory genetic intervention;
- controlling reproductive records;
- coercive copying or fork creation.

# 9. Intoxication and Capacity

Intoxication can impair decision capacity.

The game must not use intoxication as a shortcut to consent.

Systems should support:

- sober consent policies;
- withdrawal of consent;
- trusted companions;
- venue safeguards;
- harm-reduction staff;
- quiet spaces;
- reporting and care;
- memory uncertainty without automatic disbelief.

A person's incomplete memory does not prove that no harm occurred, nor does it automatically prove another person guilty. Evidence and safety processes remain distinct.

# 10. Sexual Harm

Sexual harassment, assault, exploitation, and coercion may exist in the world, but their use requires strict restraint.

Rules:

- do not depict sexual violence graphically;
- do not use it as routine villain decoration;
- do not treat survivor pain as motivation for another character;
- do not create player-performed assault mechanics;
- do not require the player to interrogate intimate details for entertainment;
- preserve survivor privacy and agency;
- distinguish immediate safety, evidence, care, legal process, and social consequence;
- allow survivors to refuse public testimony or reconciliation;
- avoid universal behavioral templates for survivors.

References should usually occur through testimony, aftermath, institutional behavior, medical care, boundary changes, and contested evidence.

# 11. Jealousy, Affairs, and Betrayal

Mature relationships may include:

- jealousy;
- infidelity;
- secrecy;
- incompatible agreements;
- resentment;
- reconciliation;
- separation;
- social scandal.

The game should not turn jealousy into proof of love or violence into romantic intensity.

An affair may be harmful without making all participants monsters. A relationship may end even after sincere repair. A betrayed person is not required to forgive.

# 12. Privacy and Information

Private intimate information includes:

- orientation;
- attraction;
- sexual history;
- reproductive status;
- health records;
- relationship agreements;
- venue attendance;
- messages and recordings;
- sex-work participation;
- body modifications linked to intimacy.

Disclosure requires consent, law, emergency necessity, or another explicit authority path.

Public figures retain private intimate lives.

Rumor systems must preserve provenance and may cause harm without becoming truth.

# 13. Disability, Aging, and Intimacy

Disabled, chronically ill, scarred, aging, and care-dependent adults retain sexuality and relationship agency.

The game must not assume:

- disability removes desire;
- care relationships are automatically sexual;
- prosthetics are fetish objects by default;
- aging makes intimacy comic or irrelevant;
- a partner must become a full-time caregiver;
- medical dependence removes consent capacity universally.

Accessibility and assistance may be part of intimate negotiation without becoming spectacle.

# 14. Human–Machine and Human–Alien Intimacy

Nonhuman relationships require explicit attention to:

- adulthood or developmental maturity;
- agency location;
- embodiment;
- translation confidence;
- copying or branching;
- memory and privacy;
- power over life support or software;
- culturally meaningful consent;
- whether the visible body is the whole participant.

Uncertainty about personhood does not grant permission to experiment sexually on an entity.

The game should avoid treating cross-species intimacy as automatically progressive, deviant, mystical, or comic.

# 15. Authoring Standard

An intimate storyline must specify:

- each participant's life outside the relationship;
- consent and refusal boundaries;
- power asymmetries;
- privacy;
- material context;
- possible non-romantic outcomes;
- what happens if the player leaves;
- consequences that do not depend on explicit depiction;
- content settings and accessibility alternatives.

The scene should not exist solely to prove that the game is mature.

# 16. Acceptance Failures

This contract fails if:

- romance points purchase consent;
- sexual access becomes a quest reward;
- intoxication produces automatic availability;
- sex workers are interchangeable information vendors;
- private attraction is exposed by UI;
- adult–minor boundaries are ambiguous;
- reproductive decisions are treated as faction inventory management;
- sexual harm becomes graphic spectacle;
- survivors are required to forgive or testify;
- skipped intimate content gives worse outcomes;
- disability or age removes adult agency by default;
- generated language changes consent state.

## Closing Principle

> **Symtropy may portray desire honestly because it treats refusal, privacy, labor, bodily autonomy, and the right to leave as equally real.**
