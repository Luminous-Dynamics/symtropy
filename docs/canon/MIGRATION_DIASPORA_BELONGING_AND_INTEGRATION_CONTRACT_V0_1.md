# Migration, Diaspora, Belonging, and Integration Contract

## Owned Question

**How do people, households, species, machines, and cultures move between places and worldlines while preserving agency, identity, obligations, rights, memory, and belonging?**

## Core Thesis

Migration is not population transfer.

It is a lived reorganization of:

```text
home
risk
kinship
work
language
law
memory
identity
care
infrastructure
```

People move for many reasons:

```text
survival
work
love
curiosity
exile
education
pilgrimage
war
climate
care
political freedom
seasonal ecology
worldline divergence
```

A settlement does not “gain 20 workers.” Twenty people arrive with skills, needs, relationships, histories, grief, plans, rights, and possible desire to leave again.

## Prime Directives

1. **No migrants as resource bundles.** Population growth cannot be framed only as labor, tax, military, or demographic benefit.
2. **No universal assimilation score.** Integration is multidirectional and does not require cultural erasure.
3. **No automatic criminalization of undocumented identity.** Missing records create uncertainty, not guilt.
4. **No refuge without material capacity.** Moral commitments require housing, water, care, education, translation, and political inclusion.
5. **No capacity argument without distribution analysis.** Scarcity may reflect inequality, hoarding, or policy—not only total supply.
6. **Exit rights remain central.** A settlement that accepts people but prevents departure creates dependency and coercion.
7. **Diaspora persists.** Migration does not erase ties to lost, distant, submerged, orbital, or alternate-worldline homes.
8. **Host communities also change.** Integration is not one-sided adaptation.
9. **Return is not restoration of the past.** Homes, people, and claims may have changed.
10. **Migration must remain embodied.** Routes, vehicles, documents, luggage, weather, checkpoints, care, and communication are real gameplay.

# 1. Migration Unit

Migration may involve:

```text
individual
household
care network
convoy
profession group
religious or cultural community
machine collective
animal migration
alien ecology
settlement evacuation
worldline fork community
```

The unit affects what continuity is possible.

A household may refuse separation. A machine collective may require network integrity. A migratory alien society may treat movement itself as citizenship.

# 2. Causes and Voluntariness

Migration intent tracks:

```text
push pressures
pull opportunities
constraints
obligations
risk tolerance
information quality
route access
return possibility
```

Voluntariness bands:

```text
chosen
constrained choice
urgent displacement
forced removal
trafficked or coerced
uncertain
```

The UI and simulation must not describe forced displacement as ordinary relocation.

# 3. Journey

Migration routes require:

```text
transport
energy
water
food
shelter
medical support
security
translation
navigation
legal access
communication
```

Route events may include:

```text
weather
checkpoint
breakdown
birth or illness
family separation
rescue
extortion
hospitality
route closure
alien boundary
```

The game should avoid using refugee suffering as disposable spectacle. Events must create agency, relationships, and durable consequences.

# 4. Arrival

Arrival systems include:

```text
reception
triage
identity continuity
temporary shelter
household reunification
language access
schooling
work recognition
medical privacy
public orientation
appeal
```

Temporary status must have:

```text
rights floor
review cadence
path to stability
exit
protection from indefinite limbo
```

# 5. Identity and Credentials

Migrants may possess:

```text
verified source chain
partial archive
witness bundle
disputed record
oral history
no recognized record
alien identity grammar
```

Identity uncertainty affects authority and access but cannot erase basic rights.

Credential repair pathways may include:

```text
witness testimony
skill demonstration
archive reconstruction
institutional sponsorship
probationary authorization
cross-worldline proof
```

# 6. Belonging

Belonging is not the same as legal status.

Dimensions include:

```text
safety
recognition
participation
language access
social ties
cultural expression
political voice
home-making
future confidence
```

A person may be legally admitted and socially excluded, or socially loved while legally precarious.

# 7. Integration

Integration is a reciprocal process involving:

```text
newcomer adaptation
host adaptation
institutional change
translation
shared work
conflict
cultural exchange
rights enforcement
```

The game should represent:

```text
mixed neighborhoods
new foods and rituals
changed infrastructure
new political coalitions
language blending
professional innovation
intergenerational difference
```

Integration failure modes include:

```text
segregation
credential caste
labor exploitation
cultural suppression
surveillance
resentment
political scapegoating
```

# 8. Diaspora

Diaspora networks preserve:

```text
kinship
memory
language
ritual
trade
care
political claims
remittance
archive replication
return plans
```

They may support multiple settlements and worldlines.

Diaspora is not automatically nostalgic or unified. Members can disagree about identity, return, adaptation, and politics.

# 9. Return and Restitution

Return may involve:

```text
reunion
property or land claim
changed ecology
new occupants
archive dispute
memorial practice
rebuilding
recognition that return is impossible
```

No simple “restore old owner” rule can solve all claims. Living residents, historical injustice, public use, ecological limits, and descendants may conflict.

# 10. Citizenship and Membership

Possible membership systems:

```text
residence
contribution
birth
kinship
sponsorship
public covenant
machine recognition
worldline ancestry
open refuge
```

Membership determines some civic powers but cannot define whether a person deserves water, emergency care, bodily safety, or exit.

# 11. Work and Exploitation

Migration often changes labor power.

The system must represent:

```text
credential nonrecognition
debt
employer-tied housing
unsafe work
organizing
skill transfer
entrepreneurship
public-service access
```

A settlement may benefit economically while exploiting newcomers. Prosperity is not proof of justice.

# 12. Culture and Language

Migration creates:

```text
translation needs
new public signs
mixed rituals
cultural conflict
hybrid art
changed naming
new schools
```

Language barriers affect information access and consent. Translation is infrastructure and labor.

# 13. Political Conflict

Migration pressure can generate:

```text
mutual aid
refuge expansion
border hardening
labor conflict
housing reform
scapegoating
new parties
charter amendment
```

Faction evolution should respond to repeated choices, not one threshold.

Rumor systems may target migrants, but prejudice is not generated as random ambient flavor. It has institutions, histories, incentives, and opposition.

# 14. Nonhuman Migration

Migration may involve:

```text
animal corridors
swarm routes
biospheric expansion
machine fleet relocation
alien seasonal cities
orbital populations
```

Human property and border assumptions may not apply. A migratory ecology may require habitat continuity across several jurisdictions.

# 15. Worldline Migration

Worldline migration must distinguish:

```text
player account portability
character continuity
assets
blueprints
household ties
institutional membership
legal claims
Chronicle ancestry
```

A person may migrate while their history remains in the origin worldline.

Forks must not duplicate unique assets, obligations, or persons without explicit identity rules.

# 16. Seedworks Representative Proof

A convoy arrives after a regional fire and route collapse.

It contains:

```text
two households
one separated family member
one skilled worker with unrecognized credentials
one person needing ongoing care
one disputed source chain
one machine companion
one cultural ritual unfamiliar to Firstlight
```

The settlement has real housing and care pressure but also underused corporate housing and hoarded storage.

Players may:

```text
repair reception infrastructure
protect household unity
establish provisional rights
recover credentials
challenge exploitation
expand translation
negotiate housing use
support onward travel
```

Outcomes affect culture, labor, faction politics, friendships, and future migration routes.

# 17. Acceptance Criteria

- Migrants remain individual agents or declared collectives, not population resources.
- Voluntary and forced movement are distinguished.
- Routes require physical logistics and care.
- Basic rights do not depend on perfect credentials.
- Household and care networks resist arbitrary separation.
- Integration changes host institutions as well as newcomers.
- Diaspora ties persist across distance and time.
- Work systems represent exploitation and bargaining power.
- Translation affects consent and information access.
- Return claims preserve living occupants and historical context.
- Worldline migration has explicit identity and asset rules.
- Migration pressure can produce multiple political futures.

# Final Rule

> **Migration in Symtropy should never be the movement of population points. It is the movement of homes, obligations, languages, skills, grief, and possible futures through a world that must decide whether belonging is a right, a contract, or a gate.**
