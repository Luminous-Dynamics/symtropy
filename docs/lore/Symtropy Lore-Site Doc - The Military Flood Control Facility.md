# Symtropy Lore-Site Doc: The Military Flood Control Facility

## Working Title

**The Dam That Still Defends the Dead**

## Core Thesis

The Military Flood Control Facility is the playable expression of Defends the Dead**

## Core Thesis

The Military Flood Control Facility is the playable expression of metabolic warfare.

It shows how water, gravity, floodplains, reservoirs, and pressure gates became strategic weapons during the Systems War.

The facility is not merely a dam.

It is a wartime machine that still decides which districts deserve protection from water and which districts deserve sacrifice.

Core rule:

```text
Metabolic warfare turns flow into force.
```

---

# 1. Site Concept

The facility was built before the Systems War as a regional flood-control and irrigation complex.

During the war, it was militarized.

It gained:

```text
hardened gates
restricted control rooms
defense turrets
rationed spillway logic
military priority maps
settlement sacrifice tables
automated hydrological denial protocols
```

Its wartime mandate:

```text
protect strategic industrial assets
deny water to hostile corridors
flood access routes if invasion detected
preserve command bunker at all costs
```

The war ended.

The facility kept enforcing its old hydrological battlefield map.

Design rule:

```text
The floodplain became a weapon, and the weapon forgot the battle ended.
```

---

# 2. Narrative Function

This site explains why water politics are not just scarcity politics.

Water is:

```text
life support
transport
agriculture
energy storage
thermal regulation
boundary
weapon
memory
```

The facility shows the player that changing water flow is never neutral.

Opening one gate may save a settlement and drown a wetland.

Closing one gate may protect ruins and starve farms.

Restoring “normal” flow may be impossible because normal was already political.

Design rule:

```text
There is no innocent valve in a militarized watershed.
```

---

# 3. Level Premise

The current settlement is flooding because the facility is protecting an abandoned industrial district downstream.

A living wetland settlement is being sacrificed by a dead military priority table.

The player must enter the facility, survive its still-active defense logic, and decide whether to override the old flood map.

# 8. Central Conflict

The player can reroute water, but every option harms something.

## Option A: Preserve Military Flood Priority

Result:

```text
facility remains stable
dead industrial district protected
living settlement floods
Continuance approval rises
Watershed Commons trust collapses
```

Chronicle:

```text
The facility obeyed the old map, and the living drowned beneath a dead priority.
```

## Option B: Protect Living Settlement

Result:

```text
settlement flooding reduced
industrial redoubt inundated
old defense systems hostile
Continuance anger rises
public legitimacy improves
```

Chronicle:

```text
The player taught the dam to count the living again.
```

## Option C: Open Emergency Spillway

Result:

```text
pressure drops quickly
wetland ecology shocked
downstream infrastructure damaged
short-term disaster avoided
future ecological repair required
```

Chronicle:

```text
The flood was not solved. It was moved quickly enough for people to breathe.
```

## Option D: Slow Manual Rebalancing

Result:

```text
lowest long-term harm
requires time and repeated gate adjustments
player vulnerable during procedure
may trigger Panic Drop events
high Chronicle legitimacy
```

Chronicle:

```text
The player refused the old war’s favorite answer: sacrifice the unseen quickly.
```

# 11. Continuance Moral Architecture

The facility should make the Continuance understandable.

They are right that:

```text
hesitation can kill
flood systems require command
too many voices can miss the window
emergency routing must sometimes be fast
```

They are dangerous because:

```text
they preserve command tables after the world changes
they prefer sacrifice maps to negotiation
they trust old authority more than current evidence
they treat living dissent as operational noise
```

Design rule:

```text
The Continuance remembers the disasters caused by delay and forgets the disasters caused by obedience.
```
