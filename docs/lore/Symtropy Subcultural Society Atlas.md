# Symtropy Subcultural Society Atlas v0.1

## Design Rule

Every society must answer six questions:

1. **What wound made them necessary?**
2. **What do they protect better than anyone else?**
3. **What do they misunderstand about themselves?**
4. **What does childhood look like there?**
5. **What happens to outsiders?**
6. **What is their failure mode under pressure?**

A society becomes believable when its fashion, rituals, music, architecture, economy, family structures, laws, and shame all come from the same survival argument.

---

# I. Opposition / Counterculture Societies

## 1. The Refusal Commons

**Lineage:** countercultures, punk, early hacker culture, anarchist scenes, anti-authoritarian youth cultures.

**Core thesis:**
Civilization fails when obedience becomes easier than conscience.

The Refusal Commons are settlements built around the legal right to disobey illegitimate authority. They maintain public strike halls, anonymous whistleblower networks, sabotage ethics schools, and civil disobedience courts.

They are not chaos-loving rebels. They are disciplined anti-domination technicians.

**Beauty:**
They are the first to notice tyranny. They protect dissent, expose hidden contracts, and keep emergency powers from becoming permanent.

**Shadow:**
They struggle to govern after they win. Suspicion becomes identity. Any durable institution begins to look like betrayal.

**Children:**
Raised on debate, repair skills, encrypted testimony, and “obedience drills” where they practice refusing immoral orders.

**Outsider treatment:**
Welcomed if honest, watched if powerful, mocked if managerial.

**Failure mode:**
Permanent opposition. They can become unable to build anything that requires trust.

**Gameplay role:**
They help players resist corporate enclaves, Null governance, and Continuance protectorates — but may also sabotage necessary coordination.

# II. Youth / Generational Societies

## 2. The Ashwave Youth Leagues

**Lineage:** punk, rave, grunge, skater, emo, scene, e-girl/e-boy, vaporwave, NPC irony, youth internet aesthetics.

**Core thesis:**
A civilization that cannot metabolize youth despair produces ghosts before adults.

The Ashwave Leagues formed among children and teenagers raised in broken infrastructures: flooded schools, failing networks, rationed futures, dead malls converted into shelters. They use music, fashion, skating, projection art, graffiti, and ritualized irony to process inherited collapse.

**Beauty:**
They turn despair into mutual recognition. Their scenes are early-warning systems for social breakdown.

**Shadow:**
They aestheticize numbness. Some factions treat detachment as wisdom and sincerity as weakness.

**Children:**
There are no “children” in the old sense. Young people run peer clinics, archive grief, map unsafe districts, and build hidden communication routes.

**Outsider treatment:**
Adults must earn credibility by listening before advising.

**Failure mode:**
Self-protective nihilism. “Nothing matters” becomes a defense against responsibility.

**Gameplay role:**
They reveal what adults refuse to see: which policies are killing the future emotionally before they kill it physically.

---

# III. Music-Derived Societies

## 3. The Resonance Syndicates

**Lineage:** rave, techno, hip-hop, punk, metal, jazz, folk, Afrobeats, reggaeton, cumbia, K-pop fandom logistics, noise scenes.

**Core thesis:**
Rhythm is infrastructure for collective nervous systems.

These societies use music not as entertainment but as coordination technology. Work crews synchronize with percussion. Emergency broadcasts are encoded in rhythm. Legal assemblies begin with call-and-response. Mourning has bass patterns. Revolt has tempo signatures.

**Beauty:**
They can coordinate massive groups without centralized command. They heal trauma through embodied rhythm and collective presence.

**Shadow:**
Charismatic performers can become political authorities. Ecstasy can drown deliberation.

**Children:**
Learn pulse literacy: how to read crowd mood, protest tempo, machine rhythm, and panic signatures.

**Outsider treatment:**
Anyone can join the rhythm, but false performers are quickly exposed.

**Failure mode:**
The society becomes governable through spectacle. Whoever controls the stage controls the city.

**Gameplay role:**
A player may need to read musical events as civic signals: strike, mourning, warning, election, riot, healing, or cult capture.

# IV. Visual / Aesthetic Societies

## 4. The Masked Houses of Public Style

**Lineage:** goth, lolita, decora, Harajuku, techwear, gorpcore, old money, mob wife, dark academia, solarpunk, steampunk, cyberpunk.

**Core thesis:**
Style is not surface. Style is law made wearable.

The Masked Houses are city districts where clothing publicly encodes obligation, grief status, profession, consent boundaries, climate role, and civic debt. Fashion becomes a readable social interface.

**Beauty:**
They make social complexity visible. A stranger can see who is mourning, who is under protection, who carries repair authority, who may not be touched, who owes public service.

**Shadow:**
They become caste-adjacent. People born into bad symbols may spend years trying to escape inherited visual status.

**Children:**
Learn textile literacy before formal law: color, seam, veil, clasp, patch, mask, badge.

**Outsider treatment:**
Outsiders are unreadable and therefore socially dangerous until “translated.”

**Failure mode:**
Aesthetic legibility becomes social control.

**Gameplay role:**
The player must learn to read clothing as civic interface, not decoration.

# V. Fandom / Interest Societies

## 5. The Myth-Keeper Conventions

**Lineage:** sci-fi/fantasy fandoms, tabletop RPGs, LARP, Trekkies, Tolkien fans, anime fandom, cosplay, zine culture.

**Core thesis:**
Shared fiction is rehearsal space for real governance.

The Myth-Keepers began as fandom communities but became charter designers, simulation facilitators, and civic ritualists. They use fictional worlds to teach ethics, disaster response, diplomacy, and constitutional imagination.

**Beauty:**
They preserve hope without pretending reality is simple. They train people to imagine other rules.

**Shadow:**
They can retreat into symbolic victory. Performing justice may replace building it.

**Children:**
Grow up roleplaying legal dilemmas, first-contact disputes, emergency councils, and ecological negotiations.

**Outsider treatment:**
Welcomed through story. Distrusted if they mock play as childish.

**Failure mode:**
Myth becomes escapism, then doctrine.

**Gameplay role:**
They are excellent trainers, diplomats, and social designers — but may miss material constraints.

---

# VI. Gaming / Digital Skill Societies

## 6. The Frame-Perfect Orders

**Lineage:** speedrunners, fighting game community, Soulsborne players, modders, MMORPG guilds, ARG communities.

**Core thesis:**
Mastery is the art of learning failure faster than collapse can punish you.

These societies treat difficult systems as sacred teachers. Their members specialize in repeated failure, optimization, pattern-reading, exploit discovery, and cooperative raid logistics.

**Beauty:**
They are incredible at disaster drills, machine debugging, combat training, and procedural mystery-solving.

**Shadow:**
They may confuse skill with worth. Those who cannot perform under pressure are quietly devalued.

**Children:**
Raised through “failure gyms,” where mistakes are replayed publicly without shame — in theory.

**Outsider treatment:**
Tested through trials. Respected if persistent.

**Failure mode:**
Elitism disguised as merit.

**Gameplay role:**
They can train players to master hostile systems, but their internal culture may be cruel to the slow, injured, old, or traumatized.

# VII. Ideological / Political Societies

## 7. The Fracture Assemblies

**Lineage:** anarchists, eco-activists, cypherpunks, libertarians, survivalists, rationalists, effective altruists, trad movements, extremist failure modes.

**Core thesis:**
Every ideology is a machine for deciding what may be sacrificed.

The Fracture Assemblies are not one society but a dangerous family of charter experiments. Each is built around a hard principle: liberty, care, purity, efficiency, tradition, autonomy, biodiversity, risk minimization, or truth-seeking.

**Beauty:**
They produce some of the most coherent settlements in Symtropy.

**Shadow:**
Coherence becomes cruelty when a single value eats the rest.

**Children:**
Raised inside moral geometry. They learn early what their society considers non-negotiable.

**Outsider treatment:**
Depends on whether outsiders violate the sacred value.

**Failure mode:**
Totalization. The founding principle becomes an excuse to override persons.

**Gameplay role:**
The player encounters ideological settlements that are not strawmen. Each solves something real and breaks something real.

---

# VIII. Religious / Spiritual Societies

## 8. The Maintenance Liturgies

**Lineage:** monastic orders, Catholic traditionalists, Sufi orders, Zen, Pentecostal/charismatic intensity, Amish/Mennonite restraint, Pagan revival, Rastafari, syncretic ritual cultures.

**Core thesis:**
What a society repeats becomes sacred.

These communities turn maintenance into liturgy. Some pray through pipe repair. Some chant filtration cycles. Some garden as covenant. Some refuse certain machines to preserve human dependence and humility.

**Beauty:**
They protect continuity, reverence, restraint, and intergenerational discipline.

**Shadow:**
Sacred practice can hide abuse, hierarchy, or technological refusal that harms dependents.

**Children:**
Raised through ritual apprenticeship: sweeping, singing, tending, repairing, fasting, witnessing.

**Outsider treatment:**
Hospitality is strong, conversion pressure varies.

**Failure mode:**
The ritual survives after the reason dies.

**Gameplay role:**
They may preserve ancient repair knowledge no technical faction remembers — but players must distinguish wisdom from inherited fear.

---

# IX. Profession / Class Societies

## 9. The Guilds of Dirty Competence

**Lineage:** truckers, fishers, miners, bikers, military, medics, lawyers, academics, architects, hackers, artists, film crews, criminal undergrounds.

**Core thesis:**
The people who keep systems alive develop cultures the polite world depends on but does not understand.

These societies form around dangerous competence: logistics, extraction, care, law, navigation, salvage, construction, emergency response, or illicit survival.

**Beauty:**
They know the real system, not the official one. They can move food, fix engines, bury bodies, cross borders, read weather, and find spare parts.

**Shadow:**
Occupational loyalty can become omertà. “We take care of our own” can mean “we hide our own crimes.”

**Children:**
Raised in yards, docks, clinics, convoys, workshops, hangars, court basements, and kitchens.

**Outsider treatment:**
Respect is earned by work, not speech.

**Failure mode:**
Guild capture. Competence becomes monopoly.

**Gameplay role:**
They are indispensable for grounded play: vehicles, repairs, logistics, salvage, medicine, law, black markets, and institutional memory.

# X. Digital-Origin Societies

## 10. The Swarm Rooms

**Lineage:** Reddit cultures, Tumblr aesthetics, TikTok niches, chan culture, meme culture, Twitch communities, Discord identity clusters, BookTok/FoodTok/CleanTok, ARGs.

**Core thesis:**
A society born online does not need territory first. It needs attention rituals.

The Swarm Rooms are distributed communities whose public life occurs through feeds, overlays, reputation bursts, meme law, annotation wars, and synchronized micro-actions.

**Beauty:**
They mobilize quickly, preserve niche knowledge, and detect emerging signals faster than institutions.

**Shadow:**
They can become mobs, cults, harassment engines, or reality-fragmentation machines.

**Children:**
Raised with identity fluidity, screenshot memory, archive anxiety, and constant social performance.

**Outsider treatment:**
Outsiders are “context-poor.” They are judged by whether they understand the room’s history.

**Failure mode:**
Attention replaces truth.

**Gameplay role:**
They shape rumors, markets, elections, panic events, and social legitimacy across settlements.

---

# XI. Gender / Sexuality / Kinship Societies

## 11. The Kinship Laboratories

**Lineage:** LGBTQ+ communities, drag culture, trans communities, leather communities, polyamory, relationship anarchy, chosen family systems.

**Core thesis:**
Biology is not enough to define family, duty, body, or belonging.

These societies develop complex kinship law: chosen parents, transition care, consent contracts, household federations, ritual names, non-biological inheritance, public protection for vulnerable bodies, and multiple recognized forms of intimacy.

**Beauty:**
They are extremely good at chosen family, body autonomy, mutual care, and identity repair.

**Shadow:**
Subcultures of liberation can still create insiders and outsiders. Fluency in language and norms may become a gatekeeping weapon.

**Children:**
Raised by households, not just couples. They learn consent, naming, body privacy, and kinship plurality early.

**Outsider treatment:**
Welcomed if respectful; rejected sharply if invasive.

**Failure mode:**
Care networks become socially mandatory and hard to leave.

**Gameplay role:**
They deepen Symtropy’s body sovereignty, identity, reconstitution, and social charter systems.

---

# XII. Body / Health Practice Societies

## 12. The Embodiment Regimes

**Lineage:** bodybuilding, CrossFit, marathoners, ultrarunners, veganism, carnivore diets, raw food, sobriety, straight edge, psychedelic communities, biohacking, longevity.

**Core thesis:**
Every society has a body doctrine, even when it pretends not to.

These cultures form around disciplined embodiment: strength, endurance, purity, altered states, longevity, sobriety, optimization, fasting, ritual eating, communal training, or body repair.

**Beauty:**
They produce resilience, health literacy, discipline, and deep bodily knowledge.

**Shadow:**
The body becomes a moral scoreboard. Illness, disability, aging, addiction, or softness may be treated as failure.

**Children:**
Raised with food ethics, movement rituals, medical literacy, and body-monitoring norms.

**Outsider treatment:**
Often judged through habit, diet, stamina, or self-control.

**Failure mode:**
Wellness authoritarianism.

**Gameplay role:**
They affect stamina systems, medicine, genetics, addiction recovery, augmentation politics, and care ethics.

---

# XIII. Regional / Place-Based Societies

## 13. The Place-Oath Cultures

**Lineage:** Appalachian, Cajun, Texan, PNW, New York, Black Belt South, Hawaiian, Geordie, Scouse, Welsh valleys, Scottish Highlands, Quebecois, Catalan, Basque, Okinawan, Sicilian.

**Core thesis:**
Place is not background. Place is memory with weather.

These societies are rooted in landscape, dialect, foodways, music, grievance, migration, labor history, and local ecological knowledge. They are the opposite of generic planetary civilization.

**Beauty:**
They understand terrain, continuity, and belonging. They preserve memory against flattening.

**Shadow:**
Place-love can become exclusion. “We are from here” can become “you never will be.”

**Children:**
Raised through local stories, routes, recipes, storms, songs, burial places, and repair obligations.

**Outsider treatment:**
Hospitality and suspicion coexist.

**Failure mode:**
Defensive localism.

**Gameplay role:**
They make procedural regions feel real. Every basin, mountain, coast, desert, orbital habitat, or moon settlement can generate its own place-oath culture.

---

# XIV. Dark / Dangerous Subculture Transformations

## 14. The Red Mirror Orders

**Lineage:** incel-adjacent, red pill, MGTOW, lookism, sigma male, extremist online masculinities, humiliation economies.

**Core thesis:**
A society that turns pain into hierarchy will eventually worship cruelty as honesty.

These are not glamorized. They are tragic threat cultures formed from alienation, body shame, sexual resentment, failed belonging, and algorithmic radicalization.

**Beauty:**
Very little at the mature stage — but the seed wound is real: loneliness, humiliation, status collapse, abandonment.

**Shadow:**
They convert suffering into entitlement. They treat care as weakness and intimacy as conquest or fraud.

**Children:**
If they control territory, children are gender-trained through paranoia, ranking, surveillance, and shame.

**Outsider treatment:**
Women, queer people, soft men, disabled bodies, and outsiders become symbolic enemies.

**Failure mode:**
Already failed: they are a social injury that became a doctrine.

**Gameplay role:**
They function as cautionary hostile societies, but with repair paths for individuals who can leave before full capture.

---

# XV. Criminal / Underground Societies

## 15. The Shadow Provisioners

**Lineage:** gangs, prison culture, cartel logistics, black markets, hacker undergrounds, outlaw biker logistics.

**Core thesis:**
When lawful systems abandon people, unlawful systems become infrastructure.

The Shadow Provisioners move medicine, identity papers, fuel, weapons, stolen machine parts, forbidden genetic tools, and people. They are not romantic criminals. They are parallel governance under scarcity.

**Beauty:**
They provide when official systems refuse. They know routes, risks, hidden needs, and abandoned populations.

**Shadow:**
Protection becomes extortion. Loyalty becomes captivity. Violence becomes administration.

**Children:**
Raised around debts, codes, favors, silence, and survival literacy.

**Outsider treatment:**
Useful outsiders are tolerated; naive outsiders are prey.

**Failure mode:**
The emergency economy becomes permanent predation.

**Gameplay role:**
Players may need them, hate them, expose them, reform them, or watch a lawful society become worse than them.

---

# XVI. Hybrid Societies for Modern Overlap

## 16. The Polyculture Cities

**Lineage:** hybrid identities and overlapping subcultures.

**Core thesis:**
No one belongs to only one world anymore.

These cities are built from overlapping scene-rights: prayer rooms beside maker labs, queer kinship houses beside repair guilds, rave clinics beside archive schools, martial arts halls beside disability workshops, food commons beside music courts.

**Beauty:**
They are adaptable, diverse, creative, and resistant to monoculture.

**Shadow:**
They are exhausting. Conflict translation never ends. Every norm has an exception, every exception has a history.

**Children:**
Raised multilingual in culture, law, interface, and identity.

**Outsider treatment:**
Everyone is an outsider somewhere and an insider somewhere else.

**Failure mode:**
Fragmentation. The city becomes a thousand rooms with no shared floor.

**Gameplay role:**
This is the most realistic late-Symtropy society type: overlapping charters, layered identities, and constant negotiation.

# Recommended Build Order

1. **Aerarium Compact** — already visually strong through the Cathedral of the Last Atmosphere.
2. **Kelp Ledger Cities** — water, food, law, ecology, coastal survival.
3. **Continuity Houses** — death, reconstitution, identity, memory.
4. **Refusal Commons** — resistance, sabotage ethics, anti-authoritarian governance.
5. **Quiet Vector** — trauma, sensory governance, soft repression.
6. **Shadow Provisioners** — illegal infrastructure and survival economies.
7. **Kinship Laboratories** — body sovereignty, chosen family, consent law.
8. **Frame-Perfect Orders** — mastery, failure, skill elitism.
9. **Maintenance Liturgies** — sacred infrastructure and ritual repair.
10. **Polyculture Cities** — hybridized late-game urban societies.

The strongest Symtropy societies should never feel like “the goth faction” or “the gamer faction.”

They should feel like this:

> A real historical pressure entered a population.
> The population survived by inventing a culture.
> The culture became beautiful.
> The beauty hid a wound.
> The wound became playable.
