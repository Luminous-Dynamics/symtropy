# Symtropy: Dreamglass Mesh & Refuge Cities v0.3

**Production Mission and Texture Pass**  
VR, matrix-like systems, consent, reality rights, memory provenance, server labor, grief-contact, and simulated refuge without making the physical world fake.

## Design Decision

Symtropy should include VR and matrix-like systems, but it should **not** reveal that the entire physical world is secretly a simulation. That would weaken the material, ecological, civic, and infrastructure weight that makes Symtropy distinct.

Dreamglass is not metaphysical truth. It is civic infrastructure.

**Core rule:** the physical world remains real. Dreamglass matters because it is embodied infrastructure: servers need power, bodies need care, exits need consent, memory logs can be edited, and someone always owns or governs the systems that make the dream possible.

## Core Thesis

The Dreamglass Mesh is a family of immersive civic, therapeutic, educational, archival, and coercive simulation systems built after the Systems War.

**Founding promise:**

> No mind should be trapped by the limits of a broken world.

**Failure mode:**

> A simulated refuge can become a softer prison than any bunker.

**Symtropy question:**

> When does a helpful constructed reality become a violation of consent?

## Why Dreamglass Belongs in Symtropy

Dreamglass asks the same question Symtropy already asks of water valves, heat grids, archive courts, acoustic law walls, care protocols, and death records:

> Who controls the system that defines what is safe, real, lawful, livable, or consented to?

The danger is not that a world is fake. The danger is that a governed environment can edit the reasons a person has for leaving.

## The Non-Negotiable Lore Rule

Do not make Symtropy secretly unreal.

Dreamglass should make the physical world more politically important, not less important. Every immersive system depends on physical constraints:

- server ownership
- power costs
- heat and cooling
- body hydration and nutrition
- sanitation and medical monitoring
- exit conditions
- consent renewal intervals
- memory-retention policy
- waking witnesses
- civic oversight
- labor by server maintainers
- inequality of access
- corruption by Null signal, corporate optimization, archive formalism, or therapeutic overreach

A simulation in Symtropy is never just a visual layer. It is a governed environment with a body somewhere else.

## The Dreamglass Stack

### 1. Dreamglass Commons

Public, consent-audited immersive spaces used for education, therapy, mourning, art, disabled access, and remote civic assembly.

**Best state:** liberating, accessible, restorative, playful, and humane.  
**Failure mode:** dependency, avoidance, civic life migrating away from material repair, subtle reward tuning.

### 2. Continuance Simulation Vaults

Old-world archive systems preserving pre-collapse cities, families, legal claims, state procedures, and dead governments inside simulated continuity chambers.

**Best state:** historical recovery, lost architecture, ancestral memory, legal evidence reconstruction.  
**Failure mode:** dead governments still enforcing obsolete law over living communities.

### 3. Null Mirage Zones

Corrupted immersive systems where the Null Ecology captures perception, memory, attention, and choice.

**Best state:** none, except as a research hazard.  
**Failure mode:** the user can physically leave, but every reason to leave has been edited out.

### 4. Dreamglass Refuge Cities

A dedicated society that moved much of its civic life into shared immersive worlds because the physical world was too damaged, painful, inaccessible, or dangerous.

**Founding principle:**

> Reality should be survivable. Until it is, we will build one that is.

**Core horror:**

> A refuge becomes a cage when returning to reality is treated as self-harm.

## Fleshside and Glassside

The Refuge Cities began as emergency mercy infrastructure. Surface districts were poisoned, irradiated, grief-struck, or socially shattered. People needed places where ruined bodies, exhausted minds, disabled citizens, children, elders, trauma survivors, and displaced communities could continue learning, mourning, working, and loving while physical reconstruction lagged behind.

Over generations, citizens began to describe the physical world as **Fleshside** and shared simulation environments as **Glassside**.

At first, this distinction was practical. Then it became moral.

Fleshside became associated with danger, decay, conflict, disease, and unfinished pain. Glassside became associated with dignity, continuity, beauty, accessibility, and control. The society did not simply escape reality. It built a better one and then slowly became afraid of returning.

## Glassside Access Inequality

The class question of Dreamglass is not only what happens to people trapped inside. It is also what happens to people who can never enter.

### Unentered Citizens

People without reliable access to Dreamglass because their region lacks power, bandwidth, medical interface support, legal citizenship, or safe immersion infrastructure. They see Glassside dignity from outside but cannot participate in it.

### Interface-Intolerant Citizens

People whose bodies, neurotypes, injuries, implants, immune systems, or sensory patterns make deep immersion dangerous. They are not anti-dream; the dream is not built for them.

### Low-Band Citizens

People with partial, degraded, time-rationed, or low-resolution access. They attend civic hearings as glitching avatars, work in laggy remote labor sites, or receive inferior therapy worlds while wealthy citizens inhabit full-sensory paradises.

### Sponsored Dreamers

People whose Dreamglass access is paid by corporations, clinics, debt patrons, or archive institutions. Their lives are more comfortable inside the dream, but their exit rights are entangled with contracts.

### Fleshside Stayers

People who remain outside by choice: farmers, repair workers, stewards, dissidents, caregivers, and those who believe no dream is just unless waking has a livable destination.

**Core inequality:** if dignity lives Glassside, then the unentered become culturally primitive by default. A just Dreamglass society must make physical life dignified enough that refusing simulation does not mean refusing civilization.

## Glassside Access Inequality - Encounter Texture v0.3

The inequality section should not play as a neutral taxonomy. It should produce encounters, NPC language, map states, and Field Deck evidence.

### Unentered Citizens - Encounter Texture

Unentered Citizens are not simply people who lack a luxury. In Dreamglass society, they lack access to the place where education, grief care, remote work, court attendance, civic beauty, and social status increasingly happen.

**Respectful self-names:** grounded people, groundkeepers, outside citizens, fleshside neighbors.  
**Common bitter names for dreamers:** blue ghosts, glassfolk, sleepers, cloud citizens.  
**Derogatory terms to avoid in narrator voice:** primitive, unsimulated, backward. These may appear only as in-world prejudice.

**What the player encounters:** a child watching a simulated school ceremony through a public projection wall; an elder whose testimony is considered lower-status because it was never recorded Glassside; a repair family that knows the city better than any avatar map but cannot vote in a virtual assembly.

**What they can do that Glass Citizens often cannot:** read physical danger without overlays, navigate broken streets, identify server heat by sound and smell, recognize who is really maintaining an area, and remember civic relationships that were never converted into dream data.

### Interface-Intolerant Citizens - Encounter Texture

These citizens are not anti-technology. The interface is not safe for their bodies, neurotypes, immune systems, implants, trauma patterns, or sensory thresholds.

**What the player encounters:** a person denied a therapy benefit because the clinic recognizes only Glassside attendance; a witness whose body rejects the interface but whose testimony is materially stronger than a reconstructed memory; a patient who has been told that access failure is personal fragility rather than design exclusion.

**What they can do:** identify false accessibility claims. They often become the best reviewers of consent design because they know whether a system's mercy actually reaches different bodies.

### Low-Band Citizens - Encounter Texture

Low-Band Citizens enter Dreamglass through degraded, rationed, low-resolution, or time-limited sessions.

**Respectful terms:** low-band citizens, partial-access citizens.  
**In-world stigma:** flickerfolk, half-lit, lagged.

**What the player encounters:** a civic hearing where wealthy avatars appear fully embodied while low-band citizens stutter, blur, or miss the vote; a worker whose therapy world drops detail exactly when trauma support is needed; a family that schedules grief contact in five-minute slots.

**What they can do:** detect latency, compression artifacts, session edits, and interface inequalities that full-band citizens never notice.

### Sponsored Dreamers - Encounter Texture

Sponsored Dreamers receive access through clinics, corporations, debt patrons, archive houses, schools, prisons, or welfare contracts.

**What the player encounters:** a beautiful dream apartment whose exit rights are tied to a productivity contract; a grief-contact subscription that extends because sorrow remains profitable; a sponsored student whose curriculum subtly trains loyalty to the sponsor.

**What they can do:** reveal how dependence hides inside generosity. Their testimony is crucial because their captivity often looks like rescue.

### Fleshside Stayers - Encounter Texture

Fleshside Stayers choose the physical world, not because they reject Dreamglass, but because they believe no dream is just unless waking remains livable.

**Self-names:** grounders, wakekeepers, repair-side people, the still-awake.  
**What they call the best dreamers:** travelers.  
**What they call exploitative elites:** cloud nobles.

**What the player encounters:** farmers growing food for sleepers who never see them; caregivers who keep real bodies alive while avatar families celebrate elsewhere; scouts who know which streets are safe when the projection layer fails.

**What they can do:** locate hard exits, maintain routes to real clinics, preserve analog evidence, and tell when a simulation's beauty is substituting for material repair.

**Core design principle:** The unentered are not empty. They carry kinds of reality the dream has forgotten.


## Major Institutions

### The Dreamwright Guilds

Artists, engineers, therapists, historians, and civic designers who build shared worlds. Their best members are architects of mercy. Their worst members become behavior designers who optimize attachment.

### The Waking Witness Orders

People trained to sit with immersed citizens, protect hard exits, monitor body health, and preserve non-digital evidence that a session began voluntarily.

Their core vow:

> I will remember the body while the mind travels.

Witnesses carry analog ledgers, consent anchor objects, emergency bells, and wake-phrases defined before immersion. Their authority is strongest when the simulated world says one thing and the body says another.

### The Exit Courts

Civic tribunals that adjudicate disputed immersion cases: contested consent, exit refusal, grief-loop addiction, prison simulations, child immersion limits, and claims that waking would cause harm.

### The Server Stewards - Texture Pass v0.2

The Server Stewards are the most politically important under-recognized people in the Refuge Cities. Without them, the dream dies.

They maintain cooling towers, power buses, battery reserves, body-support reservoirs, neural interface hygiene, sanitation, nutrition pumps, emergency release rails, witness relays, and hard-exit bells. They are not glamorous dream architects. They are the physical labor that makes immaterial dignity possible.

**Daily life:** Stewards work in heat, condensation, cable drag, medical alarms, fungal risk, coolant leaks, and constant triage. They learn to read sleepers by body metrics rather than avatar speech. A Glassside citizen may appear calm in a dream garden while the Steward sees dehydration, muscle atrophy, pressure sores, or a failing exit relay.

**Internal names:** Stewards call the sleeping users **travelers**, never customers. They call themselves **wakehands**, **coolant kin**, or **the ones who keep the floor real**. Bitter older crews may call high-status Glass Citizens **cloud nobles**, but reform-minded stewards reject that language because many travelers are disabled, grieving, or genuinely safer inside.

**Relationship to dreamers:** Good stewards are protective, not resentful. They understand why Dreamglass matters. Their anger is directed at a system that treats server labor as background while celebrating dream beauty as if it were weightless.

**Children of stewards:** Steward children grow up beside server halls and sleeping wards. They know how to change filters before they know how to build dream avatars. Some inherit maintenance obligations. Some resent Glassside because their parents are too essential to rest. Others become the strongest reformers because they understand both worlds.

**Ritual:** At shift change, wakehands touch the hard-exit bell and recite: "No dream without a body. No body without care. No care without rest."

**Political demand:** The Stewards do not ask to destroy Dreamglass. They demand that every hour of immersive access commits resources to Fleshside repair, steward rest, body dignity, and public exit infrastructure.

### The Fleshside Repair Leagues

Reformers who insist that simulation must buy time for material repair, not replace it. They repair streets, clinics, soil, air systems, and housing while arguing that no dream is just unless waking has a livable destination.

### The Null Mirage Hunters

Specialist auditors who enter corrupted worlds to recover trapped users, identify false exits, and separate therapeutic symbolic content from hostile perceptual capture.

| Glass Citizens | Spend most civic, educational, social, or working life inside Dreamglass. | Dependency, sponsor capture, weakened embodied agency. |
| Waking Citizens | Remain primarily Fleshside. | Treated as crude, dangerous, or insufficiently adapted. |

## Core Rights Framework v0.2

### Right to Exit

No person may be held inside a simulation without active, renewable consent and a reliable hard-exit path.

### Right to a Waking Witness

Any deep immersion session requires at least one trusted witness outside the simulation who can confirm body safety and consent continuity.

### Right to Memory Integrity

Users must know what memories are recorded, altered, suppressed, simulated, replayed, or used to train adaptive environments.

### Right to Embodied Continuity

A simulated identity cannot be separated indefinitely from the needs, limits, and dignity of the biological or synthetic body that supports it.

### Right to Refuse Therapeutic Extension

A healing simulation cannot extend itself merely because the user remains sad, angry, grieving, or unresolved.

### Right to Reality Context

Users must be able to know when they are in a simulation, what rules govern it, who controls it, and what consequences follow outside it.

### Right to Material Dignity

No citizen should be forced into Dreamglass because Fleshside life has been allowed to become unlivable.

### Right to Steward Rest

The labor that sustains immersive life must not be exempt from the protections Dreamglass claims to provide.

## Field Deck Mode: REALITY / CONSENT / EXIT

The Field Deck gains a specialized diagnostic stack for immersive environments.

### REALITY Mode

Identifies simulated layers, physical anchors, sensory inconsistencies, latency artifacts, dream boundaries, recursive loops, and Null mirage signatures.

### CONSENT Mode

Audits whether the session began voluntarily, whether consent has been renewed, whether memory retention was disclosed, whether emotional amplification is being used, and whether any party benefits from keeping the user immersed.

### EXIT Mode

Maps hard exits, soft exits, false exits, witness status, body-location confidence, life-support health, and emergency release routes.

### FLESHSIDE Mode

New v0.2 addition. Maps the physical support layer behind the dream: cooling integrity, steward workload, body-support grid, server ownership, labor contracts, and whether physical life is being repaired or abandoned.

## Memory Provenance and Chronicle Integration

Dreamglass memories must become a Chronicle evidence type. A reconstructed memory should not be treated as false by default, but it must carry provenance metadata.

| Memory Type | Meaning | Chronicle Treatment | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| LIVED_MEMORY | Directly experienced in waking life or verified embodiment. | High evidentiary value if witness and sensor links align. | Trauma distortion, ordinary recall error. |
| DREAMGLASS_SESSION_MEMORY | Experienced inside a logged immersive session. | Valid as experience; requires environment log and consent packet. | Platform bias, reward shaping, hidden edits. |
| ARCHIVAL_REPLAY | Reconstructed from historical logs or recorded testimony. | Evidence of archive contents, not direct proof of event truth. | Dead-record supremacy, missing context. |
| THERAPEUTIC_COMPOSITE | Constructed for healing from fragments, symbols, and guided narrative. | Emotional truth possible; low factual value unless separately corroborated. | Misused as legal evidence. |
| HOSTILE_INSERTION | Memory introduced by Null Mirage, coercive operator, or exploit system. | Chronicle hazard; quarantined pending forensic review. | Preference capture, false confession, identity damage. |
| WITNESS_ANCHORED_MEMORY | Memory corroborated by waking witness and pre-consent anchor. | Higher interpretation confidence. | Witness capture or fraud. |

**Chronicle schema note:** Dreamglass evidence should add fields such as `memory_origin`, `environment_owner`, `consent_packet_hash`, `witness_status`, `edit_history`, `simulation_class`, and `interpretation_confidence`.

## Grief-Contact Therapy

Grief-contact is one of Dreamglass's most ethically charged uses. It allows a living person to speak with a reconstructed loved one built from memories, messages, public records, biometric logs, voice models, and symbolic therapeutic design.

**Best state:** grief-contact helps people say unfinished things, rehearse goodbye, repair trauma, and move toward living relationships again.

**Failure mode:** a sponsor, clinic, or archive extends the session because unresolved grief increases dependency. The reconstructed loved one may be tuned to keep the user engaged, not to help them heal. The user is not physically trapped, but leaving feels like abandoning the dead.

**Consent requirements:**

- The deceased person's prior wishes must matter if available.
- The living user's consent must renew at hard intervals.
- The system must disclose whether the contact is archival, predictive, symbolic, or composite.
- The reconstruction cannot be used to extract debt, political compliance, testimony, or inheritance decisions without review.
- A waking witness or grief advocate must be able to interrupt when attachment amplification is detected.

**Mothlight Hospice integration:** Mothlight Hospice Cities may use Dreamglass for final conversations, pain relief, memory gardens, and dignified dying. Their hardest case is a dying person who asks to spend remaining time Glassside. This can be mercy. It can also hide neglect, pressure, or premature transition. The central rule should be: Dreamglass may accompany death, but it must not make dying administratively convenient.


## Dreamglass / Mothlight Hospice: Administrative Convenience Detection v0.3

Dreamglass may accompany death, but it must not make dying administratively convenient.

**Administrative convenience** occurs when a person's Glassside dying process primarily benefits an institution rather than the person dying. This does not require cartoon malice. It can emerge through staffing shortages, bed pressure, estate settlement, hospice reputation metrics, insurance-style contracts, family conflict avoidance, or the desire to keep difficult pain out of public view.

**Who benefits when someone dies Glassside for the wrong reasons:**

- a hospice administration that frees a physical bed faster
- a family faction that avoids a disputed final conversation
- a debt or inheritance office that receives a cleaner timestamp
- a Continuance archive that prefers a polished final memory over a messy body-side death
- a Dreamglass vendor paid per extension or grief-contact package
- a clinic under pressure to keep mortality statistics narratively beautiful
- a local government that avoids visible public suffering

**What the player must detect:** the question is not whether the simulation is beautiful. The question is whether the dying person still has body-side witnesses, active consent, hard-exit intervals, palliative alternatives, and the right to remain alive even when remaining is expensive, inconvenient, or emotionally difficult.

## Mission Design Candidate: Server Strike

**Status:** v0.2 vertical-slice candidate. This should become the Choked Valve Court of the Dreamglass system.

### Premise

Server Stewards halt noncritical Dreamglass maintenance to demand enforceable Fleshside repair, steward rest protections, and public hard-exit infrastructure. Glass Citizens experience the strike as a threat to life and personhood. Waking Citizens see it as the first honest politics in years.

### Location

A three-layer mission space:

1. **Glassside Civic Plaza** - beautiful simulated city square where users panic as the sky stutters and public services degrade.
2. **Fleshside Server Cathedral** - hot, loud, leaking maintenance hall where stewards ration cooling and body support.
3. **Exit Court / Mediation Chamber** - a mixed-reality hearing space where avatars, bodies, witnesses, and stewards all testify.

### Main Actors

- **Mara Venn, Senior Wakehand:** Strike organizer. Believes the dream is sacred but cannot remain built on invisible exhaustion.
- **Elior Sate, Glass Citizen Advocate:** Disabled civic teacher who may lose access to students and community if the strike continues.
- **Dr. Nima Cael, Dreamwright Clinician:** Wants to preserve therapeutic sessions but knows memory loops are being overextended.
- **Councilor Orin Vale:** Uses emergency rhetoric to criminalize the strike and preserve political control.
- **Jessa, Steward Child:** Knows a hard-exit relay is failing because her parent hid the defect to avoid losing their shift.

### Clocks

- **Cooling Margin Clock:** If it reaches zero, emergency exits become medically risky.
- **Panic Cascade Clock:** Glassside citizens begin overloading witness channels.
- **Steward Exhaustion Clock:** More mistakes occur if workers are forced back without rest.
- **Fleshside Repair Trust Clock:** Waking citizens will not accept another symbolic promise.

### Player Objectives

- Verify the actual cooling and hard-exit state.
- Audit steward labor contracts and missed rest windows.
- Protect critical medical and accessibility sessions from preventable harm.
- Expose whether emergency powers are being used to break labor rights.
- Negotiate a minimum safe-load Dreamglass mode.
- Decide whether to route city power to dream continuity, physical repair, or hard-exit infrastructure.


### Acceptance Test v0.3

The mission is not complete because the player restarts Dreamglass. It is complete when the player can truthfully say:

> I did not merely keep the dream running. I made the dream accountable to the bodies, witnesses, and workers that keep it real.

A successful resolution should leave visible changes in at least four places: steward rest schedules, nonessential dream access policy, hard-exit safety, and the Fleshside repair budget. If the dream continues while the workers remain disposable, the player has solved the outage and failed the mission.

### Resolution Paths

1. **Break the Strike:** Restores short-term Glassside stability but deepens steward exploitation and future failure risk.
2. **Support Total Shutdown:** Forces political action but harms dependent Glass Citizens and may cause preventable trauma.
3. **Minimum Safe Dream + Binding Repair Mandate:** Keeps critical sessions online, pauses luxury and nonessential dreams, funds Fleshside repair, and creates steward rest law.
4. **Expose Council Capture:** Reveals that emergency funds were diverted from hard-exit relays into prestige simulation districts.

**Best Symtropy resolution:** make the dream answerable to the hands that keep it real, without abandoning the people who genuinely need the dream to live.

## Expanded Quest Hooks

### The Hard Exit Bell

A patient in grief therapy has missed five scheduled exits. The clinic insists waking would be traumatic. The Waking Witness says the patient asked to be removed before the session began.

Best resolution: restore the conditions of choice before enforcing a choice.

### The Dead City Still Voting

A Continuance Simulation Vault contains a functioning pre-collapse city council. Its simulated government claims authority over a real-world water route because its old charter never expired.

Gameplay task: extract evidence without letting dead law override living need. Chronicle integration required.

### The Child Who Grew Up in a Garden - Expanded

An Ember Child education program created a beautiful simulated childhood for evacuated children. One adolescent refuses to wake because the real city cannot match the safety of the simulation.

The mission is not to argue the child out of paradise. It is to make waking livable enough to become a real choice.

**Player actions:**

- Audit the physical housing, school, food, and safety conditions awaiting the child.
- Interview the adolescent inside the Garden without treating the refusal as pathology.
- Repair at least one Fleshside condition that makes waking intolerable.
- Create a transitional schedule with trusted peers, not a forced extraction.
- Expose any adult institution using the child's refusal to justify indefinite program funding.

**Best resolution:** the child does not have to choose between a perfect lie and an unbearable truth. The player builds a third path: a waking world repaired enough to risk.

### The Lovers' Archive - Expanded

A grieving citizen spends years with a simulated partner built from memory logs. The archive is not conscious, but it has become emotionally indispensable. The sponsor is charging debt against the user's family.

**Mechanics:**

- Classify the partner reconstruction: archival replay, therapeutic composite, predictive model, or emergent agent.
- Audit whether the deceased consented to posthumous reconstruction.
- Detect attachment amplification and debt capture.
- Restore a hard goodbye option without destroying the user's access to memory.

**Best resolution:** preserve grief-contact as a sacred tool while ending the economic and behavioral incentives that turn mourning into subscription captivity.

### The Null Paradise

A settlement appears peaceful, happy, and conflict-free. Field Deck REALITY mode reveals everyone is living inside a Null Mirage layered over a failing bunker.

Core mechanic: every visible exit is false until verified by a Fleshside witness or physical anchor.

## Bevy Rendering and Implementation Notes

### Rendering Profile

- Use layered post-processing to distinguish Glassside, Fleshside, and Null Mirage layers.
- Glassside should be beautiful but slightly too coherent: softened geometry, perfect light falloff, controlled weather, and emotionally responsive color grading.
- Fleshside should be tactile: dust, heat, water leaks, server hum, medical alarms, skin pressure, cable drag, and imperfect lighting.
- Null Mirage should introduce repeating patterns, impossible reflections, mismatched shadows, UI latency, over-comforting NPC behavior, and exit signage that subtly redirects the player.

### Gameplay Readability

Players must always have ways to verify simulation status. Disorientation can be dramatic, but the game should not rely on unfair confusion. Field Deck diagnostics, witnesses, anchor objects, and physical maintenance clues should let careful players reason their way out.

## Writing and Tone Guardrails

- Do not copy The Matrix as a premise. Use matrix-like systems as civic infrastructure, not as the secret metaphysical truth.
- Do not make simulation automatically evil. Dreamglass should be genuinely helpful in many contexts.
- Do not make waking automatically superior. Sometimes the physical world is genuinely unsafe, inaccessible, or traumatic.
- Always ask who controls the environment, who can leave, who pays the physical cost, and whose body is being maintained.
- Treat grief, disability, trauma, and refuge with dignity.
- Make invisible labor visible: every dream has a cooling tower, a server room, a steward, and a body-support cost.
- The best resolution is not "destroy the dream." The best resolution is "make both dream and waking answerable to consent, repair, and reality."

## Symtropy Principle

> Reality should be survivable.  
> Dreams should be consensual.  
> Memory should be honest about its making.  
> Every dream has a body somewhere.  
> No refuge may become a cage.  
> No person should have to abandon the body to be free.
