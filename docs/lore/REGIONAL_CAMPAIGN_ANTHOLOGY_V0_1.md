# Regional Campaign Anthology

## v1.9 motive note

The campaigns below define structural pressures and institutions. Their inhabitants must also carry status, rivalry, faith, jealousy, pride, boredom, taste, revenge, and miscalculation as grounded motives. See [Human Irrationality, Status, Rivalry, Revenge, and Miscalculation Atlas](HUMAN_IRRATIONALITY_STATUS_RIVALRY_REVENGE_AND_MISCALCULATION_ATLAS_V0_1.md). Better evidence does not automatically resolve every conflict.

## Purpose

These campaigns are authored exemplars, not mandatory universal chronology. Each demonstrates how a famous place can become a bounded playable system with ordinary life, material dependency, institutional initiative, multiple resolutions, and worldline divergence.

They may be adapted, combined, procedurally transformed, or absent in a particular worldline.


## 1. The Third Wall Vote

**Place:** Nacre Delta  
**Historical root:** The Black Monsoon Retreat and two later seawall failures  
**Player promise:** Live inside a city deciding whether to build a third wall, retreat inland, or redesign itself around water.

### Ordinary Life

Market boats, roof gardens, school tide drills, seawall maintenance crews, flood-season music establish why the place matters before crisis.

### Active Pressure

A storm season arrives while the oldest district subsides and federal adaptation funding is disputed.

### Acting Groups

- Tideglass cooperative engineers
- Old Quay households
- Aureline utility concession
- Monsoon Lantern boat families
- delta ecologists
- youth housing assembly

No group is internally uniform. At least one member of each group must disagree with its public leadership.

### Playable Work

- survey subsidence and drainage
- repair pumps without locking the district into one bidder
- evacuate or defend vulnerable blocks
- trace falsified risk maps
- mediate a public design convention
- build temporary amphibious services

### Resolution Space

- third wall with public utility conditions
- managed retreat with reparative housing
- distributed amphibious adaptation
- corporate hardening and displacement
- catastrophic breach followed by diaspora

### Limits on Player Control

The storm track and several household decisions remain outside player control.

### Revisit Signals

A revisit should alter route access, maintenance sounds, public signage, work schedules, household movement, food availability, memorial practice, and what different residents believe happened. The place must remain inhabited after the climactic decision.

## Anthology-Wide Requirements

Every campaign implementation must include:

- one calm arrival sequence;
- one ordinary work activity;
- one care or household scene;
- one public disagreement;
- one physical-system task;
- one evidence conflict;
- one action by a faction during player absence;
- one resolution that preserves a disliked institution because its service remains necessary;
- one irreversible loss or tradeoff;
- one post-campaign scene about living with the result.

## Shared Anti-Patterns

Reject implementations where:

- the famous place is only a backdrop;
- one speech resolves physical infrastructure;
- every faction is waiting for the player;
- the “good” ending maximizes all values;
- defeat deletes public services;
- historical truth is revealed through one infallible log;
- the player can romance, command, or recruit every central NPC;
- the world returns to baseline after the campaign.
