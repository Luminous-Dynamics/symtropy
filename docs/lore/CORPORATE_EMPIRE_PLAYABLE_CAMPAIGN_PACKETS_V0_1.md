# Corporate Empire Playable Campaign Packets

## Purpose

These packets make corporate civilizations playable through the services they provide, the dependencies they create, the people who work inside them, and the difficult work of reform or replacement.

A corporate campaign fails if “defeat the executives” automatically solves infrastructure, medicine, transport, identity, information, manufacturing, or ecology.


## 1. The Last Meter

**Corporate lineage:** Aureline Vital Systems  
**Premise:** A district has stopped paying after discovering that emergency-water debt was compounded through a fraudulent successor ledger.  
**Player promise:** Keep water moving while workers, households, creditors, and public authorities contest ownership.

### Essential Service

The implementation must show the service functioning well for at least one ordinary scene before exposing abuse. Workers and users must be able to explain why the system earned trust.

### Internal Blocs

Every version contains:

- frontline workers protecting service continuity;
- users with unequal dependence;
- reformers inside the institution;
- leadership or investors defending control;
- a successor or public alternative with real capacity limits;
- an informal network serving excluded people.

### Playable Work

- audit meters and debt ancestry
- secure replacement membranes
- protect crews from retaliation
- design a public-service transition
- decide whether to expose records before drought allocation

### Resolution Families

- regulated concession
- worker-customer utility
- regional public takeover
- fragmented neighborhood systems
- violent cutoff and emergency aid

### Required Consequence

At least one later revisit must show who now performs maintenance, who pays, who has access, which records transferred, what happened to workers, and which dependency remains unresolved.

## Cross-Campaign Rules

1. Workers are not interchangeable with owners.
2. Customers are not a single political bloc.
3. Corporate records may be useful evidence without being complete truth.
4. Public takeover requires staff, parts, authority, finance, and transition work.
5. Mutualization may reproduce exclusion unless governance changes.
6. Informal alternatives may be caring, unsafe, exploitative, or all three.
7. A company may reform in one worldline and become Null in another.
8. No resolution erases debts, bodies, environmental damage, or lost trust instantly.
9. Service reliability is a gameplay value, not propaganda-only flavor.
10. Corporate architecture, language, interfaces, and rituals should remain recognizable in successor societies.
