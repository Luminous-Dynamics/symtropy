# Diaspora, Shadow Network, and Mobile Civilization Campaign Packets

## Purpose

Mobile and informal societies become playable through routes, households, services, portable institutions, and internal politics—not through a generic refugee, pirate, or black-market faction template.


## 1. The Route Remembers

**People or network:** Monsoon Lantern Diaspora  
**Premise:** A receiving city offers permanent housing only if boat families abandon their mobile schools and rescue obligations.  
**Player promise:** Protect movement, care networks, and host-city rights without romanticizing permanent displacement.

### Required Internal Positions

- continuity faction protecting inherited practice;
- reform faction adapting to current conditions;
- people seeking settlement or legal recognition;
- people defending mobility or secrecy;
- workers carrying the service burden;
- at least one household divided by the decision.

### Physical Requirements

The campaign must simulate the route, vehicle, clinic, archive, water system, seed store, shelter network, or salvage operation that makes the group viable. Destroying or legalizing the group changes real service capacity.

### Playable Activities

- perform one ordinary service alongside members;
- follow one cross-jurisdiction route;
- resolve an access or custody conflict;
- investigate both an external accusation and an internal failure;
- protect a person without making the group immune from accountability;
- support, oppose, or reshape one institutional transition.

### Resolution Space

Possible outcomes include legal recognition, federated autonomy, internal reform, route relocation, public-service integration, protected illegality, capture by predatory leadership, fragmentation, or displacement. No outcome makes every member agree.

### Revisit Requirement

A later visit must reveal who still receives service, how routes or household ties changed, which practice survived, and who bears the cost of the chosen settlement.

## Shared Guardrails

- Mobility is not cultural incompleteness.
- Settlement is not automatically liberation.
- Illegality is not evidence of immorality.
- Informal service is not automatically safe or egalitarian.
- Host communities have real capacity and rights.
- Children and dependents are not bargaining assets.
- Private identity, health, and route data remain protected.
- The player cannot become the unquestioned spokesperson for the group.
- Internal conflict must not collapse into “good moderates versus irrational radicals.”
- Worldline migration preserves ancestry without granting automatic local claims.
