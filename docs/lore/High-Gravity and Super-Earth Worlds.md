# High-Gravity and Super-Earth World Design Addendum

## Core Thesis

High-gravity worlds in Symtropy are not simply “large planets.”

They are worlds where weight becomes law.

```text
Gravity shapes bodies.
Gravity shapes architecture.
Gravity shapes labor.
Gravity shapes access.
Gravity shapes care.
Gravity shapes freedom.
```

A high-gravity world should make the player feel that every movement has civic meaning.

The central question is:

```text
Who gets to move freely when movement itself is expensive?
```

---

# 1. Gravity Bands

## 1.1 Earthlike Band

Gravity:

```text
0.8g – 1.2g
```

Use:

```text
baseline traversal
ordinary human settlement
normal walking, climbing, carrying, and repair
```

Design rule:

```text
Players can treat movement as normal.
```

---

## 1.2 Heavy World Band

Gravity:

```text
1.2g – 1.5g
```

Use:

```text
physically demanding settlements
shorter jumps
slower climbing
faster fatigue
heavier tools
more rest stations
```

Gameplay:

```text
carry limits matter
vertical evacuation is harder
elder and child routes matter
labor politics intensify
```

Design rule:

```text
The player can move unassisted, but the world should make them respect effort.
```

---

## 1.3 Super-Earth Frontier Band

Gravity:

```text
1.5g – 1.8g
```

Use:

```text
exo-suit recommended
assisted labor
low architecture
short exterior excursions
gravity-aware settlement planning
```

Gameplay:

```text
exo-suit calibration
fall prevention
assist-gear access disputes
medical triage
mobility-rights politics
```

Design rule:

```text
Unassisted movement is possible only briefly, carefully, and at personal risk.
```

---

## 1.4 Massive Surface Band

Gravity:

```text
1.8g – 2.2g
```

Use:

```text
exo-suit required
gravity-assist vehicles required
dense local settlements
limited exploration radius
high-risk repair missions
```

Gameplay:

```text
route planning
vehicle dependency
rescue logistics
pressure fatigue
civic access disputes
```

Design rule:

```text
The player should never forget that the planet is stronger than the body.
```

---

## 1.5 Extreme Gravity Band

Gravity:

```text
2.2g – 3.0g
```

Use:

```text
vehicle-only surface travel
remote drones
crawler caravans

nonhuman settlement dominance
```

Gameplay:

```text
remote repair
tethered sorties
gravity shelter routing
vehicle failure emergencies
native-body diplomacy
```

Design rule:

```text
Do not design this as normal walking space.
```

---

## 1.6 Prohibited Normal Surface Band

Gravity:

```text
3.0g+
```

Use:

```text

remote science
orbital observation
```

Gameplay:

```text
drones
probes
```


# 2. Symthaea Exo-Suit Integration

Symthaea-derived exo-suits are permitted on high-gravity worlds, but they must not erase gravity.

They provide:

```text
joint unloading
spinal support
fall arrest
tool stabilization
load sharing
respiration support
blood-pressure assistance
controlled descent
emergency lift
```

They do not provide:

```text
superhero jumping
free flight
unlimited carrying
perfect gravity cancellation
casual cliff traversal
```

Core rule:

```text
The suit helps the player negotiate gravity.
It does not delete gravity.
```

---

# 3. Gravity-Assist Vehicles

High-gravity worlds should use vehicles as civic infrastructure.

Vehicle families:

```text
compression crawlers
gravity sleds
inertial rescue litters
field-rail trams
buoyancy walkers
exo-frame work rigs
tether descent platforms
low-profile habitat rovers
dense-atmosphere gliders
submerged pressure ferries
```

Gameplay roles:

```text
settlement access
rescue
water transport
medical evacuation
repair crews
migration
public assembly access
emergency voting access
```

Core conflict:

```text
Who controls mobility during crisis?
```

Design rule:

```text
On high-gravity worlds, transportation is a rights issue.
```

---

# 4. High-Gravity Life Design

Native life should visibly reflect gravity.

Common traits:

```text
low centers of mass
wide bodies
many limbs
short necks
dense bones or equivalent structures
slow powerful movement
distributed support organs
ground-contact sensory systems
thick protective undersides
low social postures
```

Plant and fungal forms:

```text
mat forests
root-web plains
low pressure canopies
thick-stem groves
ground orchids
wide fungal shelves
wind-sheltered bloom basins
creeping sensory vines
```

Design rule:

```text
Tallness should be rare, costly, or symbolic.
```

---

# 5. High-Gravity Architecture

High-gravity architecture should be:

```text
low
broad
terraced
submerged
buried
arched
buttressed
ramp-based
rest-node rich
cable-supported
fall-safe
```

Avoid:

```text
casual skyscrapers
thin towers
long unsupported bridges
steep stairs
large open vertical drops
fragile decorative height
```

Allowed exceptions:

```text
weather pylons
sacred gravity towers
relay masts
ancient nonhuman structures
field-supported architecture
crystalline compressive structures
```

Core rule:

```text
Architecture should look like it has made peace with weight.
```

# Final Principle

High-gravity worlds should not ask:

```text
Can the player still move?
```

They should ask:

```text
What kind of civilization forms when movement itself must be shared, repaired, rationed, and defended?
```

In low gravity, freedom looks like flight.

In high gravity, freedom looks like being carried by a society that chose not to leave you behind.
