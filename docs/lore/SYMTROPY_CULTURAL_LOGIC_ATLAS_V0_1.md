# SYMTROPY_CULTURAL_LOGIC_ATLAS_V0_1.md

## Symtropy Cultural Logic Atlas

**Version:** 0.1  
**Purpose:** Convert cultural, subcultural, philosophical, ecological, spiritual, professional, and aesthetic lineages into ethical fictional design systems for Symtropy.  
**Status:** Foundation draft for society generation, region design, concept art, Field Deck UI, Chronicle hooks, and charter mechanics.

---

# 0. Prime Directive

Symtropy does **not** turn real cultures into factions.

Symtropy turns cultural wisdom, civic experiments, material traditions, subcultural practices, and relational philosophies into **fictional systems under pressure**, then tests those systems through survival, repair, consent, governance, ecology, memory, and failure.

```text
Real tradition → abstract design principle → fictional transformation → gameplay mechanic → failure mode → sensitivity check
```

Symtropy should never ask:

```text
How do we make the Māori faction?
How do we make the Zapatista faction?
How do we make the Rojava faction?
How do we make the Ubuntu faction?
How do we make the Buddhist faction?
```

It should ask:

```text
What does river personhood teach us about watershed law?
What does rotating stewardship teach us about emergency power?
What does consensus teach us about legitimacy?
What does relational personhood teach us about care and citizenship?
What does ritual maintenance teach us about continuity?
What does visible repair teach us about history?
What does subculture teach us about alternative civilization rehearsal?
```

Core rule:

```text
Do not copy cultures.
Do not exoticize cultures.
Do not flatten sacred traditions into game flavor.
Do not use living political struggles as decoration.
Do not turn marginalized pain into aesthetic texture.
```

Instead:

```text
Study the civilizational capability.
Abstract the principle.
Transform it through Symtropy's fictional history.
Attach it to material conditions.
Give it dignity.
Give it contradictions.
Give it a failure mode.
Make the player face consequences.
```

# 1. Relationship to Existing Symtropy Design Spine

This atlas extends existing Symtropy rules:

```text
Every social system is a hypothesis about how to keep life alive without becoming cruel.
```

```text
Architecture is civic evidence.
```

```text
A faction is not a brand.
A faction is a society under pressure.
```

```text
Civilization is not only how people survive.
Civilization is what people believe survival is for.
```

```text
The body is not merely code.
It is memory, metabolism, consent, kinship, labor, hunger, grief, and risk.
```

The Cultural Logic Atlas gives Symtropy a disciplined way to generate societies that feel historically real rather than randomly aesthetic.

A society should feel like this:

```text
A real pressure entered a population.
The population survived by inventing practices.
The practices became law.
The law became architecture.
The architecture became childhood.
Childhood became memory.
Memory became beauty.
Beauty hid a wound.
The wound became playable.
```

# 2. The Non-Extractive Adaptation Firewall

Whenever Symtropy draws design resonance from a real tradition, movement, religion, subculture, profession, or regional culture, run it through this firewall.

## 2.1 Adaptation Pipeline

```text
1. Reference Lineage
   What real tradition, movement, practice, or subculture inspired the question?

2. Civilizational Capability
   What does this tradition help us think about?
   Governance? Care? land? memory? water? ritual? repair? kinship? consent?

3. Abstraction
   What is the transferable design principle, separated from sacred names, real community identity, and protected symbols?

4. Fictional Transformation
   What material condition in Symtropy made this logic necessary?
   Flooding? air rationing? reconstitution? machine rule? biospheric contact? orbital isolation?

5. System Expression
   How does it appear in law, childhood, architecture, economy, UI, clothing, rituals, conflict, and public space?

6. Failure Mode
   What happens when the society's central value becomes uninterruptible?

7. Player Encounter
   What does the player do with it?
   Repair? vote? expose? negotiate? witness? refuse? join? leave? reform?

8. Sensitivity Check
   Did we copy sacred language, ceremonial objects, real trauma, real iconography, or living people too directly?
```

## 2.2 Forbidden Moves

Avoid:

```text
one-to-one faction analogues
sacred symbol copying
random spiritual vocabulary
aesthetic borrowing without social logic
using Indigenous knowledge as mystical flavor
using living political struggles as costume
turning real oppression into exotic scenery
turning a real people into aliens or monsters
using marginalized subcultures only as danger aesthetics
flattening religion into superstition or tech mysticism
```

Prefer:

```text
comparative precedent
fictional legal systems
new invented symbols
new invented names
local material causes
procedural cultural mixtures
consultation if ever using direct references in public-facing material
explicit failure modes
internal debate inside every society
```

## 2.3 Language Rule

Prefer these terms:

```text
reference lineage
design resonance
cultural logic
ethical analogue
comparative precedent
inspiration field
```

Avoid overusing:

```text
source material
real-world faction
tribe template
ethnic inspiration
```

Reason:

```text
Living cultures are not resource packs.
They are communities, histories, laws, wounds, and futures.
```

# 4. Society Generation Rule

A generated society should not equal one cultural logic module.

Use blends:

```text
Society = material pressure + historical wound + charter dimensions + 2–4 cultural logic modules + local ecology + current crisis
```

Example:

```text
Drowned Charter City
= flooding + failed property law + water commons charter
+ Kaitiakitanga-like guardianship logic
+ right-to-repair material culture
+ gift-economy emergency logistics
+ wetland biospheric intelligence
+ current storm surge crisis
```

This produces societies that feel real because they are internally layered.

Avoid:

```text
The solarpunk city.
The gothic city.
The Māori-inspired city.
The rave faction.
The hacker faction.
```

Prefer:

```text
A flood-adapted coastal charter where ecological evidence has legal standing, repair guilds control pump access, youth leagues organize storm warnings through music, and old corporate water ledgers still determine who gets insurance.
```

# 6. First Twelve Cultural Logic Modules

These modules are intentionally fictional. They draw from design resonances without mapping one-to-one onto real cultures.

## Module 01 — Watershed Personhood Commons

**Reference lineages:** river personhood precedents, Te Ao Māori design resonance, kaitiakitanga-like guardianship, TEK, animist ecological diplomacy, permaculture, wetland restoration movements.

**Civilizational capability:** legal and civic standing for water systems, wetlands, rivers, reefs, fungi, migrations, and ecological processes.

**Core thesis:**

```text
A watershed is not property. It is a living party to civilization.
```

**Founding wound:**

A flood-control regime saved elite districts while channeling toxins, sewage, and storm surge into lowlands. The river was treated as infrastructure until it answered through collapse.

**Protected value:**

Ecological continuity before extractive convenience.

**Sacred question:**

```text
Who speaks when the water has been injured?
```

**Governance logic:**

Watershed councils include human delegates, downstream residents, repair guilds, ecological witnesses, seasonal observers, and Field Deck-mediated ecological evidence. No development vote is valid unless habitat impact is recorded and contested by trained public witnesses.

**Ecological relation:**

The river, wetland, reef, or fungal basin is treated as a civic whole with standing. Its “voice” is not magically translated; it is inferred through multi-year signals, contamination logs, species behavior, hydrology, microbial signatures, and contested human interpretation.

**Economy and labor:**

Desalination, reed filtration, shellfish remediation, kelp protein, flood agriculture, sediment management, boat maintenance, habitat monitoring, and toxin cleanup.

**Care and kinship:**

Children learn water safety, flood memory, local species recognition, and “downstream kinship.” A household that pollutes upstream owes care downstream.

**Knowledge transmission:**

Tide classrooms, floodline murals, toxin-stain ceramics, fish migration ledgers, wetland elder walks, ecological testimony training.

**Material grammar:**

Stilt courts, floating archives, reed clinics, tide-mark walls, shellcrete public halls, transparent evidence tanks, wetland boardwalks, floodline memorials.

**Rituals:**

Seasonal consent walks, first-rain hearings, pollution confession, restoration oath, flood remembrance, child river-name ceremony.

**Internal factions:**

```yaml
Reef-Law Advocates:
  belief: nonhuman systems require legal standing
  fear: ecological personhood will be watered down into advisory symbolism
  pressure_response: demand binding vetoes

Flood Survivors' Bloc:
  belief: no ecological restoration without human reparations
  fear: poor districts will be sacrificed for wetlands again
  pressure_response: block habitat projects until displacement debt is addressed

Hydro-Technicians:
  belief: public emotion must not override hydrology
  fear: charismatic ecological claims will break engineering discipline
  pressure_response: insist on measured evidence and slow approvals

Wetland Devotees:
  belief: the basin knows more than humans can model
  fear: instruments create arrogance
  pressure_response: privilege ritual observation and seasonal restraint
```

**Outsider policy:**

Outsiders may settle if they accept watershed obligations. Extractive outsiders are forced into public ecological literacy before permits.

**Failure mode:**

Ecological legitimacy can become a weapon. Human interpreters may claim to speak for the watershed while silencing actual downstream people.

**Player roles:**

Ecological witness, repair mediator, pollution investigator, charter drafter, flood engineer, disputed advocate, saboteur of illegal diversion.

**Field Deck bias:**

Highlights flow, contamination, habitat stress, repair debt, consent uncertainty, and human interpretation layers separately.

**Chronicle hooks:**

```text
CHRONICLE: The settlement recognized the basin as a civic party.
CHRONICLE: The player restored flow but ignored displacement debt.
CHRONICLE: Ecological evidence overturned a private water claim.
CHRONICLE: A human council falsely claimed watershed consent.
```

**Concept-art motifs:**

```text
floating wetland court with ecological evidence tanks
children reading floodline walls
river personhood charter ceremony
reed filtration clinic after storm surge
pollution confession before downstream elders
shellcrete tribunal over living reef nursery
Field Deck showing conflicting river signals
flood survivor blocking restoration vote
bioluminescent wetland intelligence at night
sediment archive built into tidal wall
```

**Red lines:**

Do not copy specific sacred language, ceremonial dress, tattoos, carvings, or living Indigenous legal forms directly. Invent Symtropy-specific signs and protocols.

## Module 02 — Nested Commons Charter

**Reference lineages:** Zapatista autonomy, Rojava democratic confederalism, Quaker consensus, worker councils, municipal commons, mutual aid, Athenian assembly, Andean reciprocal labor.

**Civilizational capability:** layered self-governance without centralized domination.

**Core thesis:**

```text
Authority must remain close enough to be interrupted.
```

**Founding wound:**

A state emergency system saved the settlement but never expired. Later generations inherited functioning infrastructure and dead legitimacy.

**Protected value:**

Legitimate participation.

**Sacred question:**

```text
Can power serve without owning?
```

**Governance logic:**

Household circles, work crews, neighborhood assemblies, watershed councils, machine-override boards, and regional charter congresses. Delegates rotate. Major decisions require public record, dissent archive, and emergency-expiry schedule.

**Ecological relation:**

Ecological commons have representation through trained witnesses and evidence protocols. Commons law treats water, soil, energy, and repair capacity as survival rights.

**Economy and labor:**

Public works, repair guilds, food commons, tool libraries, water councils, mutual credit, rotating labor obligations.

**Care and kinship:**

Care rosters and mutual aid are charter obligations, not charity. Households can be biological, chosen, guild-based, or refugee compact groups.

**Knowledge transmission:**

Children attend assembly simulations, repair apprenticeships, public budget days, dispute circles, and emergency drills.

**Material grammar:**

Public amphitheaters, charter walls, repair halls, open ledgers, rotating steward booths, dispute kitchens, commons clocks showing emergency expiry.

**Rituals:**

Mandate return, public objection, dissent recording, emergency expiry ceremony, tool library oath, repair day.

**Internal factions:**

```yaml
Rotating Stewards:
  belief: power decays unless rotated
  fear: expertise becomes domination
  pressure_response: shorten terms and decentralize authority

Continuity Operators:
  belief: infrastructure fails if expertise rotates too quickly
  fear: popular suspicion will kill the grid
  pressure_response: demand professional custody of complex systems

Refugee Compact Bloc:
  belief: commons legitimacy depends on newcomers being fully included
  fear: old families will preserve soft ownership
  pressure_response: push citizenship expansion and housing rights

Fast Response Caucus:
  belief: consensus cannot manage acute crisis alone
  fear: deliberation will kill people
  pressure_response: request temporary emergency powers
```

**Outsider policy:**

Outsiders may attend assemblies but gain decision rights through residency, labor contribution, care obligations, or refugee protection status.

**Failure mode:**

Process can become theater. Informal power hides behind “the sense of the meeting.” Emergency delegations may never fully return authority.

**Player roles:**

Delegate, mediator, emergency steward, dissent archivist, infrastructure operator, charter reformer.

**Field Deck bias:**

Shows legitimacy gradients, unresolved objections, emergency authority timers, missing witnesses, and public-trust decay.

**Chronicle hooks:**

```text
CHRONICLE: The emergency mandate expired on schedule.
CHRONICLE: The assembly ignored recorded dissent.
CHRONICLE: Refugees were given full charter standing.
CHRONICLE: The player accepted fast authority during crisis and failed to return it.
```

**Concept-art motifs:**

```text
nested assembly hall during water vote
emergency-expiry clock above public square
rotating steward returning keys
children roleplaying crisis council
dissent archive alcove
commons kitchen after flood
public machine override hearing
repair guild arguing with assembly delegates
refugee compact citizenship ceremony
field deck civic legitimacy overlay
```

**Red lines:**

Do not present any real-world movement as clean utopia. Preserve internal contradiction, external pressure, and historical specificity.

## Module 03 — Visible Repair Civilization

**Reference lineages:** kintsugi, wabi-sabi, right-to-repair, repair cafés, maker culture, vernacular architecture, communal maintenance traditions, public works realism.

**Civilizational capability:** material continuity and repair as public memory.

**Core thesis:**

```text
A repair should be visible enough to become accountable.
```

**Founding wound:**

Disposable systems failed during collapse. Communities survived only by repairing unglamorous infrastructure others had abandoned.

**Protected value:**

Durability with memory.

**Sacred question:**

```text
Who has the right to touch the broken thing?
```

**Governance logic:**

Repair authority is distributed but certified by public witness. Critical systems require paired operators: technical custodian plus civic witness.

**Ecological relation:**

Repair includes ecological damage, not just machines. Soil, water, buildings, bodies, robots, and archives can all carry repair lineage.

**Economy and labor:**

Tool libraries, salvage yards, apprenticeship guilds, parts cooperatives, machine naming, public maintenance days, repair credits.

**Care and kinship:**

Elders are valued as maintenance memory. Children learn repair stories of objects and places. Disability access is built into repair standards.

**Knowledge transmission:**

Annotated objects, repair tags, manuals with lineage, apprenticeships, public failure reports, broken-machine classrooms.

**Material grammar:**

Exposed seams, gold/bright repair inlays, tagged pipes, stamped patch plates, annotated walls, visible conduits, scarred civic buildings, modular replaceable panels.

**Rituals:**

First repair, public reseal, broken-object testimony, tool inheritance, repair apology, maintenance feast.

**Internal factions:**

```yaml
Open Toolkeepers:
  belief: everyone should learn repair
  fear: guilds will monopolize survival
  pressure_response: publish manuals and run public workshops

Certified Custodians:
  belief: critical infrastructure needs disciplined access
  fear: amateur repair will kill people
  pressure_response: tighten permissions

Scar Aesthetes:
  belief: visible wounds teach history
  fear: polished restoration erases truth
  pressure_response: block cosmetic concealment

New-Build Modernists:
  belief: endless repair can become nostalgia
  fear: old systems trap the future
  pressure_response: advocate replacement when repair is irrational
```

**Outsider policy:**

Outsiders are asked what they can maintain, not what they own. Anyone can begin as apprentice, but critical trust must be earned.

**Failure mode:**

Repair lineage becomes caste. The people who kept the system alive begin claiming ownership over everyone who depends on it.

**Player roles:**

Repair apprentice, salvage investigator, guild negotiator, manual publisher, dangerous amateur, public witness.

**Field Deck bias:**

Shows repair history, failed patch risk, hidden proprietary locks, provenance, and unresolved maintenance debt.

**Chronicle hooks:**

```text
CHRONICLE: The player made the repair public.
CHRONICLE: The guild hid a dangerous patch.
CHRONICLE: A child learned the machine's name.
CHRONICLE: The settlement replaced a sacred machine instead of repairing it.
```

**Concept-art motifs:**

```text
public resealing of floodgate
repair-tagged civic hall
child learning from annotated pump
kintsugi-style bridge repair
right-to-repair tribunal
salvage yard as school
machine naming ceremony
guild conflict over dangerous patch
Field Deck reading repair lineage
elder teaching obsolete valve language
```

**Red lines:**

Do not romanticize poverty or imply communities should endure broken systems because decay is beautiful.

## Module 04 — Care Charter Society

**Reference lineages:** Ubuntu, care ethics, Disability Justice, queer chosen family, mutual aid, community health work, monastic hospitality.

**Civilizational capability:** making dependency, access, care, and personhood explicit civic systems.

**Core thesis:**

```text
A person is not less real when they need others.
```

**Founding wound:**

An old productivity regime abandoned the disabled, elderly, chronically ill, traumatized, young, grieving, addicted, and nonconforming. The survivors built a society where dependency could not be hidden.

**Protected value:**

Relational personhood.

**Sacred question:**

```text
Who is being made invisible by our definition of usefulness?
```

**Governance logic:**

Care councils hold veto power over infrastructure plans that create exclusion. Civic legitimacy depends on access, dependency support, and consent audit results.

**Ecological relation:**

Care extends to multispecies dependency: pollinators, animals, fungi, soil, assistive robots, companion species, and ecological caregivers.

**Economy and labor:**

Care work is counted as infrastructure labor. Care fatigue is simulated. Mutual aid credits cannot be converted into ownership claims over recipients.

**Care and kinship:**

Chosen family is legally recognized. A person may designate care kin, witness kin, death kin, body-advocacy kin, and emergency interruption kin.

**Knowledge transmission:**

Children learn consent, access, first aid, nonverbal communication, body privacy, grief literacy, and care boundaries.

**Material grammar:**

Accessible paths, rest alcoves, sensory rooms, public kitchens, kinship courts, care ledgers, adjustable furniture, body-rights clinics, consent thresholds.

**Rituals:**

Naming witness, care release, dependency oath, refusal-of-care hearing, access audit walk, grief meal.

**Internal factions:**

```yaml
Access First Bloc:
  belief: no public good is legitimate if inaccessible
  fear: emergency speed will erase disabled people
  pressure_response: veto inaccessible infrastructure

Care Realists:
  belief: care systems need limits and staffing discipline
  fear: infinite obligation will burn caregivers out
  pressure_response: ration care and enforce rotations

Body Sovereignty Advocates:
  belief: care without consent becomes control
  fear: dependency will justify coercion
  pressure_response: strengthen refusal rights

Kinship Expansionists:
  belief: family law must reflect actual care networks
  fear: old inheritance law will recapture bodies
  pressure_response: expand chosen-family standing
```

**Outsider policy:**

Outsiders are welcomed, but must learn consent norms and access obligations. Invasive curiosity about bodies or kinship is socially sanctioned.

**Failure mode:**

Care becomes soft surveillance. “We know what you need” becomes a velvet cage.

**Player roles:**

Care coordinator, body-rights witness, access auditor, kinship mediator, consent advocate, caregiver under strain.

**Field Deck bias:**

Shows access barriers, care load, consent status, dependency networks, body-risk alerts, and coercion risk.

**Chronicle hooks:**

```text
CHRONICLE: The settlement counted care as infrastructure.
CHRONICLE: A person refused care and was respected.
```

## 8.5 CULTURE Mode

# 10. Concept Art Prompt Grammar

Use this grammar to keep cultural art deep and non-cheesy.

```text
Create a full-scene cinematic concept art image for Symtropy.
Subject: [fictional society / module].
Show: [specific civic event, ritual, crisis, or daily practice].
The scene should reveal: [resource, law, care, ecology, memory, body, repair, governance].
Include: [architecture, people, public signs, tools, evidence, conflict, children/elders if appropriate].
Tone: [dignified / tense / wounded / beautiful / morally complex].
Avoid: [utopian polish / villain coding / costume faction / aesthetic collage].
Style: grounded sci-fi, public-works realism, repair-culture sacredness, lived-in infrastructure, realistic people.
```

## 10.1 Good Prompt Pattern

```text
Show a public water-rights hearing in a floating kelp court where ecological evidence tanks, tide ledgers, brine kitchens, child divers, and displaced land claimants all occupy the same civic space. The scene should make clear that food, law, and ecology are one system, while revealing tension between restoration justice and human displacement.
```

## 10.2 Weak Prompt Pattern

```text
Make a Māori-inspired water city with tribal designs and mystical nature vibes.
```

Reason weak:

```text
It copies real cultural identity, flattens meaning, and lacks fictional pressure, mechanics, or failure mode.
```

# 11. Red-Line Checklist for Designers

Before shipping a society, ask:

```text
1. Is this a one-to-one analogue of a real people?
2. Did we use sacred symbols, language, dress, rituals, or trauma without consultation?
3. Is the society defined only by aesthetics?
4. Does it have internal disagreement?
5. Does it solve a real survival problem?
6. Does it have a failure mode connected to its virtue?
7. Are children, elders, disabled people, outsiders, and nonhuman parties considered?
8. Can the player interact with its systems, not just look at its style?
9. Does the Field Deck preserve uncertainty?
10. Does the Chronicle remember consequences?
```

If any answer fails, revise.
