# Diasporas, Stateless Peoples, and Mobile Civilizations Atlas

## v1.9 cross-contamination note

Cultural entries are design anchors rather than claims of ideological uniformity. Unrelated institutions, minority practices, mixed households, class divisions, assimilation, heritage markets, reactionaries, and ordinary nonparticipants are developed in [Cultural Cross-Contamination, Minorities, Mixed Households, and Ordinary Nonparticipants Atlas](CULTURAL_CROSS_CONTAMINATION_MINORITIES_MIXED_HOUSEHOLDS_AND_ORDINARY_NONPARTICIPANTS_ATLAS_V0_1.md).

## Purpose

Not every civilization should be represented by a colored territory.

Some societies persist through routes, households, rituals, technical practice, archive ancestry, portable infrastructure, or a shared memory of a place that no longer exists.

> **A stateless people is not a people without institutions. It is a people whose institutions travel, overlap, or survive without exclusive territory.**

Each group below is internally diverse. No culture is defined only by victimhood, exile, or purity.

# 1. The Monsoon Lantern Diaspora

**Origin:** communities displaced during the Black Monsoon Retreat.

**Continuity infrastructure:** household-route ledgers, portable clinics, floodcraft guilds, multilingual schools, annual Lantern Count.

**Cultural traits:** water literacy, strong reunification law, flexible household structures, cuisine built around preserved sour foods and floating gardens.

**Internal divide:** return movements seek ancestral delta claims; settlement movements argue that generations of new homes are equally real.

**Playable tension:** a city wants Lantern engineers but refuses route-based residency rights to their families.

# 2. The Red Route Families

**Origin:** multigenerational rescue, tug, depot, and convoy crews formed during the Belt Rescue Schism.

**Territory:** transfer windows and service obligations rather than fixed land.

**Institutions:** route councils, rescue oaths, shipboard schools, custody courts, memorial plate exchanges.

**Cultural trait:** people may identify by the rescues their household answered rather than by birthplace.

**Internal divide:** universal-rescue traditionalists versus crews who believe the oath is used to exploit their labor.

**Playable tension:** a crew inherits an ancestral obligation to a settlement that no longer recognizes the compact.

# 3. The Unverified

**Origin:** people excluded from or refusing Palinode-style identity authority after the Archive Wars and War of the Returned.

**Continuity infrastructure:** plural witness circles, body marks, family memory objects, local cryptographic roots, ritual reintroduction.

**Strength:** extraordinary methods for living with uncertain identity without denying personhood.

**Vulnerability:** travel, inheritance, licensing, and reconstitution become difficult where centralized credentials dominate.

**Internal divide:** some seek eventual recognition; others treat verification itself as a political danger.

**Playable tension:** a highly skilled clinician cannot prove credentials during an epidemic.

# 4. The Open Seed Kin

**Origin:** farmers, ecologists, smugglers, and clinic networks who resisted proprietary biological dependency during the Seed Famine.

**Territory:** seed libraries, microbial cultures, gardens, and seasonal exchange routes.

**Institutions:** reproductive commons, ecological review circles, quarantine mediators, apprenticeship kitchens.

**Culture:** every gift of living material carries a story of origin, environmental assumptions, and known harms.

**Internal divide:** rapid famine relief versus cautious protection of uncertain ecologies.

**Playable tension:** a seed package can save a settlement but may erase a poorly understood native biosphere.

# 5. The White Ledger Caravans

**Origin:** the White Ledger Exodus from Antarctic treaty habitats.

**Mobility:** heat crawlers, seasonal coastal stations, subglacial routes, research support convoys.

**Institutions:** warmth mutuals, storm witness councils, portable archive courts, shared greenhouse modules.

**Culture:** hospitality is measured in heat-time rather than room or food alone.

**Internal divide:** those who seek permanent treaty recognition versus those who consider settlement another form of capture.

**Playable tension:** a caravan’s heat reactor fails inside a jurisdiction that recognizes no mobile residency.

# 6. The Sargasso Children

**Origin:** descendants and survivors of the Sargasso Ring Air Strike dispersed across orbital habitats.

**Continuity:** public pressure telemetry standards, environmental-coercion law, Three Minutes of Ordinary Air, survivor engineering schools.

**Culture:** ventilation sound and seal condition carry emotional meanings outsiders often miss.

**Internal divide:** memorial preservation versus refusal to let one catastrophe define every descendant.

**Playable tension:** a corporate habitat adopts Sargasso safety symbols while rejecting survivor oversight.

# 7. The Black Choir Artisans

**Origin:** Vanta foundry workers, defectors, and Free Tool movements.

**Territory:** machine shops and calibration lineages spread across planets and habitats.

**Institutions:** tool libraries, singing calibration schools, open-quality courts, traveling repair festivals.

**Culture:** technical standards are performed as music and embodied practice so knowledge survives archive failure.

**Internal divide:** strict quality traditionalists versus improvisers adapting to local materials.

**Playable tension:** an open workshop’s emergency part works but violates a politically captured standard.

# 8. The Tideglass Houses

**Origin:** neighborhoods lost to managed coastal retreat whose residents maintained legal, ritual, and architectural continuity after relocation.

**Institutions:** submerged-site pilgrimages, collective land trusts, reconstruction kitchens, family tide maps.

**Culture:** homes are remembered by water depth, smell, seasonal light, and objects recovered from ruins.

**Internal divide:** heritage protection versus letting drowned places become new ecologies.

**Playable tension:** a reef society claims standing over a submerged ancestral district proposed for salvage.

# 9. The Forkborn

**Origin:** people, households, and machine persons with remembered ancestry across worldline forks or divergent reconstitution histories.

**Continuity infrastructure:** ancestry graphs, branch testimony, nonexclusive mourning, duplicate-claim mediation.

**Culture:** identity is narrated through paths rather than one authoritative biography.

**Vulnerability:** institutions fear asset duplication, espionage, or impossible inheritance claims.

**Internal divide:** branch reunion movements versus cultures that consider divergence a protected right.

**Playable tension:** two Forkborn communities inherit the same promise but possess different evidence of what it meant.

# 10. The Quiet Pilgrims

**Origin:** scientists, religious communities, ecological witnesses, and former contact profiteers who committed themselves to protected noncontact zones.

**Mobility:** long circuits between signal ecologies, observatories, silent habitats, and restraint schools.

**Institutions:** quiet vows, evidence custody, noninterference training, seasonal observation camps.

**Culture:** absence of action can be a skilled civic contribution.

**Internal divide:** strict noncontact versus carefully negotiated reciprocal signaling.

**Playable tension:** a rare signal appears while outsiders arrive demanding public disclosure and access.

# 11. The Company Afterlives

**Origin:** former employees and residents of collapsed corporate civilizations.

A Company Afterlife may preserve:

```text
uniforms
jargon
safety rituals
technical standards
pensions
founder holidays
mutual aid
brand songs
shared shame
```

Some become worker commons. Some become nostalgic restoration movements. Some are treated as collaborators by neighboring societies.

**Playable tension:** descendants value a corporate identity that outsiders associate with exploitation.

# 12. The Unfinished Sky People

**Origin:** residents of incomplete arks and abandoned interstellar projects.

**Identity:** neither settled planet, ordinary habitat, nor active mission.

**Institutions:** mission-review conventions, exposed-structure maintenance guilds, destination cults, stay movements, navigation schools.

**Internal divide:** restart the ancestral mission, preserve the ship-city, dismantle it for other settlements, or launch only those who freely choose.

**Playable tension:** a newly available gate or propulsion system makes the old mission physically possible but socially devastating.

# 13. Diaspora Gameplay Requirements

Every diaspora needs:

```text
origin without reducing identity to origin
portable institutions
ordinary family and economic life
internal disagreement
relationship to host societies
relationship to return
material culture
language or register change
skills or services
vulnerabilities without helplessness
multiple political futures
```

Diasporas must not function as generic refugee populations or bonuses to cultural diversity.

They should create:

- overlapping jurisdiction;
- long-distance obligations;
- portable infrastructure;
- disputed archives;
- hybrid art and language;
- economic corridors;
- new forms of belonging;
- conflicts between heritage, adaptation, and return.
