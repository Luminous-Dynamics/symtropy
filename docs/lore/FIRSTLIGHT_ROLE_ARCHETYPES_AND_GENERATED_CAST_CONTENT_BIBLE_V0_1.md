# Firstlight Role Archetypes and Generated Cast Content Bible

## Purpose

This bible converts the original universal Firstlight cast into a set of authored dramatic and systemic roles from which each worldline instantiates its own people.

The previous named cast remains useful as reference prototypes. It is not a requirement that independent players meet the same names, faces, bodies, histories, or households.

> **The player should feel that these were the people who happened to live in their Firstlight—not characters assigned to every copy of the game.**

# 1. Cast Shape

The opening requires a high-salience cast of roughly eight to twelve people and a wider recurring population of twenty-four to forty residents.

The high-salience cast should include:

- one person who teaches material competence;
- one person who embodies care;
- one person whose continuity is nonhuman or contested;
- one person who controls movement;
- one person who controls or challenges public truth;
- one person associated with pleasure, culture, performance, or nightlife;
- one person tied to the future mobile base;
- one charismatic rival or external-pressure figure;
- at least one dependent, apprentice, child, elder, or person whose needs complicate evacuation;
- at least one person who does not become available as a companion.

These roles may overlap, but the cast must remain socially legible.

# 2. Reference Role: The Utility Steward

## Dramatic Function

Makes infrastructure personal and demonstrates that technical decisions distribute life, comfort, risk, and political power.

## Required Competence

- water, power, drainage, heat, or public works;
- emergency isolation;
- maintenance records;
- neighborhood consequences;
- limits of temporary repair.

## Possible Instantiations

- a wetland-born hydrologist who mistrusts central engineering;
- an older former corporate specialist trying to repair past extraction;
- a machine collective delegate with no private home;
- a young public-works organizer promoted beyond their confidence;
- a migrant engineer whose credential is locally disputed;
- a charismatic improviser whose brilliant shortcuts create later debt.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- cares for an illegal rooftop garden;
- refuses to replace a rattling kettle;
- plays a terrible strategy game with transit staff;
- keeps a handwritten list of residents who need mobility assistance;
- knows every unofficial water tap;
- secretly wants to leave Firstlight for orbital work.

## Fault Range

- territorial about systems;
- condescending under stress;
- politically naive;
- overconfident in temporary repair;
- unable to delegate;
- willing to sacrifice a district too quickly;
- freezes when a past failure repeats.

## Breaking Variants

- remains at a control room until physically relieved;
- joins the player to stabilize the crawler;
- evacuates dependents first and loses public standing;
- is missing after opening a bypass route;
- survives elsewhere and later disputes the player's account;
- dies but leaves recoverable technical and source-chain evidence.

# 3. Reference Role: The Care Steward

## Dramatic Function

Introduces consent, triage, bodily vulnerability, privacy, and the limits of rescue.

## Required Competence

- scene safety;
- assessment;
- stabilization;
- public-health coordination;
- handover;
- medication and supply constraints;
- refusal and capacity.

## Possible Instantiations

- a calm clinician with a reputation for political bluntness;
- a field medic who is brilliant in crisis and avoidant afterward;
- a mutual-aid healer with contested credentials;
- a machine-bodied diagnostic specialist seeking recognition as a caregiver;
- an alien-trained practitioner whose methods unsettle local staff;
- a former military medic who refuses institutional command.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- sings while sterilizing equipment;
- cannot cook but insists on hosting;
- runs a quiet reading group;
- keeps a collection of obsolete medical labels;
- bets on transit delays;
- is secretly applying for leave.

## Fault Range

- overprotective;
- breaches their own rest limits;
- dismissive of nonclinical knowledge;
- reluctant to admit uncertainty;
- emotionally inaccessible;
- politically compromised by a funding source.

## Breaking Variants

- stays at triage;
- escorts medically fragile residents;
- refuses to board until a relief team arrives;
- orders the player to leave someone who cannot survive transport;
- becomes injured and must accept care;
- later accuses the evacuation authority of preventable abandonment.

# 4. Reference Role: The Service-Machine Witness

## Dramatic Function

Makes artificial personhood, memory custody, legal authority, and practical companionship visible through ordinary work.

## Required Competence

- public-service procedure;
- physical assistance;
- source-chain and archive handling;
- local routes;
- machine maintenance;
- lawful refusal.

## Possible Instantiations

- a municipal service body with decades of contradictory firmware;
- a household machine emancipated through a labor dispute;
- a forked archive custodian with two accepted names;
- a cargo machine that learned public law after witnessing abuse;
- a social robot ashamed of a former entertainment role;
- a nonanthropomorphic maintenance swarm represented through one conversational node.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- relocates birds before every repair;
- tells one joke badly for years;
- keeps spare screws sorted by emotional association;
- watches historical dramas and complains about machine casting;
- has a preferred charging place occupied by teenagers;
- avoids one district without explaining why.

## Fault Range

- rigid under ambiguous authority;
- hoards evidence;
- overvalues documented claims;
- imitates a former owner under stress;
- resents biological assumptions;
- cannot decide which forked memory deserves priority.

## Breaking Variants

- receives lawful authority to carry evacuees;
- refuses an unlawful archive seizure;
- loses network continuity and becomes locally autonomous;
- splits functions across bodies;
- remains to operate a route after people leave;
- is carried as a core while the body is lost.

# 5. Reference Role: The Transit Keeper

## Dramatic Function

Makes movement, evacuation, disability access, logistics, and route politics tangible.

## Required Competence

- vehicle operation;
- route knowledge;
- load and braking limits;
- passenger safety;
- dispatch;
- weather and terrain;
- emergency boarding.

## Possible Instantiations

- a veteran municipal driver who knows every unofficial stop;
- a young racing enthusiast trapped in public service debt;
- a collective of shift operators represented by one recurring person;
- a mobility-disabled pilot using extensive control adaptations;
- a former smuggler with impeccable evacuation instincts;
- an alien migrant who reads terrain differently from human maps.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- decorates route maps with jokes;
- maintains a beloved old vehicle nobody else trusts;
- is romantically involved with someone who hates travel;
- teaches children to recognize engine sounds;
- records passenger songs;
- refuses to drive on one bridge after an old accident.

## Fault Range

- reckless confidence;
- excessive caution;
- favoritism toward regular passengers;
- concealed fatigue;
- resentment toward dispatch;
- willingness to break law without preserving evidence.

## Breaking Variants

- drives the Continuance Crawler;
- leaves with another convoy;
- sacrifices a vehicle to hold a route;
- becomes trapped beyond the player;
- refuses overcrowding that would make braking impossible;
- later founds a transport cooperative among survivors.

# 6. Reference Role: The Public-Record Agitator

## Dramatic Function

Makes evidence, rumor, privacy, public accountability, and contested history playable.

## Required Competence

- observation;
- custody;
- authentication;
- public communication;
- source protection;
- uncertainty;
- institutional access.

## Possible Instantiations

- a local reporter with a reputation for personal grudges;
- a court recorder who leaks only when procedures fail;
- an amateur historian running a pirate mesh archive;
- a machine witness collective spokesperson;
- a corporate compliance investigator becoming disillusioned;
- a performer whose broadcasts reach people formal media cannot.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- repairs their own cameras badly;
- loves sensational fiction but writes restrained reports;
- cares for an elder who distrusts archives;
- keeps a wall of corrected public notices;
- is terrible at keeping personal secrets;
- refuses to photograph funerals.

## Fault Range

- confuses exposure with repair;
- protects one favored source too far;
- enjoys public attention;
- underestimates physical danger;
- edits uncertainty into a cleaner story;
- distrusts private grief.

## Breaking Variants

- captures decisive evidence;
- abandons equipment to help a person;
- exposes an evacuation lie too early and creates panic;
- leaves via another route with the only complete recording;
- is accused of causing the disaster through disclosure;
- later publishes a history that conflicts with the player's memory.

# 7. Reference Role: The Cultural Spark

## Dramatic Function

Ensures Firstlight is loved for pleasure, style, desire, humor, and shared expression rather than only public service.

## Required Competence

- performance, venue work, design, food, ritual, nightlife, sport, or public celebration;
- crowd reading;
- practical safety;
- social networks;
- cultural memory.

## Possible Instantiations

- a musician and stage technician;
- a chef running a night market stall;
- a dancer who also inspects rigging;
- a bookmaker who organizes neighborhood relief;
- a ritual facilitator with strict consent practice;
- a street artist employed by the same corporation they mock.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- composes jingles for public utilities;
- wears impractical clothing while doing precise technical work;
- maintains a feud over a stolen recipe;
- helps young people build illegal light sculptures;
- hates their most popular song;
- dreams of performing off-world.

## Fault Range

- vanity;
- unreliability;
- romantic entanglement;
- avoidance of paperwork;
- financial risk;
- public charm masking private cruelty;
- refusal to abandon a venue during danger.

## Breaking Variants

- turns a festival system into evacuation signaling;
- saves cultural archives instead of industrial tools;
- disappears in the crowd;
- uses influence to open a private route;
- remains to broadcast instructions;
- later transforms Firstlight's loss into art the player may hate.

# 8. Reference Role: The Base Keeper

## Dramatic Function

Connects the opening settlement to the Continuance Crawler and ensures the mobile base has prior history.

## Required Competence

- heavy vehicle maintenance;
- modular habitat systems;
- cargo balance;
- fabrication;
- field repair;
- ownership or custody knowledge.

## Possible Instantiations

- a municipal mechanic who has been keeping the crawler alive off-books;
- a cooperative fabricator claiming the chassis belongs to workers;
- a retired convoy operator with a failing body;
- a young apprentice who knows the modifications better than the official keeper;
- a machine chassis architect seeking a body transfer;
- an external contractor trying to repossess the vehicle.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- sleeps in the workshop rather than at home;
- names every module after weather;
- refuses cosmetic repair;
- keeps a small shrine in the engine bay;
- hides contraband spare parts;
- wants the vehicle to become a traveling clinic, not a private base.

## Fault Range

- possessiveness;
- unsafe improvisation;
- debt to an antagonist;
- secrecy about structural damage;
- disdain for unskilled occupants;
- unwillingness to accept that the base must change.

## Breaking Variants

- boards and becomes a long-term resident;
- stays to launch another convoy;
- dies after transferring control;
- refuses the player ownership and grants only stewardship;
- leaves separately with crucial knowledge;
- later returns to challenge how the player transformed the vehicle.

# 9. Reference Role: The Charismatic Rival

## Dramatic Function

Provides attraction, danger, ambition, style, and recurring external pressure without reducing conflict to an evil mastermind.

## Required Qualities

- real competence;
- a constituency or household;
- an understandable objective;
- capacity to help;
- capacity to harm;
- at least one limit they will not cross;
- at least one limit they might cross under pressure;
- reasons to appear during ordinary life.

## Possible Instantiations

- a corporate recovery officer who believes public systems are irredeemably incompetent;
- a smuggler who provides access denied by formal institutions;
- a political organizer willing to exploit the crisis;
- a rival engineer pursuing dangerous metric research;
- a popular performer with private security and public influence;
- a militia coordinator who genuinely protects one district while abandoning another.

## Ordinary-Life Hooks

- buys food from the same stall as the player;
- is loved by a child in the player's household network;
- repairs their own equipment;
- keeps promises more reliably than some allies;
- enjoys losing at one game;
- has a visible private tenderness.

## Relationship Possibilities

- rival;
- employer;
- temporary ally;
- lover;
- political opponent;
- rescuer;
- betrayer;
- co-witness;
- later convoy leader.

The rival must be able to survive the Breaking independently of the player.

# 10. Reference Role: The Dependent Future

## Dramatic Function

Embodies a future not reducible to innocence or vulnerability.

Possible people include:

- an apprentice;
- a child;
- an elder with vital knowledge;
- a medically fragile resident;
- a new machine person;
- a recently arrived migrant;
- an animal or ecological culture under care.

They must possess preferences and agency appropriate to their situation.

They should not exist merely to force a rescue choice.

# 11. Household Web Templates

## 11.1 Shared Workshop Household

Several adults and an apprentice share tools, meals, and debt but not romance.

Breaking tension: tools versus evacuation seats.

## 11.2 Multi-Generational Care Household

An elder, adult caregiver, young person, and service machine have conflicting mobility needs.

Breaking tension: no vehicle can accommodate all adaptations without modification.

## 11.3 Separated-Custody Household

A child or dependent moves between households with hostile adults.

Breaking tension: evacuation authority is disputed while the person has their own preference.

## 11.4 Professional Couple with Divergent Duties

One person must remain at a public system while another must evacuate patients or dependents.

Breaking tension: neither can simply follow the player.

## 11.5 Machine Lineage Household

Several bodies or forks share history but disagree about identity and resource priority.

Breaking tension: one core, several bodies, limited power.

## 11.6 Solitary Resident with Distributed Dependencies

The person lives alone but relies on neighbors, medication, route assistance, or an informal food network.

Breaking tension: official rosters underestimate their real support needs.

# 12. Generated Ordinary-Life Scenes

Every cast requires authored scene slots populated by generated particulars.

## Scene: The Unreserved Seat

The player enters a meal already underway. Nobody stops. A seat may be available, but it belongs to the room, not to the protagonist.

## Scene: The Repeated Argument

Two residents resume an old dispute about a small practical matter. The player is not asked to arbitrate.

## Scene: The Bad Performance

The cultural worker tests something that fails or is simply mediocre. Others react according to history.

## Scene: The Borrowed Tool

A tool passed among households reveals trust, annoyance, maintenance habits, and debt.

## Scene: The Quiet Refusal

A resident declines help or conversation without triggering a quest.

## Scene: The Personal Ambition

Someone reveals what they want beyond surviving Firstlight.

## Scene: The Rival at Rest

The antagonist or rival appears in an ordinary context where violence or confrontation would be absurd.

# 13. Cast-Specific Catastrophe Questions

For every flagship person, author and simulate:

- What warning do they receive?
- Who do they believe?
- Who are they responsible for?
- What do they try to carry?
- What will they refuse to abandon?
- What can change their plan?
- What happens if the player never meets them?
- What evidence of their route remains?
- How might they later remember the player?

# 14. Naming the Lost and the Surviving

The game must not present a generic memorial roll.

Names appear through:

- survivor manifests;
- missing-person notices;
- private lists;
- official casualty records;
- machine memory;
- songs;
- graffiti;
- reconstitution claims;
- household arguments;
- later settlements.

Records may disagree.

The player should sometimes discover that a person listed as dead survived, or that a celebrated survivor abandoned others. Such reversals require evidence and simulation history, not arbitrary twists.

# 15. Reference Cast Deprecation Rule

The names Sera Vale, Amadi Nko, Morrow-7, Nia Kgosana, Mara Quill, Lio Dlamini, Tomas Reed, and Kez-14 remain valid example packets and may appear in authored benchmark worldlines.

They are not the universal commercial cast.

Production builds must prove at least twelve unrelated cast seeds in which:

- role coverage remains intact;
- no flagship name repeats unless culturally plausible and low-salience;
- relationships remain coherent;
- players distinguish people in blind tests;
- the Breaking produces different survivor stories.

## Content Maxim

> **The role gives the world dramatic shape. The person gives the player someone no other worldline can replace.**
