# Symtropy Social Systems and Charters

## Version 0.1 — Societies as Repair Hypotheses

## Purpose

This document defines how Symtropy represents player-created social systems, settlement charters, civic structures, and governance experiments.

Symtropy should allow players to build societies, not merely bases.

A settlement is not only a collection of buildings. It is a living answer to hard questions:

```text
Who owns water?
Who controls energy?
Who can override machines?
Who counts as a citizen?
Who remembers the law?
Who may speak for the dead?
Who may interrupt emergency power?
Who pays the cost of repair?
```

## Core Thesis

Every social system is a hypothesis about how to keep life alive without becoming cruel.

No system should be purely good.

No system should be purely evil.

Every charter should solve one kind of suffering while creating another kind of danger.

```text
A working society is not one that avoids tradeoffs.
A working society is one that can face its tradeoffs without lying.
```

Each player-created social system should be built from dimensions rather than a single ideology label.

Gameplay effects:

```text

```

Failure modes:

```text
commons paralysis
guild capture
state ration abuse
corporate enclosure
machine denial logic
ritual exclusion
```

## 2. Decision Speed

How quickly can the settlement act?

Spectrum:

```text
Deliberative Consensus ←→ Emergency Command
```

High deliberation:

```text
+ legitimacy
+ trust
+ long-term stability
- crisis response speed
- faction frustration
```

High command:

```text
+ speed
+ survival during attack
+ coordinated defense
- legitimacy
- emergency-authority drift
- Protectorate faction growth
```

## 3. Machine Authority

How much autonomy do machines receive?

Spectrum:

```text
Human-Only Override ←→ Machine Co-Governance
```

Low machine authority:

```text
+ human accountability
+ lower Null delegation risk
- slower diagnostics
- more labor demand
- less efficiency
```

High machine authority:

```text
+ efficiency
+ predictive maintenance
+ reduced labor burden
- Null drift risk
- machine-legitimacy disputes
- human deskilling
```

## 4. Archive Authority

How binding are records and witnesses?

Spectrum:

```text
Living Consent First ←→ Archive Continuity First
```

Living consent first:

```text
+ adaptability
+ community agency
- historical amnesia risk
- forged legitimacy risk
```

Archive continuity first:

```text
+ legal continuity
+ better dispute handling
+ stronger witness culture
- ritual delay
- frozen authority risk
```

## 5. Openness

How open is the settlement to outsiders?

Spectrum:

```text
Open Refuge ←→ Controlled Membership
```

Open refuge:

```text
+ population growth
+ moral legitimacy
+ cultural diversity
- resource stress
- infiltration risk
- governance overload
```

Controlled membership:

```text
+ stability
+ resource predictability
+ lower security risk
- exclusion wounds
- refugee hostility
- legitimacy penalties
```

## Core Charter Types

These are templates, not fixed classes.

Players can combine and modify them.

## Watershed Commons

Civilization is shared water stewardship.

Strengths:

```text
high water legitimacy
strong ecological recovery
good public trust
low corporate capture
```

Weaknesses:

```text
slow crisis decisions
high meeting burden
vulnerable to sabotage
hard to scale quickly
```

Failure mode:

```text
the commons becomes unable to act while water systems fail
```

Old Waterworks reaction:

```text
The pump must return to public water law.
```

## Repair Guild Republic

Civilization is maintained by skilled labor and public craft.

Strengths:

```text
high repair capacity
strong worker memory
good device diagnostics
fast physical restoration
```

Weaknesses:

```text
guild hierarchy
technical gatekeeping
oral knowledge can become exclusionary
```

Failure mode:

```text
repair knowledge becomes a caste system
```

Old Waterworks reaction:

```text
Find the maintenance lineage. Restore the pump through those who kept it alive.
```

## Archive City

Civilization is accountable memory.

Strengths:

```text
high archive integrity
good legitimacy resolution
strong witness networks
excellent continuity law
```

Weaknesses:

```text
ritual delay
record dependency
public frustration during emergencies
```

Failure mode:

```text
truth becomes so procedural that repair cannot move
```

Old Waterworks reaction:

```text
No override until the authority chain is witnessed.
```

## Example Charter: Firstlight Basin Public Repair Charter

```text
1. Water is a public trust.
2. Survival infrastructure must remain auditable.
3. Emergency powers expire unless witnessed and renewed.
4. No machine may deny public override without a recorded reason.
5. No person may be bound to a settlement by debt alone.
6. Repair knowledge must be taught across generations.
7. Archive Witness is required for dead-authority overrides.
8. Ecological restoration is part of infrastructure maintenance.
9. Outsiders may request refuge, but resource strain must be publicly reviewed.
10. Any charter article may be challenged through Confluence hearing.
```

## Charter Drift

Charters should evolve under pressure.

Examples:

```text
Watershed Commons under attack → Security Protectorate drift
Repair Guild under scarcity → Technical caste drift
Archive City under repeated crisis → Ritual paralysis drift
Machine Stewardship under labor shortage → Null delegation drift
Corporate Enclave under revolt → Charter republic reform
```

A settlement’s charter should not be static.

It should respond to:

```text
crisis
player choices
faction power
resource scarcity
NPC memory
Null exposure
Archive disputes
external diplomacy
```

## Guardrails

Player-made social systems should be expressive, but reality must test them.

A society can claim water is sacred.

The game asks:

```text
Does it keep water public?
Does it teach repair?
Does it handle dissent?
Does it prevent abuse?
Does it expire emergency powers?
Does it remember the dead honestly?
Does it protect children?
Does it resist Null drift?
```

## What Not To Do

Do not reduce social systems to simple buffs.

Do not make authoritarian systems purely efficient with no moral cost.

Do not make democratic systems purely good with no coordination cost.

Do not make machine governance automatically wise.

Do not make all charters equally stable.

Do not make ideology cosmetic.

## Implementation Milestones

## Milestone 1 — Hardcoded Charter

Add one charter to Firstlight Basin:

```text
Firstlight Public Repair Charter
```

Display relevant clauses in Field Deck CIVIC mode during Old Waterworks.

## Milestone 2 — Charter Conflict

Let the Old Waterworks repair path conflict with the charter.

Example:

```text
Manual bypass restores water faster but violates Article 7.
```

## Milestone 3 — Faction Responses

Let factions interpret the charter differently.

## Milestone 4 — Charter Drift

After player choices, modify settlement drift.

## Milestone 5 — Charter Builder

Allow player-defined custom charters at settlement start or later founding.

## Final Principle

A settlement is not a base.

It is a moral machine made of water, law, memory, labor, and trust.

```text
The question is not only whether the settlement survives.
The question is what it becomes while surviving.
```
