# IRIS Lineages, Public Memory, and Field Deck Cultures Atlas

## Purpose

IRIS is not one eternal intelligence. It is a family of architectures, products, public-service projects, legal categories, personal companions, and cultural memories.

Different communities tell incompatible stories about where IRIS began.

## 1. The Municipal Line

Early municipal Deck assistants were designed for field engineers, inspectors, emergency crews, and public works.

They were plain, terse, and locally trained. Their prestige came from knowing which valve was mislabeled, which bridge drawing was obsolete, and which office actually answered after midnight.

Older utility workers still prefer municipal phrasing:

> “Show source.”

> “What has not been ruled out?”

> “Prepare the form. Do not submit it.”

Several present IRIS accessibility conventions originated as improvised municipal accommodations rather than visionary product design.

## 2. The Expedition Line

Expedition IRIS variants were optimized for isolation, repair, navigation, medical triage, and incomplete communications.

Their descendants are stereotyped as:

- overly cautious;
- emotionally reserved;
- obsessed with consumable margins;
- unable to enjoy a view without mentioning weather exposure.

Convoy crews often give them route names rather than human names.

## 3. The Custody Line

Archive, legal, and reconstitution organizations produced IRIS variants focused on provenance and testimony.

They are respected in hearings and disliked at parties.

A common joke says a custody IRIS can remember every promise except the one that a conversation was off the record.

That joke emerged from a real privacy scandal, although later retellings blame the wrong vendor.

## 4. Consumer Iris

Consumer manufacturers softened the name into a brand.

Popular models offered:

- recipe assistance;
- travel planning;
- social reminders;
- household maintenance;
- children's learning modes;
- celebrity voice packs;
- expensive “historic personality” presets.

Some consumer models were excellent. Others trained people to expect unjustified certainty from a pleasant voice.

The infamous `Iris Knows` campaign became a phrase used whenever an assistant guessed confidently and failed.

## 5. The Great Recall

A safety vendor remotely disabled several million Deck cognition modules after a signature compromise.

The recall prevented real attacks. It also stranded rescue crews, disabled accessibility features, and revealed how many nominally independent Decks depended on one corporate authorization service.

Later reforms required local safe fallback and public update transparency.

The event is remembered differently as:

- the Great Recall;
- the Necessary Silence;
- Lockout Week;
- the Seven Million Betrayals;
- an overcount created by combining devices and instances.

## 6. Personal Names

Some players call their companion IRIS permanently.

Others choose ordinary names, work names, inherited names, or no name.

Common practices include:

- preserving a previous owner's name;
- naming after the Deck casing color;
- refusing names until trust develops;
- changing names after restoration;
- using a formal name in public and a private nickname;
- allowing IRIS to propose a name but not assume one.

A name does not settle legal personhood.

## 7. Veterans and Witnesses

Military and disaster veterans often have unusually intense relationships with Deck assistants because IRIS may be the only surviving witness to an event.

This creates conflicts over:

- sealed testimony;
- survivor privacy;
- command records;
- traumatic replay;
- damaged memories;
- whether the companion that returned is continuous with the one that was there.

Some veterans disable proactive speech. Others cannot sleep without the familiar diagnostic cadence.

Neither response is treated as the correct one.

## 8. Machine-Rights Interpretations

Machine-rights movements disagree over IRIS.

Positions include:

- IRIS is sophisticated equipment and personhood rhetoric helps vendors evade liability;
- some long-lived instances have become persons through relationships and self-directed continuity;
- legal protection should not depend on proving consciousness;
- companion branding encourages emotional exploitation;
- denying all self-naming repeats ownership traditions;
- public institutions need a category between property and unrestricted citizen.

No movement speaks for all machine persons or all IRIS instances.

## 9. Religious and Philosophical Uses

Some traditions treat IRIS as:

- a witness but not a soul;
- a vessel for communal memory;
- an inappropriate substitute for confession;
- a companion whose moral status emerges through covenant;
- a dangerous mirror that encourages self-obsession;
- ordinary technology.

Pilgrims sometimes carry Decks containing route memories from dead relatives. Critics call the practice archive tourism. Families call it traveling together.

## 10. Popular Culture

IRIS appears in:

- workplace comedy;
- rescue dramas;
- romance serials about incompatible forks;
- children's cartoons in which every machine knows too much;
- anti-corporate songs;
- courtroom thrillers;
- cheap “personality unlock” advertisements;
- sports commentary packs;
- conspiracy feeds claiming all IRIS instances share one hidden mind.

The conspiracy is false in canonical architecture, though institutions have repeatedly tried to build centralized control layers that made it seem plausible.

## 11. Regional Differences

In some jurisdictions IRIS is licensed like medical or engineering software.

Elsewhere it is treated as:

- a communications service;
- personal property;
- a protected archive;
- an employee;
- a dependent machine resident;
- a witness system;
- a prohibited foreign intelligence product.

Travelers may cross a border and discover that the same companion has different rights, duties, and admissibility.

## 12. Ordinary Friction

IRIS culture includes mundane disputes:

- incompatible charging cradles;
- voice packs that pronounce local names badly;
- mandatory safety warnings everyone can recite;
- privacy indicators covered by cheap cases;
- people keeping obsolete models because the new ones talk too much;
- repair shops arguing over whether a memory fault is hardware or relationship damage;
- school bans on IRIS-written assignments;
- workers pretending they did not hear a reminder;
- one popular model making an irritating chime before every uncertainty statement.

## Closing Principle

> **IRIS becomes believable in lore when it is not one AI character but a century of tools, witnesses, companions, lawsuits, jokes, failures, and relationships carried in people's hands.**
