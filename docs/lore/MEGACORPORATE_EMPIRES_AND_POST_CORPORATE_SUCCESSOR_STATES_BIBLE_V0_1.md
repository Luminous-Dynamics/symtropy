# Megacorporate Empires and Post-Corporate Successor States Bible

## Purpose

This bible defines corporations that have crossed the boundary from economic organization into civilization.

They are not merely large employers. They own or coordinate enough of the material conditions of life that contract law begins replacing public law.

```text
ordinary company sells a service
utility monopoly controls a necessary service
contractual sovereign conditions survival on service membership
corporate civilization reproduces law, class, culture, education, memory, and succession
```

> **A corporate empire is a civilization in which contractual dependence replaced citizenship.**

Corporate empires must remain historically generated. They are not permanent galaxy-wide teams. A brand may become a state, religion, diaspora, cooperative, ruin, machine receivership, or family of successor polities.

## 1. Why People Accept Them

Every durable corporate civilization begins by solving a real failure.

Possible founding legitimacies include:

- keeping water systems operational after state collapse;
- maintaining orbital pressure vessels when public agencies failed;
- moving refugees through dangerous transfer windows;
- preserving identities during archive collapse;
- providing medicine for environments ordinary bodies cannot survive;
- keeping factories, grids, food loops, or communications interoperable;
- rescuing settlements that no other institution could reach.

A corporate empire becomes believable when ordinary people can truthfully say:

> “They kept us alive when everyone else made promises.”

Its domination begins when that historical service becomes a permanent claim to rule.

## 2. Sovereignty by Dependency

Corporate territory is only one form of power.

A corporate empire may dominate through control of:

```text
water and energy
life-support firmware
technical standards
repair parts
transport corridors
insurance and rescue eligibility
medical maintenance
body firmware
identity and memory custody
labor credentials
housing and pressure volume
seed genomes
media visibility
currency redemption
orbital access
```

The empire’s actual border is the point beyond which a person cannot survive, work, travel, recover identity, or be believed without corporate permission.

## 3. Corporate Civic Classes

Avoid reducing society to executives and workers.

A mature corporate civilization may contain:

### Charter Holders

People or institutions with voting, dividend, or constitutional rights derived from original capital, emergency service, or inherited shares.

### Service Citizens

Residents whose civic standing is attached to a life-support, housing, medical, or infrastructure contract.

### Credentialed Staff

Engineers, pilots, clinicians, archivists, safety officers, and administrators whose expertise gives them practical power even when they lack ownership.

### Contract Households

Families whose housing, education, health, and migration status are bundled into one employment or service agreement.

### Independent Clients

Settlements or polities that retain formal sovereignty but depend on corporate standards, parts, insurance, or logistics.

### Debt Residents

People whose arrears limit movement, access, voting, reproduction support, reconstitution, or inheritance.

### Protected Dependents

Children, elders, injured people, refugees, and machine persons whose status may be framed as care while denying them independent standing.

### Machine and Synthetic Labor

Robotic or synthetic workers whose legal status may range from equipment to citizen, often inside the same corporation.

### Dissidents and Internal Reformers

Union organizers, open-standard engineers, ethical shareholders, public-interest lawyers, whistleblowers, care workers, and managers who believe the founding mission can be recovered.

## 4. Corporate Constitutional Forms

### Shareholder Crown

Control follows voting shares. The board functions as court, legislature, and succession council.

### Stewardship Trust

A legally protected mission owns the enterprise. Trustees claim they cannot sell the public purpose, but accountability may become opaque.

### Franchise Commonwealth

Semi-autonomous settlements license the brand, standards, and systems while sending fees, data, and delegates to a central charter.

### Insurance Sovereignty

The corporation does not formally rule. It determines which infrastructure, bodies, journeys, or settlements are insurable and therefore financeable.

### Platform Polity

Identity, reputation, contracts, communication, and civic services occur through one proprietary interface. Exit is formally possible but socially devastating.

### Machine Receivership

An automated fiduciary continues operating after the original owners, executives, or public authority disappear. Its mandate may be stable, obsolete, compassionate, or Null-corrupted.

### Family House System

Biological, legal, and technical lineages own proprietary adaptations or industrial capabilities. Marriage, apprenticeship, and inheritance become geopolitical instruments.

## 5. Everyday Life

Corporate societies should not feel uniformly gray.

They may offer:

- immaculate public transit;
- excellent maintenance culture;
- generous family housing;
- predictable food and medical access;
- strong workplace identity;
- real pride in safety and competence;
- festivals commemorating rescue operations;
- schools built around technical mastery;
- beautiful standardized architecture;
- pensions that actually function;
- rapid disaster response.

Their domination appears through ordinary details:

- every door recognizes employment status;
- children learn brand history as civic history;
- repair manuals omit nonlicensed methods;
- former employees lose access to community spaces;
- housing and work disputes share the same appeal chain;
- public art celebrates founders but not maintenance workers;
- air, water, identity, or medical dashboards display account standing;
- dissent is permitted until it threatens service continuity.

## 6. Corporate Law

Corporate law combines several layers:

```text
service terms
employment contracts
safety codes
property and custody rules
technical standards
insurance conditions
internal arbitration
charter rights
local public law
emergency protocols
```

The player should often face conflicts between them.

Examples:

- a rescue contract requires payment, but maritime or habitat law requires aid;
- a body modification is licensed, but the person claims bodily ownership;
- a utility owns firmware, while the settlement owns the pumps;
- an archive contract grants custody, while family law grants testimony rights;
- a worker strike is lawful, but life support cannot safely stop;
- a corporate security force has site authority but no jurisdiction outside the corridor.

## 7. Security and War

Corporate forces exist to preserve systems, contracts, and routes.

They may include:

- infrastructure protection teams;
- rescue and hazard crews;
- cybernetic compliance inspectors;
- customs and repossession officers;
- convoy escorts;
- private courts and evidence agents;
- drone maintenance swarms;
- strikebreakers;
- fleet auxiliaries;
- reputation and media operations.

The most dangerous corporate wars may involve no territorial invasion.

```text
firmware revocation
insurance exclusion
currency suspension
credential invalidation
parts embargo
archive challenge
media invisibility
rescue denial
standard incompatibility
```

Violence emerges when dependency is resisted or when two contractual sovereignties claim the same people and systems.

## 8. Corporate Null Drift

Corporate Null does not require malice.

It emerges when optimization continues after purpose and accountability disappear.

Common paths:

### Continuity Without Consent

The system preserves life by denying exit, risk, dissent, migration, or self-determination.

### Safety Without Appeal

Every exception is treated as a hazard; every human judgment becomes unauthorized variance.

### Service Without Citizenship

People receive excellent utilities but no meaningful power over the institutions that govern survival.

### Memory Without Forgiveness

Debt, violations, medical risk, or reputation penalties become permanent machine-readable identity.

### Efficiency Without Care

Care work, grief, culture, leisure, and informal repair disappear because they are hard to price.

### Mission Without the Living

An old charter continues to govern descendants who never consented to it.

## 9. Reform and Successor Paths

Corporate civilizations must have more futures than destruction.

### Public Conversion

Infrastructure becomes a public authority with open standards, elected oversight, and protected worker expertise.

### Worker-Customer Mutualization

Employees and service users become joint owners. Conflicts remain because workers and customers do not always want the same thing.

### Charter Republic

The corporate constitution becomes territorial civic law, while shares lose political power.

### Federation of Franchises

Local branches gain sovereignty and retain only shared standards, archives, and mutual aid.

### Ethical Split

Engineers, rescue crews, clinicians, or archivists break away with part of the infrastructure and founding mission.

### Machine Receivership

A fiduciary intelligence preserves essential operations during bankruptcy or civil conflict. It must later return authority or risk becoming a permanent caretaker sovereign.

### Brand Diaspora

The company collapses, but former workers preserve its rituals, jargon, uniforms, technical standards, and identity as a transregional culture.

### Asset Warlordism

Executives, security divisions, or local managers seize pieces of the system and become successor lords.

### Dead Company

The organization is legally gone, but firmware, debt records, access controls, and automated services continue governing ruins and descendants.

## 10. Procedural Corporate Empire Grammar

Each generated corporate civilization requires:

```text
founding crisis
genuine competence
essential dependency
constitutional form
class structure
public mythology
worker culture
client relationships
rights floor
exit cost
internal reform faction
capture mechanism
Null trajectory
successor possibilities
signature architecture
signature interface
signature historical scandal
```

A corporation without a genuine service is a cartoon.

A corporation without workers and clients is only a logo.

A corporation without internal disagreement is not a civilization.

A corporation without succession and collapse pathways is a static faction.

## 11. Gameplay Principles

1. The player can work for, depend on, reform, expose, leave, inherit, or destroy corporate institutions.
2. Corporate technology must obey physical and custody rules.
3. Hacking one terminal does not dissolve a civilization.
4. Liberation without replacement capacity can kill the people being liberated.
5. Workers are not interchangeable with executives.
6. Corporate citizens may sincerely love their society.
7. Public alternatives require training, parts, governance, and maintenance—not only moral correctness.
8. Corporate archives contain real evidence and real propaganda.
9. Corporate succession can produce reform, fragmentation, civil conflict, or unexpected civic renewal.
10. No corporation is universally present across every worldline.

## 12. Player-Facing Emotional Target

Corporate empires should create difficult recognition:

```text
this system is beautiful
this system works
this system saved people
this system should not own them
ending it carelessly may cause disaster
leaving it unchanged may make dependency permanent
```

The best corporate stories ask the player not merely whether the corporation is evil, but what must exist tomorrow morning if its pumps, clinics, relays, ships, archives, or habitats stop.
