# Naming Plurality, Local Register, and Linguistic History Atlas

## Purpose

Symtropy contains many evocative names. The problem is not that they are evocative. The problem appears when every institution, district, conflict, saint, vehicle, and criminal network arrives already polished into the name historians will use a century later.

People do not inhabit one canonical vocabulary.

A place may simultaneously have:

```text
an administrative name
an inherited local name
a corporate brand
a route-code abbreviation
a military grid designation
a schoolbook name
a tourist translation
a hostile exonym
a family nickname
a joke name
a name nobody uses anymore except legal software
```

This atlas adds linguistic depth without forcing every conversation to explain etymology.

## Core rule

> **A proper noun is evidence of who named something, when, for whom, and with what power.**

The neutral narration may use the most legible name, but world-facing content should expose competing names when they matter.

## Naming layers

### 1. Legal name

The name found in charters, contracts, customs records, maps, and court proceedings.

Legal names are often long, obsolete, politically compromised, or inherited from a predecessor that no longer exists.

Examples:

- Southern Estuary Flood Adaptation District Three;
- Ceres Transfer, Arbitration, and Ice Custody Authority;
- Kestrel Orbital Casualty Underwriting Cooperative, successor in interest;
- Autonomous Settlement Vehicle 04-11, provisional municipal hull;
- Aureline Municipal Continuity Group, Southern Grid Division.

### 2. Endonym

What residents call the place or group in ordinary speech.

Endonyms may differ by neighborhood, generation, language, class, and political allegiance.

### 3. Exonym

What outsiders call it.

Exonyms may be convenient, affectionate, inaccurate, commercialized, or insulting. A translated exonym may become more famous than the local name.

### 4. Occupational register

Operators, pilots, soldiers, medics, dockworkers, teachers, smugglers, and children shorten places differently.

Examples:

```text
pressure workers: Blue Six
freight crews: C-Port
schoolchildren: the Spinny Bit
insurance clerks: KHA-CE-14
salvagers: the Bone Market
older residents: New Pier, though it is 83 years old
```

### 5. Historical layer

Cities are renamed after revolutions, mergers, disasters, saints, executives, elections, reunifications, and embarrassing branding campaigns. Old people and old software often keep using the previous name.

### 6. Private name

Households, lovers, crews, and small communities invent names that never enter public records. These may be the emotionally most important names in the world.

### 7. Translation name

Nonhuman and multilingual names may be represented through several imperfect translations. The Field Deck must preserve the original signal or source expression where possible.

A translated poetic name may be a human interpretive convenience rather than the entity's self-description.

## Flagship place registers

### Nacre Delta

- **Legal:** Southern Estuary Flood Adaptation Districts 1–11.
- **Regional endonym:** Nakar, derived from an older river-market term whose exact origin is disputed.
- **Global media:** Nacre Delta.
- **Pump crews:** Three Walls.
- **Insurance systems:** Coastal Risk Zone S-44.
- **Teen slang:** Wet City, usually ironic.
- **Hostile inland exonym:** the Sump.
- **Older displaced families:** the Delta, with no adjective.

“Nacre Delta” began as an architecture magazine caption. It became internationally famous after the Black Monsoon Retreat. Many residents tolerate it because tourists can pronounce it.

### Aster Shade Republic

- **Constitutional:** Republic of Aster Canyon Municipalities.
- **Local:** Aster, Ashter, or simply the Canyon depending on dialect.
- **Mining records:** Excavation Cluster E-7.
- **Freight traffic:** ASCAN.
- **Youth sports chants:** Red Roof.
- **Property lobby branding:** Aster Heights.
- **Political insult:** Shade Hoarders.

“Aster Shade Republic” is the schoolbook form used outside Mars. Local legal advocates prefer “Aster Canyon Municipalities” because the shade commons predates the republic.

### Ceres Concordance Port

- **Legal:** Ceres Transfer, Arbitration, and Ice Custody Authority.
- **Dockworker usage:** C-Port.
- **Migrant usage:** Concordance, when emphasizing legal standing.
- **Route code:** CER-03.
- **Old Belt slang:** the Claim Pit.
- **Tourism brand:** Concordance Port.
- **Machine-witness register:** Custody Node Ceres/Primary/Current.

### Sargasso Ring

- **Construction registry:** Composite Habitat Assembly 7R.
- **Resident endonyms:** the Ring, Seven-R, Sarga, or district names.
- **Old industrial crews:** the Scrap Halo.
- **Memorial literature:** Sargasso Ring.
- **Children's rhyme:** Saggy Ring, a joke adults dislike.
- **Navigation:** SAR-7R.

The famous name came from a rescue pilot comparing drifting modules to a sea of trapped ships. Residents born there sometimes find the metaphor patronizing.

### Nine Pumps Commons

- **Legal:** Lower Basin Water and Power Cooperative Number Nine.
- **Local:** Nine Pumps.
- **Utility operators:** LB-9.
- **Campaign slogan:** Water Without Owners.
- **Corporate press:** the seized district.
- **Neighboring settlements:** Pump Town.
- **Children:** Niners.

“Commons” is a post-takeover constitutional word. Older invoices and several residents still say Aureline Branch 9.

### Palinode Deep

- **Legal:** Palinode Custody Arbitration Habitat, Deep Archive Division.
- **Residents:** the Deep.
- **Clerks:** PAD.
- **People contesting identity:** the Queue.
- **Tourist media:** Palinode Deep.
- **Hostile slang:** Copy Town.
- **Older maintenance maps:** Vault District 6.

### The Unfinished Sky

- **Hull designation:** Autonomous Settlement Vehicle 04-11.
- **Founding crews:** Four-Eleven.
- **Later residents:** Skyhold, Shipcity, or the Hull.
- **Religious usage:** the Unfinished Sky.
- **External diplomacy:** ASV 04-11 Municipal Commonwealth.
- **Mocking exonym:** the Ship That Quit.

The poetic name was originally a sermon title, not an official designation.

### Lumen Confluence

- **Municipal:** Lumen River Conurbation Authority.
- **Older local name:** Lumina, still common in household speech.
- **Festival brand:** Lumen Confluence.
- **Freight crews:** LC Junction.
- **Conservative congregations:** River City.
- **Critics:** Glowtown.
- **Teenagers:** Lume, pronounced differently by district.

The phrase “Eros Confluence” belongs primarily to tourism boards and several adult ritual institutions. Most residents do not use it to describe the entire city.

## Diaspora naming plurality

Diaspora names often differ between institutions and people.

### Monsoon Lantern Diaspora

The famous name comes from portable storm lamps used during the Black Monsoon Retreat. Many members call themselves Delta families, route families, or by town ancestry. Younger people sometimes shorten the public name to “Lanterns”; some elders consider that disrespectful. Successful shipping families have commercialized the lantern symbol, which poorer families resent.

### The Unverified

“Unverified” began as an administrative status. Activists reclaimed it, but many people living under uncertain identity status reject it as an identity altogether. Local terms include paperless, pending, continuance-disputed, the Queue, and specific family or route names.

### The Forkborn

“Forkborn” is useful to historians and insulting in some worldlines. People may instead identify by branch, origin worldline, household, or the place where their continuity was accepted.

### Company Afterlives

This is an academic term. Actual communities preserve old staff-club names, pension associations, former product identities, sports teams, branch numbers, and household nicknames.

## Personal names and titles

Historical titles should be treated as contested artifacts.

### “The Child Called June”

This is a media label created by the Lucent documentary *June Returned Twice*. The adult person used the private name **Jun Adebayo-Serrano** in protected records and publicly asked institutions to stop using childhood footage. Some legal histories retain “June Case” because it appears in precedent citations. NPCs who respect Jun should not address them as “the Child.”

### “Sister Mica-Blue”

Mica-Blue is a service name used in the Restored Breath order. She was born **Mikayla Blue Ndlovu**, worked for twelve years as a municipal respiratory technician, and joined the order after the Seed Famine. “Sister” is functional, not mystical. Old friends call her Kay. Court records call her M. B. Ndlovu. Children at one clinic called her Blue Auntie.

### “The Unknown Ward Mothers”

The phrase is a later memorial category combining several unrelated emergency-care networks. Some participants rejected the gendered term. Some politicians falsely claimed membership. Several documented Ward Mothers denied care to suspected saboteurs. The neutral system should refer to specific people when evidence permits.

### Nkiru Voss

“The Executive Who Nationalized Herself” is a famous headline from *Ceres Noon*. Contemporary legal records call the event the **Voss Asset Conversion and Municipal Transfer**. Voss herself hated the headline because she considered it unserious and because thousands of workers made the transfer possible.

## Naming generation constraints

Procedural or authored naming tools should preserve unevenness.

### Required fields for major entities

```text
canonical_reference_name
legal_names[]
local_names[]
external_names[]
historical_names[]
slang_names[]
name_origins[]
name_users[]
name_conflicts[]
translation_confidence
```

Not every entity needs every field. Missing names are more believable than mechanically complete taxonomies.

### Proper-noun density

A scene should not introduce more named institutions than the player can distinguish through function, people, and place.

Recommended soft limit for a first encounter:

```text
1 place name
1 institution name
1 person name
1 historical reference
```

Additional terms should emerge through use.

### Poetic-name allocation

Poetic names are strongest when their source is visible:

- a hymn;
- a media title;
- a tourism campaign;
- a survivor phrase;
- a hostile insult;
- a translated metaphor;
- an archive chapter;
- a memorial inscription.

They should not automatically be the administrative name.

### Bad names are allowed

Real institutions produce bad names:

- committee compromises;
- acronyms that accidentally resemble insults;
- failed rebrands;
- numbered districts;
- inherited company names;
- founders' initials;
- legal names nobody says aloud.

A universe where every name is beautiful feels designed. A universe with names people shorten, mock, mispronounce, and refuse feels inhabited.

## Player-facing use

The Field Deck may show a primary name with source-sensitive alternatives:

```text
Ceres Concordance Port
local: C-Port
legal: Ceres Transfer, Arbitration, and Ice Custody Authority
older route charts: Claim Pit
```

It should not flatten political disputes by declaring one name objectively correct.

NPC language should reflect relationship and context:

- an executive says “Aureline Southern Grid”;
- an operator says “Yellow Grid”;
- a disconnected resident says “Taplords”;
- a historian says “AMG successor utilities”;
- a child says “the warm pipes.”

## Closing principle

> **The same place should sound different in a contract, a love letter, a maintenance call, a school lesson, a joke, and an enemy broadcast.**
