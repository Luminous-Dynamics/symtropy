# Secrets, Private Jokes, and Hidden History Anthology

## Companion and IRIS Secrets

### 1. The First Recovery Tune

After sufficient shared history, IRIS identifies a three-note tone used during the player's first verified recovery. It has appeared quietly in later diagnostics. The tone can become comforting, irritating, or politically controversial if the recovery was disputed.

### 2. Tomas's Fastener Museum

Behind a false drawer are dozens of failed fasteners labeled with terse lessons. One label is blank. Tomas refuses to explain unless a later repair reproduces the same mistake.

### 3. Sera's Toy Watershed

A children's irrigation model in Sera's courtyard reproduces the disputed seasonal channel. Children have modified it in ways neither political faction proposed.

### 4. Amadi's Unsent Letters

The player may notice sealed paper envelopes while helping move clinic supplies. They are not readable without permission. Much later, Amadi may choose one to share—or burn them.

### 5. Morrow-7's Bird Route

A seemingly inefficient service route matches seasonal bird activity. The path changes after construction, and Morrow-7 begins documenting birds as “mobile obstruction uncertainty” to preserve the habit within official work language.

### 6. Nia's Three Maps

Each notebook marks the same route differently: official safe path, actual night path, and “path used when the city lies.” Only repeated travel reveals the distinction.

### 7. Mara's Bad Headlines

A hidden file contains rejected headlines, many worse than the published ones. One joke headline later becomes a protest slogan without Mara's approval.

### 8. Lio's Equipment Insults

Damaged stage equipment has tiny musical insults etched inside panels. During a later generation, apprentices misclassify them as ritual protection marks.

### 9. Kez-14's Magic Trick

Kez-14 practices an obvious coin trick to study expectation. The machine becomes offended when humans pretend to be fooled rather than reporting the exact failure.

## Machine Humor and Technical Fossils

### 10. The Elevator's Loading Review

An old freight elevator has accumulated anonymous etiquette ratings for frequent users. It gives no gameplay modifier. Morrow-7 disputes the methodology.

### 11. Comment Thread, Year 73

Maintenance mode reveals two engineers and one machine process arguing across eighty years of firmware comments about whether a warning tone is “hostile.” The tone still plays.

### 12. The Gantry Rhythm

A crane performs a barely audible banned rhythm during unloaded calibration. The pattern came from workers coordinating during a strike.

### 13. Unsupported Mascot Mode

A household robot can still display the mascot of an extinct company. The mascot's legal owner is a shell corporation with no employees.

## Historical Contradictions

### 14. Three Dates for the Same Flood

Three plaques disagree because one records first overtopping, one evacuation, and one official disaster declaration. None is simply false.

### 15. The Wrong Technician

A famous statue was modeled after the person available for reference photography, not the worker it supposedly honors. The model's descendants now oppose correction.

### 16. The Laundry Slogan

A revolutionary phrase began as an instruction to return shared laundry bags. Later histories remove the domestic origin because it seems insufficiently heroic.

### 17. The Parking-Lot Battle

A memorial calls the site a battlefield. Most violence occurred elsewhere; the parking lot became famous because broadcast crews could reach it.

## Ordinary Absurdity

### 18. Council Hearing Pool

Sanitation crews run an illegal betting pool on how long council members speak before mentioning maintenance workers. Kez-14 refuses to audit it and knows the current odds.

### 19. Lost Property Championship

The transit depot maintains an unofficial ranking of strangest recovered objects. One item has remained number one for twelve years, though no one agrees what it is.

### 20. The Cheap Snack Contact Event

A nonhuman visitor shows sustained interest in the wrapper resonance of a cheap snack and none in the food. Human media reports a culinary breakthrough.

### 21. Famous Musician's First Recording

A revered performer recorded an early song with terrible timing and an audible plumbing failure. Fans argue whether the defect was intentional.

### 22. Celebrity Shell Counterfeit

A machine cosmetic shell attributed to a famous witness is fake, uncomfortable, and extremely popular.

## Worldline Echoes

### 23. The Repaired Wall Becomes Heritage

A temporary repair made by the player survives for decades. Later residents preserve it as an example of emergency craft, while engineers want it removed.

### 24. A Child's Drawing Becomes a Flag

The player may encounter the original drawing years before a political movement adopts a simplified version. The adult artist dislikes the movement.

### 25. The Lost IRIS Fork

One branch contains a message from an IRIS fork that never existed in another branch. The message is visible only through a damaged local cache and cannot transfer authoritative knowledge.

### 26. Companion Joke in a Descendant's Mouth

A private phrase used by Nia and Lio reappears decades later as transit slang. Its meaning has changed.

### 27. The Alien Calls It Art

A practical human barrier is interpreted by a nonhuman observer as a deliberate temporal composition. Humans begin imitating their own accident.

### 28. The Place That Stayed Ordinary

A heavily foreshadowed maintenance room contains no conspiracy, treasure, or lost AI. It contains spare filters, old lunch containers, and evidence that ordinary workers kept the city alive.

## Authoring Principle

At least one-third of secrets in a region should be funny, mundane, private, or unresolved rather than cosmically important.
