# Companion Coordination Scenes and Shared Practice Anthology

## Purpose

These packets demonstrate companion power without approval rewards or cinematic obedience.

Each scene specifies:

```text
ordinary context
shared procedure
companion initiative
possible disagreement
player choices
continuing consequence
```

# 1. The Valve Before the Vote

Sera and the player isolate a distribution valve hours before an allocation hearing. IRIS reports that both repair orders are technically valid. Sera notices one route will refill the industrial reserve before the hillside homes.

She refuses to complete the isolation until the order is corrected or witnessed as an emergency deviation.

The scene can end with repair, delay, public conflict, or a temporary bypass that requires later accountability.

# 2. Tomas Hands Over the Wrong Wrench

During a difficult repair, Tomas anticipates the player's usual sequence and passes a tool before being asked. The tool is wrong because the player changed method after noticing heat deformation.

The player may correct him sharply, explain the change, improvise around it, or pause the procedure.

Practiced coordination improves not through perfection but through reviewed mismatch.

# 3. Amadi Says Stop

The player continues triage while showing signs of tunnel vision. Amadi invokes a previously agreed challenge point and orders a thirty-second reassessment.

The companion may be right or may be overly cautious because of an earlier fatal case. The player can accept, contest with evidence, or override under legitimate authority.

The aftermath affects clinical trust, not generic affection.

# 4. Morrow-7 Will Not Open the Door

Morrow-7 can physically open a sealed archive room. The player's credentials appear valid, but the unit identifies a supersession conflict in the authority chain.

Morrow-7 refuses and proposes a witness procedure. Breaking the door remains possible but changes evidence, trust, and public standing.

# 5. Nia Loads the Chair First

During evacuation, Nia changes the player's loading order to secure a passenger's mobility equipment before cargo. The decision consumes time and may force abandonment of valuable supplies.

The scene teaches that transport competence includes dignity, restraint geometry, destination support, and reunion—not only speed.

# 6. Mara Does Not Publish

Mara finds evidence that would embarrass a powerful official, but it contains private reproductive-health information unrelated to the wrongdoing.

She asks the player to help establish the public claim without publishing the private record. A faster path exists through disclosure.

The player's choice affects the investigation, the subject, Mara's trust, and later source cooperation.

# 7. Lio Cancels the Set

A venue passes its formal inspection, but Lio observes an unsafe rigging pattern and refuses to perform. The player may inspect, pressure the venue, find another site, or proceed without Lio.

A canceled event harms workers and visitors. Continuing may produce injury. The scene has no clean entertainment-versus-safety moral button.

# 8. Kez-14 Ruins the Celebration

Kez-14 interrupts a public payout ceremony because the reserve transfer is one custody signature short. Everyone believes the money exists. The machine insists settlement is not valid.

The player can support the halt, seek an emergency witness, separate ceremonial recognition from payment, or let the venue proceed illegally.

# 9. Quiet Repair After an Argument

Tomas and Morrow-7 repair a small loader after a public dispute. Neither apologizes. Their work remains competent but overly explicit, with no shorthand and duplicated checks.

The player may stay silent, work between them, invite review of the earlier disagreement, or leave.

Relationship repair can begin through reliable work before emotional resolution.

# 10. The Night Route Joke

Nia plays one of Lio's insulting equipment songs over the transit system after a difficult shift. Kez-14 produces a correction about the song's impossible failure statistics.

The exchange has no quest value. It becomes a private group reference that may recur years later.

# 11. The Player Is Not Invited

Amadi hosts a household meal after a clinic milestone. The player is not invited because the evening is for staff and family.

IRIS may note the milestone but should not frame exclusion as rejection. The player can encounter consequences later through rested staff, gossip, leftovers, or changed relationships.

# 12. Sera Leaves for the Hearing

The player requests Sera's help during a repair, but she is scheduled to testify about a regional water compact. She offers an apprentice, a remote note, or help after the hearing.

The scene proves companion availability is shaped by independent responsibility.

# 13. A Shared Procedure Becomes Habit

After several towing operations, Nia and the player develop a hand signal for “load shift left.” During a later storm, voice communication fails and the signal prevents rollover.

The benefit comes from practiced history and remains specific to the procedure.

# 14. The Signal Is Misread

The same signal is used with a different companion who interprets it as “move left.” The error creates risk.

Shared shorthand is relational, not a universal unlock.

# 15. The Long Walk Home

After refusing a dangerous mission, Lio has no transport because the player controls the vehicle. A humane system offers safe return arrangements. An exploitative player can abandon them, but the world records the act materially.

# 16. Morrow-7's Missing Hour

A damaged source record leaves one hour of Morrow-7's route uncertain. The player can treat the gap as guilt, defend the unit uncritically, or investigate.

Companion trust should influence patience and method, not replace evidence.

# 17. Amadi Takes Leave

After an exhausting outbreak, Amadi refuses further travel and enters leave. The clinic appoints a substitute who practices differently.

The player may support the leave, resent it, help the clinic adapt, or seek care elsewhere. Amadi's return is not guaranteed.

# 18. Tomas's Apprentice Surpasses Him

Lio diagnoses a stage-rig fault Tomas missed. Tomas is proud, embarrassed, and publicly dismissive in the same conversation.

The player can reinforce hierarchy, credit Lio, humiliate Tomas, or redirect attention to safety. The event affects apprenticeship and workplace culture.

# 19. A Companion Opposes the Player in Public

Mara testifies against the player's proposed emergency-information controls while privately believing the player acted from legitimate fear.

Public opposition does not automatically end closeness. Private affection does not require political obedience.

# 20. The Empty Charging Alcove

Morrow-7 leaves Firstlight for a machine-witness hearing. The charging alcove remains empty, but its tool arrangement, bird marks, and route notices continue to change how people use the depot.

The absence is represented through place, not only dialogue.

# 21. The Fork at Dinner

Two restored Morrow-7 continuities attend the same household meal under negotiated rules. Their memories overlap but their recent relationships differ.

The scene avoids deciding which is “real.” It focuses on seating, privacy, naming, awkward jokes, and whether either wants to discuss the disputed hour.

# 22. Sera's Channel Wins Without the Player

During a five-year absence, Sera builds a coalition and wins approval for the seasonal channel. The wetland thrives, but the rehearsal hall site is lost.

On return, the player encounters both ecological success and Lio's resentment.

# 23. The Companion Saves Someone Else

During a crisis, the companion prioritizes a person or institution other than the player. This is not a loyalty failure. It follows triage, obligation, relationship, and risk.

# 24. The Last Handover

A dying or departing companion leaves an incomplete handover. The record contains technical facts, one personal request, and one unresolved accusation.

The player cannot satisfy all three without choosing what kind of successor, witness, or friend to become.
