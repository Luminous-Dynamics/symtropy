# 1. Core Regional Thesis

The White Ledger Territories are about sanctuary becoming jurisdiction.

Antarctica was once politically defined by restraint: no ordinary ownership, no normal state sovereignty, no simple extraction frontier. By 2168, climate pressure, refugee crises, archive loss, geothermal opportunity, and planetary memory have made that restraint difficult to preserve.

The central question:

```text
Who may inherit a continent humanity once promised not to own?
```

Design rule:

```text
Every Antarctic settlement should feel like a legal exception trying not to become a colony.
```

Antarctica is not warm enough to become an ordinary open-air civilization.

It is more livable than before in some coastal and peninsula zones, but its durable civilizations remain:

```text
closed-loop
heated
engineered
geothermal
archive-centered
logistically fragile
politically sacred
```

It is Earth's closest thing to Mars while still being Earth.

---

# 2. Regional Tone

The White Ledger Territories should not feel like generic ice sci-fi.

They should feel like:

```text
silence under law
auroras over sealed habitats
old treaty flags behind pressure glass
ice-core testimony chambers
warm greenhouses beneath lethal cold
penguin colonies protected by armed non-ownership rangers
geothermal civic halls humming under blue ice
refugee queues at a continent that has no citizens
archive vaults where extinct states still have paperwork
```

The beauty is severe.

The horror is not cold alone.

The horror is that a sanctuary can become an exclusion regime without ever admitting it became a state.

# 7. Mechanical Modifiers

```yaml
mechanical_modifiers:
  water_logic:
    - water is abundant as ice but expensive as life-support water
    - melting, filtering, mineral balancing, and storing water requires heat and power
    - water contamination is rare but catastrophic in closed habitats
  power_logic:
    - geothermal nodes are strategic civic centers
    - wind and seasonal solar vary sharply
    - battery and heat storage determine settlement legitimacy
  food_logic:
    - most food comes from greenhouses, algae loops, imported stock, or closed-loop bioreactors
    - greenhouse allocation is political and emotional
  travel_logic:
    - travel requires weather windows, crevasse mapping, heat reserves, and rescue contracts
    - exterior exposure is a real mission timer
  access_logic:
    - entry requires treaty status, refuge hearing, research authorization, or emergency rescue token
    - informal settlement is possible but dangerous and legally explosive
  construction_logic:
    - every structure must prove non-ownership compliance or emergency exception status
    - exterior expansion creates ecological and treaty debt
  device_bus_logic:
    - older research stations may use machine-maintained protocols from dead agencies
    - high-trust habitats require redundant analog life-support controls
  field_deck_logic:
    - ARCHIVE and CIVIC modes are unusually rich
    - DIAG prioritizes heat, air, water loop, and seal integrity
    - NULL mode flags obsolete research protocols and exclusion loops
  social_logic:
    - trust depends on shelter discipline, heat fairness, and record honesty
    - legitimacy drops sharply after hidden expansion or unrecorded refugee denial
  ecology_logic:
    - wildlife corridors and protected breeding zones override many travel plans
    - restoration is limited; preservation often matters more than rewilding
  combat_logic:
    - gunfire inside pressure habitats risks catastrophic damage
    - exterior combat is dominated by exposure, visibility, rescue time, and equipment failure
  chronicle_logic:
    - every settlement exception can become planetary precedent
    - refugee denial, treaty alteration, archive tampering, and heat exile are high-significance events
```
