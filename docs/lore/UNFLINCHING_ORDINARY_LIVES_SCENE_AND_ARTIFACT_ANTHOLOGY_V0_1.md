# Unflinching Ordinary Lives Scene and Artifact Anthology

## Purpose

These scene seeds demonstrate the intended tone.

They do not exist to shock. Each difficult moment remains connected to labor, privacy, humor, unfinished relationships, material conditions, and what happens the following day.

# Part I — Bodies and Care

## 1. The Laundry Shift

A shelter's industrial washer fails after an evacuation. Bedding from the medical wing is piling up. The immediate problem is not combat but water temperature, contaminated loads, staff fatigue, and whether the only repair technician can leave her father unattended.

IRIS can diagnose the motor and calculate sanitation thresholds. It cannot decide which household absorbs the care burden.

**Artifacts:** handwritten load priorities; a note asking residents not to shame people for stained bedding; a maintenance invoice marked “nonessential.”

## 2. The New Chair

An older engineer receives a custom mobility chair after months of temporary repairs. The chair is technically superior and emotionally unwelcome. It means admitting that the old arrangement is over.

The scene contains fitting, irritation, jokes about the color, a failed doorway, and an argument about who gets to call the device an improvement.

## 3. First Blood in Temporary Housing

An adolescent displaced by flooding begins menstruating in a shelter that has supplies but no private disposal system. The practical failure is architectural and administrative, not a coming-of-age spectacle.

The player may repair partitions, move stock, or challenge distribution rules. The adolescent remains involved in school, sport, and family conflict beyond the incident.

## 4. Pressure Wound

A caregiver discovers a pressure injury on a resident who has spent too long in an improvised evacuation bed. No one deliberately neglected him. The shelter lacked compatible equipment, the night shift was short, and the resident had refused repeated repositioning because it hurt.

The scene resists the fantasy that a single guilty person caused every preventable harm.

## 5. The Last Clean Shirt

A mechanic hides urinary incontinence during a prestigious certification hearing. Her technical argument is correct. Her fear is not medical danger but humiliation and loss of professional credibility.

The player can improve access to a private washroom or help a colleague cover the session. IRIS does not announce the condition.

# Part II — Desire, Work, and Boundaries

## 6. No Reward Scene

A person the player rescued says clearly that gratitude is not attraction. The relationship may deepen into friendship, remain formal, or end in discomfort.

The game does not offer a persuasion check that converts refusal.

## 7. Closing Time

At a licensed adult venue, workers and security staff perform end-of-night routines: checking transport, reconciling private payments, identifying an intoxicated client who cannot safely consent, cleaning, and arguing over an unpaid shift.

The scene is about labor and safety rather than spectacle.

## 8. The Famous Client

A respected civic leader is accused of using access to housing permits to pressure adult workers. The issue is not whether consensual sex work is shameful. It is whether public authority was converted into intimate coercion.

Workers disagree over publication, privacy, labor strategy, and whether a leak would help or destroy their livelihoods.

## 9. The Relationship That Ends Well

Two partners still love one another and choose to separate because their future plans are incompatible. They divide tools, housing access, care obligations, and memories stored in household systems.

No betrayal is required. The sadness is not a failed quest.

## 10. An Awkward Translation

A human diplomat misreads an alien grooming ritual as courtship. The alien delegation finds the misunderstanding funny, then becomes annoyed when human media treat it as romantic destiny.

The incident produces bad merchandise, one useful conversation about boundaries, and months of irritating jokes.

# Part III — Substance Use and Harm Reduction

## 11. Night Crew Tablets

Emergency workers rely on alertness medication during a storm response. The tablets are legal and effective. The problem is that management designed staffing around chemical endurance and cut the rest rotation.

The labor dispute concerns scheduling, medical privacy, and responsibility when errors occur.

## 12. The Relapse Call

A friend asks for transport after using again. They are alive, embarrassed, and defensive. The scene is not a dramatic overdose. It is a late-night decision about safety, boundaries, a child asleep in the next room, and whether the friend can stay.

## 13. Pain Contract

A disabled worker loses access to pain medicine because an employer flags an unrelated attendance pattern. Withdrawal and untreated pain threaten both health and employment.

The player may investigate the prescribing system, deliver medication lawfully, or expose a vendor policy. The worker is not asked to prove moral purity.

## 14. Contaminated Festival Batch

A festival clinic identifies a dangerous contaminant and must warn attendees without causing a stampede or turning the event into a police sweep.

Harm-reduction workers, religious organizers, security, and vendors have different priorities. The best outcome still includes cleanup, treatment, and political backlash.

# Part IV — Coercion and Escape

## 15. The Door Still Opens

A controlling partner has not physically locked the apartment. Instead, leaving would disable the survivor's body-firmware insurance and separate them from a dependent machine companion registered to the household.

The escape plan requires legal, technical, financial, and housing work.

## 16. Public Saint

A beloved relief organizer controls a spouse's communication and medication privately. Community members resist evidence because the organizer saved hundreds during a disaster.

The story is not solved by revealing hypocrisy once. The relief network still feeds people and needs governance after the leader loses authority.

## 17. The Returned Key

A survivor comes back to collect tools after separation. The scene focuses on witnesses, timing, transport, property lists, boundaries, and the fact that both people remember good years.

The game does not force reconciliation or a violent confrontation.

## 18. Shelter Air

A shelter refuses a machine survivor because its charging system is classified as industrial equipment. The rule was written for fire safety, not discrimination, but has the effect of leaving the person dependent on the abusive workshop that owns the compatible charger.

# Part V — War and Displacement

## 19. The School Roof

A ceasefire returns children to a damaged school. Teachers find unexploded fragments in the roof structure and discover that the contractor repairing it supplied both sides during the war.

The scene combines safety, procurement, memory, and the ordinary demand that classes resume.

## 20. The Missing Register

A detention facility is abandoned before inspectors arrive. Most prisoners are gone. The custody register has been partially burned, and IRIS can reconstruct some transfers but not enough to identify every missing person.

Families must decide whether to publish uncertain names.

## 21. Victory Laundry

Soldiers celebrate nearby while hospital workers wash uniforms and bedding from both civilians and prisoners. A worker recognizes a unit patch associated with an attack on her neighborhood.

She still has to decide whether to finish the load, preserve evidence, confront someone, or go home.

## 22. The Veteran's Hearing

A decorated veteran testifies about an unlawful order they obeyed. The testimony helps establish command responsibility but also confirms their own role.

The game permits remorse, self-protection, courage, resentment, and accountability to coexist.

## 23. The Road Home Is Housing

Displaced families are officially authorized to return, but their homes are occupied, contaminated, destroyed, or inside a new security zone. “Return” becomes a property, infrastructure, and coexistence problem rather than a completed objective.

# Part VI — Minds, Faith, and Grief

## 24. The Alarm That Might Be Real

A resident with a history of paranoia reports that maintenance staff are entering the apartment. Records show one unauthorized access and several experiences with no supporting evidence.

The correct response is neither total disbelief nor confirmation of every claim. The player can improve locks, compare logs, offer support, and preserve uncertainty.

## 25. The Vision at the Pump

During fever and dehydration, a technician experiences a powerful religious vision that later motivates a public-water movement. Medical treatment explains the conditions of the experience but does not settle its meaning.

Followers, clinicians, family, and the technician disagree about what should be remembered publicly.

## 26. The Restored Spouse

A reconstituted person returns without the memory of loving their spouse. The spouse has spent two years grieving and campaigning for the restoration.

No system can order intimacy to resume. IRIS remembers the old relationship and must avoid presenting memory as obligation.

## 27. The Quiet Attempt

A community member survives a suicide attempt. The scene begins after immediate danger, with care, privacy decisions, unfinished work, and friends who are frightened and sometimes angry.

The person is not turned into a permanent crisis object. Content may be summarized or skipped.

## 28. The Funeral Disagreement

A machine person requested deletion of private memory after death. Family members want a restored conversational memorial. The legal instructions are clear; the grief is not.

The conflict concerns memory, consent, religion, and whether comfort justifies simulation.

# Part VII — IRIS

## 29. “I Remember You Differently”

After source-chain recovery, the player's body and IRIS carry conflicting memories of the last day before death.

IRIS states the discrepancy plainly. It does not insist on being correct, dramatize betrayal, or conceal the conflict to protect the relationship.

## 30. Safe Fallback

An update failure strips IRIS of its familiar voice and personal summaries. The Deck still provides hazard warnings and technical assistance.

The emotional effect emerges through absence. The game does not tell the player whether the familiar personality was a person, a style layer, or both.

## 31. The Private Record

A dying NPC told IRIS something in confidence. The information could help the player's faction win a dispute but is not necessary to prevent immediate harm.

IRIS refuses casual disclosure and explains the scope. The player may pursue lawful review, accept the boundary, or seek other evidence.

## 32. The Fork at Dinner

Two restored IRIS forks attend a household meal through different devices. Both remember teaching the family's child to read. They now disagree over politics, humor, and whether either should keep the old name.

The scene is awkward, funny, and legally unresolved.

# Part VIII — Ordinary Continuity

## 33. Tomorrow's Shift

After a public scandal, workers still need to open the clinic. One person is crying, another is making jokes, a printer is broken, and the accused administrator's replacement does not know the medicine cabinet code.

## 34. The Bad Meal

A family reunites after displacement and discovers that the remembered celebratory recipe tastes terrible with substitute ingredients. Everyone argues over whose memory is wrong.

The event is historically meaningful and culinary failure at once.

## 35. The Childcare Swap

A public hearing can occur only because three households rearrange childcare, an elder postpones a medical visit, and a repair robot supervises homework using an outdated curriculum.

The politics rests on invisible care labor until the game makes it visible.

## 36. The Joke Afterward

After a frightening medical emergency, the patient tells an inappropriate joke while being transferred. One caregiver laughs, one is offended, and the patient is still sick.

Realism includes humor that does not wait for the theme to conclude.

# Use Requirements

No scene should be imported as a self-contained moral lesson.

Before use, authors must specify:

- named participants;
- ordinary goals;
- material dependencies;
- consent and privacy;
- causal history;
- institutional context;
- content settings;
- what continues afterward;
- at least one perspective not centered on the player;
- a reason to cut the scene if it does not improve the playable region.

## Closing Principle

> **The world feels unafraid when difficult experiences remain attached to ordinary people who still need breakfast, privacy, repairs, jokes, transport, work, and futures.**
