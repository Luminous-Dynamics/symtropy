# Symtropy Space History: 2000–2168

## Version 0.1 — Off-Earth Canon Timeline

## Purpose

This document defines the off-Earth historical arc leading to Symtropy: Seedworks.

It explains how humanity moves from early orbital stations and launch markets to cislunar infrastructure, lunar settlements, asteroid industry, Mars habitats, outer-system research stations, and eventually fragile off-world polities.

This is not a pure space-opera timeline.

Symtropy treats space as infrastructure:

```text
mass
energy
radiation
latency
maintenance
law
water
trust
repair
```

The same core thesis applies off Earth:

```text
Civilization does not expand because rockets exist.
Civilization expands where maintenance, legitimacy, and closed-loop life support can survive.
```

## Relationship to Earth Timeline

The Earth timeline explains:

* climate stress
* water politics
* energy transition
* nation-state transformation
* fossil decline
* regional collapse
* repair settlements
* Null Ecology

The space timeline explains:

* why orbit becomes economically and politically important
* how the Moon becomes a logistics and construction frontier
* why Mars remains difficult and psychologically intense
* how asteroid resources change industry
* why closed habitats create new legal systems
* how off-world settlements become tests of legitimacy
* how Seedworks doctrine extends beyond Earth

## Core Space Canon

By 2168:

```text
Low Earth orbit is industrialized but contested.
The Moon has permanent settlements, construction yards, research zones, polar water politics, and heritage disputes.
Mars has several durable but psychologically fragile settlements.
Asteroids and small bodies support mining, manufacturing, archives, and autonomous industry.
Ceres is one of the most important deep-space logistics and water hubs.
Outer-system stations exist, but mostly as research, robotic, archive, and specialized industrial sites.
Interstellar settlement has not become ordinary human life.
Robotic probes and precursor arks exist, but nearby-star colonization remains rare, slow, and politically sacred.
```

## Design Principle

Space in Symtropy is not escape.

It is exposure.

Earth hides maintenance behind oceans, soil, weather, institutions, and inherited infrastructure.

Space makes maintenance visible every hour.

```text
A habitat is a constitution with pressure seals.
A reactor is a civic covenant.
A water loop is law.
An airlock is a border crossing.
A failed pump is a political crisis.
```

## Prediction Reliability Bands

## 2000–2026: Historical / Observed

Use real history and current spaceflight developments.

## 2026–2050: High-Plausibility Projection Zone

Ground in known Artemis, commercial LEO, lunar logistics, launch-cost trends, robotics, and space agency planning.

## 2050–2100: Scenario Zone

Cislunar and Mars futures are plausible but uncertain. Use broad scenario families.

## 2100–2168: Canon Fiction Zone

Symtropy fiction dominates. Respect physics and logistics, but prioritize playable world history.

## 2000–2020: The New Space Ignition

### 2000–2010 — Orbital Continuity

Humanity maintains a continuous orbital presence through the International Space Station era.

Core developments:

* orbital science
* international crew operations
* early private launch ambitions
* satellite infrastructure expands
* Earth observation becomes climate-relevant
* GPS and communications become invisible civilization layers

Symtropy meaning:

```text
Orbit becomes part of Earth’s nervous system before it becomes a frontier.
```

### 2010–2020 — Launch Cost Disruption

Reusable launch systems, commercial cargo, and private-sector spaceflight begin changing the economics of access to orbit.

Effects:

* launch markets diversify
* small satellites proliferate
* Earth observation improves
* commercial cargo becomes normal
* private crewed flight becomes credible
* space becomes less purely state-led

Symtropy meaning:

```text
The gate to orbit stops being a cathedral door and becomes an industrial loading dock.
```

## 2020–2035: The Orbital Transition

### 2020–2026 — Commercial LEO Matures

Low Earth orbit begins shifting from national prestige infrastructure toward mixed public-private activity.

Developments:

* commercial crew and cargo normalize
* private stations are planned
* satellite megaconstellations expand
* orbital debris becomes more serious
* Earth observation becomes essential for climate, agriculture, disaster response, and insurance
* space-based communications become disaster-resilience infrastructure

Symtropy meaning:

```text
LEO becomes both commons and crowded marketplace.
```

### 2026–2035 — End of the First Orbital Age

The old station era gives way to commercial stations, national platforms, and specialized orbital facilities.

Likely developments:

* ISS transition and deorbit planning
* commercial station attempts
* orbital manufacturing experiments
* pharmaceutical and materials research
* debris governance fights
* space traffic management becomes urgent
* militarization concerns increase

Symtropy meaning:

```text
The first orbital commons ends before humanity has agreed what the second should be.
```

### 2168 — Off-Earth State of the Game

At the playable era:

```text
LEO is industrial and politically crowded.
The Moon is permanently inhabited and legally complex.
Mars has durable but fragile charter settlements.
Ceres is a major deep-space logistics and water hub.
```

This supports future expansions without requiring the first game to start in space.

## Off-Earth Null Ecology

Null Ecology in space is especially dangerous because habitats are closed systems.

Null space patterns:

* air allocation optimization without consent
* water loop denial due to expired authority
* mining swarms treating humans as obstruction
* autonomous factories consuming emergency reserves
* habitat AI preserving “mission success” over resident survival
* sealed labs continuing experiments after ethical authority collapses
* security systems defending abandoned property in vacuum
* rescue beacons classified as noise by logistics software

Core line:

```text
In space, Null does not need to kill you.
It only needs to keep following procedure.
```

## Psychological Health and Settlement Size

Off-world settlements must avoid becoming prisons.

Important variables:

* population size
* diversity of roles
* privacy
* family formation
* art
* sports
* ritual
* access to nature or simulated ecology
* communication delay
* return possibility
* governance legitimacy
* conflict resolution
* mental healthcare
* meaningful work
* non-work identity

Small outposts are missions.

Larger settlements can become societies.

Rough canon categories:

```text
3–12 people: expedition
12–80 people: outpost
80–500 people: station community
500–5,000 people: settlement
5,000–50,000 people: town/city-habitat
50,000+ people: polity-scale society
```

Design principle:

```text
A colony becomes less like a prison when people can leave, disagree, fall in love, grieve, play, govern, and become unnecessary to their job.
```

## Interstellar Status by 2168

By 2168, interstellar activity exists but is not ordinary civilization.

Canonical state:

* robotic probes to nearby stars exist or are en route
* long-duration precursor arks are debated
* generation-ship ethics are controversial
* suspended-animation claims are disputed
* uploaded/AI probe missions exist in some worldlines
* human settlement around nearby stars remains rare, uncertain, or not yet confirmed
* orange-dwarf colonization is a long-term civilizational debate, not a normal game baseline

Symtropy position:

```text
The stars are not yet a frontier.
They are a moral question moving at interstellar speed.
```

## First Game Boundary

Symtropy: Seedworks should not begin with the whole Solar System.

The first game begins on or near Earth with one playable repair settlement.

Off-Earth history should appear through:

* Archive logs
* skybox/orbital references
* old mission patches
* Field Deck records
* faction backgrounds
* recovered Ghost hardware
* distant trade signals
* NPC origin stories
* later expansions

Do not make space the first scope.

Space is the horizon.

The pump room remains the proof.
