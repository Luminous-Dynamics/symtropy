# Firstlight 40-Hour Campaign and Ordinary-Life Anthology

## Purpose

This anthology provides authored material for the opening without turning Firstlight into a chain of scripted quests.

Each packet includes:

- trigger;
- people and places;
- physical activity;
- social or institutional context;
- absence path;
- consequence;
- ordinary-life continuation.

The packets can overlap, reorder, or remain unresolved.

# Part I — Arrival and First Usefulness

## Packet 01 — Rain in the Cabinet

**Window:** first ten minutes.

**Trigger:** transit crawler stops beside the Bent Pump House during storm damage.

**People:** Sera, Bram, passing residents.

**Physical work:** drainage, bracing, isolation, injury stabilization.

**Player choices:** prioritize cabinet, slope, Bram, or information.

**Consequence:** defines repair state, injury, later rumor and Sera's technical trust.

**Ordinary continuation:** Bram later argues about tea while pretending the hand does not hurt.

## Packet 02 — The Corridor Is Not a Workshop

**Window:** first hour.

**Trigger:** player leaves wet or hazardous equipment in residence access.

**People:** neighbor Laleh Morn, sanitation worker Jo Venn.

**Conflict:** personal convenience versus shared access and safety.

**Resolution:** move equipment, create drying space, argue, ignore, or ask why the rule exists.

**Consequence:** corridor use, neighbor familiarity, future evacuation access.

**Ordinary continuation:** children later use the approved drying frame to hang handmade storm kites.

## Packet 03 — Three Doors

**Window:** first hour.

The player may begin through maintenance, clinic, or public record work.

Each path reveals the other two professions as real dependencies rather than alternate classes.

No path closes the others.

## Packet 04 — Dinner Is Already Happening

**Window:** first evening.

The player arrives at the Commons kitchen after people have already begun eating.

Possible moments:

- no seat reserved;
- someone makes space;
- the player buys food;
- helps wash;
- overhears ordinary argument;
- leaves quickly;
- meets a resident who never becomes a campaign giver.

The purpose is belonging without ceremonial welcome.

# Part II — Work and Recurrence

## Packet 05 — The Pump That Passes

**Trigger:** a pump passes telemetry but a worker reports an unhealthy sound.

**Profession:** maintenance.

**Complication:** shutting it down disrupts clinic sterilization and garden flow.

**Truth:** bearing damage is real, but the worker also has a labor grievance.

Both can be true.

**Consequence:** repair quality, service schedule, worker trust, public narrative.

## Packet 06 — The Patient Who Leaves

**Trigger:** adult patient seeks care after a fall but becomes anxious about records and cost.

**Profession:** medicine.

**Player role:** explain, treat, adjust, provide transport, involve Yara, or let the person leave with bounded advice.

**Failure:** coercing treatment may stabilize the injury while damaging trust and legal standing.

**Ordinary continuation:** the patient later appears at the market using an improvised brace if support failed.

## Packet 07 — Yesterday's Fold

**Trigger:** an old-looking maintenance notice contains a recent fold and dust mismatch.

**Profession:** witness.

**Question:** was it planted, recently rediscovered, or routinely reused?

**Consequence:** reveals an archive-handling shortcut and a larger disputed claim without proving conspiracy.

## Packet 08 — Return the Crawler

**Trigger:** the player receives temporary vehicle access.

**Task:** deliver parts or patient support and return by shift change.

**Complications:** flooded route, optional rescue, cargo request, personal exploration.

**Consequence:** Nia's trust, next shift delay, shared-vehicle availability.

The player can keep the vehicle, but the world treats that as taking shared capacity rather than collecting loot.

## Packet 09 — Tool Library Argument

Tomas's hall has one calibrated torque tool and three crews needing it.

Options:

- reschedule;
- recalibrate another tool;
- improvise;
- claim emergency authority;
- ask a companion;
- perform lower-risk work first.

The scene teaches scarcity through work, not inventory menus.

# Part III — Danger and Aftermath

## Packet 10 — Bridge Seven

The service bridge partially fails.

Concurrent needs:

- trapped pedestrian;
- unstable crawler;
- energized rail;
- expired machine exclusion routine;
- possible scavenger intervention.

The player cannot perform every task alone.

Aftermath includes:

- route closure;
- clinic follow-up;
- cargo claims;
- public report;
- workers cleaning debris;
- a child asking when transit will return.

## Packet 11 — The Missing Tool Roll

After Bridge Seven, a medical roll is missing.

Possibilities:

- lost during rescue;
- taken for legitimate use;
- stolen;
- incorrectly checked in;
- still under debris.

The case can be trivial or become important if medicine later runs short.

Do not force a theft plot.

## Packet 12 — Cleanup Shift

No major reward.

Activities:

- sort debris;
- recover personal property;
- mark hazardous pieces;
- wash surfaces;
- record damage;
- share food;
- listen to people reinterpret the event.

This packet is optional but emotionally valuable.

## Packet 13 — The Hero Story

A public post credits the player for a rescue actually completed through several workers.

The player may:

- correct it;
- accept it;
- add names;
- remain silent;
- challenge why a machine worker was omitted;
- avoid public discussion.

Correction does not guarantee everyone believes the revised account.

# Part IV — Culture and Ordinary Pleasure

## Packet 14 — Bad Serial Night

Lio hosts a viewing of an infamously poor historical drama.

The player may:

- watch;
- mock it;
- defend it;
- repair the projector;
- leave during the second act;
- discover two rivals share sincere affection for it.

No campaign consequence is required.

## Packet 15 — Floodline Table

A regulated local card or probability game.

The player can learn:

- actual odds;
- local etiquette;
- who plays seriously;
- who is self-excluded;
- why Kez-14 dislikes one popular rule variant.

Winning money is possible but not the primary content value.

## Packet 16 — Wetland Morning

A quiet walk or work shift.

Possible activities:

- monitor water;
- watch birds;
- repair a fence;
- help a child identify insects;
- sit with Morrow-7;
- take no action.

The region needs scenes where attention itself is valid play.

## Packet 17 — Studio Wall

Lio's all-ages studio needs sound treatment, but residents object to lost storage and evening noise.

The player can help with materials and design, but adoption requires negotiation and continued management.

## Packet 18 — Laundry Notice

A sharply worded laundry-room notice becomes a joke, then a chant, then an accidental political slogan during the later crisis.

The player may never learn the full chain.

# Part V — Lower Works and Hidden History

## Packet 19 — Junction Nine

The player enters Lower Works through a profession-specific route.

Findings:

- current drainage function;
- unauthorized sleeping area or storage;
- obsolete corporate controls;
- conflicting maintenance dates;
- one harmless machine joke;
- one genuinely dangerous system.

## Packet 20 — Annex 4C

Competing claims involve:

- public utility access;
- corporate ownership;
- worker safety;
- behavioral data;
- private resident records;
- useful cooling equipment.

The player cannot responsibly “download everything.”

## Packet 21 — The Room With Nothing In It

A heavily sealed maintenance room contains filters, lunches, and an old heater.

It may still matter because workers used it privately during a prior emergency.

Not every secret is a conspiracy or loot cache.

## Packet 22 — Wrong Technician

The public statue depicts the wrong worker.

The actual history is complicated:

- the depicted person did contribute;
- another worker performed the famous act;
- the statue is now beloved for unrelated reasons;
- correction may erase a community meeting place or add context instead.

# Part VI — Festival Preparation

## Packet 23 — Load Sheet

Lio's venue, vendors, transit, clinic, sanitation and public utility submit incompatible festival demand estimates.

The player may help reconcile physical loads but cannot dictate cultural priority.

## Packet 24 — Consent Gate Inspection

Where adult or altered-state content is enabled, a venue's threshold system passes formal tests but workers report practical circumvention.

The player can inspect technology, labor practice, privacy and exit routes.

## Packet 25 — Quiet Room Argument

Some organizers want the quiet room converted into premium seating.

The conflict concerns revenue, accessibility, harm reduction and public pressure.

The player may influence but not own the venue decision.

## Packet 26 — Children Paint the Barrier

A temporary flood barrier becomes public art.

Later, responders hesitate to remove or damage it even when the barrier must move.

The art changes the emotional meaning of infrastructure without making it sacred by rule.

## Packet 27 — Sera Says No

Sera refuses a festival power arrangement that would create unrecorded risk for night crews.

The player can:

- support her;
- challenge the model;
- redesign demand;
- find another authority;
- proceed unofficially;
- help workers document the refusal.

High trust does not turn refusal into compliance.

# Part VII — Firstlight Night

## Packet 28 — The Lights Stay On

The event begins successfully if preparation supports it.

Players may:

- dance;
- perform;
- work;
- gamble;
- eat;
- patrol;
- help a companion;
- leave.

The crisis must not trigger immediately upon arrival. Let pleasure exist.

## Packet 29 — Three Failures at Once

Prepared causal combinations produce the crisis.

Possible elements:

- old bypass fails under load;
- false machine evacuation order;
- sabotage through Lower Works;
- contaminated product alert;
- transit bottleneck;
- structural fire;
- targeted evidence destruction.

The player selects a front.

## Packet 30 — Someone Else Saves Them

A person the player cannot reach is saved, lost, or stabilized by another companion or ordinary worker.

The world is not waiting for the protagonist.

## Packet 31 — The Route Through Music

Lio uses venue sound infrastructure to communicate evacuation rhythm or route changes when ordinary systems fail.

This works only if equipment and worker relationships were maintained.

## Packet 32 — Morrow-7's Refusal

Morrow-7 refuses an invalid emergency command while still providing another form of aid.

The player may initially experience the refusal as obstruction.

## Packet 33 — The Last Trip

Nia must decide whether to make one more transit run under worsening conditions.

The player can help evaluate route, passengers, vehicle, and alternate shelter. The decision remains Nia's within her authority.

# Part VIII — Aftermath, Hearing, and Continuity

## Packet 34 — Monday Morning

Required ordinary aftermath:

- cleanup;
- late school start;
- reopened food stall;
- tired workers;
- damaged music equipment;
- updated route board;
- no orchestral victory cue.

## Packet 35 — Whose Record

Mara has incomplete evidence. Public pressure demands a culprit.

The player may support a narrow claim, delay, disclose, redact, or challenge custody.

## Packet 36 — Work Refusal Hearing

A worker refusal may have prevented worse harm or contributed to service loss.

The hearing examines procedure, pressure, authority, and material conditions—not loyalty.

## Packet 37 — The Returned Worker

One worldline includes a worker or machine restored after death or presumed loss.

Questions include:

- identity;
- household standing;
- work credential;
- private memory;
- public testimony;
- whether the returned person wants involvement.

## Packet 38 — Repair Without Ceremony

The player can help restore a damaged service with no public event.

A later resident simply uses it.

## Packet 39 — The Song Changes

The festival song acquires a line about the crisis. Different groups disagree over whether the line honors workers, exploits grief, or is simply catchy.

The player does not own the interpretation.

# Part IX — Absence and Return

## Packet 40 — What Happened While You Were Gone

After a chosen in-world absence:

- Sera's overflow project advances or changes;
- Amadi's mobile clinic gains or loses support;
- Morrow-7's route access report affects policy;
- Nia's cooperative route reaches a vote;
- Mara publishes or withholds findings;
- Lio's studio changes use;
- one resident moves;
- one project fails quietly.

IRIS provides an optional bounded summary.

## Packet 41 — The Patch Has Aged

The opening repair now shows actual aging.

The player can:

- inspect;
- replace;
- learn another crew modified it;
- discover it failed;
- find it became standard practice;
- decide it was always temporary and accept replacement.

## Packet 42 — Your Room Was Used

Depending on absence, housing need and agreement, the player's room may:

- remain private;
- store emergency supplies;
- house someone temporarily;
- be reassigned lawfully;
- contain a message;
- show only dust.

The game must respect prior rules and not stage violation solely for drama.

## Packet 43 — Departure Platform

The player may prepare to leave Firstlight.

Possible reasons:

- orbital work;
- another region;
- profession opportunity;
- investigation;
- relationship;
- settlement founding;
- simple curiosity.

Leaving is not framed as betrayal.

## Packet 44 — Stay

The player may choose no grand departure.

Staying can mean:

- deepen profession;
- join public service;
- build a project;
- maintain relationships;
- explore remaining routes;
- live ordinary days.

# Anthology Composition Rules

The selected forty-hour worldline should include:

- at least 8 ordinary-life packets;
- at least 5 profession packets;
- at least 3 danger packets;
- 1 festival or cultural event;
- 1 hearing or institutional process;
- 1 continuity, grief, or death-related packet where configured;
- 1 absence and return;
- no more than 2 severe personal crises centered on the same companion;
- at least 1 event the player misses;
- at least 1 successful outcome without hidden punishment;
- at least 1 unresolved question.

## Anthology Maxim

> **The opening becomes memorable when repair, danger, laughter, work, disagreement, music, and absence all happen to the same places and people.**
