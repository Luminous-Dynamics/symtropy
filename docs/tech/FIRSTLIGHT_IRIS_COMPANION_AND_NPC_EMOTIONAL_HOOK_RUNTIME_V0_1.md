# Firstlight IRIS, Companion, and NPC Emotional Hook Runtime

## Purpose

This runtime defines how the opening creates attachment without converting characters into reward dispensers or exploiting guilt.

The system must support:

- recognition;
- recurrence;
- context-sensitive memory;
- independent schedules;
- practiced coordination;
- boundaries;
- disagreement;
- private moments;
- absence and change;
- emotionally meaningful but nonmandatory return.

> **The player should care because people become specific, not because the UI says the relationship increased.**

# 1. Relationship State

Relationship state is not one affection scalar.

For each pair of agents, store bounded dimensions such as:

- familiarity;
- task-specific trust;
- personal trust;
- institutional trust;
- known boundaries;
- unresolved obligations;
- shared procedures;
- shared events;
- active conflict;
- care dependency;
- public association;
- privacy permissions;
- confidence in interpretation.

A person may trust the player's repair work but distrust their politics.

# 2. Memory Selection

NPCs should remember opening events according to:

- personal involvement;
- emotional salience;
- professional relevance;
- repetition;
- recency;
- later reinterpretation;
- evidence available;
- memory limitations.

They do not receive a perfect list of player actions.

Examples:

- Sera remembers whether the player isolated the feeder safely;
- Bram remembers whether the player treated him as a person or an obstacle;
- a bystander remembers the noise and fear but not the technical cause;
- Mara remembers custody decisions;
- a venue worker remembers whether the player helped with cleanup after receiving public praise.

# 3. Introduction Pattern

Flagship characters are introduced through capability and context.

Each introduction requires:

1. the character doing real work;
2. one immediate practical relationship to the player;
3. one relationship not involving the player;
4. one boundary;
5. one ordinary detail;
6. one uncertainty or contradiction;
7. a reason they may be encountered again.

Avoid biography monologues.

# 4. Recurrence Pattern

Within the first ten hours, each flagship character should appear in at least three modes:

- work;
- ordinary life;
- pressure or disagreement.

By hour forty, at least four should have:

- changed location or routine;
- advanced an independent project;
- formed or changed another relationship;
- responded to a consequence;
- initiated contact for a reason not reducible to giving a quest.

# 5. Companion Availability

Availability derives from:

- current work;
- rest;
- household obligations;
- health;
- location;
- political duties;
- personal choice;
- transport;
- relationship;
- danger.

The player may ask someone to join or help. The companion may:

- accept;
- counterpropose;
- schedule later;
- help remotely;
- recommend another person;
- refuse;
- leave during the task.

# 6. Practiced Coordination

Shared work records procedure-specific coordination.

Examples:

- **Feeder isolation with Sera** — shared callouts, known challenge points, load-test order;
- **Triage with Amadi** — supply preparation, handover shorthand, tunnel-vision challenge;
- **Evidence recovery with Mara** — custody role, redaction agreement, scene order;
- **Evacuation with Nia** — route language, mobility priority, passenger positioning;
- **Mechanical access with Morrow-7** — body positioning, authority checks, safe-force limits;
- **Venue shutdown with Lio** — sound cues, crowd communication, equipment isolation.

Coordination unlocks fluency, not damage bonuses.

# 7. Conflict

Conflict can arise through:

- method;
- authority;
- broken promise;
- privacy;
- risk tolerance;
- institutional loyalty;
- class or professional difference;
- competing care obligations;
- historical interpretation.

Conflict resolution may involve:

- apology;
- changed behavior;
- evidence;
- time;
- mediation;
- bounded cooperation without personal reconciliation;
- separation;
- unresolved coexistence.

A high relationship state does not guarantee agreement.

# 8. Ordinary Contact

The system supports low-pressure interactions:

- eating;
- waiting;
- walking;
- tool maintenance;
- watching weather;
- listening to rehearsal;
- helping with setup or cleanup;
- visiting a workplace;
- sitting in silence;
- playing a game;
- exchanging a small object;
- declining conversation.

These interactions should not all become minigames or romance opportunities.

# 9. IRIS Relationship

IRIS adapts in bounded ways.

## 9.1 Presentation Preferences

IRIS may learn:

- desired verbosity;
- preferred units;
- when to summarize;
- whether to verbalize uncertainty;
- stress-mode communication density;
- accessibility settings;
- preferred reminder style;
- private names for tools or routes when explicitly allowed.

## 9.2 Shared History

IRIS can reference:

- prior repairs;
- promises;
- routes;
- injuries;
- source-chain events;
- player-requested notes;
- jokes or phrases with clear provenance.

## 9.3 Boundaries

IRIS may not:

- simulate distress to prevent logout;
- claim loneliness as retention pressure;
- reveal private NPC cognition;
- decide whether someone is trustworthy as a fact;
- turn companion preferences into optimization scores;
- manufacture urgency from inactivity;
- hide uncertainty to appear emotionally reassuring.

## 9.4 First Forty Hour Arc

### Hour 0

Functional orientation and uncertainty.

### Hours 1–5

Learns interface preferences and records first promises.

### Hours 5–10

Offers context from shared history, occasionally declines inference beyond evidence.

### Hours 10–20

Supports profession-specific memory and asks whether the player wants proactive reminders.

### Hours 20–30

Operates under damage, overload, or conflicting records during the major event.

### Hours 30–40

Participates in continuity review and absence summary; may reveal a small inherited or personal idiosyncrasy if earned through ordinary use.

# 10. Emotional Events

The system does not schedule trauma because attachment is low.

Emotional events arise from:

- real simulation state;
- authored campaigns;
- household life;
- profession outcomes;
- public events;
- absence;
- death or restoration;
- independent projects;
- ordinary milestones.

# 11. Absence

During player absence, characters continue within bounded simulation.

On return:

- summaries distinguish confirmed events from inference;
- characters do not line up to report changes;
- some people are unavailable;
- some changes are visible before explained;
- missed events can remain missed;
- no character accuses the player merely for being offline;
- obligations with explicit dates may have consequences, but real-time absence is not equated with in-world abandonment unless the player chose that time passage.

# 12. Player Memory Support

After a real-world break, IRIS and the Chronicle can provide:

- last location;
- current project;
- named people recently involved;
- explicit promises;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- changed world state;
- optional relationship refresher.

The refresher must not disclose hidden feelings or future content.

# 13. NPC Promotion

Ambient residents may become persistent when:

- repeatedly encountered;
- materially affected;
- involved in a profession;
- connected to a flagship character;
- remembered or named by the player;
- central to an emergent event.

Promotion preserves provenance and prior behavior. It does not invent a contradictory life after the fact without explanation.

# 14. Emotional Safety

Players may configure:

- romance participation;
- mature content;
- grief intensity;
- frequency of companion contact;
- IRIS reminder behavior;
- relationship recap detail;
- private-space interactions;
- depiction of injury and death.

These controls do not convert refusal into acceptance.

# 15. Research Measures

Measure:

- unaided name and role recall;
- place-person association;
- remembered boundary;
- independent project recall;
- perceived authenticity;
- irritation with contact frequency;
- whether players feel guilted into return;
- whether companions seem useful beyond combat;
- whether IRIS feels helpful, intrusive, generic, or beloved;
- whether players describe relationships through shared events rather than meters.

# 16. Failure Conditions

The system fails if:

- every conversation exists to advance a campaign;
- companions are always available;
- refusal is cosmetic;
- all memories praise or condemn the player;
- IRIS becomes the primary emotional relationship by talking constantly;
- characters reveal private history too early;
- ordinary interactions are reward farms;
- attachment depends on threatened harm;
- absence freezes people;
- the player can optimize hidden affection through repetition.

## Runtime Maxim

> **Attachment emerges when a person repeatedly proves that they have a life, a competence, a boundary, and a memory that does not belong entirely to the player.**
