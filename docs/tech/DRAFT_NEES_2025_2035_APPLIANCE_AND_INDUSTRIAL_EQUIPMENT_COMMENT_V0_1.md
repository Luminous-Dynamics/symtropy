# Draft Technical Comment on South Africa's National Energy Efficiency Strategy 2025–2035

**Status:** consultation draft for internal review; not submitted.  
**Prepared:** 21 November 2026  
**Comment deadline:** 30 December 2026 at 12:00 (Government public-comment listing for General Notice 4221).  
**Potential contributor:** Luminous Dynamics, LLC — confirm legal entity name, authority and any contact details before submission.  
**Scope:** household appliances, industrial equipment, testing infrastructure, measurement and verification, product data and manufacturing capability.

## 1. Purpose and overall position

Luminous Dynamics supports the draft National Energy Efficiency Strategy's direction to treat energy efficiency as a measurable resource for energy security, affordability, industrial competitiveness and resilience.

We recommend strengthening the implementation design around one principle:

> Efficiency improvements should be measured as improvements in useful service delivered per unit of energy and other resources, under transparent, reproducible and independently verifiable conditions.

An appliance consuming fewer kilowatt-hours while delivering less cleaning, cooling, hot water or useful work is not necessarily more efficient. A factory reporting lower energy consumption after reducing production or closing a production line has not necessarily improved its process efficiency. A pump operating at a high component efficiency while failing to deliver the required pressure and flow is not providing the intended service.

The strategy already recognizes the risk of inappropriate metrics and the need to improve physical-output-based sector indicators. We support that direction and recommend operationalizing it through product-specific test protocols, accredited measurement capacity, public data and independent measurement-and-verification (M&V).

This submission is a technical recommendation, not a claim that any proposed Luminous product, laboratory or service has been built, accredited, tested or qualified.

## 2. Priority recommendations

### Recommendation 1 — Build a national, product-specific accredited testing-capacity roadmap

**Relevant draft areas:** Part Three, indicators I and J (MEPS and labels); Strategic Goal 2; Objective 2.1; the implementation roadmap.

The draft identifies limited enforcement capacity and a lack of reliable accredited test laboratories for appliance MEPS verification. This is a foundational constraint: tightening a standard without a credible and accessible means of testing and market surveillance can create paper compliance without effective performance improvement.

We recommend a costed national roadmap that:

1. Maps existing South African laboratories, their ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation status, product scopes, methods, capacity, turnaround times and geographic access.
2. Identifies gaps by product family: refrigeration, washing machines, dishwashers, dryers, electric cooking, air conditioning, water heating, motors and other regulated equipment.
3. Establishes staged investments in reference instruments, calibration chains, fixtures, proficiency testing and qualified test personnel.
4. Uses inter-laboratory comparison and proficiency-testing exercises to detect systematic differences before results are used in regulation or public procurement.
5. Allows a transparent transition path using suitably accredited regional or international facilities where local capacity is not yet available, while building domestic capability.
6. Publishes the exact scope of each accepted test method and laboratory accreditation. A laboratory's general accreditation must not be treated as proof that every appliance type or method is within scope.

The roadmap should distinguish research rigs, manufacturer laboratories, accredited third-party laboratories and regulatory market-surveillance facilities. These serve related but different functions.

### Recommendation 2 — Update appliance MEPS and labels using useful-service denominators

**Relevant draft areas:** Indicator I (MEPS), indicator J (Energy Labelling Systems), Objective 2.1.

We support regular benchmarking and progressive strengthening of MEPS and the redesign of appliance labels. For washing machines, a specific transition issue merits attention: IEC 60456:2024 (Edition 6) is now the current IEC household performance method and adds/updates cleaning performance, rinse/LAS, textile gentleness, low-power modes, load temperature and other measures; IEC 60704-2-4:2025 updates the aligned noise test method. Public South African documents still expose legacy and differing references (including SANS 60456 / IEC 60456:2003 and SANS 1695 / EN 60456 in a 2018 UNDP/SABS tender). These records do not establish the present SANS adoption or the current accredited laboratory scope. We therefore recommend that the Department publish a product-class-by-product-class standards crosswalk (current SANS edition, IEC/ISO source edition, transition/equivalence status, test method and laboratory scope) before setting compliance deadlines against a revised test method.

We recommend that the revision process:

- Uses current international test methods and market data while confirming exact South African adoption, product scope and measurement conditions.
- Defines a functional unit for each product family, such as energy per standardized wash load, per accepted dishwasher load, per usable litre-day of refrigeration, or per unit of hot water delivered at a stated temperature and draw profile.
- Retains quality and service requirements as independent gates: cleaning and rinsing, drying endpoint, temperature stability, noise, capacity, cycle time and safety should not be sacrificed to achieve a lower energy number.
- Reports annualized energy estimates with transparent assumptions for duty cycles, ambient conditions, temperature settings, standby operation and capacity.
- Separates energy, water and other resource metrics where combining them could conceal trade-offs.
- Includes test uncertainty, tolerances, sampling and conformity assessment rules that can be implemented by laboratories and checked by market surveillance.

A redesigned consumer label should remain readable. We recommend a simple visual rating and a QR-linked machine-readable record containing the exact model identity, test standard and edition, measured performance, annual-use assumptions, water consumption where relevant, capacity, test date, issuing laboratory, certification or approval scope, and revision history.

We do **not** recommend placing an unvalidated composite repairability or sustainability score on the front label. Repair information, spare-part availability, service manuals, repair tiers, expected consumable replacement and end-of-support policy are useful and should be published in a separate detailed product-information record until metrics are validated for each product family.

### Recommendation 3 — Define industrial efficiency at the delivered-service boundary

**Relevant draft areas:** Industry, Objective 3.1.1 (industrial process heating, motors and compression optimisation programme); Objective 3.1.2 (energy management plans); Goal 5 (data and M&V).

Industrial equipment must be evaluated as part of the system in which it delivers service.

We recommend sector-specific measurement profiles, including:

| System | Suggested primary metric | Mandatory service-quality context |
|---|---|---|
| Pumping systems | kWh per m³ delivered at the declared delivery point | Flow, pressure/head, continuity, fluid, static head, duty cycle, storage and leakage |
| Compressed-air systems | kWh per Nm³ delivered at specified pressure and air-quality conditions | Pressure stability, moisture/contamination class, leakage, treatment, pressure drop and demand profile |
| Process heating | Primary energy per unit of useful heat and per accepted unit of production | Temperature grade, heat quality, throughput, product quality, heat recovery, storage and operating schedule |
| Motors and drives | Input energy per defined mechanical work/service over the operating profile | Load and speed distribution, drive losses, duty cycle and control strategy |
| Refrigeration/cooling | Energy per unit of maintained cooling service | Temperature band, ambient conditions, load, door/production cycles and thermal stability |

The pump-system assessment boundary should align with recognized whole-system practice, including ISO/ASME 14414:2019 where applicable. Pump component acceptance testing, such as ANSI/HI 14.6-2022, should not be confused with installed-system energy assessment. Similar distinctions should be maintained between motor nameplate ratings, package performance and the actual industrial service delivered.

For process heat, the programme should prioritize stream-level heat recovery and temperature-grade matching before sizing thermal storage or selecting heat pumps. Heat pumps, waste-heat recovery, electric boilers, direct electrification, hybrid systems and thermal storage should be compared under matched process output, temperature, schedule, energy-price and reliability conditions.

No single efficiency percentage should be applied across unlike sectors or operating conditions.

### Recommendation 4 — Protect physical-output-based metrics against economic and operating distortions

**Relevant draft areas:** Part Three discussion of metrics; Part Four sectoral targets; Goal 5 monitoring and evaluation.

We support the strategy's concern that energy-intensity indicators can be distorted by deindustrialisation, plant closures, loadshedding, shifts in production mix, behind-the-meter generation and changes in economic value.

We recommend:

1. Use physical-output indicators as the primary measure where a defensible unit exists (for example, GJ per tonne of a defined material grade, kWh per m³ of water delivered, or kWh per accepted product unit).
2. Retain GVA-based intensity as a contextual indicator, not the sole basis for claims of technical improvement in sectors where prices, inflation, exchange rates and product mix can change substantially.
3. Report output volume, operating hours, capacity utilization, quality/reject rates, shutdowns, fuel switching, self-generation and major process changes alongside energy intensity.
4. Preserve base-year data and document every baseline adjustment. A plant closure, production contraction or relocation must not be reported as an efficiency improvement.
5. Distinguish site energy use, purchased energy, on-site generation and useful energy delivered. Do not allow behind-the-meter solar or backup generation to disappear from the energy boundary.
6. Require uncertainty ranges and disclose missing data rather than treating missing observations as zero.

### Recommendation 5 — Establish independent M&V and persistence requirements for savings claims

**Relevant draft areas:** Objective 2.1, Objectives 3.1.1 and 3.1.2, Goal 5.

Efficiency projects should be assessed through a documented sequence:

- Define baseline and service boundary before the intervention.
- Record production/service output and relevant operating conditions.
- Identify meters, calibration status, data quality and uncertainty.
- Record the exact equipment, configuration, control settings and intervention.
- Measure post-intervention energy and service on a matched basis.
- Adjust for justified changes in operating hours, throughput, weather, product mix or service demand.
- Report failed, partial and interrupted measurements.
- Confirm that savings persist over a declared observation period.

We recommend that public incentives and energy-performance contracts distinguish forecast savings, independently measured savings and persistent savings. An engineering model or vendor estimate may support project screening but should not be reported as verified field savings.

The strategy should encourage measurement plans proportional to project risk and value, without making high-quality M&V prohibitively expensive for smaller enterprises.

### Recommendation 6 — Use public procurement to create demand for efficient, repairable equipment

**Relevant draft areas:** Indicator F (public-sector mandates); Objective 2.1; 2028 implementation milestone for procurement standards.

The draft identifies an absence of dedicated public procurement guidelines for energy-efficient products while recognizing the potential of life-cycle costing.

We recommend procurement guidance that evaluates:

- verified energy and water performance at the required service level;
- purchase, installation, energy, water, maintenance and end-of-life costs;
- warranty duration and scope;
- spare-parts availability, price transparency and delivery lead times;
- service documentation and access to repair information;
- expected maintenance, repair and replacement intervals;
- local service capability and technician training;
- measured performance under representative operating conditions.

Tender documents should identify the exact product class, capacity and duty profile; state accepted test methods and evidence; and avoid requiring a single brand or proprietary interface where a performance-based specification can achieve the objective.

A life-cycle cost comparison should disclose energy-price and usage assumptions and should not treat missing repair, service or end-of-life costs as zero.

### Recommendation 7 — Improve affordability and local service capability alongside efficiency

**Relevant draft areas:** residential water heating, low-income thermal retrofit pilots, financing strategy, skills and capacity-building.

Efficiency improvements are not accessible if the upfront price is unaffordable, a replacement part is unavailable, or a local technician cannot safely service the equipment.

We recommend that household programmes combine:
- targeted support for low-income households;
- safe installation and commissioning;
- efficient appliance and water-heating options at multiple price points;
- repair and maintenance services;
- transparent warranty and spare-parts support;
- training and tools for local technicians;
- independent post-installation verification on a risk-proportionate basis.

Programmes should compare replacement with repair where repair is safe and economically sensible. The aim should be durable access to useful household service, not simply the number of efficient products sold.

For industrial SMEs, the strategy's proposed audit co-funding and grant support should be paired with affordable metering, technical assistance, equipment leasing, performance contracting and practical access to qualified service providers.

### Recommendation 8 — Publish an anonymized, machine-readable product and programme performance dataset

**Relevant draft areas:** Objectives 5.3, 5.4 and 5.5 (data collection, annual reporting, national survey and repository).

We recommend a public dataset with model-level and programme-level records where commercially and legally permissible. Product records should preserve:

- manufacturer, brand, exact model/SKU and hardware/firmware revision;
- declared capacity and intended use;
- applicable MEPS and label specification;
- test-method edition, date, laboratory and scope;
- measured values and uncertainty;
- approval/LOA or conformity-evidence reference where applicable;
- sample size and sampling method;
- field energy-use observations where available;
- repair, parts and warranty information where available;
- superseded records and change history.

Industrial programme records should contain anonymized site and equipment classes, duty profiles, baseline method, output denominator, energy sources, intervention, measured savings range and persistence status.

Confidential process data, household information, security-sensitive site details and legitimate commercial secrets should be protected. Data release should use defined access tiers and aggregation thresholds rather than withholding all useful performance information.

Machine-readable data and stable identifiers would enable regulators, researchers, manufacturers, financiers and consumers to compare products and verify claims without repeatedly reconstructing inconsistent datasets.

## 3. Suggested implementation sequence

| Period | Suggested deliverable |
|---|---|
| 2026/27 | Publish testing-laboratory and accreditation-scope inventory; define priority product classes, standards and test-method gaps; establish industrial pilot measurement templates. |
| 2027/28 | Launch priority lab-capacity investments and proficiency testing; publish revised appliance label data fields; begin matched-service industrial audits and public procurement guidance. |
| 2028/29 | Expand laboratory/product coverage; publish machine-readable model and programme datasets; integrate verified savings into public programme reporting and energy planning. |
| 2029 onward | Review MEPS and labels on a scheduled basis using market, laboratory and field data; tighten requirements only where test capability, cost-effectiveness and enforcement are demonstrably ready. |

These are proposed implementation priorities for consideration, not claims about the Department's approved programme or budget.

## 4. Suggested indicators

We recommend monitoring:

1. Number of relevant product-specific test scopes available from ISO/IEC 17025-accredited laboratories.
2. Laboratory turnaround time, testing capacity and geographic access.
3. Inter-laboratory reproducibility for priority methods.
4. Share of regulated model families with current, scope-valid test evidence.
5. Market-surveillance sampling coverage and non-conformance disposition.
6. Household appliance energy and water per standardized useful-service unit.
7. Industrial energy per physical unit of accepted output by subsector.
8. Pumping energy per m³ at matched flow/head and service continuity.
9. Compressed-air energy per standardized delivered volume and pressure/quality.
10. Useful process heat per accepted unit of production by temperature grade.
11. Independently verified savings and the share still persistent after a defined follow-up.
12. Repair turnaround, parts availability and warranty/service performance for publicly supported products.

Indicators must be accompanied by data coverage, measurement uncertainty and exclusions. They should not become a single composite score that allows a safety, quality or service failure to disappear through averaging.

## 5. Requested clarifications in the final Strategy and implementation plan

We respectfully request clarification on:

- The exact product categories and technical schedules covered by the revised VC 9008 and the relationship between MEPS, product labels, SANS adoptions and international test methods.
- The funding, owners, governance and milestones for the accredited-laboratory roadmap.
- How the revised household appliance labels will expose product identity, standard edition, annual-use assumptions and source evidence.
- Which industrial equipment families will be included in the first measurement and optimisation networks.
- How physical-output metrics will be selected and normalized in sectors where product mix, grade and process route vary.
- The arrangements for independent verification of programme savings and persistence.
- The access model, privacy protections and publication schedule for the National Energy Data Repository.
- How small enterprises, repair businesses and local technicians can access training, testing and finance.

## 6. Conclusion

South Africa can improve appliance and industrial-equipment efficiency while strengthening its own engineering, manufacturing, testing and repair capability.

The most durable path is not a competition over labels or nameplate efficiencies. It is a transparent national system in which product requirements, test methods, accredited laboratories, equipment service data, independent measurement and verification, financing and local service capability reinforce one another.

We encourage the Department to make useful-service measurement, laboratory capability, transparent data and independent verification central implementation priorities. This would strengthen the credibility of energy savings, improve consumer protection, support local industry and make efficiency investments more bankable.
