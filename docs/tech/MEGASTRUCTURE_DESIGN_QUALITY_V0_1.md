# Megastructure Design Quality v0.1

**Status:** design-policy and benchmark specification; not a design certification  
**Parent:** [Symtropy #5384 — MEGASTRUCTURE-DESIGN-QUALITY-001](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/5384)  
**Related:** Symtropy #5280 / #5295 (rotating-habitat architecture and benchmark), Symthaea #12319 / PR #12322 (design intelligence ↔ qualified physics), Sol-Atlas #393 / PR #394 (spaceport ecosystem geography).

## 1. Governing principle

> **Every megastructure should be designed to be worthy of being lived with, worked in, encountered and inherited. Beauty is a design obligation; it is never a substitute for safety, function, rights, maintainability, ecological responsibility or evidence.**

This is a process and quality obligation, not a universal style prescription. Monumental, restrained, vernacular, organic, crystalline, industrial, civic, quiet, playful and other coherent architectural languages may all be appropriate. Affected communities and intended users must participate in defining the context and evaluating alternatives.

The principle applies to orbital rings and support nodes, rotating habitats, orbital shipyards, PORS terminals, launch facilities, planetary energy systems and other large, long-lived infrastructure.

## 2. Non-compensatory precedence

The review must preserve the following ordering:

1. **Hard constraints first.** Physical safety, structural integrity, operational safety, legal and community rights, accessibility obligations, environmental protection and applicable debris/end-of-life requirements are separate admission gates.
2. **Unknown is not pass.** An unknown, unsupported or unsubstantiated hard gate prevents the affected design from being treated as admissible.
3. **Design quality remains required.** Passing technical gates does not make a design complete if human experience, cultural context, beauty, repairability or ecological relationship have been ignored.
4. **No compensatory beauty score.** Aesthetic preference cannot compensate for a failed safety, rights, accessibility, environmental or maintenance gate. A high average preference cannot erase material dissent or a rights objection.
5. **Explicit deferral is possible.** Emergency or temporary infrastructure may defer non-critical aesthetic refinement, but the reason, duration, responsible owner, minimum human-dignity requirements and improvement path must be recorded. Deferral never waives safety or rights.

Do not combine hard gates and aesthetic dimensions in one weighted sum. Do not publish a single “beauty score” as if it were an objective physical property.

## 3. Design-quality dimensions

Assess each dimension separately and preserve its evidence, rationale and disagreement.

### 3.1 Formal coherence and legibility

- Coherent hierarchy, proportion, rhythm, massing, circulation and relationship between large-scale form and human-scale spaces.
- Comprehensible arrival, orientation, wayfinding and operational states.
- Structural systems, load paths, access zones and maintenance routes made legible where doing so improves understanding.
- Visual complexity may be intentional; accidental clutter, misleading cues and decoration that hides hazards are not quality.

### 3.2 Human experience and dignity

- Evaluate crew, residents, construction workers, maintainers, visitors and future users as distinct groups.
- Consider orientation, view, privacy, sensory comfort, lighting, glare, acoustics, accessibility, rest, social connection and emergency wayfinding.
- Review occupied and working spaces, not only exterior imagery.
- Include ordinary operations, isolation, degraded operation, emergency response and long-duration use.

### 3.3 Material honesty, craft and repairability

- Make material, joint, assembly and renewal logic understandable.
- Evaluate inspection access, tool clearance, replaceable modules, service routing, repair ergonomics, graceful aging and end-of-life disassembly.
- Record any mass, power, thermal, cost or maintenance burden added for visual effect.
- Avoid hidden cladding or fragile ornament that obscures inspection or introduces failure modes.

### 3.4 Ecological, planetary and orbital relationship

- Terrestrial projects: evaluate land, water, habitat, biodiversity, landscape, heritage, community impacts, cumulative burdens and the surrounding built environment.
- Orbital projects: evaluate debris generation, breakup prevention, servicing, end-of-mission disposition, glare and light spill where relevant, thermal/radiative effects and the effect on the night sky and planetary view.
- “Space is empty” is not an environmental assessment.
- Apply standards only within their stated scope. ISO 23312:2026 is a debris-mitigation standard for unmanned spacecraft in Earth orbit; it must not be casually described as a complete orbital-ring or megastructure standard.

### 3.5 Pluralism, place and cultural authorship

- Identify affected groups, intended users and relevant cultural/heritage authorities before visual review.
- Preserve preference distributions by group, minority reports, abstentions and reasons for disagreement.
- Do not infer consent from majority aesthetic preference.
- Support multiple architectural languages and local adaptation rather than forcing a universal Luminous style.
- Do not use “beautification” to justify displacement, exclusion, surveillance, cultural erasure or denial of access.

## 4. Required review pack

Each candidate must be reviewed using the same immutable design identity and matching version of geometry, mass properties, materials, systems layout and evidence.

Required views:

1. Planetary/orbital-scale silhouette and context.
2. Approach, arrival and departure sequences.
3. Human-scale occupied spaces and commons.
4. Workspaces, control rooms and service areas.
5. Inspection, maintenance, repair and replacement views.
6. Construction, assembly and deployment states.
7. Degraded, emergency and recovery states.
8. Day/night, lighting, glare and low-visibility conditions where relevant.
9. Accessible routes, evacuation and emergency egress.
10. Environmental, heritage, rights and infrastructure-dependency overlays where relevant.
11. End-of-life, disassembly and debris/disposition views for orbital assets.

A polished render is one view in this pack, not the review.

## 5. Versioned evidence contract

Each review package should bind:

- stable project, candidate and design-generation IDs;
- exact geometry/configuration identity and digest;
- requirements set and intended mission/use case;
- material/product/process state and provenance;
- exact mass, power, thermal, structural and maintenance baselines;
- review-view IDs and source design identity for every image/VR/mockup;
- affected and participating stakeholder groups;
- review protocol, accessibility and sampling method;
- hard-gate status, evidence class, source and accountable authority;
- dimension-specific observations, task results and dissent;
- design deltas attributable to aesthetic or experiential changes;
- unresolved assumptions, change triggers and claim ceiling.

A view generated from a stale geometry, missing assembly component or different material configuration must be labelled stale or rejected. Do not assume that an attractive rendering depicts the analysed candidate.

## 6. Benchmark method

### 6.1 Matched candidates

Compare design alternatives under identical functional requirements and declared fidelity. Keep the no-build or conventional baseline where relevant. If a design adds a decorative canopy, facade, window, garden, lighting feature or architectural volume, record its mass, power, thermal, cost, maintenance and environmental deltas.

Do not compare a detailed, rendered, human-reviewed candidate against a low-fidelity placeholder and call the preference result meaningful.

### 6.2 Human-centred evaluation

Use an iterative process combining CAD, VR, task simulation, physical mockups/prototypes and human-in-the-loop evaluation as appropriate to the design stage. Pre-register the questions and analysis plan for formal studies.

Report separately:
- preference distributions by declared stakeholder group;
- dissent, minority reports, abstentions and the reasons behind them;
- inter-rater agreement appropriate to the data type;
- wayfinding task completion, errors and time;
- accessibility task outcomes for relevant user needs;
- operational, inspection and maintenance task success, time and hazard exposure;
- visual comfort, glare and lighting outcomes;
- privacy, orientation, acoustic and sensory findings;
- ecological, heritage and rights evidence;
- lifecycle mass, power, thermal, cost, repair and end-of-life deltas.

Choose sample sizes and statistical methods from the study design and intended inference. Do not invent a universal participant count or preference threshold.

### 6.3 Non-aggregating report

The benchmark report must keep separate panels for:
- hard-gate disposition;
- dimension-by-dimension architectural critique;
- stakeholder preference distributions and dissent;
- human task performance;
- lifecycle and physical deltas;
- uncertainty, missing evidence and claim ceiling.

A Pareto view may be used for genuinely comparable quantitative objectives after hard gates. It must not convert rights, safety or cultural consent into exchangeable credits.

## 7. Adversarial synthetic benchmark

The frozen v0.1 corpus is defined in `megstructure-design-quality-cases-v0.1.json` and constrained by its JSON Schema.

It includes:
- visually polished but structurally unsafe candidate;
- majority-preferred candidate with unresolved rights and suppressed dissent;
- technically admissible candidate with design-quality review missing;
- pluralistic, context-aware candidate with complete review and preserved disagreement;
- visually expressive candidate with measurable mass/power/thermal burden requiring a trade study;
- emergency/temporary candidate with a time-limited and accountable design deferral;
- beautiful candidate with inaccessible maintenance and failed maintainability gate;
- candidate with unresolved ecological/night-sky impacts.

The corpus is synthetic policy-test data. It is not a preference survey, a real design ranking, or evidence that any Luminous design is beautiful.

Required policy outcomes:
- 100% of synthetic hard-gate failures and unknowns remain blocked from full admission.
- No preference distribution may override a rights or safety gate.
- No single aggregate beauty score is emitted.
- A complete design review requires all applicable views and explicit stakeholder/dissent handling.
- A technical pass with design review omitted remains incomplete.
- An emergency deferral must carry a reason, expiry/review date and improvement owner.

## 8. Industry and standards comparison

NASA's Center for Design and Space Architecture describes an iterative human-centred practice that combines CAD, virtual reality, full-scale mockups, prototyping, task simulation and crewed human-in-the-loop evaluation. Luminous should match that seriousness and extend the design thread so that all review views, physical model outputs, requirements and lifecycle deltas are bound to the same versioned design identity.

NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2 Revision F (approved 2026-07-14 and listed active) is a relevant human-factors, habitability and environmental-health reference for applicable human-rated space systems. It is not a universal aesthetic standard and applicability/tailoring must be determined for the mission and system.

ISO 23312:2026 (published 2026-10) addresses detailed debris mitigation requirements for unmanned spacecraft in Earth orbit. Apply it within scope and retain the broader debris, servicing and end-of-life issues for large infrastructure in the appropriate engineering assurance process.

Luminous's competitive opportunity is not to claim superior taste. It is to connect architectural quality, stakeholder evidence, physical trade-offs, repairability, rights and ecological impact in one reviewable engineering evidence package.

## 9. Ownership boundaries

- **Symtropy:** policy contract, non-aggregating review protocol, benchmark fixture integrity and lifecycle trade-off links. It does not become a renderer, universal architecture solver, human-factors authority or rights adjudicator.
- **Symthaea:** proposes design alternatives, identifies missing evidence and selects the next useful review/analysis. It may not self-certify aesthetic, physical or social claims.
- **Qualified engineering tools:** remain authoritative for structural, thermal, orbital, electrical and other physical calculations within their admitted scopes.
- **Sol-Atlas:** supplies read-only geography, heritage, environmental, infrastructure and community-context evidence; it does not silently convert a mapped layer into a rights or site approval.
- **Mycelix/evidence infrastructure:** preserves provenance, permissions and authorized decisions; a signed preference or source does not establish universal beauty or physical truth.

## 10. Claim ceiling

This document defines a pluralistic design obligation and a synthetic review benchmark. It does not certify an architectural design, establish a universal standard of beauty, replace professional architecture/engineering/human-factors review, prove community consent, or establish safety, sustainability or feasibility of any megastructure.

## 11. Source and standards anchors

These references inform process and applicable constraints; none defines universal beauty or substitutes for project-specific engineering and community review.

- NASA Center for Design and Space Architecture (CDSA): describes iterative human-centred space-architecture practice using CAD, virtual reality, full-scale mockups, prototyping, task simulation and crewed human-in-the-loop evaluation. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/jsc-human-factors-performance/
- NASA-STD-3001 Volume 2, Revision F, *Human Factors, Habitability, and Environmental Health*: active revision approved 2026-07-14. Determine mission applicability and tailoring; this is not an aesthetic standard. https://standards.nasa.gov/node/237
- ISO 23312:2026, *Space systems — Detailed space debris mitigation requirements for unmanned spacecraft in Earth orbit*: published 2026-10; apply within its stated unmanned-spacecraft/Earth-orbit scope, not as a complete megastructure standard. https://www.iso.org/standard/92666.html
- NASA-STD-7009B, *Standard for Models and Simulations*: use for model/simulation credibility and lifecycle assurance where applicable. https://standards.nasa.gov/node/263
- NASA JSC 65829 Rev B, *Loads and Structural Dynamics Requirements for Spaceflight Hardware*: applicable reference for dynamic load events, structural-dynamics analysis and modal-model correlation where the hardware and mission fall within scope. https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/JSC/JSC-65829

The benchmark should borrow the iterative, human-in-the-loop seriousness of the CDSA practice while making the exact design identity, engineering evidence, physical deltas, review protocol, stakeholder distribution and dissent reproducible.
