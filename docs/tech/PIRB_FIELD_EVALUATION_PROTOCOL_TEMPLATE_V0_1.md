# PIRB Field Evaluation Protocol Template v0.1

## Status and safety boundary

This is an **uncompleted protocol template**, not a study registration, ethical approval, deployment authorization, or claim that PIRB or any Luminous Dynamics project has improved peace or service outcomes. Do not fill it in from synthetic benchmark values. A real study requires legitimate local partners, affected-community participation, a context-specific conflict and protection analysis, independent review, and authority to stop or change the work.

The evaluator is not the source of community legitimacy. Participation, refusal, dissent, appeal, and withdrawal must remain meaningful. No person-level violence propensity score, coercive targeting, automated aid denial, or disclosure of sensitive testimony or vulnerable locations is within scope.

## 1. Governance and local ownership

- **Local convenor(s) and their authority:** [to be agreed and independently checked]
- **Affected communities and service users consulted:** [describe who was invited, who participated, and who may be missing]
- **Representation and dissent plan:** [include how minority views, non-participation, and challenges are recorded without exposure]
- **Independent ethics / protection / legal reviewer:** [not yet appointed until confirmed]
- **Complaint, appeal, safeguarding, and stop-work channels:** [roles, safe access, response commitments]
- **Data controller, data steward, and independent evaluator:** [separate responsibilities where practicable]
- **Review and renewal date:** [locally agreed]

Do not treat attendance, ledger entries, signatures, or silence as proof of free and informed consent or of legitimate representation.

## 2. Intervention hypothesis—not a global peace score

- **Problem as described by affected people:** [specific, bounded, contestable]
- **Population, service, place, and time window:** [define denominator and exclusions]
- **Intervention actually offered:** [what changes in practice; what remains unchanged]
- **Causal mechanism hypothesized:** [how the intervention could change the outcome]
- **Necessary conditions and dependencies:** [authority, resources, access, staffing, infrastructure, trust]
- **Alternative explanations and rival hypotheses:** [include policy, funding, weather, displacement, and other concurrent changes]
- **Foreseeable harms and misuse paths:** [including retaliation, exclusion, surveillance, capture, and benefit displacement]
- **What would disconfirm the hypothesis:** [observable failure or contradictory evidence]

Keep a chain of claims separate: commitment accepted → resources available → action performed → service/output observed → population outcome → causal contribution. Evidence for one stage does not automatically prove the next.

## 3. Outcomes and measurement plan

For every outcome, complete:

| Field | Pre-specified value |
|---|---|
| Outcome name and rationale | [locally agreed] |
| Primary / secondary / safety outcome | [classify before observing results] |
| Operational definition and unit | [include numerator, denominator, and direction] |
| Baseline and measurement window | [source, reference period, uncertainty] |
| Disaggregation plan | [only where lawful and safe; suppress identifying small cells] |
| Data source and custodian | [authority, access, provenance] |
| Measurement frequency and known lags | [specify] |
| Missingness / contradiction handling | [UNKNOWN is not zero or success] |
| Independent verification method | [who verifies, how conflicts are challenged] |
| Harm / displacement indicator | [neighboring groups, adjacent areas, shifted burdens] |
| Stop / redesign threshold | [locally agreed before intervention] |

Avoid a single aggregate “peace score.” Report distinct outcomes and who benefits, who bears costs, what is displaced, and whose experience is not measured. Essential-service improvements must be assessed against rights and minimum-service constraints rather than traded away for aggregate throughput.

## 4. Evaluation design and counterfactual

- **Primary estimand / question:** [what effect or contribution is to be estimated]
- **Target population and analysis unit:** [person-level data is not a default; prefer the least identifying defensible unit]
- **Intervention assignment / exposure mechanism:** [document non-random allocation and selection]
- **Comparison strategy:** [randomized, quasi-experimental, comparative time series, matched comparison, contribution analysis, qualitative process tracing, or other]
- **Why this design is ethically and contextually appropriate:** [include risks of withholding or delaying essential support]
- **Identification assumptions and how they will be tested:** [list, do not imply they are guaranteed]
- **Interference and spillover plan:** [services, people, resources, and violence risks may cross area boundaries]
- **Sample size / precision rationale:** [method, assumptions, and infeasibility if not estimable]
- **Missing data, attrition, and population movement:** [pre-specify analysis and limits]
- **Sensitivity and falsification checks:** [placebo outcomes, alternate windows, negative controls, rival explanations]
- **Independent analysis and decision on causal language:** [who can reject an unsupported claim]

Randomization is not automatically ethical or appropriate. Do not withhold urgent protection or minimum services to manufacture a control group. If a credible counterfactual cannot be established, report descriptive change or a bounded contribution assessment and say that causal impact is not identified. A before/after difference alone is insufficient where other factors or spillovers can explain the result.

## 5. Warning-to-response event protocol (when applicable)

Record only the minimal, safe evidence necessary to evaluate the process. Do not publish operationally sensitive routes, precise vulnerable-household locations, private testimony, or exploitable infrastructure weaknesses.

| Event | Minimum field to define before study |
|---|---|
| Warning issued | [trusted source, issue-time basis, uncertainty] |
| Warning received / acknowledged | [receiver role, receipt evidence, clock basis] |
| Authorized ownership accepted | [authority boundary and accountable role] |
| Resources confirmed | [what capacity was genuinely available and when] |
| Response dispatched | [event time and non-sensitive evidence reference] |
| Response arrived / access obtained | [event time, independent evidence plan] |
| Action completed | [service or protection output actually delivered] |
| Outcome independently verified | [independent verifier, evidence provenance, dispute process] |
| Adverse effect / non-response | [who records it, safe escalation, stop conditions] |

Pre-specify how lead-time uncertainty, response-latency bounds, resource failure, and safety reserves will be estimated locally. A synthetic buffer is not a policy threshold. Count dispatch, arrival, completed action, verified output, and impact separately. An actionable warning is not proof that a response occurred; a verified response is not proof that harm was prevented.

## 6. Data protection, access, and retention

- **Purpose limitation and lawful basis:** [review before collection]
- **Data minimization:** [justify every field; prefer aggregated service data]
- **Threat model and likely adversaries:** [include insider misuse, retaliation, re-identification, and coercive requests]
- **Access roles, encryption, and audit trail:** [specify]
- **Identity linkage:** [keep separately governed; never place direct identity, political affiliation, or precise vulnerable-household location in the shared benchmark record]
- **Retention / deletion / legal holds:** [time-bounded, justified, and communicated]
- **Small-cell suppression and safe publication review:** [method and responsible person]
- **Incident notification and harm response:** [workflow, responsibility, safe contact]
- **Community access, correction, and challenge rights:** [practical routes]

Do not collect sensitive information merely because a model could use it. A privacy-protective aggregate is still unsafe if its combination of time, geography, or rare attributes allows re-identification.

## 7. Pre-analysis, reporting, and independent challenge

Before looking at post-intervention outcomes, time-stamp and version the protocol containing: primary outcomes, exclusions, design/estimand, analysis plan, expected limitations, stopping criteria, and intended causal-claim ceiling. Preserve amendments with their reason, date, authority, and whether results had already been observed.

Publish or share safely with affected communities:

- null and adverse results as well as positive results;
- measured outcomes and uncertainty, participation/non-response limits, data gaps, and contradictory evidence;
- impacts on neighboring populations and possible burden displacement;
- the assumptions and sensitivity checks that change the conclusion;
- a plain-language summary and a way to challenge errors or request correction.

An independent evaluator should be able to reproduce aggregate calculations from governed records without being given unnecessary identities or access to private testimony. Suppress or delay disclosure when publication itself would expose people or critical services to risk, and document that limitation.

## 8. Go / revise / stop review

Do not proceed until every applicable item has an accountable owner:

- [ ] Affected-community review and legitimate local authority confirmed.
- [ ] Rights, protection, conflict sensitivity, data protection, and safeguarding reviewed.
- [ ] Outcomes, primary estimand, comparison strategy, and claim ceiling pre-specified.
- [ ] Essential-service floors and non-negotiable protections are explicit.
- [ ] Displacement, spillover, inequity, and adverse-event monitoring are operational.
- [ ] Safe complaint, appeal, withdrawal, and stop-work channels are tested.
- [ ] Data access, retention, incident response, and safe-publication rules are approved.
- [ ] Independent verification capacity and required resources are in place.
- [ ] Stop / redesign thresholds and authority to invoke them are documented.

Any missing safety-critical authority or evidence is UNKNOWN/BLOCKED, not PASS. Passing this checklist does not itself establish that the intervention is effective or ready for deployment.

## Research anchors

- UNDP Independent Evaluation Office, [Impact Evaluation Guidelines published](https://www.undp.org/evaluation/news/impact-evaluation-guidelines-published) (19 March 2026): choosing suitable methods and credible counterfactuals is central to causal claims.
- ODI, [Early warning and early response to violence for civilian safety and security](https://odi.org/en/publications/early-warning-and-early-response-to-violence-for-civilian-safety-and-security/) (16 October 2025): warns that response links are often overlooked and points to the value of community-led systems.
- UNDRR Regional Office for Africa, [Ensuring early warnings and early action reach fragile, and conflict-affected people](https://www.undrr.org/news/ensuring-early-warnings-and-early-action-reach-fragile-and-conflict-affected-people) (18 December 2025): documents gaps between warning systems and operational action in fragile contexts.
- Alliance for Peacebuilding, [Guiding Standards for Peacebuilding Design, Monitoring, and Evaluation](https://www.allianceforpeacebuilding.org/afp-publications/guiding-standards-for-peacebuilding) (March 2025): motivates explicit evaluation standards and accountability.
- ICRC, [Handbook on data protection in humanitarian action](https://www.icrc.org/en/data-protection-humanitarian-action-handbook) (2024): anchors data protection in dignity, safety, and humanitarian protection.

These sources motivate this template; they do not endorse PIRB, approve a particular study, or validate any Luminous Dynamics implementation.
