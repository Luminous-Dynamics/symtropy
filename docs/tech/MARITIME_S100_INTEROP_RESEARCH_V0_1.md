# Maritime interoperability profile research v0.1

**Status:** design research and profile mapping only. This document does not implement IHO S-100 parsing, certify any dataset, perform hydrographic calculations, or authorize navigation.

## Finding: use official product specifications as versioned adapters, not as a new internal ontology

The International Hydrographic Organization (IHO) S-100 family is a strong interoperability seam for the marine-freight work. Its current *Standards in Force* page lists the following product editions with entry into force on 1 January 2026:

| Product | Edition listed in force | Intended role in this architecture |
|---|---:|---|
| S-101 Electronic Navigational Chart | 2.0.0 | Chart-feature profile; source identity and applicable chart/update metadata must survive intake |
| S-102 Bathymetric Surface | 3.0.0 | Bathymetric coverage profile; preserve depth reference, uncertainty/quality information, coverage, and edition |
| S-104 Water Level Information for Surface Navigation | 2.0.0 | Water-level observations/predictions; preserve the vertical reference, validity interval, product/status and time semantics |
| S-111 Surface Currents | 2.0.0 | Current information; preserve direction/speed units, reference frame, spatial/time coverage and quality |
| S-124 Navigational Warnings | 2.0.0 | Warning records; preserve issuer, warning identifier, issue/revision and validity/withdrawal state |
| S-129 Under Keel Clearance Management | 2.0.0 | Interoperability profile relevant to UKC management; not itself evidence that a local clearance calculation is valid or authorized |

Sources: [IHO Standards in Force](https://iho.int/en/s-100-standards-in-force-0), [IHO S-101 to S-199 product specifications](https://iho.int/en/iho-s-101-to-s-199), and [S-100 developer resources and test data](https://iho-ohi.github.io/S100Resources/). Re-check the authoritative registry and exact edition at implementation/review time; do not treat this table as a hard-coded, permanently current registry.

**Critical distinction:** a dataset being public, having an S-100-shaped encoding, or conforming to a product schema does not prove publisher authenticity, currentness, correctness for a particular location, permission for a proposed use, or qualification for navigation. Likewise, general bathymetry used for planning/modeling must not be silently promoted into an official navigation source. NOAA NCEI explicitly distinguishes its Bathymetric Data Viewer planning/modeling data from data suitable for navigation: [NCEI Bathymetric Data Viewer](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/maps/bathymetry/).

## Source-discovery lanes (do not confuse discovery with operational authority)

- **US / NOAA:** CO-OPS is a candidate for water-level, prediction, current and station products; preserve its product-specific datum and preliminary/verified semantics. NCEI hydrographic/BAG products can carry depth and uncertainty grids, but NCEI's viewer explicitly limits its data to planning/modeling rather than navigation.
- **Europe / EMODnet:** its Bathymetry service provides a harmonized European-seas digital terrain model and access to underlying survey metadata. The exact access/permission differs: EMODnet says EU-produced data products are generally CC BY 4.0 unless otherwise indicated, but underlying third-party source datasets keep their own terms and many survey downloads require permission. Review record-level metadata and originator terms for each intended file: [EMODnet Bathymetry](https://emodnet.ec.europa.eu/en/bathymetry), [EMODnet terms](https://emodnet.ec.europa.eu/en/terms-use-emodnet-online-services-data-and-data-products).
- **Global ocean environment / Copernicus Marine:** its data store exposes global analysis/forecast products, including physics products with sea-surface height and velocity, and product-level quality information, metadata and dataset identifiers. Those are gridded analysis/forecast products—not a replacement for local surveyed bathymetry, official charts, or a qualified nearshore hydrodynamics model. Pin product/dataset identity, coverage, release, subset request, quality documentation and exact downloaded bytes: [Copernicus Marine Data Store](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/products), [Global Ocean Physics Analysis and Forecast](https://data.marine.copernicus.eu/product/GLOBAL_ANALYSISFORECAST_PHY_001_024/services).
- **South Africa / SANHO:** the South African Navy Hydrographic Office identifies itself as the national producer of paper charts, ENCs and navigation publications, and publishes maritime safety information/notices to mariners. Its terms explicitly protect Hydrographic Office products; official South African ENCs are distributed through authorized value-added resellers. Treat SANHO as the official-product/authority lead for South African waters, not as an assumed open-licence download. Record the exact edition, last-update information and acquisition rights before use: [SANHO](https://www.sanho.co.za/), [SANHO ENC ordering and catalogue](https://www.sanho.co.za/products/ordering_products_ENC.htm), [South African Hydrographic Act 35 of 2019](https://www.gov.za/documents/acts/hydrographic-act-35-2019-english-isixhosa-03-jun-2020).

These lanes form a source-discovery matrix, not a single “trusted source” ranking. A forecast, public research grid, general bathymetric model, navigation chart, station observation, and port-facility record carry different meanings and legal/access conditions. Do not blend them merely because their values have compatible units. Missing regional coverage is not evidence that a feature does not exist, and public visibility is not blanket permission to redistribute.

## Implementation status: separate metadata profile guard

The repository now has a separate [S-100 profile metadata schema](./MARITIME_S100_PROFILE_V0_1.schema.json) and a 20-case synthetic corpus at `crates/tools/maritime-data-receipt/tests/fixtures/s100-profile-corpus-v0.1.json`. The receipt crate exposes an offline `--validate-s100-profile <profile-json> <receipt-json> [artifact-override]` mode. It rejects profile/schema drift, unpinned editions/revisions, product-incompatible units, missing required datum/validity metadata, expired windows, malformed timestamps, unsupported product codes, unreviewed public-data terms, unreceipted transformations, and authority promotion. It cross-checks the profile's source kind, product/domain, dataset ID/revision, CRS, vertical reference, units, time coverage, receipt ID and receipt SHA against the exact generic receipt, then verifies that receipt's artifact bytes. Its output remains `NotAdmitted`.

This is only a profile metadata envelope; it is **not** an S-100/S-102/S-104/S-111 payload decoder, IHO validation engine, publisher-authentication mechanism, or physical qualification. The example bound S-104-like record and all profile-corpus values are synthetic. Source-specific product metadata still must be reviewed and tied to actual source artifacts. Exact-head CI must pass before treating even this metadata guard as software-qualified.

It lists current editions for S-100 and S-101/S-102/S-104/S-111/S-124/S-129, while some S-158 product-validation-check entries still show placeholder edition/date values on that page. Do not pin a placeholder such as `x.x.x` as a deployed validator: retrieve the exact published check document and edition, verify its release status, then pin it. These source lanes form a discovery matrix, not a single trust ranking. ## Macro trade and network-resilience data (separate from vessel-level evidence)

- **UNCTADstat Liner Shipping Connectivity Index (LSCI):** country-level, currently monthly and quarterly, assembled from scheduled ship calls per week, deployed container capacity, regular services, service providers, maximum ship size and direct country connectivity. The monthly dataset was updated through September 2026 in the Data Hub checked for this note. It is useful for baseline comparisons and network-shock scenarios, not an exact port-call feed or a real-time routing service. The methodology was revised/rescaled, including a 2024 rescaling, so each receipt needs the series name, temporal frequency, methodological vintage, revision/retrieval date and exact downloaded artifact. Sources: [UNCTAD LSCI metadata](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/datacentre/reportInfo/US.LSCI), [UNCTAD data updated 28 September 2026](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights/theme/246).
- **South African port connectivity discovery (secondary display, not a fixture):** a maritime data page citing UNCTADstat displayed Q3 2026 PLSCI values of Durban 230.1, Cape Town 186.7, Coega 105.8, Port Elizabeth 94.7, Richards Bay 17.3, and East London 10.8. The same display showed South Africa's Q3 2026 country LSCI as 114.7 (rank 46 of 178 economies with data). Treat these as source-discovery values only: the direct UNCTAD table export and exact source artifact were not captured or hashed during this work, so the values must not be promoted to a receipt-bound fixture yet. Source display: [South Africa maritime connectivity, citing UNCTADstat](https://www.elestrechodigital.com/en/connectivity/countries/sudafrica). The label “Coega” must not be silently equated to a specific terminal or to a differently named UN/LOCODE location such as “Port of Ngqura”; the exact source code, location release and relationship evidence must be resolved first.
- **UNCTADstat Port Liner Shipping Connectivity Index (PLSCI):** quarterly port-level connectivity for around 900 ports when observations are available. The published methodology uses shipping-line schedules; availability is not complete for every port/period. Use for comparative structural resilience and possible dependency concentration, not as evidence that a particular vessel called at a port, that berth capacity is presently available, or that a port is safe to access. Source: [UNCTAD PLSCI metadata](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/datacentre/reportInfo/US.PLSCI).
- **Latest published global comparison (discovery only):** UNCTADstat's public insight page, updated 28 September 2026, reports Q3 2026 PLSCI overview values of Shanghai 2,439; Ningbo-Zhoushan 2,110; Singapore 1,883; Busan 1,655; and Qingdao 1,422. The same overview identifies Egypt as Africa's highest LSCI-performing economy for Q3 2026. These are rounded insight-summary values rather than a captured raw series artifact; do not copy them into a verified fixture until the exact table export and matching metadata are captured and digested. This does not establish any specific South African port's index. Source: [UNCTAD maritime connectivity insights](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights/theme/271) and [country-level insight](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/insights/theme/246).
- **Location identity joins — UNECE UN/LOCODE:** UN/LOCODE is a five-character location code system maintained in biannual releases. Its function code "1" means a maritime port exists within the location boundaries; it does not mean that the code identifies one specific port terminal. The official latest release page currently lists production release 2025-1 (January 2025) and a continuously updated pre-release that may change before official publication. On 15 October 2026, an earlier indexed search result displayed 2 October as the pre-release update date, while a later live page fetch displayed 15 October. Treat that timestamp as mutable page metadata, not as an artifact version or capture receipt. For reproducible mappings, pin the official release/version, preserve change indicators and status/function codes, and review the exact download terms; do not use a mutable pre-release as a permanent identifier source. Most importantly, keep the mapping as a versioned relation (UN/LOCODE location ↔ source port-statistic entity ↔ map feature/terminal) with evidence and ambiguity state, not a fuzzy name join. Missing/ambiguous/multi-terminal matches stay unresolved. Sources: [UNECE UN/LOCODE](https://unece.org/trade/uncefact/unlocode), [official releases and pre-release status](https://unlocode.unece.org/publications/), [UN/LOCODE function-code definition](https://unlocode.unece.org/recommendation16/). The publication page's footer says UN/CEFACT standards are free under CC BY 4.0, while the generic site terms limit site materials to personal/non-commercial use unless a more specific licence applies. Before redistributing a copied release archive or derived code table, resolve and record the licence for that exact release/package rather than treating the web-page footer as a complete terms review: [UN/LOCODE terms](https://unlocode.unece.org/terms/).
- **UNCTAD container port throughput and seaborne cargo:** its annual container-throughput series is reported in TEUs, and the current Data Centre listings show 2010–2024 for the 2026 publication. UNCTAD notes that country totals can omit smaller ports and may carry repeated estimates where series have gaps; those estimates and coverage notes must not be silently treated as observed port-level counts. World seaborne trade by cargo type is another annual macro input. Sources: [container throughput metadata](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/datacentre/reportInfo/US.ContPortThroughput), [UNCTADstat Data Centre and release dates](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/datacentre/).
- **South African National Ports Authority monthly statistics (discovery lead):** the [Transnet National Ports Authority Port Statistics page](https://www.transnetnationalportsauthority.net/Commercial%20and%20Marketing/Pages/Port-Statistics.aspx) states that its cargo statistics are based on actual cargo dues invoiced and presents totals, TEUs and vessel arrivals. The indexed page exposed month/year aggregates through June 2025 and carries a disclaimer about inconsistencies and interpretation. The page could not be retrieved reliably for byte capture during this work, so this is not an ingested or verified dataset. Determine whether a field is national aggregate or port-specific, preserve the publication period and units, review the exact terms, and hash the exact export before using it. Do not infer individual port calls or terminal performance from a national aggregate.
- **UNCTADstat reuse terms:** the UNCTAD Data Hub's [About/Terms of use](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/EN/About.html) and [FAQ](https://unctadstat.unctad.org/EN/FAQ.html) say Data Hub materials are under CC BY 3.0 IGO and data/metadata may be copied and redistributed provided UNCTAD Data Hub is cited. This makes the listed LSCI/PLSCI/throughput series a practical first candidate for a public reproducible macro fixture. Preserve the source citation, table/series identifier, release date, methodology notes and retrieved artifact digest; UNCTAD also notes historical values can change after provider updates or methodology revisions. Check each table's metadata for special notes before freezing a release.
- **Global Fishing Watch (GFW) — only if use rights fit:** its API terms state noncommercial use only under CC BY-NC 4.0, with attribution and usage limits. That makes it inappropriate to build into a commercial Luminous Dynamics service unless the rights holder grants a suitable additional permission. Its data caveats also describe tracked “port visits” and other events as algorithmically inferred/apparent; AIS gaps, incomplete reception, misidentification and false events are possible. Do not turn a GFW port-visit label into proven port access, cargo delivery, violation, intent or an authoritative movement record. Sources: [GFW license/rate limits](https://globalfishingwatch.org/our-apis/documentation/docs/license-rate-limits), [GFW data caveats](https://api-doc.globalfishingwatch.org/our-apis/documentation/docs/v3/general-api-doc/data-caveats).

**How to use the macro layer:** construct a versioned port/country network with distinct observations for service connectivity, schedule-based capacity, estimated throughput, actual custodian-reported events, and scenario-generated disruptions. Keep those edge types separate. Correlated derived indicators from the same shipping schedules are not independent confirmations. A scenario may test a hypothetical loss of a hub or liner service against this structural baseline, but the result is an analytical what-if, not a prediction that a real service will fail. Record units, source revision, series methodology, coverage, estimate/imputation flags, retrieval time, allowed-use review, and a conservative claim ceiling on every input.

## Port-identity crosswalk guard

A separate [port crosswalk schema](./MARITIME_PORT_CROSSWALK_V0_1.schema.json) and synthetic adversarial corpus have been added to the Rust receipt crate. The crosswalk records a pinned production UN/LOCODE release, source-series snapshot metadata, explicit terms-review states, spatial entity granularity, candidate code/function/status, target feature, match method, evidence references and an explicit resolved/ambiguous/unresolved disposition. The offline `--validate-port-crosswalk` mode rejects mutable release/source revisions, malformed code metadata, duplicate source entities, name-only resolution, port-statistic-to-terminal joins and unresolved mappings that carry targets. Its output remains `NotAdmitted`; syntactically valid codes and digest-shaped fields do not authenticate a source or prove a geographic match.

The committed crosswalk fixture is synthetic and is a shape-test only. A real South African port crosswalk remains pending retrieval of the exact official production package and a source snapshot, followed by package-specific terms review and exact-byte digest capture. The browser was able to inspect official release metadata and directory listings, but the ZIP download endpoint was not retrievable in this run; no extracted release file has been substituted or fabricated.

## Recommended data path

1. **Discover** a source/product and retrieve its exact metadata, edition, producer/provider identity, spatial and temporal coverage, update/revision identifier, and use terms.
2. **Capture** original bytes without normalization. Record the exact request/source URI, retrieval time, response metadata and digest. A digest verifies byte identity only against bytes actually held.
3. **Record a receipt** using the existing Symtropy v0.1 provenance/byte-integrity contract. Keep its claim ceiling: the verifier is offline-only and always emits NotAdmitted.
4. **Validate a product profile** in a separate versioned adapter. S-100 product edition, S-100 edition, producer/code, dataset/cell identity, issue/update sequence, native encoding, validity/coverage and product-specific quality semantics belong in the profile, not in generic unit strings.
5. **Normalize only as a new artifact.** Each transform has its own input digest(s), tool/version, declared CRS/datum/unit operations, parameter set, output digest, and validation report. Preserve source bytes and unknown fields/qualifiers; never silently rewrite a datum, interpolate a missing field, or collapse preliminary and verified status.
6. **Apply a declared use/freshness policy.** Separate publication time, capture/retrieval time, observation/survey time, validity time, data coverage, update age and policy-evaluation time. A failed refresh, absent warning, missing tile, or expired update means unknown/stale/indeterminate—not clear water or zero risk.
7. **Admit to a scenario only under explicit capability/model gates.** The manifest must bind exact source receipt(s), product-profile version, transform receipts, model/capability census, scenario/configuration and output. S-129, S-102, or any other product cannot bypass Symtropy's model-credibility and independent-qualification gates.

## Cross-project ownership

- **Symtropy:** immutable input receipts, deterministic scenario manifest/replay, source-to-run binding and explicit Unsupported / Unknown outcomes. No new physical model is implied by ingesting a product.
- **Sol Atlas (sol-atlas-leptos):** renderer-neutral asset discovery, geospatial/temporal projection, visible uncertainty/status and click-through provenance. Rendering must not promote evidence or qualification.
- **Symthaea:** route/vessel/energy/terminal trade-off analysis only after inputs and supported model paths are admitted. Separate infeasibility constraints from Pareto objectives; do not let cost, throughput or a high confidence estimate compensate for a failed hard constraint.
- **Mycelix:** cargo, pack, provider, handoff and port-call event provenance using existing logistics and EPCIS/CBV interoperability work. Event integrity, custody interpretation, delivery acceptance, conformity and legal/operational authority remain separate.

Reference: [GS1 EPCIS/CBV](https://www.gs1.org/standards/epcis) defines interoperable supply-chain visibility events about what, when, where, why and how assets move; use it as an adapter rather than replacing Mycelix's existing evidence semantics.

## First safe vertical slice

Use frozen synthetic S-100-shaped metadata/fixture examples for S-102, S-104 and S-111 profile tests before attempting real payload parsing. Then select one small, explicitly permitted public example only after its source-specific licence, edition, coverage, quality metadata and redistribution constraints have been reviewed. Do not download or publish real facility-security data merely because it is accessible.

Required negative cases:

- mutable latest or missing product edition/update identity;
- valid digest but wrong/unavailable source artifact;
- S-104 water-level value missing vertical reference or validity semantics;
- S-102-like bathymetry missing depth reference, uncertainty/quality or coverage;
- S-111-like current missing units, direction/reference-frame or time semantics;
- S-124-like warning that is expired, withdrawn, superseded or from an unqualified source;
- two products share a URL but differ in edition/cell/update sequence;
- normalization changes CRS, datum, units, grid, or timestamps without a transform receipt;
- correlated provider feeds incorrectly counted as independent corroboration;
- missing/failed update rendered as zero, safe, clear, or current;
- schema-conforming data promoted to navigation clearance, seaworthiness, or engineering approval;
- renderer, Symthaea recommendation or Mycelix event mutates source/qualification status.

## Claim ceiling

Passing these checks can establish metadata/receipt/profile conformance for an exact artifact and ruleset. It cannot establish that a chart is current for voyage planning, bathymetry is accurate enough for clearance, a vessel is seaworthy, a berth is safe, a route is navigable, a forecast is physically correct, or a real-world operation is authorized. Operational navigation remains with the official products, approved equipment/procedures, competent operators and applicable authorities.

## Exact-byte verification improvement (15 October 2026)

The port crosswalk guard now has a second mode, `--verify-port-crosswalk-snapshots`, that accepts the crosswalk JSON and explicit local paths for the declared source and UN/LOCODE registry artifacts. Unlike the structural-only mode, this path computes SHA-256 over both supplied files and fails closed if either exact-byte digest differs from the crosswalk declaration. The report names the paths checked and indicates that digest equality was verified. The result still remains `NotAdmitted`: hash equality is not publisher authentication, licence clearance, freshness validation, semantic identity resolution, or operational authority.

The positive CI fixture uses two deliberately synthetic byte files with known digests. It does not contain real South African location mappings or a downloaded UN/LOCODE release. The next source-capture milestone remains a real, permitted UNCTAD data export and/or official port-statistics artifact, each preserved without normalization, hashed, and paired with exact-series metadata and a reviewed use decision. The official UNECE publication page is the release discovery source; the published production release and continuously changing pre-release must not be conflated: [UNECE UN/LOCODE Publications](https://unlocode.unece.org/publications/). The page's display update timestamp is not a substitute for retrieval-time metadata or a captured artifact digest.
