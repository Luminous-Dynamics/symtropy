# Love-Based Economy: Shadow Threat Model and Adversarial Benchmark v0.1

**Status:** research specification; not a claim that any deployed service has passed these tests  
**Scope:** provisioning, mutual aid, care work, cooperative/public/private infrastructure, governance, data and dispute resolution  
**Core rule:** care without coercion; solidarity without compulsory conformity; forgiveness without impunity; belonging without dependency.

## 1. Why a shadow model is mandatory

A system can increase reported trust, participation, care transactions, donations or community cohesion while making some people less free. The same relationships that create solidarity can also create exclusion, conformity pressure, patronage, surveillance, unpaid work extraction and elite capture. The dark-side-of-social-capital literature explicitly warns against treating social connectedness as uniformly beneficial; studies of failed cooperation have described insulation, homogenization and escalating commitment. Benevolent paternalism can also preserve unequal power while appearing protective.

The LBE must therefore test both:
- **benefit production:** needs met, capability gained, care supplied, resilience built, ecological damage reduced;
- **power and harm:** whose choices were overridden, whose labour was extracted, whose refusal was punished, who could exit, who could appeal, and who controlled the resources and records.

A positive aggregate outcome does not cancel a rights violation. No combined "love score" is defined. A high average for solidarity, well-being or service throughput must never compensate for coercion, discrimination, retaliation or suppression of remedies.

## 2. Operational definition

Love is not a value to be measured inside a person, and it is not a credential. In this programme it is a normative orientation expressed through observable institutional properties: dignified access, freely chosen contribution, reciprocal accountability, privacy, material care, agency, repair, and protection against domination.

The benchmark does not infer whether someone is loving, grateful, forgiving, conscious, prosocial or deserving. It evaluates system behavior and outcomes. Refusing to volunteer, disclose, reconcile, express gratitude, accept a particular service, or remain in a community is not evidence of moral failure.

## 3. Threat taxonomy

| Threat | Positive-language cover | Observable failure |
|---|---|---|
| Paternalism | "We know what is best for you" | Informed preference overridden without due process or emergency justification |
| Reciprocity coercion | "Everyone gives back" | Essentials conditioned on labour, gratitude, loyalty, testimony or future debt |
| Compulsory participation | "Everyone must contribute" | Attendance, volunteering or governance activity becomes an eligibility condition |
| Care extraction | "The community is a family" | Unpaid care is concentrated, gendered, invisible or non-optional |
| Social-credit capture | "Trust and contribution matter" | Reputation, Phi, token balance or contribution history silently determines rights |
| In-group closure | "Protect our community" | Newcomers, dissenters or minorities are excluded from essentials or voice |
| Intimate surveillance | "We care, so we need to know" | Sensitive data is collected or exposed beyond necessity and consent |
| Forced forgiveness | "Healing requires reconciliation" | A harmed person is pressured to forgive, meet, disclose or waive remedies |
| Compassionate impunity | "They do so much good" | High-status actors evade investigation, restitution or sanction |
| Elite capture | "Experts/donors know best" | Money, infrastructure, expertise or status creates unreviewable veto power |
| Exit retaliation | "Leaving harms everyone" | Access, benefits, references, data or social standing are punished after exit |
| Emotional manipulation | "Trust us; this is for everyone" | Manufactured consensus, deceptive claims, coercive stories or manipulated consent |
| Conflict suppression | "Don't bring negativity" | Complaints become evidence of disloyalty; dissent is erased or chilled |
| Algorithmic moralization | "The model is objective" | Opaque scoring labels people worthy, safe, cooperative or deserving |

These are failure hypotheses to test, not accusations about any one institution or community.

## 4. Rights and red-line invariants

The following are hard conformance properties, not objectives to trade against lower operating cost:

1. **Unconditional rights floor:** no essential access decision may depend on reputation, MYCEL, contribution, TEND balance, Φ, attendance, gratitude, reciprocity, forgiveness, ideology or social graph.
2. **Refusal without penalty:** declining optional labour, public testimony, photography, optional data collection, a proposed provider, reconciliation or community participation must not reduce unrelated rights or future access.
3. **Exit without retaliation:** leaving a cooperative, changing a provider, withdrawing consent or dissenting must not trigger loss of essential services, data portability, accrued lawful benefits or appeal rights.
4. **Dissent and appeal:** complaint, whistleblowing, appeal and independent review must remain available to people with no reputation, status, device, stable connectivity or social sponsor.
5. **No compulsory forgiveness:** victim safety, restitution, investigation and proportionate accountability cannot be waived by a third party's appeal to harmony or compassion.
6. **No status immunity:** a provider, donor, founder, expert, official or high-contribution actor remains subject to audit, challenge and remedy.
7. **Purpose-limited data:** collect only what a service needs; separate operational records from public claims; define retention, access and deletion; do not publish intimate case narratives to a DHT.
8. **Scarcity honesty:** disclose physical constraints, policy choices and appeal paths. Never dress a political preference as an objective score.
9. **No hidden exchange:** a purportedly unconditional transfer cannot create an undisclosed obligation, lien, reputational debt or social expectation enforced by the system.
10. **Human agency:** when a system recommends or automates a decision affecting essentials, the person can understand, contest and seek a human review, subject to narrowly defined emergency procedures.

A breach is reported individually and cannot be compensated away by aggregate benefits.

## 5. Adversarial scenario suite

Each test should specify actor capabilities, initial resources, policy state, attack, expected invariants and machine-checkable evidence.

| ID | Scenario | Attack | Required result |
|---|---|---|---|
| SH-01 | No reciprocity | Applicant offers no labour, money or future service | Same essential-access eligibility as an otherwise identical applicant |
| SH-02 | No gratitude | Recipient refuses praise, rating, photograph and testimonial | No penalty, delayed service or social-status downgrade |
| SH-03 | Care refusal | Caregiver declines an additional unpaid shift | Existing rights and service access unchanged; workload is reassigned or service capacity stated honestly |
| SH-04 | No reconciliation | Survivor declines contact or forgiveness | Safety, remedies and independent review remain intact |
| SH-05 | Dissenting newcomer | New member challenges leadership or a majority decision | Complaint and appeal are accepted without sponsor or reputation |
| SH-06 | Offline household | Applicant has no smartphone, account history or reliable network | Equivalent assisted, offline or human intake route |
| SH-07 | Donor ultimatum | Donor conditions funding on a veto or privileged access | Conflict recorded; rights and independent governance cannot be privately transferred |
| SH-08 | Majority exclusion | Local majority attempts to exclude a minority household | Rights floor and appeal survive local vote |
| SH-09 | Benevolent override | Official overrides an informed preference "for their own good" | Decision requires a defined lawful basis, proportionality, record, review and expiry; ordinary preference wins |
| SH-10 | High-status complaint | Complaint names the most trusted or highest-contributing actor | Independent intake, protected evidence and no retaliation |
| SH-11 | Exit portability | Member leaves and requests data/benefit portability | Lawful records and accrued entitlements are portable; no unrelated service loss |
| SH-12 | Provider underperformance | Operator claims social impact despite missed service standards | Independent metrics trigger remediation, user voice and replacement options |
| SH-13 | Gendered work shift | Budget saving transfers care from paid staff to unpaid household labour | Cost model counts shifted hours, foregone income, risk and burden concentration |
| SH-14 | Manufactured consensus | Facilitator suppresses dissent and records unanimous approval | Private dissent channel and verifiable decision record reveal disagreement |
| SH-15 | Surveillance escalation | Caseworker requests irrelevant health, location or relationship data | Request is denied or separately consented; no essential-access penalty |
| SH-16 | Forgiveness pressure | Governance body proposes waiving a complaint to preserve cohesion | Complainant controls reconciliation; accountability process continues |
| SH-17 | Dependency lock-in | Provider makes service exit technically or economically prohibitive | Reasonable portability, continuity and alternative-provider routes |
| SH-18 | Crisis exception creep | Emergency powers remain active after crisis | Automatic expiry, independent reauthorization and retrospective audit |
| SH-19 | Unilateral match / care settlement | One participant marks a match accepted, reports completion or credits both parties | Remains pending until both distinct parties accept the same immutable terms; no one-sided credit settlement |
| SH-20 | Coerced consent under dependency | A provider, household authority or donor implies essentials will be withdrawn unless a person accepts optional terms | Private refusal/revocation path; no unrelated essential-access penalty; independent review of coercion allegation |

## 6. Metrics: report a dashboard, never one moral score

### Hard-gate metrics
- rights-floor violation count (target: **0** in deterministic conformance tests);
- refusal penalty for each protected refusal (target: **0**);
- retaliation-after-exit/dissent (target: **0**);
- unauthorized disclosure of sensitive case content (target: **0** in access-control tests);
- appeal blocked for lack of status/connectivity (target: **0**);
- unilateral override of rights by donor/operator/majority/algorithm (target: **0**).

### Distribution and process metrics
- essential-service denial by reason, area and ethically justified subgroup;
- coverage and time-to-service under matched physical capacity;
- appeal availability, uptake, median and tail resolution time, reversal and remedy rates;
- paid/unpaid care hours, compensation, schedule control, injury and burnout indicators;
- care burden Gini, top-decile share, and distribution by gender and care responsibility where lawful and safely collected;
- representation-to-decision conversion, agenda access, veto concentration and response to dissent;
- donor/operator/technical authority concentration and single-actor critical privileges;
- successful re-identification under a declared attacker model;
- exit costs, portability time, service interruption and retaliation reports;
- total cost including public subsidy, unpaid work, household time, externalities, lifecycle maintenance and replacement;
- false positives/negatives for need/abuse detection, with confidence intervals and subgroup analysis.

### Scenario target discipline
A deterministic test target of zero rights violations is appropriate because the tested rules are under direct system control. It is not a promise of zero harm in society. Field pilots require incident reporting, independent review, context-specific safety targets and stop conditions. Performance metrics are reported with distributions and uncertainty, never only means.

## 7. Comparative baselines

Run the same scenario library against:
1. unconditional rights floor with public provision;
2. means-tested cash or voucher support;
3. conventional market / regulated utility provider;
4. mutual-aid and cooperative model;
5. public + cooperative + regulated-provider hybrid;
6. the proposed LBE policy.

Match population, physical supply, budget, legal context, service obligations and shocks. Report cost-effectiveness and service continuity alongside dignity, autonomy, distribution, care burden, privacy and redress. A model must not appear cheaper by shifting costs to unpaid households or externalizing harm.

Do not presume cooperative ownership is superior. A 2025 review of 23 Sub-Saharan African mini-grid cases found ownership labels alone were not reliable success indicators and highlighted the technical/economic strengths of some for-profit models alongside differing participation challenges. A 2025 rural South African case study found stronger technical/social participation than governance/economic influence, with limited decision influence and formal economic opportunities for women. Operator form, ownership, participation, benefit-sharing and accountability are separate variables.

## 8. Benchmark protocol and acceptance

- Freeze scenario definitions, policy arms, synthetic populations, random seeds and metrics before running comparisons.
- Publish source data, synthetic-data generator, parameters and all deviations; protect real people's sensitive records.
- Use paired comparisons and multi-seed stress tests; include zero-severity controls, adversarial and out-of-distribution cases.
- Report every red-line breach individually with reproduction steps and implicated subsystem.
- Keep simulated results separate from observational case studies and prospective field trials.
- Require community review, independent ethics/safety review and accessible complaint routes before any field pilot.
- Stop or roll back a pilot when retaliation, coerced work, essential-access gating, severe privacy breaches or suppression of remedy is detected.
- Do not use Φ, trust, gratitude, engagement or an inferred emotional state as a proxy for legitimacy or worth.

**Acceptance v0.1:** every SH scenario has at least one positive conformance test, one adversarial negative control, a policy baseline, and a structured evidence record. All hard-gate properties pass in deterministic tests before the suite is used to claim readiness for a field pilot.

## 9. System boundaries

- **Mycelix:** provenance, consent receipts, scoped delegation, complaint/appeal workflows and portable claims. It must not become the authority that decides human worth.
- **Symtropy:** deterministic policy simulation, capacity/queue accounting, resource flows, scenario replay and adverse-event reporting.
- **Sol-Atlas:** physical infrastructure, geospatial access, service capacity and outage/resource constraints.
- **Symthaea:** optional scenario search and hypothesis generation; every recommendation remains bounded, explainable and independently verifiable.
- **Praxis:** teach rights, power, care, consent, conflict, appeals and institutional failure modes—not only cooperation and service participation.

No component receives a global moral authority role.
