# MINE-SYSTEM-001 — Underground Mine Campaign Benchmark V0.1

**Status:** design/contract draft; synthetic specification only.  
**Tracking:** [Symtropy issue #1933](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/1933).  
**Claim ceiling:** this document does not qualify a mine plan, excavation solver, geotechnical assessment, energy architecture, reserve/resource, SMR deployment, or commercial business case.

## 1. Purpose

Compare end-to-end underground production campaigns under the same declared geological scenario, product requirements, resource budget and faults. The unit of success is **accepted product meeting specification**, not machine movement, tunnel metres, tonnes excavated or nominal utilization in isolation.

The benchmark composes existing domain owners. It does not create a second terrain/material authority, geology/GIS system, robot capability authority, procurement ledger, physics solver, mine-safety case or nuclear authority.

## 2. Frozen objective vector

Report each objective independently; do not collapse this set into a single default weighted score.

**Maximize**
- accepted saleable output meeting declared grade and quality specifications;
- recovered yield from qualified feedstock;
- verified service continuity during disturbances;
- productive equipment availability, subject to safety and quality constraints.

**Minimize**
- lifecycle cost per accepted saleable tonne;
- energy per accepted saleable tonne, with subsystem breakdown;
- freshwater withdrawal and treatment burden per accepted saleable tonne;
- lifecycle emissions and uncredited waste/residue burden where evidence supports calculation;
- unplanned downtime, repair delay and worker exposure to hazardous tasks;
- uncertainty that is decision-relevant and can actually be reduced by additional measurements.

Every candidate must retain hard feasibility constraints separately from optimization objectives. Report the Pareto frontier and reasons for ineligibility. There is no implicit exchange rate between worker safety, cost, water, energy, or product quality.

## 3. Evidence and model tiers

Each input, parameter, observable, result and claim has an explicit evidence class:

| Tier | Meaning | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|
| E0 | Synthetic fixture / generated scenario | Contract behavior on the declared fixture only |
| E1 | Public or partner field/laboratory observation with provenance and units | What that observation measured, within its sampling and applicability limits |
| E2 | Model calibrated against identified evidence | Prediction within the declared calibration and applicability envelope |
| E3 | Evaluation on held-out evidence not used for tuning | Performance on that specific held-out corpus |
| E4 | Independent hardware-in-the-loop or bounded field evaluation | Performance only in the tested configuration and operating envelope |

The tiers are not automatic promotions. A digital twin that runs, converges, or replays deterministically is not thereby physically validated. Synthetic data generated by the same model under test is not independent calibration or validation evidence.

Every evidence record should preserve source/provider and license, source version/date, collection and valid time where applicable, units, coordinate frame, coverage, uncertainty, calibration/processing lineage, model/code version, digest and limitations. Missing, stale, conflicting or out-of-domain inputs remain unresolved—not zero, false, safe, or favorable.

## 4. Campaign boundary and conservation

A benchmark campaign may include:

1. characterized site/geology and uncertainty;
2. excavation tool/material interaction and ground support;
3. loading, haulage, hoisting and spoil movement;
4. ventilation, thermal control, pumping/dewatering, communications and egress requirements;
5. crushing, beneficiation and downstream processing;
6. consumables, repair, inspection, spares and equipment lifecycle;
7. energy supply, conversion, storage, distribution and demand;
8. recovered products, coproducts, tailings, wastes and closure/rehabilitation liabilities.

Every included stage declares its input/output boundary. Omitting a real demand is allowed only when the benchmark labels it as omitted and prevents full-system cost/energy claims.

### 4.1 Material accounting

For each declared material stream and time interval:

    opening_inventory + admitted_inputs
      = accepted_products + explicit_residues + closing_inventory + measured_losses

The residual must be emitted with units and tolerance. Do not combine unlike materials just because a friendly label is the same. Solid removed volume and loose-spoil volume are distinct; any swell/fragmentation relation requires its own declared model/profile. Recovered coproducts are not saleable products unless their independent grade, yield and downstream qualification are represented.

### 4.2 Energy accounting

For each declared energy carrier and time interval, report:

    opening_storage + supplied_energy + recovered_energy
      = cutting + traction/haulage + hoisting + ventilation/cooling
      + pumping + processing + auxiliaries + explicit_losses
      + closing_storage

Use consistent boundaries and units. Do not add electrical, chemical and thermal energy without an explicit conversion/basis. Separate generation-side energy from energy delivered to each load; include distribution and conversion losses where the claim requires them. A balance that cannot close from available evidence is Incomplete, not an inferred pass.

### 4.3 Resource and output identity

Preserve these non-equivalences:

    occurrence != characterized deposit != recoverable resource
      != acquired lot != qualified feedstock != accepted product

Likewise:

    excavated mass != processed mass != recovered constituent
      != specification-grade saleable product

A source grade is local evidence; it is not automatically a spatially uniform property for an entire deposit or campaign. Unknown coverage and sample representativeness must remain visible.

## 5. Energy architecture comparison

Treat an energy architecture as a declared supplier-and-delivery chain with explicit capacity, availability, ramp/transient behavior, storage, distribution, maintenance, cost basis, emissions basis and failure modes.

Potential candidate classes for the same campaign load trace:

- diesel-powered reference where relevant;
- grid-fed electrical distribution;
- battery-electric equipment with declared charging/swap logistics;
- trolley/cable-assisted haulage or wired excavation equipment where physically applicable;
- hybrids with bounded storage buffers;
- a stationary, externally licensed SMR feeding a declared site microgrid.

The SMR candidate is **not a reactor design and not a reactor installed on a moving TBM**. It is an opaque facility-side energy-source interface composed with civil/site and balance-of-plant evidence. Do not locally invent reactor protection requirements, safety-system behavior, operating setpoints, licensing status or reactor dynamics. Missing site, regulatory, supplier or interface evidence blocks the corresponding feasibility claim.

Compare architectures against the same load trace and accepted-output requirement. Count auxiliary and common services, include peak-power/transient needs, distribution losses, energy buffers, unavailability and common-mode failures. Low energy price alone does not establish the cheapest lifecycle architecture.

### 5.1 Thermal symbiosis and heat-grade matching

The campaign should distinguish unwanted heat that must be rejected to maintain safe working conditions from heat that can be recovered, stored or delivered as a useful service. The initial benchmark may declare these quantities and test accounting; it must not assume a generic recoverable heat fraction.

Compose the existing [process-stream heat integration work](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/1899), [multi-period thermal integration work](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/1916), Symthaea's [mine geothermal evidence program](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/issues/6147), and general [symbiotic campus profile](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symthaea/issues/6146), rather than creating another thermal solver.

Candidate sources/sinks include rock and mine-water heat, equipment/process waste heat, cooling loads, ventilation air, surface buildings, water treatment, and seasonal thermal storage. Each source/sink needs temperature grade, flow/capacity, availability schedule, transfer loss, parasitic load, measurement/evidence status and a declared delivery boundary. A thermodynamic quantity is not useful service unless its temperature and timing satisfy a real demand.

Compare:
- safe heat rejection / conventional refrigeration baseline;
- heat-pump recovery where source/sink temperature and flow evidence supports it;
- heat recovery to facility or process loads with explicit exchanger approach and parasitic power;
- bounded thermal storage for mismatched supply/demand times;
- candidate deep-mine geothermal extraction/storage designs, classified as modelled until separately supported by long-duration field data.

Report useful thermal service delivered by temperature band, parasitic electrical energy, heat actually rejected, thermal-storage loss, cooling service quality, and lifecycle cost. No credit for the same heat both being delivered to a useful sink and being counted as rejected or stored. Electrical output from low-grade heat recovery must be independently accounted and may not be assumed useful merely because a numerical multiphysics model predicts it.

**Evidence caution:** 2026 research includes numerical concepts for geothermal power/storage tunnels in deep mines, while a recent evidence-grading review distinguishes more mature mine-water/abandoned-mine heat-pump applications from active deep-mine heat recovery that still needs stronger long-term monitoring. These are research leads, not permission to size a real cooling or geothermal system from headline results.


## 6. Fixed baseline comparisons

Run the identical frozen scenario realization through:

- **B0 — fixed/reference plan:** predeclared equipment, routes, schedules and maintenance policy.
- **B1 — rule-based recovery:** fixed, reviewable fault-response and recovery rules.
- **B2 — evidence-bounded adaptive plan:** plans/replans from admitted current evidence and declared machine/tool capabilities.
- **B3 — exact small-instance oracle:** independently implemented exhaustive solution for tractable instances only; upper bound, not deployable performance or safety evidence.
- **B4 — measured project/field reference:** only when comparable source data and geological applicability are documented.

Freeze the corpus and metrics before tuning. Hold back independent cases from planner tuning and model calibration. Run identical faults and resource limits across candidates. Report all failed, blocked, incomplete and out-of-domain cases, not only successful runs.

## 7. Scenario manifest V0.1

Each case binds scenario_id, semantic version, fixed seed, initial state and evidence digests, units/frames, product specification, model/tool/power/process identities, resource limits, fault schedule, expected hard constraints, observable definitions, verifier version, stop/fallback rules, expected disposition, and claim ceiling.

| Case | Purpose | Required challenge |
|---|---|---|
| MS-001 Nominal control | Validate basic accounting and replay | Complete declared evidence; all stage boundaries explicit |
| MS-002 Abrasivity transition | Test cutting and maintenance coupling | Change rock class partway through; wear and specific energy must respond through a declared model |
| MS-003 Missing/stale geology | Test fail-closed planning | Critical spatial coverage missing or stale; dependent production claim must not remain eligible |
| MS-004 Water ingress | Couple uncertainty to real mine-service constraints | Pump/energy capacity, treatment and safety envelope bind; no free water removal |
| MS-005 Fleet/tool disruption | Test system output during failure | Cutter jam or unavailable machine plus route blockage; count repair/replanning/output consequences |
| MS-006 Power interruption | Compare energy resiliency | Source unavailable, buffer depleted or distribution limited; respect machine and site load envelopes |
| MS-007 Processing bottleneck | Detect displaced bottlenecks | Excavation increase cannot become saleable output when processing/quality capacity is binding |
| MS-008 Product/coproduct integrity | Prevent double counting | Recovery, grade or downstream qualification absent; do not credit a coproduct as saleable |
| MS-009 Economics under uncertainty | Test economic feasibility | Grade/price/energy/maintenance/closure assumptions change; report sensitivity and dominated/ineligible choices |
| MS-010 Compound disturbance | Test graceful degradation | Combine selected failures while preserving explicit stop, retreat, inspection and recovery semantics |

These cases are synthetic definitions, not a model of any actual mine or a claim that one source/architecture is suitable for a particular site.

## 8. Required outputs

Every run emits a canonical result/receipt with:
- corpus and schema version, scenario identity, exact input/source/model/tool/controller/power-profile and verifier digests;
- initial/final world generation or snapshot identity;
- accepted product quantities and quality disposition;
- subsystem energy and material ledgers, residuals, tolerances and uncertainty;
- excavation/haulage/processing rates with stated denominator and active-time convention;
- wear, downtime, failures, repairs, rework, reserves and recovery outcome;
- cost/emissions/water estimates with included/excluded items, calculation basis and evidence class;
- all hard-constraint outcomes, unresolved requirements, faults, stop/fallback actions and out-of-domain reasons;
- exact software revision and execution environment.

Canonical results must be independent of irrelevant input ordering and runtime scheduling. Missing required evidence, invalid units, non-finite quantities, stale result bindings, unsupported profiles or contradictory output must produce a typed non-success disposition.

## 9. Acceptance gates

**Contract correctness**
- deterministic replay of identical synthetic inputs;
- order-independent canonicalization where order is semantically irrelevant;
- exact unit/bounds validation and explicit absent/unknown/conflict states;
- material and energy residual emitted for every declared balance;
- fail-closed negative controls for missing/stale geology, unsupported tools/models, over-capacity loads, invalid units, stale results and hard-constraint breaches;
- zero false-safe eligible outcomes on the frozen hostile corpus.

**Engineering usefulness**
- baselines use identical exogenous conditions and resource budgets;
- metrics are stratified by geology, task and operating envelope;
- cost/energy improvement receives no credit if product quality/recovery or hard safety/environment constraints regress;
- uncertainty intervals are evaluated on held-out evidence when available;
- results identify the dominant bottleneck and the next decision-relevant measurement.

**Performance hypothesis**
On a preregistered matched synthetic scenario family, test whether B2 can reduce energy **or** delivered cost per accepted saleable tonne by at least 10% versus B0 without worsening accepted quality/recovery or breaking hard constraints. This is a falsifiable target, not a promised result. A null/adverse result should identify why adaptation failed.

No claim about real field productivity, commercial viability, mine safety or site suitability is allowed at E0. Hardware/field evidence requires separate review and is never inherited from a passing synthetic corpus.

## 10. Benchmark metrics dictionary

Every metric record must declare name, version, value, unit, numerator/denominator, aggregation window, source/evidence class, uncertainty, applicability, and inclusion/exclusion basis.

- **Production:** accepted saleable tonnes/shift; grade/specification; recovered yield; rejected/reworked lots.
- **Excavation:** advance metres per productive hour stratified by geology; removed volume; overbreak/support quantities; cutting energy per m³; cutter wear/replacement per removed volume.
- **Logistics:** kWh/t moved; queue/idle time; route completion and return reserve; hoist/conveyor availability.
- **Processing:** energy and water per tonne feed and per tonne saleable product; product yield/grade; residues/tailings; qualified versus uncredited coproducts.
- **Mine services:** ventilation, cooling, pumping and water-treatment energy/capacity; unserved demand and constraint violations.
- **Reliability:** productive availability, unscheduled downtime, repair lead time, spare-part stockout, mission recovery, inspection rejection/rework.
- **Economics/environment:** capex/opex basis; cost per accepted saleable tonne; sensitivity ranges; energy/emissions basis; water withdrawal/treatment; closure/rehabilitation cost and omitted-cost disclosure.
- **Risk and evidence:** hard-constraint false-safe count; stops/retreats; unknown/conflicting evidence dispositions; interval coverage on held-out measurements; unresolved applicability requirements.

A single weighted score is not a substitute for these component metrics and constraints.

## 10.1 External reference studies to reproduce or compare

These papers are reference baselines, not transferable guaranteed outcomes. Preserve their source, boundary, scenario, inputs and evidence tier in any comparison.

- [OECD Nuclear Energy Agency, SMRs for Mining: Opportunities and Challenges for Small Modular Reactors (2024)](https://www.oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_96131/smrs-for-mining-opportunities-and-challenges-for-small-modular-reactors): techno-economic screening for remote mines; cost estimates carry substantial uncertainty and are not proof of current site viability.
- [She et al., hard-rock TBM specific-energy / penetration optimization (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tust.2025.106585): a 219-interval database across five Chinese tunnel projects underlies a cutter-thrust and operating-parameter method. Reproduce only with attention to geology, TBM class, data leakage and external validation.
- [Zhang et al., digital-twin-enabled TBM real-time control (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2023.105240): a Singapore tunnel-project case reports a 21.12% aggregate improvement under its own chosen model, objectives and evaluation. This figure is not comparable by itself to cost/energy changes in other studies.
- [PHM Society European Conference (2026), trolley-assisted battery-electric underground LHDs](https://www.papers.phmsociety.org/index.php/phme/article/view/5024): simulation using a real Chilean copper-mine layout reports route-dependent charging/throughput and energy-cost findings. Retain its assumed vehicle model, route and cost boundary; don't generalize the headline maximum.
- [Xu et al., mine heat-hazard control, geothermal power and storage tunnel (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2026.140453): the paper reports a multiphysics numerical concept for combined heat regulation, storage and thermoelectric generation. Treat it as simulation evidence, not field-proven power output or a mine-cooling design.
- [Technology System and Engineering Evaluation of Mine Geothermal Resource Utilization Based on Evidence Grading (online September 2026)](https://cje.ustb.edu.cn/en/article/doi/10.13374/j.issn2095-9389.2026.07.08.007): distinguishes occurrence/potential from deliverable engineering service and notes that active-deep-mine heat recovery still needs stronger long-term monitoring than more mature mine-water/abandoned-mine heat-pump applications.

Never rank literature studies on their headline percentage alone. Different studies optimize different outputs, geologies, system boundaries, base cases, capital assumptions and evidence tiers.

## 11. Implementation sequence

1. Freeze this contract and small synthetic manifest.
2. Implement a dependency-light deterministic Rust reference evaluator and hostile tests; no networking, hosted runners or production Python dependency.
3. Bind the evaluator to existing Symtropy excavation, world-generation, tool-capability and fleet-resilience contracts only after exact interfaces/currentness are inspected.
4. Add explicit adapters for Symthaea site/resource/engineering evidence, Sol Atlas spatial evidence, Mycelix material/procurement/work provenance and externally owned energy-source interfaces.
5. Calibrate on documented published/partner data; evaluate on held-out cases. Keep real-data ingestion and any field/hardware campaign independently reviewable.

### Explicit non-goals

No new universal mine ledger; no geological resource/reserve certification; no proprietary site data assumption; no reactor/primary-system design, nuclear safety controls or licensing decision; no unvalidated fracture solver; no automatic real-world equipment dispatch; no universal economic score; no production/energy claims from generated data.

**The first successful result is an auditable bottleneck diagnosis and an honest comparison of feasible alternatives, not an assertion that a global underground economy is already technically or commercially solved.**
