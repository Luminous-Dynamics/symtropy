# Destination Portfolio Decision Protocol: From Attraction Optimization to Living Destinations

**Status:** research protocol v0.1; not a site assessment, certification, forecast, or investment recommendation.  
**Programme:** [GEO-DEST-001](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/1658)  
**Companion:** [regional adaptation guide](regional-adaptation-guide-v0.1.md) · [regional profile manifest](regional-adaptation-profiles-v0.1.json) · [experience portfolio](experience-portfolio-v0.1.json)

## Thesis

Do not optimize a destination for maximum attendance, land use, revenue, or attraction count in isolation. Design a portfolio of experiences and services that remains feasible under hard safety, legal, ecological, resource and rights constraints; performs usefully across plausible futures; creates demonstrable local value; and preserves options when evidence is weak.

Four governing rules:

1. **Constraints are not preferences.** Safety, lawful access, consent, resource limits and other legitimate hard constraints cannot be traded away for revenue or guest satisfaction.
2. **Unknown is a first-class result.** Missing local evidence is not a zero, a default coefficient, or permission to assume feasibility.
3. **Reversible decisions can beat nominally optimal irreversible ones.** A low-cost, informative pilot can be preferable to a large fixed asset when key demand, climate, community or engineering assumptions remain uncertain.
4. **There is no universal winning destination.** Keep Pareto trade-offs visible. A result for a water-stressed arid site must not silently become a recommendation for a temperate coast, cruise port or alpine valley.

## Paradigm shifts

| From | To | Engineering consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Attractions as independent products | Destination as a coupled service system | Model guest journeys, transit, utilities, workforce, habitat, host community and infrastructure together. |
| One fixed masterplan | A portfolio of composable, replaceable modules | Every module declares site needs, resources, accessibility, dependencies, closure modes and evidence gaps. |
| Attendance/revenue as primary success | Feasible experience quality plus local and ecological outcomes | Report dimensions separately; do not hide harms in a weighted average. |
| Static sustainability claims | Measured changes against a declared baseline | Track absolute loads, intensity metrics, distributions and uncertainty over a stated boundary and period. |
| One forecast and one “best” scenario | Robust decisions across divergent futures | Use scenario ensembles, Pareto comparisons, regret/fragility analysis and explicit no-build options. |
| Large up-front commitment | Stage-gated real options | Prefer reversible pilots and adaptive reuse when they generate information before irreversible capital expenditure. |
| Growth as the default | Right-sized, distributed and seasonal portfolios | Compare compact venues, existing assets and networks of local operators against new-build proposals. |
| Personalization through pervasive tracking | Guest agency, privacy and voluntary participation | Use aggregate or opt-in signals; provide non-digital access and equivalent offline routes. |
| Global brand with local decoration | Locally authored destination identity | Local rights-holders and communities govern cultural knowledge, customary access and place stories. |

OECD work on resilient destinations and social benefits supports place-specific strategies, diversified economies, local governance and evidence beyond visitor counts. GSTC destination criteria include local economic benefit, decent work, local entrepreneurs and traditional access. These are starting references, not claims that this system confers certification.

## The module contract: design for graceful degradation

Represent an experience as a module in a versioned **experience graph**, not a free-floating attraction card. A module must declare:

- **Intent:** the human experience it aims to support, without claiming a design guarantees an emotion or outcome.
- **Site requirements:** space, terrain, utilities, weather envelope, access and ecological dependencies.
- **Resource budget:** water, energy, materials, transport and waste flows with units, boundaries and evidence references.
- **Access profile:** physical, sensory, cognitive, language, cost and digital-access needs; identify unmet needs rather than assigning one generic accessibility score.
- **Operations:** staffing, maintenance, throughput range, service dependencies and responsible operator.
- **Hard constraints:** applicable safety/engineering reviews, permits, local rights, ecological limits and authority references.
- **Degraded modes:** reduce capacity, switch to a non-dependent alternative, suspend the module, or close it under named conditions.
- **Evidence:** baseline, provenance, model/code identity, assumptions, uncertainty, independent checks and unresolved questions.
- **Reversibility:** removal/restoration obligations, committed capital, conversion options, sunk-cost exposure and next decision gate.
- **Local value:** local procurement, workforce and supplier arrangements, ownership/benefit distribution and grievance/accountability channels.

The presence of a field does not prove a real safety envelope, accessibility conformity, ecological tolerance, forecast or legal approval. A complete schema is not proof of a feasible place.

### One intent, many local realizations

The intent **cool, rest and recover** might be expressed as a shaded garden, quiet indoor room, ventilated pavilion, public cooling partner network, or a water-adjacent space only where local safety, water and ecological conditions support it. The platform compares their trade-offs; it does not export the same architecture everywhere.

A snow-dependent module needs explicit natural-snow, weather, staffing, resource and closure assumptions. A shore excursion belongs to a coupled ship–port–transport–community itinerary, not only to a ship's attraction inventory.

## Decision protocol

### Gate 0 — establish the rights and feasibility envelope

Before ranking concepts, identify the site boundary and competent authorities; document relevant rights-holders and community processes; gather site-specific hazard, ecology, water, energy, transport, land-use and cultural-access evidence. Set hard constraints with accountable owners.

Fail closed when required authority, safety evidence, legal basis or critical site input is absent. This means **not qualified to proceed**, not necessarily that the concept is universally impossible. Keep the missing evidence visible and state how it could be obtained.

### Gate 1 — compare meaningfully different alternatives

For every high-commitment proposal, include alternatives appropriate to the site:

1. Current/base case, including no new build.
2. Maintenance or adaptive reuse of existing assets.
3. A reversible seasonal or pop-up pilot.
4. The proposed new-build or infrastructure-heavy alternative.
5. A smaller or distributed portfolio using local operators and existing facilities.
6. A managed-transition or closure path where an environmental or operational dependency may become infeasible.

Not every site needs every option; record why an option is excluded. “Do nothing” is a comparator, not an assumption that the status quo is harmless.

### Gate 2 — construct a scenario set, not a point forecast

Include locally material combinations of demand, seasonality, severe weather, utility availability, costs, workforce, transport, regulation and community-defined priorities. Separate measured inputs, sourced projections, expert judgements and synthetic test values. Record units, time horizon, dependencies and uncertainty.

Use paired comparisons: same starting state and scenario, changing one declared intervention. Add negative controls for zero demand, unavailable services, closed routes, stale/missing observations, resource-limit breach and model/version mismatch. Use independent accounting checks where possible. Keep calibration data separate from held-out evaluation.

### Gate 3 — enforce hard constraints before trade-offs

For every scenario classify candidates as:

- **Infeasible:** at least one established hard constraint is violated.
- **Not evaluable:** critical evidence is missing, stale, unqualified or disputed.
- **Feasible under declared assumptions:** encoded constraints pass within declared scope; this is not real-world certification.

Neither an aggregate score nor a strong financial return may override a hard failure or not-evaluable status. Safety-related scenario outputs remain decision-support only until the accountable qualified authority has reviewed and accepted them.

### Gate 4 — expose the outcome vector

Report dimensions separately, with baselines, units, boundaries, source quality and uncertainty. At minimum consider:

- **Visitor experience:** journey and wait-time distributions, crowding, accessibility gaps, comfort, affordability and choice; use opt-in or aggregate feedback without requiring identity tracking.
- **Workforce:** job quality, pay and contract conditions, seasonality, training, safety, workload, retention and advancement.
- **Host community:** resident-defined priorities, access to shared places, transport/public-service burden, noise/light, housing pressure where relevant, grievance resolution and legitimacy.
- **Local economy:** local procurement and wage flows, retained value, local SME participation, ownership/benefit distribution, seasonal resilience and economic leakage.
- **Ecology and resources:** absolute and per-visitor water/energy/material use, wastewater and waste, habitat/noise/light impacts, restoration obligations and measured change from baseline.
- **Climate and mobility:** operational and embodied emissions where evidenced, guest/staff transport, shipment dependencies and sensitivity to energy/fuel assumptions.
- **Resilience and option value:** performance across scenarios, dependency concentration, recovery/closure costs, capital at risk, conversion/removal costs and future choices preserved.

Use both absolute and intensity indicators. Intensity can improve while total burden grows; neither view alone is enough. Do not combine incomparable or ethically non-substitutable outcomes into an opaque “regeneration score.”

### Gate 5 — select robust options, not an unqualified winner

First remove infeasible and not-evaluable alternatives from the qualified set. Then:

1. Identify Pareto-dominated candidates on the agreed outcome vector.
2. Show the trade-offs among remaining candidates.
3. Test whether conclusions survive plausible input changes and declared stakeholder preferences.
4. Report scenario-specific failures, downside, regret and dependency concentration.
5. Identify which additional observation is most likely to change the decision.
6. Preserve multiple candidates where evidence cannot support a defensible ordering.

Weights, normalization, missing-data treatment and stakeholder preferences must be declared before comparative results are viewed. Do not use one global weight set for all places. If a single decision is required, document who has legitimate decision authority, which trade-offs were accepted, who bears the downside and why.

### Gate 6 — buy information before buying irreversibility

Every material investment needs a hypothesis, a reversible test where feasible, a maximum exposure, a duration, an owner, an independent review method, predeclared stop/continue criteria and a restoration/exit plan.

A pilot passes only for the tested claim and context. It does not automatically license scaling, another region, another season, or a safety claim. Failed pilots should produce reusable evidence, not be relabelled as successes. If no safe or ethical pilot exists, seek qualified studies, compare existing evidence or reject the proposal.

### Gate 7 — publish a decision receipt

Record candidate and scenario identities, data/source versions, code and model hashes, transformations, assumptions, hard constraints, independent checks, results and uncertainty, disagreements, community/authority decisions, unresolved objections, selected and rejected alternatives, investment gate and reviewer sign-off. Preserve negative controls and adverse outcomes.

Public outputs should expose evidence without disclosing personal, commercially sensitive or culturally restricted information. A community's choice not to disclose sacred or sensitive knowledge must not be treated as missing consent the optimizer can work around.

## Suggested system responsibilities

- **Symtropy:** versioned scenario manifests, experience graphs, paired comparisons, constraint evaluation, resource/flow accounting interfaces, Pareto and robustness reports, and auditable decision receipts.
- **Symthaea:** advisory analysis, hypotheses, anomaly discovery, uncertainty summaries and selection of the next informative experiment. It must not silently change hard constraints, invent missing local data, certify safety, or autonomously actuate physical equipment.
- **Mycelix:** optional, privacy-preserving records of commitments, community-approved governance decisions, local supplier/benefit agreements and reviewable evidence claims. Technical attestations do not replace legal authority, informed consent or a community's right to say no.
- **Human decision owners:** community rights-holders, competent regulators, qualified engineers and safety professionals, local operators and accountable project owners retain real-world authority.

These are integration hypotheses and proposed boundaries, not claims that the current repositories already implement them.

## A low-capital path to learning and commercial value

The first product should be an **evidence-backed destination design and option analysis service**, not a speculative mega-park.

1. Deliver a synthetic demonstration that makes constraints, assumptions, trade-offs and unknowns inspectable.
2. Offer paid, bounded discovery for an existing attraction, small resort or local destination network: baseline, option set, flow/resource map, community questions, pilot design and decision receipt.
3. Build reusable Rust tooling around repeated needs: manifest validation, scenario comparison, units/provenance, independent oracles and report generation.
4. Run a supervised pilot only with site access, data permission, qualified reviewers and a clear validation plan.
5. Turn repeated validated workflows into licensable tooling and ongoing monitoring; do not market synthetic outputs as proof of real impact or regulatory approval.

The customer value proposition is reduced decision uncertainty and fewer avoidable commitments—not a promise that a design will succeed. Scope and price discovery around a specific deliverable and accountable customer decision; avoid capital-heavy construction before demand, feasibility and execution partners are evidenced.

## Initial falsifiable research programme

| Experiment | Hypothesis | Required comparator | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portfolio vs. attraction-level planning | Coupled models expose constraints or costs missed by isolated attraction optimization | Same demand, site and assumptions; independent modules vs. coupled operations | No material difference under declared scenarios, or differences fail independent checks |
| Reversible pilot vs. early fixed capex | Staged pilots reduce downside or improve information value when uncertainty is high | Predeclared pilot cost, decision delay and outcomes vs. irreversible option under matching scenarios | Pilot adds cost/delay without meaningful information or risk reduction |
| Uniform template vs. local profiles | Local profiles change feasible options and trade-offs | Same candidate portfolio with generic assumptions vs. locally sourced evidence | Local inputs do not change decisions where expected, or changes cannot be independently explained |
| Attendance vs. local-value measures | High attendance can diverge from retained benefits and resident outcomes | Attendance/revenue dashboard vs. local-value and resident outcome measures | Measures stay aligned across tested cases; retain the simpler system if it suffices |
| Single score vs. Pareto/robustness report | A weighted score hides important trade-offs or brittle choices | Predeclared scalar ranking vs. per-outcome and multi-scenario output | No meaningful trade-off is hidden in tested decisions |

Publish results whether positive, null or adverse. Synthetic fixtures prove software behavior, not real-world impact. Use site observations and qualified review for empirical claims. One successful case does not prove generality.
