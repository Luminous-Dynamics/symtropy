# Grid Route Legitimacy — v0.1 research baseline

**Owner:** [GRID-ROUTE-LEGITIMACY-001, Symtropy #6148](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/issues/6148)  
**Status:** Synthetic process/evidence contract. No legal interpretation, actual consent, permit, land right or work authorisation is claimed.  
**Snapshot date:** 2026-11-18

## 1. Why route deliverability needs its own model

A least-cost electrical/geospatial corridor may not be a deliverable corridor. Route delivery can depend on title/tenure, customary and collective rights, statutory consent, public participation, environmental/water/cultural review, servitudes, access arrangements, compensation or mitigation instruments, appeals and workfront-specific conditions.

The IEA's *Building the Future Transmission Grid* identifies permitting as a primary cause of transmission project delays. The UK government's 27 April 2026 response treats consents, land access and land rights as distinct delivery issues. The European Commission's 2025 grids communication records delays to electricity Projects of Common Interest and identifies public acceptance alongside permitting as a barrier. South Africa's DFFE February 2024 electricity infrastructure standard describes registered servitudes, with widths varying by power-line class and local context.

These are jurisdiction-specific sources and reported observations, not a universal permitting-duration benchmark.

The DFFE standard's referenced servitude widths (15–80 m depending on line class/context) are not a generic global setback or a substitute for site-specific legal/engineering requirements.

## 3. Paradigm shift: legitimate, deliverable pathways

Route alternatives should be evaluated across:
- electrical and civil feasibility;
- land/tenure and rights-holder evidence;
- environmental, water, biodiversity and cultural constraints;
- public participation and due process;
- mitigation and benefit commitments;
- schedule, cost and construction access.

Do not collapse these into one opaque cost penalty. Publish the impact vector and its distribution. A route that is cheaper but causes unresolved rights impacts is not an automatically acceptable Pareto candidate.

The model deliberately keeps these concepts distinct: stakeholder participation; consultation/response records; community agreement; private or collective land agreement; servitude/land-access instrument; statutory environmental authorisation; other competent-authority decision; and workfront-specific release.

None substitutes for another. Silence or absence of objections is never evidence of consent.

## 4. v0.1 process contract

The schema defines a jurisdiction profile and source URLs; an explicit evidence snapshot date; route and service boundary; anonymised rights-holder/stakeholder categories (no personal names/contact data); evidence records with maturity and source boundary; decision instruments with distinct decision types/status/effective periods; process gates with accountable role, evidence and exact required decision types; engagement records, access/language/notice and concern-resolution records; workfront status, required gates and release evidence; and impact records kept as separate dimensions.

A gate marked complete must carry evidence. Public participation, land access/servitude, statutory-decision and compensation gates require explicit decision types when marked complete. The referenced decision type must match the gate, and the decision must be effective and unexpired at the case snapshot date. A released workfront must have every required gate complete and release evidence with an authority/instrument class. Synthetic fixtures can never assert a released workfront.

The synthetic fixture intentionally has incomplete rights-holder, participation, environmental and land-access gates. Its workfront is not released.

## 5. Rights and privacy principles

- Store stakeholder categories and anonymised IDs, not unnecessary personal information.
- Record representation basis and verification gaps.
- Distinguish private owners, tenants, workers, communal/customary holders, Indigenous peoples where applicable, residents, businesses, public users, municipalities and authorities.
- Record notice delivery, language and accessibility.
- Preserve concerns, objections, responses, resolution status and appeal status.
- Never infer consent from non-response, meeting attendance, survey participation, map proximity or absence of an objection.
- A community agreement cannot replace a statutory authorisation; a statutory authorisation cannot itself prove that a private/collective land-access instrument was executed.
- Expose unknowns, disputed issues and unresolved commitments instead of optimistically defaulting them to complete.

## 6. Proposed benchmark metrics

Measure:
- p50/p90/p95 duration by process gate where records support distributions;
- statutory review/public participation windows separately from applicant rework and project inactivity;
- first-submission completeness, clarification loops and redesign attributable to unresolved constraints;
- affected parcels/rights-holder categories by tenure and impact type;
- outstanding rights-holder identification and representation gaps;
- grievance age, response time, resolution and appeal outcomes;
- workfront release time and access interruption;
- mitigation/benefit commitments due, funded, delivered and verified;
- land footprint, livelihood, biodiversity, water, cultural heritage, access and displacement impacts separately;
- cost of consent, redesign, compensation/mitigation and delay;
- distribution of impacts and benefits without collapsing them into one equity score.

Proposed internal gates (not achievements):
- 100% of legal/authority gates and workfront conditions linked to a jurisdiction, current source, accountable owner and evidence or explicitly marked unknown;
- zero false acceptance of consent, land access, statutory approval or workfront release;
- zero inferred consent from silence or lack of objections;
- reduce p95 avoidable applicant rework/process time by 20% on one independently reviewed archetype without shortening statutory participation windows, worsening impact distribution or increasing unresolved grievances;
- publish disputes, appeals, stop-work events and unresolved commitments next to schedule outcomes.

## 7. Claim boundary

This is research and audit-support only. It does not provide legal opinions, determine sufficiency of consultation, adjudicate rights, set compensation, grant permits, establish social licence or authorize access/construction. A validator pass is not a permit, consent, servitude, compensation agreement or approval. Competent authorities, landowners, affected rights holders and qualified professionals retain those roles.
