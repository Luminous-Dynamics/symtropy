# Non-GNSS construction-site integrity benchmark track — 22 November 2026

## Strategic rationale

A 2025 survey of localization integrity literature reports that 73.3% of surveyed papers focus on GNSS, while 26.7% explore non-GNSS approaches such as fusion, optimization and SLAM; it also identifies relatively few protection-level calculations in non-GNSS work. This is a useful gap to investigate, not proof that any one non-GNSS architecture is novel.

Reference: https://doi.org/10.3390/s25020358

## Hilti–Trimble–Oxford 360 visual-inertial construction benchmark

The 2026 Hilti–Trimble–Oxford benchmark adds a realistic construction-site VIO/SLAM and floor-plan localization track. It contains 30 visual-inertial recordings across ten building levels/floors over an eight-month active construction project. Data include two 30 Hz dual-fisheye camera streams, 1 kHz IMU, floor-plan priors, calibration artifacts and per-sequence LiDAR-inertial reference trajectories.

### Official benchmark results and direct comparators

The official 2026 leaderboard records:

- SLAM winner ACDC-K / KAIST: **2,410.0 / 2,500**.
- Floor-plan localization winner ETH–THU–Stuttgart: **2,196.3 / 2,400**.
- SLAM runner-up SPARO / Inha University: **2,393.9 / 2,500**.
- Localization runner-up SnT-ARG / University of Luxembourg: **2,161.4 / 2,400**.

The SLAM winner ACDC-VSLAM uses spherical equal-area feature selection, point-and-line constraints, reliability/depth-weighted local bundle adjustment and two-stage intra-/cross-camera loop closure with adaptive SE(3), Sim(3) or 4-DoF corrections. The winning localization pipeline uses OKVIS2-X with online extrinsic calibration and floor-plan-based global anchoring.

SPARO's second-place report states 0.109 m mean RMSE, 0.095 m mean position error and 99.85% mean trajectory coverage. These are published challenge results, not Luminous reproductions.

### What the official benchmark does not measure

The official evaluator emphasizes trajectory completeness and an exponential per-pose position-accuracy score. It requires at least 99% reference-pose coverage or assigns zero for that run. It scores 25 SLAM sequences and 24 localization sequences. It does not by itself establish protection-level coverage, calibrated integrity risk, false-safe exposure, breach duration, service-state correctness, time-to-alert or qualified recovery.

That distinction is the opportunity: reproduce the official accuracy score, then report the evidence and service dimensions alongside it.

### Critical data and oracle limitations

- The reference is generated by a Hesai XT32 LiDAR-inertial system rigidly attached to the camera rig; the raw LiDAR measurements are not released. The reference cannot be regenerated from the public camera/IMU inputs alone.
- The reference LiDAR/IMU system is hardware synchronized by PPS. The independent Insta360 camera/IMU clock is aligned using gyro-norm correlation and nonlinear time-offset/clock-scale refinement, with gyro-bias optimization.
- The published data have a constant 10,000-second timestamp shift; the official evaluator excludes the first five seconds because the reference starts later than the camera.
- Floor plans are stated to be not officially registered. Floor-plan map accuracy and map-to-reference alignment therefore require separate qualification.
- The repository commit pins source/config/documentation, not the Hugging Face payload bytes. File-level hashes and a frozen payload revision are still required.

## Three-track physical benchmark portfolio

| Track | Dataset / scope | Main comparison | Primary unresolved qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Urban GNSS and multisensor integrity | UrbanNav-HK-Medium-Urban-1 | GNSS, GNSS/INS, LiDAR/INS, Student-t PL, adaptive PID PL | payload hashes, F9P malfunction interval, SPAN-CPT/IE truth lineage and 1 Hz vs 400 Hz time alignment |
| Indoor UWB and cooperative vision | MILUV | UWB/SINS, camera/IMU, multi-UAV fusion and active sensing | exact payload/license and independent experiment-level splits, clock/frame calibration |
| Construction-site visual-inertial / floor-plan SLAM | Hilti–Trimble–Oxford 360 VI | OpenVINS, Stella-VSLAM, OKVIS2-X, ACDC-VSLAM, floor-plan anchoring | CC BY-NC-SA restrictions, no raw reference LiDAR, clock correlation and unregistered floorplans |

Do not pool these datasets into one headline number. Treat them as distinct operating domains and compare both per-domain and worst-domain performance. A single-domain win is not cross-domain generalization.

## Common integrity extension

On every track, preserve the dataset's official accuracy baseline and add:

1. support provenance: fresh qualified measurement, bounded dead reckoning, unqualified measurement, interpolation, extrapolation or missing output;
2. protection-level coverage, pointwise exceedance, breach episodes, false-safe exposure and maximum contiguous breach;
3. abstention/unavailable intervals and valid-reference coverage; never silently discard them;
4. error growth with time/distance since last qualified observation;
5. detection delay, safe fallback and requalification/recovery;
6. source-dependency and common-cause failure slices;
7. p50/p95/p99 runtime, compute, memory and energy per route.

The post-matched trajectory-support evaluator is implemented in [PR #6781](https://github.com/Luminous-Dynamics/symtropy/pull/6781). It reports the 99%-style output coverage independently from evidence-backed pose coverage and NORMAL-without-qualified-support exposure. This is an evaluation layer only; it is not a localization estimator or physical result.

## Recommended sequence

1. Admit and reproduce UrbanNav Medium-Urban-1 first because it directly matches the published adaptive-PL work.
2. In parallel, freeze and qualify one Hilti early-release development run and at least one held-out run, preserving CC BY-NC-SA terms; run official accuracy baselines before integrity extensions.
3. Use MILUV as the UWB/indoor fusion track after payload and rights qualification.
4. Require whole-session/floor/date/device splits and independent cluster-level uncertainty. Do not treat 30 Hilti sequences as 30 independent missions without analysing shared building, equipment, clock, mapping and data-collection dependencies.
5. Publish accuracy, evidence-backed coverage, false-safe exposure, recovery and resource results separately.
