## Why this candidate matters

The 2026 Hilti–Trimble–Oxford benchmark provides a second, non-GNSS track alongside UrbanNav and MILUV. The dataset contains 30 visual-inertial recording sequences across ten building levels/floors collected over an eight-month active construction project. The task is both ordinary SLAM trajectory estimation and floor-plan-referenced localization.

- Dataset: https://hilti-challenge.com/dataset-2026

## Sensors, calibration and reference truth

The released ROS 2 recordings contain two 30 Hz wide-FOV fisheye camera streams and raw IMU at 1,000 Hz. The camera is an Insta360 One-RS 1-Inch 360 Edition. The repository supplies camera/IMU calibration for pinhole-equidistant and EUCM camera models, floor-plan priors, ROS tools and per-sequence reference trajectories.

The reference trajectory was generated by a Hesai XT32 LiDAR-inertial mapping device rigidly attached to the camera rig. The raw LiDAR measurements are not included in the released dataset. The reference is therefore useful for evaluation, but it cannot be independently regenerated from the released input modalities alone.

Time alignment is a critical qualification item. Ground-truth LiDAR and IMU are hardware-synchronized by PPS. The independently running Insta360 system is aligned by correlating gyroscope-norm signals, followed by nonlinear refinement of time offset and clock-scale factor and joint gyroscope-bias optimization. The published recordings also apply a constant 10,000-second shift to avoid negative IMU timestamps. The first five seconds of every run are excluded by the official evaluator because the reference LiDAR trajectory starts later than the camera.

The registry conservatively records time and spatial-reference status as `unqualified`, calibration as `partial`, truth as `partial`, and clock-state truth as `unknown`. These are admission states, not assertions that the upstream work is defective. The exact sensor transformations, time mapping, floorplan registration and reference independence still need an external audit.

## Direct SOTA / industry baseline

The official challenge evaluates:

- SLAM in an arbitrary reference frame: 25 of the 30 sequences; five early-release sequences with public ground truth are excluded.
- Floor-plan Localization: 24 of the 30 sequences; the same five early-release sequences plus one run without a floorplan are excluded.
- Each submitted run must cover at least 99% of its reference poses or its score is set to zero.
- The score is an exponential per-pose position-error score (100 at zero error and 1 at 10 m), summed over the evaluated runs.

The published 2026 leaderboard lists ACDC-K / KAIST at 2,410.0/2,500 for SLAM and ETH–THU–Stuttgart at 2,196.3/2,400 for floor-plan Localization. The winning SLAM approach, ACDC-VSLAM, combines spherical equal-area feature distribution, point and line constraints, reliability/depth-weighted local bundle adjustment, and intra-/cross-camera loop closure with adaptive SE(3), Sim(3) or 4-DoF correction. The winning Localization approach uses OKVIS2-X with online extrinsic calibration and floorplan-based global anchoring.

A second-place SPARO report states 2,393.9/2,500, 0.109 m mean RMSE, 0.095 m mean position error and 99.85% mean trajectory coverage. These are team-reported official evaluator results, not Luminous reproductions. They provide a concrete matched accuracy target.

The official score is an accuracy/completeness leaderboard, not an integrity evaluation. It does not measure protection-level coverage, false-safe exposure, breach duration, time-to-alert, uncertainty calibration, service abstention or recovery correctness. Luminous should preserve the official score as a compatibility track while introducing these additional integrity and service tracks.

## Proposed Luminous benchmark protocol

### Track A — official accuracy reproduction

Reproduce official challenge trajectory output format, coverage threshold and score exactly. Use the five early-release sequences only for development/calibration; do not score them as held-out test data. Match the official first-five-second exclusion and the camera-to-map pose convention.

Required baselines:

- Official OpenVINS configuration (including the challenge's EUCM/rolling-shutter/time settings).
- Stella-VSLAM on the prescribed stitched panoramas.
- OKVIS2-X baseline and the published localization path where licensing/configuration permits.
- ACDC-VSLAM / winning team result as a published reference; inspect source availability and licence before code reuse.
- A reproducible high-performing public submission, such as the SPARO second-place report, as an additional comparator.

### Track B — integrity and uncertainty

Keep the same predicted poses and reference trajectory, but add independent offline evaluation:

- Position-error p50/p95/p99/max and along-/cross-track errors in a declared local frame.
- Empirical coverage of declared horizontal/3D protection levels at per-check and run level.
- Pointwise exceedance, distinct breach episodes, total exposure above PL, maximum contiguous breach and maximum bound exceedance.
- Unavailable/abstained trajectory intervals and valid coverage, never silently dropped.
- Error growth versus time/distance since last trusted observation and map/loop closure.
- Calibration by run/floor/illumination/texture/motion condition, with whole-run/floor/date held-out partitions.
- Time-to-detect and time-to-integrity-alert after controlled or naturally labelled degradation.

Do not use the official 99% coverage threshold as permission to omit the remaining 1% from integrity reporting. Report all missing/abstained intervals and whether a safe downstream service could continue.

### Track C — service and recovery

Use whole runs and dates as the indivisible units. Assess repeated traverses, changes in lighting and construction state, dynamic workers, aggressive motion, low texture, cross-camera revisits, initialization gaps and floor-plan mismatch. Measure:

- service-state confusion and time spent NORMAL/DEGRADED/INTEGRITY_UNCERTAIN/UNAVAILABLE/RECOVERING;
- false restoration to NORMAL;
- time to detect degradation, safe fallback and requalification;
- continuity of the estimated trajectory and map consistency;
- compute time, memory and energy per run on a declared machine.

Recovery tests should include initialization gaps, loop-closure outliers, cross-camera false matches, camera occlusion/low-light bursts, IMU bias shifts, timestamp perturbations, floor-plan registration offsets and a stale or inconsistent map. Synthetic perturbations must be distinguished from faults actually present in the dataset.

## Leakage-resistant splits

Use entire route sessions as one group. For generalization, use leave-one-floor-out, leave-one-date-out and leave-one-condition-class-out folds. Keep repeated or overlapping sequences from the same physical recording together. The five early-release runs may serve as development data but must not be used to claim held-out performance on the leaderboard subset.

Do not claim 30 independent mission trials merely because 30 recordings exist: many share the same site, building, sensors, capture rig, mapping device and collection workflow. Use grouped or hierarchical uncertainty estimates and disclose the independence unit.

## What a Luminous advantage would mean

A superior leaderboard score alone would mean improved trajectory accuracy under the official scoring function. It would not establish integrity. The stronger claim to pursue is:

1. match or exceed the official accuracy/coverage frontier on the held-out official sequence set;
2. provide calibrated uncertainty/protection levels whose empirical upper confidence limits satisfy a declared profile-specific budget;
3. reduce false-safe exposure and maximum contiguous breaches without sacrificing availability;
4. detect degradation and abstain before a downstream hazard-response deadline;
5. recover without false return to NORMAL;
6. preserve or improve compute/energy performance under a frozen hardware budget.

No one of these is currently achieved by Luminous on this dataset. No Hilti payload files have been downloaded or admitted in this work.
