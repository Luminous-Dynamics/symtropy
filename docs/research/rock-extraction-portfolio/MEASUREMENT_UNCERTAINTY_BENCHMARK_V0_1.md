# Correlated measurement uncertainty benchmark v0.1

**Status:** bounded Monte Carlo propagation implementation with fabricated conformance data. It does not qualify physical probability models or implement the entire GUM/JCGM suite.

## 1. Standards basis

### JCGM 100 Amendment 1:2026 — nonlinear measurement models

The 2026 amendment to JCGM 100:2008 addresses nonlinearity in measurement models. When nonlinearity is significant, the amendment points to Monte Carlo propagation under JCGM 101 or retention of higher-order Taylor terms; it also generalizes the treatment of non-independent normally distributed inputs.

Official amendment:
https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/2071204/JCGM_100_Amd1_2026.pdf/

### JCGM 101:2008 — one output quantity

JCGM 101 specifies propagation of input probability distributions through a measurement model by Monte Carlo methods. It treats an arbitrary number of input quantities and one output quantity.

https://www.bipm.org/en/doi/10.59161/jcgm101-2008

### JCGM 102:2011 — multiple output quantities

JCGM 102 extends the method to models with any number of output quantities. This matters when energy intensity, recovery, residual and mass-balance outputs derive from the same correlated physical measurements.

BIPM GUM suite:
https://www.bipm.org/en/web/guest/publications/guides

### NIST AMS 100-85 (5 October 2026)

NIST recommends harmonizing uncertainty quantification with metrology, using distribution propagation where appropriate, and scaling VVUQ to model influence and decision consequence.

https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AMS.100-85

## 2. Paradigm shift

Do not calculate a derived value and attach an unrelated uncertainty number after the fact.

Instead, carry the joint input distribution through the same nonlinear equation that produces the measurand. Examples:
- total-boundary energy / accepted solid volume;
- accepted / generated volume;
- floor residual / generated volume;
- generated − accepted − fines − reject − deposit − residual;
- accepted dry-solid mass / complete-cycle elapsed time.

The covariance between inputs can materially change the output distribution. A ratio of uncertain inputs is not necessarily symmetric or approximately normal. The output may be skewed or heavy-tailed even when the input models are simple.

## 3. v0.1 joint input model

Each input declares:
- unique ID, measurement unit, seven-component SI dimension vector;
- conversion from its declared unit into coherent SI;
- a NORMAL, LOGNORMAL, UNIFORM or TRIANGULAR marginal distribution;
- parameter values in the declared input unit (lognormal parameters in natural-log space);
- calibration, method, source and parameter-validation references;
- evidence role (independent measurement, external calibration, expert elicitation or simulation only).

All inputs are coupled by a **latent Gaussian-copula correlation matrix**. The matrix coefficients are correlations of the latent standard-normal variables, not generally Pearson correlations of the physical marginal inputs. The matrix must be symmetric, have a unit diagonal, have coefficients in [-1,1], and be strictly positive definite. v0.1 refuses singular/invalid matrices and does not silently add jitter.

Correlated latent normals are generated by Cholesky transformation of independent standard normals. NORMAL marginals use the latent values directly; LOGNORMAL, UNIFORM and TRIANGULAR marginals use their declared inverse-marginal transformations. The Gaussian CDF uses the documented Abramowitz–Stegun 7.1.26 approximation for uniform/triangular transforms; the approximate CDF's finite precision is part of the v0.1 numerical method.

The Gaussian copula is an explicit dependence assumption, not a universal truth. It can fail to represent tail dependence, nonlinear rank dependence, common-cause calibration drift or mixture regimes. The matrix itself needs physical or statistical evidence; its presence in a schema does not prove it is correct.

## 4. Measurement equation and dimensional analysis

The measurement equation is a reverse-Polish instruction sequence, not arbitrary executable code. v0.1 supports:
- INPUT and CONSTANT;
- ADD and SUBTRACT;
- MULTIPLY and DIVIDE;
- SQRT, LOG, EXP and ABS.

Each value carries a seven-exponent SI dimension vector in this order:
`[length, mass, time, electric_current, thermodynamic_temperature, amount_of_substance, luminous_intensity]`.

Input values are converted to SI before evaluation. Rules:
- addition/subtraction require equal dimensions;
- multiplication adds exponents;
- division subtracts exponents;
- square root requires even exponents;
- logarithm/exponential require a dimensionless argument;
- declared output dimensions must match the equation result;
- output conversion from SI is explicit.

Dimensional analysis does not validate the physical adequacy of the measurement equation itself. That remains the responsibility of the equation owner/reviewer and its source references.

## 5. Sampling and numerical result

The engine uses deterministic SplitMix64 + Box–Muller standard-normal generation, with an explicit seed and PRNG version. It is reproducible on the supported implementation/target; it is not cryptographic randomness, and transcendental functions may differ across platform math libraries.

Each sample:
1. generates independent standard-normal variates;
2. applies the latent correlation Cholesky factor;
3. transforms to each declared marginal;
4. converts inputs to SI;
5. evaluates all output equations from the **same input draw**;
6. records invalid/domain-failing outputs rather than clipping, resampling or zero-filling.

For each output the report provides:
- requested/valid/invalid sample counts and invalid fraction;
- mean, sample standard deviation and standard error of mean;
- median and configured quantiles;
- declared central coverage interval;
- finite-Monte-Carlo confidence interval for each estimated quantile;
- batch-wise numerical drift and terminal-stability disposition.

The finite-Monte-Carlo interval for an output quantile is a distribution-free order-statistic interval. For n independent Monte Carlo draws and target quantile p, the number B of draws at or below the true quantile follows Binomial(n,p). An interval `[X_(r), X_(s)]` has coverage `P(r <= B <= s-1)`. The engine selects equal-tailed binomial ranks at the declared confidence. This interval quantifies numerical sampling uncertainty in the estimated output quantile; it is different from the physical output's central coverage interval.

Batch stability is a diagnostic. The evaluator tracks cumulative mean, standard deviation and quantiles, records the last transition exceeding declared drift tolerances, and requires a terminal stable run after it. A stable-looking early batch does not override a later drift. Stability is not a proof of convergence or a confidence interval for every distributional feature.

No single sample count is asserted to be universally adequate. The required number depends on output tails, coverage, precision and the declared model.

## 6. Synthetic golden case

The bundled synthetic fixture has four inputs:
- energy: normal, 30 ± 1.2 kWh;
- accepted volume: lognormal in m³;
- generated volume: triangular in m³;
- residual volume: uniform in m³.

The fixture declares a latent Gaussian-copula correlation matrix and three simultaneous outputs:
- specific energy in kWh/m³;
- recovery fraction;
- residual fraction.

It uses seed 20261107, 262,144 trials and eight batches of 32,768. Independent reference replay gives approximately:

| Output | Mean | Sample SD | p05 | p50 | p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Specific energy (kWh/m³) | 3.01317 | 0.30112 | 2.54239 | 2.99894 | 3.53051 |
| Recovery fraction | 0.83606 | 0.06670 | 0.73073 | 0.83343 | 0.95005 |
| Residual fraction | 0.02912 | 0.00953 | 0.01426 | 0.02914 | 0.04386 |

The synthetic specific-energy p05 estimator's 99% Monte Carlo confidence interval is approximately [2.53961, 2.54503], with achieved binomial coverage around 0.99002. The shared draws also yield a strong negative output correlation between specific energy and recovery fraction (approximately -0.892).

All parameters, dependencies and outputs above are fabricated arithmetic/conformance data, not mine measurements or economics. Because the fixture inputs are simulation-only, the evaluator must report SYNTHETIC_ONLY and must not treat its PASS gates as physical qualification.

## 7. Tournament and negative controls

- linear independent-Gaussian equation versus closed-form mean/variance;
- correlated linear-Gaussian equation versus analytical covariance;
- nonlinear ratio versus independent reference simulation;
- correlated versus independent latent input structures;
- singular / near-singular correlation rejection without jitter;
- asymmetry/skew of ratio output;
- dimensional addition mismatch;
- invalid SQRT/LOG/DIVIDE operations;
- exponential overflow and non-finite outputs;
- invalid samples counted, never clipped, resampled or zero-filled;
- finite-Monte-Carlo quantile precision at a deliberately insufficient sample count;
- late batch drift resetting terminal numerical stability;
- repeated same-seed replay on the supported target;
- joint output covariance and correlation compared with an independent reference.

Software gates are conformance targets. They do not qualify any physical model, instrument or mine.

## 9. Non-claims

This is not a complete GUM implementation, not JCGM certification, and not proof that supplied marginal PDFs or latent correlations represent a real mine. It does not learn unknown correlations, perform Bayesian inference, simulate physical fragmentation/transport, set operating conditions or authorize engineering decisions. Measured-input outputs require independent metrology and engineering review; simulation-only results stay synthetic.
